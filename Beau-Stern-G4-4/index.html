Error #1: Run-On Sentence

This is a Step 5 skill: proofread for sentence boundaries

Beau, on Q6 your answer joins two complete ideas without proper punctuation:

Q6 — YOUR ANSWER (2 out of 3 weighted points)
"Music can be instromental this is mostly because it does not have words and people just listen."

Two problems:

  • Run-on: "Music can be instrumental" and "this is mostly because..." are two complete thoughts jammed together without a period or connector.
  • Spelling: "instromental" should be instrumental.
FIXED VERSION
"Music can be instrumental. This means it does not have words, so people focus on the melody and rhythm instead of lyrics."
THE FIX: SPLIT OR CONNECT
When you write a sentence, check: is there a place where two complete thoughts sit next to each other?

• If yes: add a period between them, OR
• Connect them with because, and, or so

Read your answer slowly. If you can put a period in the middle and both halves make sense alone, you need punctuation there.
Which version correctly separates the two ideas?
A"Music can be instrumental it does not have words so people just listen to the sounds."
B"Music can be instrumental. This means it does not have words, so people focus on the melody and rhythm."

Error #2: Answer Too Brief

This is a Step 4 skill: develop your answer with detail + explanation

Beau, on Q8 your answer was too short to earn full points. The question is worth 3 weighted points and expects you to name a specific detail AND explain why it matters.

THE FIX: DETAIL + EXPLANATION
For 3-point questions, always include:

1. A specific detail from the passage (names a thing, quotes the text, gives a fact)
2. An explanation of why it matters or what it shows

If your answer is only one sentence, it is probably missing the explanation part. Add "This shows..." or "This is important because..."

Error #3: Do Not Start with "Hello"

This is a Step 6 skill: plan your topic sentence in the outline

Beau, on both G4.3 and G4.4, your paragraph starts with:

Q11 — YOUR OPENING (same pattern as G4.3)
"Hello, in this passage I will talk about..."

The problem: "Hello" and "in this passage I will talk about" are meta-commentary. They describe what you are going to do instead of actually doing it. The grading feedback does not award idea points for this because it does not answer the question.

START WITH YOUR ANSWER
Instead of: "Hello, in this passage I will talk about how music affects people."

Write: "Music affects people by changing their mood and helping them focus."

This is your topic sentence. It answers the question directly and earns idea points immediately.
THE RULE: NO "HELLO" AND NO "I WILL TALK ABOUT"
Your topic sentence should:

• Answer the question directly (not announce that you will answer it)
• Name the specific thing you will explain
• NOT start with "Hello," "Hi," "In this passage," "I will talk about," or "I am going to explain"

Plan your T.S. in the scratchpad first. Make sure it answers the question, not describes the question.
Which is a proper topic sentence for a paragraph about how music affects people?
A"Hello, in this passage I will talk about how music affects people in different ways."
B"Music affects people by changing their mood and helping them focus on tasks."
