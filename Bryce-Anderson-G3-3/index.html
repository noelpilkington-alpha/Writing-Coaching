THE RULE: THE ANSWER AS A FULL STATEMENT, THEN BECAUSE, THEN A REASON
A one-sentence answer has three parts: the answer as a statement that stands alone (The most meaningful moment was when…), because, and a reason that says something new. Starting at When, Because or That hands in a piece, not a sentence. The check: cover everything from because onward. Is what is left a sentence you could say on its own?
YOU WROTE (Q7, 1 OUT OF 3)
“When Anna named the seal Sally because it was the first name for the seal.” STARTS AT WHEN; THE REASON REPEATS THE FACT
Cover the because part and When Anna named the seal Sally is left, which is not a sentence. And it was the first name says the naming again instead of why it mattered.
ANSWER, BECAUSE, A REASON THAT SAYS SOMETHING NEW
The most meaningful moment was when Anna named the seal Sally, because giving her a name showed the family already cared about her.
Cover the because part: The most meaningful moment was when Anna named the seal Sally. A sentence on its own. Three points.

Fix 4: An Appositive Renames with a Noun

Q3: where Sally swam, instead of who Sally is
THE RULE: AN APPOSITIVE IS A NOUN PHRASE THAT RENAMES THE NOUN NEXT TO IT, INSIDE COMMAS
Sally, the baby seal, swam to the other seals. The appositive says who or what Sally is. It does not say where she swam or how. The check: swap the appositive in for the noun. The baby seal swam to the other seals is still a sentence, so it passes. In the ocean swam to the other seals is not, so it fails.
YOU WROTE (Q3, 1 OUT OF 2)
“Sally swam to the other seals in the ocean.” WHERE, NOT WHO
A good detail, and the wrong job. In the ocean says where. The instruction asked for a renaming.
THE APPOSITIVE
Sally, the baby seal, swam to the other seals.
A noun phrase, inside commas, right after the name it renames. Swap test: The baby seal swam to the other seals. Passes.
