Polish Fix: Relative Clause vs. Appositive

Q4 — 1 out of 2 weighted points

Leonie, Q4 asked you to add an appositive to a sentence. You wrote "that are still popular," which is a relative clause, not an appositive. Both add information, but they work differently.

Q4 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2 weighted points)
"Little Golden Books, that are still popular, have been loved by children for decades."

What you used: A relative clause (starts with "that" or "which" and contains a verb).

What the question asked for: An appositive (a noun phrase that renames or describes the noun next to it, with no verb of its own).

CORRECT APPOSITIVE
"Little Golden Books, a beloved series of children's stories, have been loved by children for decades."
HOW TO TELL THEM APART
Appositive: A noun phrase (no verb) that renames the noun. It answers "What is it?"
• "Little Golden Books, a series of small, affordable children's books, were first published in 1942."

Relative clause: Has a verb and starts with "that," "which," or "who."
• "Little Golden Books, which are still popular today, were first published in 1942."

Quick test: Does your addition contain a verb? If yes, it is a clause, not an appositive. Remove the verb and turn it into a noun phrase.
The question asks you to add an appositive to: "The sunken museum _____ attracts divers from around the world." Which is an appositive?
A The sunken museum, an underwater art gallery off the coast of Mexico, attracts divers from around the world.
B The sunken museum, which was built in 2009, attracts divers from around the world.
YOUR QUICK CHECK
When a question asks for an appositive, write your addition and then ask: "Does this have a verb?" If yes, rewrite it as a noun phrase. Remove "that are," "which is," or "who was" and just describe the noun directly.
