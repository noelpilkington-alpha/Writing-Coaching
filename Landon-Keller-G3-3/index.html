The 4 sentence types — with clear examples
Telling Declarative — tells something, ends with a period.
The dog ran fast.
Question Interrogative — asks something, ends with a question mark.
Did the dog run fast?
Excited Exclamatory — shows strong feeling, ends with an exclamation point.
The dog ran so fast!
Command Imperative — tells someone to do something, usually ends with a period.
Run fast, dog.

The 30-Second Paragraph Plan

Paragraph Outline — the habit that makes Q11 automatic
💡 Pattern note: On G3.1 you planned before writing and scored 17/20. On G3.2 and G3.3, no plan — the paragraph fell apart. Planning before writing is the single biggest difference between your 17/20 paragraph and your 2/20 paragraph.

Landon, here's the whole trick. Before you write a single sentence on Q11, take 30 seconds to fill in a Paragraph Outline (also called an SPO — Single Paragraph Outline). Three boxes. That's it.

The 30-Second Paragraph Plan
1. TOPIC Topic sentence: What's your one big idea? Restate the prompt in your own words.
2. DETAILS 2–3 details from the passage: Specific things the passage said. Names, events, facts. Quoted or paraphrased — either is fine.
3. CLOSE Closing sentence: Wrap up the big idea. Don't just repeat the topic — finish the thought.

Here's how it would have looked for your actual Pokemon prompt:

Your Pokemon SPO — 30 seconds of planning
1. TOPIC Tajiri's childhood led him to create Pokemon.
2. DETAIL 1 He loved collecting bugs as a kid.
2. DETAIL 2 Later he got hooked on video games and arcades.
3. CLOSE Those two interests combined into Pokemon.

Once you have those 4 lines, writing the paragraph is just turning each line into a full sentence. That's the whole move.

THE EVIDENCE SANDWICH (for each detail)
Every detail sentence should be a mini-sandwich:

• Top bread: State the point in your own words.
• Middle: Use a specific detail from the passage — quoted or paraphrased. Either works.
• Bottom bread: So what? Why does that detail matter?
See an Evidence Sandwich example
Point: Tajiri loved catching bugs.
Detail (paraphrased): The passage says he spent hours in the woods hunting for beetles.
So what? That gave him the idea of "catching" creatures — which later became the core of Pokemon games.
