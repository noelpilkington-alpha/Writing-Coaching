Step 2: Same Error Twice — Q3 Conjunctions

You used "because" when the logic needed "but" — same mistake as G6.1

Recurring error: On both G6.1 and G6.2, Q3 asked you to choose or use a conjunction. Both times you picked the wrong one. The logic of the sentence matters — "because" shows a reason, "but" shows a contrast, "so" shows a result.
Q3 — YOUR ANSWER (0 out of 1 point)
You used "because" where the sentence needed "but" (showing contrast, not cause).

The logic test: Before choosing a conjunction, ask what the relationship is between the two ideas:

  • "because" = the second idea is the REASON for the first
  • "but" = the second idea CONTRASTS with or contradicts the first
  • "so" = the second idea is the RESULT of the first
HOW TO PICK THE RIGHT CONJUNCTION
"She studied hard but still failed the test." → Contrast (unexpected result)
"She studied hard because the test was important." → Reason (why she studied)
"She studied hard, so she passed the test." → Result (what happened because she studied)
Which conjunction fits: "He wanted to play outside ___ it was raining too hard."
A because
B but
WHY THIS MATTERS
Q3 was worth 1 point and you got 0 — for the second test in a row. The fix: before writing, ask "Do these ideas contrast, or does one explain the other?" Contrast = "but." Reason = "because." Result = "so." One second of thinking, one point earned.

Conjunction Logic — Lock It In

The same error two tests in a row means we need to break the habit

Duke, this is the second test where you picked the wrong conjunction on Q3. Let's make sure it does not happen a third time.

THE THREE CONJUNCTION RELATIONSHIPS
CONTRAST (but, although, however): The two ideas push in opposite directions.
"He trained every day but lost the race." (training should lead to winning — contradiction)

CAUSE/REASON (because, since): The second idea explains WHY the first happened.
"He won the race because he trained every day." (training is the reason)

RESULT (so, therefore): The second idea is WHAT HAPPENED because of the first.
"He trained every day, so he won the race." (winning is the result)
"The team practiced for months ___ they still lost the championship." Which conjunction fits?
A but
B because
THE ONE-SECOND CHECK
Before submitting any conjunction answer, ask: "Do these ideas push in the same direction or opposite directions?"

Same direction → "because" or "so"
Opposite directions → "but" or "although"

This takes one second and catches the error you have made twice.
