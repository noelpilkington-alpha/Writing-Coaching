Comma Splices

The one pattern costing you multiple points
🔎 Pattern spotted: On Q8, Q9, and Q10 you joined two complete thoughts with just a comma. This is called a comma splice and it cost you points on all three questions.
Q8 — 2 OUT OF 3
"A unique plant or flower I have seen is a lily pad, what makes it interesting is that frogs hop on it and the lily pad d..."

Two complete thoughts: (1) "A unique plant I have seen is a lily pad" and (2) "What makes it interesting is that frogs hop on it." You joined them with just a comma.

Q9 — 2 OUT OF 3
"A flower, tree, or place that feels special to me or my family is California, it is special to my family because that is..."

Same pattern: "...is California" is a complete thought, and "it is special to my family..." is another complete thought. A comma alone cannot connect them.

Q10 — 2 OUT OF 3
"A fun event or tradition I enjoy during spring, fall, summer, or winter is traveling abroad for 3 months in the summer,..."

Once again: two complete ideas stitched together with only a comma.

Which version correctly fixes the comma splice?
A A unique plant I have seen is a lily pad, what makes it interesting is that frogs hop on it.
B A unique plant I have seen is a lily pad. What makes it interesting is that frogs hop on it.
TWO WAYS TO FIX A COMMA SPLICE
Option 1 — Period + new sentence:
"...is a lily pad. What makes it interesting is that frogs hop on it."

Option 2 — Add a conjunction (and, but, because):
"...is a lily pad, and what makes it interesting is that frogs hop on it."
THE TEST
Read what comes before the comma and what comes after. Can each part stand alone as a complete sentence? If yes, you have two complete thoughts. You need either a period or a comma + conjunction to connect them. A comma alone is never enough.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
This one habit cost you points on three different questions. Fixing it is the single biggest thing you can do to raise your score. On your next test, after you finish writing each answer, check: "Did I put a comma between two complete thoughts?" If yes, add a period or a conjunction.

Keep Both Ideas When Combining

When the prompt says "combine," both sentences must survive

On Q1, the prompt asked you to combine two sentences. That means the content from both sentences must appear in your new sentence. Here is what happened:

Q1 — 1 OUT OF 2
"Roadsides, hills, and meadows take on a whole new look."

You kept the idea from one sentence but lost an element from the original. When combining, every key detail from both sentences needs to make it into your answer.

Which answer correctly combines two sentences into one?
A Roadsides, hills, and meadows take on a whole new look.
B Roadsides, hills, and meadows take on a whole new look when bluebonnets cover them in spring.
THE RULE
"Combine" = both sentences live inside one new sentence.

Step 1: Identify the main idea in each sentence.
Step 2: Pick a connecting word (when, because, and, as, while).
Step 3: Write one sentence that includes both ideas joined by that word.

If your combined sentence only says what one of the originals said, you have not combined.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Q1 is worth 2 points. You scored 1 out of 2. The fix is simple: next time you see "combine," check that both original ideas appear in your answer. One quick self-check saves the full point.

Detail + Explanation in Your Paragraph

Turning 16/20 into 18+ with stronger development

Arman, you scored 16 out of 20 on your paragraph about "Are People Ready for Robots?" That is a solid score, but 4 points are available if you develop your ideas more fully.

WHAT "DEVELOPMENT" MEANS
Development = Evidence + Explanation. After you state your point, you need to:

• Give specific evidence from the passage (a fact, detail, or example)
• Explain what it means (tell the reader WHY that evidence supports your point)
Strong Paragraph Pattern
TOPIC State your position clearly in one sentence
EVIDENCE A specific detail or fact from the passage
EXPLAIN "This shows..." or "This matters because..."
EVIDENCE A second detail from the passage (if time allows)
EXPLAIN Explain why that second detail supports your point
CONCLUDE Wrap back to your main idea
Tap to see what stronger development looks like
The difference between 16/20 and 18+/20
EXAMPLE: WEAK VS. DEVELOPED
Weak: "Robots can help people. They do many things."

Developed: "Robots can help people with dangerous tasks. For example, the passage states that robots are used to explore areas that are too risky for humans. This shows that robots make it possible for scientists to gather information without putting lives in danger."

The developed version has a specific fact and then explains why it matters.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Your paragraph structure is already there. The points you are missing come from needing more specific evidence from the passage and more explanation of what that evidence means. Add one more "This shows..." sentence after each piece of evidence and you will see a jump.
