Fix 1: Two Different Facts, Each with a This Shows

Q11: a lot of reasons, and the same one given twice
THE RULE: THE ANSWER, TWO REASONS, A FACT AND A THIS SHOWS FOR EACH
The first sentence answers the question and names your two reasons. A lot of reasons names none. Then each reason gets a DETAIL · SHOW, a fact from the article you can point to, and a DETAIL · SO WHAT: This shows plus what the fact proves. Then an ending that puts the two reasons together. The check: read your two facts side by side. Are they two different things from the article, or one thing said twice?
YOU WROTE (Q11, 12 OUT OF 20)
“There is a lot of reasons for why Satoshi Tajiri’s decided to make Pokemon. One of them is his collection of bugs. Another one is that collecting bugs was his hobby. those are the reasons Satoshi Tajiri’s decided to make Pokekemon.” ONE FACT, SAID TWICE; NO THIS SHOWS
The first sentence promises a lot of reasons and names none. The two reasons are the same fact in different words. Nothing says what the bugs have to do with the game, and the last sentence is the first one again. The article has the second fact ready: he turned bug catching into a game about catching creatures.

Two reasons, and they are different. Now each one gets its own fact from the article.

Facts you can point to in the article, and one that is the first fact again:

True, and it is the bug fact in other words. A second detail has to be a second thing.

Fix 2: Do the Job the Instruction Names

THE RULE: FIND THE JOB WORD, THEN CHECK YOUR ANSWER DOES THAT JOB
Every Q1 to Q5 instruction names a job: change into a question, add an appositive, what type of sentence, combine. The check: after you write, read the job word again and look at your answer. A question ends with a question mark and starts with a word like Did, Does or Is. A type is one word. A combine is one sentence made from two.

THE JOB DONE

Did at the front, every fact kept, a question mark at the end. Two points.
