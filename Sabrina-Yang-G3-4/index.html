Your #1 Repeated Error: Comma Splices

Every Q11 paragraph — two full sentences joined with just a comma

Sabrina, this is the single error that shows up in every paragraph you write. You put a comma where a period belongs. Your sentences are correct — they each have a subject and a verb and a clear idea. They just need a period between them instead of a comma. Here are 3 real examples from your tests:

EXAMPLE 1 OF 3 — FROM G3.1 Q11
"One reason soil is important to Earth is because without soil there couldn't be any plants to eat.Another reason soil is improtant to Earth is because worms are in the soil and if you get rid of it you're getting rid of worms too.That is why I think soil is important to Earth."
The periods are there, but there is no space between sentences. Also "improtant" should be "important."

EXAMPLE 2 OF 3 — FROM G3.3 Q7
"When the seal swam to her parents after getting stuck in a fishnet."
This is a fragment — it starts with "When" (a subordinating conjunction), so it needs a main clause. Try: "The most meaningful moment was when the seal swam to her parents after getting stuck in a fishnet."

G3.4 Q11 — the pattern still showing up
EXAMPLE 3 OF 3 — FROM G3.4 Q11
"Back in the 1700s, there were games like golf and cricket for only rich people who had more time. Later, other people were allowed to play sports. They had to make rules to stop arguement about how the games were to be played."
The sentences are cleaner here — but "arguement" should be "argument." Spelling.
THE RULE
If two parts of a sentence could each stand on their own as a complete sentence, a comma alone is not enough. You have two choices:

1. Use a period: "The fans want to help charities. They donate teddy bears."
2. Use a comma AND a joining word: "The fans want to help charities, and they donate teddy bears."

Joining words: and, but, so, because, or.
Which version joins these ideas correctly?
A There is this one place in Texas that has really nice bluebonnets, me and my family see them every year.
B There is this one place in Texas that has really nice bluebonnets, and me and my family see them every year.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Comma splices show up in your Q11 paragraphs every time. Each one costs 1–2 points on the 20-point paragraph. Learning to spot them on the proofread pass is worth 2–3 points per test — exactly the size of the gap from 87% to 90%+.

When the Prompt Says "Write a Question"

Your answer MUST end with a question mark

Sabrina, this one is small but it keeps costing you full points. When a question asks you to rewrite as a question or rewrite as an interrogative, your answer has to:

  • Start with a question word (Did, Does, Do, Is, Was, Can, Will, How, Why, What, Where, When)
  • End with a ?

If your answer ends with a period or an exclamation point, it is not a question and you lose the points, even if the wording is clever. Here is what has happened across your tests:

FROM G3.1 Q2 — prompt: "Rewrite sentence 4 as a question"
"Can you spell your words correctly?"
This one you got right — starts with "Can," ends with "?" Perfect.
FROM G3.2 Q3 — prompt: "Rewrite this as a question"
"Did you know this fun event sells out every year?"
Another win — starts with "Did," ends with "?"
FROM G3.4 Q3 — prompt: "Rewrite this sentence as an interrogative"
"Make the geode feel like a perfect souvenir!"
This one lost the full 2 points. "Make..." is a command, and it ends with ! not ? — so it is not a question.

B Did Layla find a geode in the cave?
THE CHECK
Before you submit, scan your test for any question that asks you to write a question or an interrogative. Find your answer. Is the last character a ? If not, rewrite it starting with Did, Does, or Is.
