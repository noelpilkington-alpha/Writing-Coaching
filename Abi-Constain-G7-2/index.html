Fix 1: Point to the Fact, then So What

Every supporting detail starts with something a reader could find in the passage
THE RULE: POINT TO THE FACT, THEN SO WHAT
Inside every body paragraph, each supporting detail is a SHOW, a fact a reader could point to in the passage (a thing, a number, a name, a quoted phrase), then a SO WHAT that starts This shows. The check: can you put your finger on the sentence in the passage? If not, it is not evidence yet. If it is not in the passage at all, it costs points.

Fix 2: The Introduction and the Conclusion

General, specific, thesis. Then the same road back
THE RULE: THREE STEPS IN, THREE STEPS OUT
Introduction: a general statement about the topic, a specific statement that narrows to this passage, then the thesis, which answers the prompt and names the three ways the body paragraphs will take. Conclusion: the thesis in new words, a specific statement, a general statement. The check: does the thesis name all three body paragraphs? If it says in many ways, it has not done its job yet.

Your introduction:

YOU WROTE
THESIS, TOO WIDEThe bike lane project improved life in town in many ways. SPECIFIC STATEMENTAccording to Safe Streets, Stronger Town, the town built protected bike lanes on main roads to make transportation better. NO GENERAL STATEMENT
The thesis comes first and never says which ways. The specific statement is good. There is no general statement, so the reader arrives with no runway.
