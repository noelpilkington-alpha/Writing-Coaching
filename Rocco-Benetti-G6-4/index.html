Question 5 Fix — Kernel Expansion

Choosing specific detail over generic statements

You scored 0/1 on this question.

The question asked: Which sentence best expands the basic kernel by adding clear, specific detail? Kernel: "Children hear language."

Which sentence adds more specific detail to "Children hear language"?
A "Children hear language by listening to others speak."
B "Children hear the new language during lessons, group work, and simple daily conversations."
YOU CHOSE
"Children hear language by listening to others speak."
THE RULE: SPECIFIC BEATS GENERIC
When you expand a kernel (a basic subject + verb sentence), the goal is to add specific, concrete details. Ask yourself: Where? When? How exactly? What kind?

• Generic: "Children hear language by listening to others speak." (This is basically just restating the kernel in longer words.)
• Specific: "Children hear the new language during lessons, group work, and simple daily conversations." (This tells you exactly when and where.)
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Kernel expansion is the same skill you use in your essay when you add evidence. Choosing specific details over vague ones is what makes your writing convincing. If you can spot specific vs. generic in a multiple-choice question, you can apply the same thinking to your own paragraphs.

POSSESSIVE RULES

Singular nouns: Add 's → class's, teacher's, Rocco's

Regular plurals (ending in s): Add just an apostrophe → students', butterflies'

Irregular plurals (not ending in s): Add 's → people's, children's, women's

PROOFREADING STRATEGY
You have 8 errors, but they are all typos and small grammar mistakes — not thinking errors. Your ideas are excellent. Here is a strategy:

1. Read your essay backwards — start with the last sentence, then the second-to-last, and so on. This forces your brain to look at each word instead of skipping ahead.
2. Check every long word letter by letter (neighborhood, classrooms, problem-solving).
3. Search for double words — read slowly and watch for repeated words like "to to."
