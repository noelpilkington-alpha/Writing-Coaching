Complete Sentences — Q2 & Q8

Two small structure slips worth ~1 point together

Eva, the other place you lost points is sentence structure on Q2 and Q8. Both answers had good ideas, but the shape of the sentence had small issues. Let's walk through each one.

Q2 — “Combine using since”
"Since, Layla saw a butterfly, she took out her camera."
A comma right after "Since" breaks the sentence. "Since" is a connector word — it belongs glued to what comes after it, with no comma between.
A FULL-CREDIT VERSION OF Q2
"Since Layla saw a butterfly, she took out her camera."
No comma after "Since" — just one comma, after "butterfly," between the two parts of the sentence.
THE SUBORDINATING CONNECTOR RULE
Words like since, because, when, if, after, before are subordinating connectors. They glue two thoughts together:

Since Layla saw a butterfly, she took out her camera.
  (connector) (first thought)       (comma) (second thought)

The comma goes between the two thoughts, not right after the connector word.
Q8 — “Most important moment”
"When the girl become honest and loyal by following the guides rule even though she really wanted to take the geode back home."
This starts with "When..." but never finishes the thought. It's a fragment — there's a setup but no main sentence. Also "become" should be "became" and "guides" needs an apostrophe.
A FULL-CREDIT VERSION OF Q8
"The most important moment was when the girl became honest and loyal by following the guide's rule, even though she really wanted to take the geode home."
Starting with "The most important moment was..." gives you a complete sentence with a clear main idea. Your whole thought is still in there — just with a proper subject and verb up front.
Which answer is a complete sentence that earns full credit?
A When the girl became honest by following the guide's rule.
B The most important moment was when the girl became honest by following the guide's rule.
THE "FINISH THE SENTENCE" HABIT
If your answer starts with When, Since, Because, If, or After, those are setup words — they need a main sentence to follow.

A trick that works: start your answer with the main idea first, then add the when / since / because part. For Q8, try: "The most important moment was when..." or "The biggest moment was when..."
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Q2 came in at 0.5/1 and Q8 at 2/3 of a point. Fixing the comma on "Since" and starting Q8-style answers with "The most important moment was..." recovers about 1 point — and the same habit protects you on every future short answer.

YOUR PARAGRAPH — POLISHED TO 18–19/20

Did you know that modern sports go all the way back to the 1700s? That's right — modern sports like golf and cricket were first played in Britain by rich people. There were two big things that changed modern sports forever: rules and leagues. Without rules the games wouldn't be fair, so people kept changing the rules until they made sense. Leagues are groups formed inside the game. They helped people play as a team against other teams. Today anyone can play games whether you're rich or poor. Just remember the history of how the games were created.

Notice: every single idea is yours. The hook, the "rules and leagues" structure, the Britain detail, the closing — all the same. Only spelling, homophones, and verb endings changed. That alone is worth 3–4 points on Q11.

ONE SPECIFIC DETAIL FROM THE PASSAGE

Graders love it when your paragraph proves you read the article. One way to prove it: name a specific sport and a specific time period from the passage. Instead of "modern sports go all the way back to 1700s," you could write:

"Modern sports like golf and cricket were played in Britain during the 1700s, and by the 1800s leaders started writing down rules so everyone would play fair."

One specific detail (like a year or the sport name paired with the country) shows the grader you understood the article. On G3 paragraphs, that single move is often worth 1–2 points.
