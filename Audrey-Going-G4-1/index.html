The 3-Point Sentence Formula
1 Start with your answer — your action, your choice, your opinion.
2 Join with "because" (or "so that" / "since") to introduce your reason.
3 Finish with a specific reason — tied to the passage when possible, or a clear real-world "why."
! Keep it to ONE sentence. If you see a period in the middle, you’ve written two.

• Q9 rewrite: "I would keep a small part and donate the rest to a charity because sharing money with people who need supplies, like the students in the passage needed, helps far more kids than keeping it all for myself."

Paragraph — From Retell to Analyze

You hit 17/20 on Question 11. Here’s the jump to 19/20.

Audrey, your Question 11 paragraph on Surprise Kick was strong. You had a clear beginning, middle, and end. You retold the plot — Cody and Mud and the championship kick — with good structure. That earned 17 of 20, which is already in polish territory.

The jump from 17/20 to 19/20 isn’t more retelling. It’s analysis — answering why something in the story matters, not just what happens.

Topic Sentence Supporting Detail 1 Supporting Detail 2 Closing Missing: Why It Matters
RETELL vs ANALYZE
Retell: "Cody didn’t like Mud at first, but then Mud made the winning kick."
Analyze: "Cody changed his mind about Mud because the kick showed him that skill matters more than how someone looks or acts at first." ← this is the 19/20 move.
Evidence Sandwich (How to Use Passage Details)
TOP Your claim — what you think is happening or why it matters.
FILL The passage detail — a specific moment, action, or fact from the text. Paraphrase it in your own words.
BOTTOM Your "because" / "this shows" sentence — connect the detail back to your claim. This is the analysis.
Do (Paraphrase)
  • Use your own words to describe what happens
  • Name characters and specific moments
  • Add a "this shows" or "because" sentence after
  • Keep the detail short — one clear moment
Do Not (Paraphrase)
  • Don’t copy full lines word-for-word from the passage
  • Don’t list 3–4 events with no "why"
  • Don’t leave the detail unexplained
  • Don’t make it so general it could fit any story
Do (Quoting)
  • Only quote a short phrase when the exact words matter
  • Put the quoted words inside quotation marks
  • Still add a "because" or "this shows" after
  • Paraphrase is usually enough — quoting is optional
Do Not (Quoting)
  • Don’t quote a whole sentence when a phrase will do
  • Don’t drop a quote with no explanation
  • Don’t feel you must quote — paraphrasing is great
  • Don’t quote just to fill space

Because / But / So — Cause First, Effect After

Question 5 on G4.1 — the order was flipped
★ When you combine two ideas with because, the cause goes before "because" and the effect comes after. Flipping that order is the #1 way this question type loses a point.
G4.1 QUESTION 5 — 1 OF 2
"The enrollment has doubled and more students are now attending college, because of is efforts and dedication."
(Two issues: the cause/effect order is flipped — enrollment doubling is the effect, not the cause. Also, "is" should be "his.")
Because / But / So — What Each One Does
BECAUSE Shows why something happens. Structure: Effect because cause. (Or: Because cause, effect.)
BUT Shows a contrast — two ideas that push against each other. Idea 1, but surprising idea 2.
SO Shows a result. Structure: Cause, so effect. (The opposite order of "because.")
The Peter Tabichi cause/effect:
• Cause: Peter Tabichi’s dedication and effort
• Effect: enrollment doubled; more students are attending college

With because: "Enrollment has doubled and more students are attending college because of his efforts and dedication." (effect — because — cause)

With so: "Peter Tabichi put in enormous effort and dedication, so enrollment has doubled and more students are attending college." (cause — so — effect)
Which sentence uses "because" in the correct cause/effect order?
A Peter Tabichi worked very hard because enrollment at the school has doubled.
B Enrollment at the school has doubled because of Peter Tabichi’s efforts and dedication.
YOUR QUESTION 5 REWRITE
"Enrollment at the school has doubled and more students are now attending college because of his efforts and dedication."
(Cause and effect are now in the right order, and "his" is spelled correctly.)
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
This is a quick self-check before you submit any because/but/so sentence: ask yourself, "Which part is the cause? Which is the effect?" If they’re flipped, swap them. One rule, worth 1 point per test.
