THE RULE FOR "BECAUSE"
When "because" joins two parts of the same sentence, no comma. The "because" part is not a separate idea; it is the reason the first part happened, so it is glued right on.

✗ "Carter planted milkweed, because it attracts monarchs."
✓ "Carter planted milkweed because it attracts monarchs."

TRANSITIONS CHEAT SHEET
To add more: Also, In addition, Furthermore
To contrast: However, On the other hand, In contrast
To show cause: Therefore, As a result, Because of this
To show time order: First, Next, Then, Finally
To show at the same time: Meanwhile, While, At the same time
To give an example: For example, For instance, Such as

MODEL BODY PARAGRAPH (19–20/20 level)

The first way people can help monarch butterflies stay alive is by building way stations. According to the passage, Carter created a way station in his own backyard by planting milkweed, the only plant monarch caterpillars can eat. This shows that even one person can give monarchs a safe place to rest and lay eggs — something they cannot do without milkweed. Way stations also provide shelter during the long migration, which means more butterflies survive to reach Mexico. By planting the right kind of garden, people directly replace the habitat monarchs have lost to farming and development.

Which body paragraph structure earns more points on the essay rubric?
A Topic sentence + general description + restatement. (Your current G6.3 style.)
B Topic sentence + specific fact from passage + "This shows..." + second fact + closing.
Your Body Paragraph Blueprint
TOPIC State one way people help monarchs (you already do this!)
FACT A specific detail from the passage (names, numbers, plants)
SHOWS "This shows..." — explain what the fact proves
FACT 2 A second specific detail (adds depth)
CLOSE Wrap the idea and connect back to your thesis
THE 10-MINUTE PLAN STEP
Before you draft, jot down on scrap paper:
• Thesis: one sentence answering the prompt
• Body 1 topic + 2 facts from the passage
• Body 2 topic + 2 facts from the passage
• Body 3 topic + 2 facts from the passage
• Conclusion: what the reader should take away

Ten minutes of planning makes drafting twice as fast and catches 2–3 extra points on the evidence rubric.

Revise for Spelling & Clean Sentences

Small slips cost "Edit your writing" rubric points

McKayla, the "Edit your writing" rubric on the essay is worth 3 points. You lost points here on G6.3 because small spelling and sentence slips snuck through. These are the easiest points on the whole test to win back — a revise step catches almost all of them.

FROM YOUR G6.3 ESSAY INTRO
"People help monarch butterflies stay alive by building way stations, donating money, and taging butterflies."
Should be: tagging (two g's — "tag" + "ing")
FROM YOUR G6.3 ESSAY BODY 1
"The first way that people can help monarch butterflies stay alive are way stations."
Subject-verb: "The first way... is way stations." (Singular "way" needs "is.")
FROM YOUR G6.3 ESSAY BODY 3
"People tag butterflies with small stickers, count caterpillars, and record monarch sighting."
Should be: sightings (plural — people record many sightings, not one)
YOUR TOP "WATCH OUT" WORDS & RULES
These are the exact kinds of slips showing up across your essays:

tagging (not taging) · sightings (not sighting) · stopping (not stoping)
The rule: When a word ends in a single consonant after a short vowel (tag, stop, plan), double the consonant before adding -ing or -ed.

Subject-verb match: "The first way is..." (singular) vs "Three ways are..." (plural). Find the real subject before you pick is/are.
Which version would earn full credit on the "Edit your writing" rubric?

THE REVISE TRICK
When you finish drafting, read your essay out loud in your head one sentence at a time. Every word longer than 5 letters: stop and ask "does this look right?" Every -ing or -ed word: check for a doubled consonant. Every "is/are" or "was/were": find the real subject.

Fifteen minutes of revising catches almost every error in this whole review.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
The "Edit your writing" rubric is worth 3 out of 20 on your essay. Clean spelling, matching subjects and verbs, and correct plurals get you the full 3. On top of that, essays that read cleanly earn higher scores on the other rubrics too — graders can see your ideas when they are not tripping over errors.
