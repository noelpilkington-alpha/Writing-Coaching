Your #1 Win: Capital Letters for Names

The fastest points on any test — a habit, not a skill

Dei-Undre, the biggest pattern across your answers is names written with lowercase letters. This showed up in Q5 and then four times in your paragraph. Look:

FROM Q5 — the right subordinator
You wrote: "Since there was no sign of the seals, the family placed sally in a fish box..."
Sally is the seal's name. It needs a capital S every time you use it.
FROM Q11 — the paragraph
You wrote: "Satoshi tajiri's childhood influenced him making the game pokemon... i believe that Satoshi tajiri's..."
Tajiri is a last name. Pokemon is the game's name. And "I" is always capital when it stands alone.
THE CAPITAL-LETTER RULE
A word needs a capital letter when it is a name. Names include:

• People (first AND last): Anna, Jim, Satoshi Tajiri, Sally
• Places: Austin, Texas, Japan
• Days & months: Tuesday, January, Friday
• Special things: Pokemon, Pokeball (the name of a game or item)
• The word "I" — always, every time, no exceptions

If you could point at it and say "that exact one," it probably needs a capital.
Which sentence has the capital letters in all the right places?
A i think Satoshi tajiri made pokemon because he loved bugs.
B I think Satoshi Tajiri made Pokemon because he loved bugs.
THE "POINT AND NAME" CHECK
Before you move on from any answer, scan back through and look for any name. If you wrote a person's first OR last name, a place, a day, the name of a game, or the word "I," make sure every one of them starts with a capital.

This is the single easiest habit to build, and it earns real points on every single test.

Your #2 Win: "wouldn't of" vs. "wouldn't have"

One tiny word swap that trips up a lot of writers

Dei-Undre, on Q9 you wrote a sentence that has a really common speaking habit hiding inside it. Look at what you wrote:

FROM Q9 — what you would do differently
You wrote: "If I were Anna or Jim I wouldn't of did anything."
Two small fixes: "wouldn't of" should be "wouldn't have," and "did" should be "done."

This one happens because when people say "wouldn't have," it sounds like "wouldn't uv" — so it gets written as "wouldn't of." But "of" is a preposition, and it does not belong there.

THE "HAVE" RULE
After would, could, should, might, must, and will, the next word is almost always have (not "of"):

• I would have helped. (not "would of")
• She could have stayed. (not "could of")
• You should have seen it. (not "should of")

And when you use "have" like this, the verb after it changes shape: did → done, saw → seen, went → gone.

So: "I wouldn't have done anything" (not "wouldn't of did anything").
Which sentence uses the right words?
A I would of helped the seal if I saw it.
B I would have helped the seal if I saw it.
QUICK CHECK
Any time you write would, could, should, might, or must, check the next word. If it is "of," swap it for have. Then check the verb after that — it probably needs its "done" or "seen" form, not its "did" or "saw" form.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
On Q9 this one grammar mix-up cost you almost two-thirds of a point (0.33 out of 1). Getting this fix in could push that question from partial to full credit all by itself. Over a full test, one extra full-credit answer is the difference between 77% and the low 80s.
