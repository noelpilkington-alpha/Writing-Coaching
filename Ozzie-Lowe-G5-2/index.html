Fix 1: Use the Article You Were Given

Q11: a point, a how, and not one fact from the page
THE RULE: THE POINT, THE HOWS, THEN ONE FACT FROM THE ARTICLE PER HOW
The first sentence answers the question (POINT) and names the two reasons the paragraph will prove (HOW). Each how gets a DETAIL · SHOW, a fact from the article with a number, a name or a few of its exact words, and a DETAIL · SO WHAT: This shows plus what the fact proves about that how. The CONCLUDING SENTENCE names both hows and says what they add up to. The check: for each sentence in the middle, point to it in the article. If you cannot point, it is your opinion, and opinions do not earn the evidence points.

Fix 2: No Comma Before Because

Six times on this test. Free in Q6 to Q10, not free in the paragraph
THE RULE: BECAUSE JOINS WITHOUT A COMMA
Because glues a reason to a sentence on its own: It feels amazing because you learn from the best. No comma before it, and never one after it. The check: every time you type because, look at the character before it and the character after it. Both are letters. In the paragraph, conventions are five points, and six stray commas spend them.
YOU WROTE (Q6–Q10, FULL MARKS, AND Q8)
“I say it feels amazing, because you get to learn that skill…”   “Practicing over time can lead to success, because if you pratice enough…”   “Small details can make a big difference because, if you don’t have the small details…”   “That person might feel amazing, because when you work really hard…”
Every one-sentence answer earned its three points; the ideas were right and the reasons were there. The comma before because is a habit, and in Q8 it moved to after because. On the paragraph, where conventions are scored out of five, the same habit costs.
WITHOUT THE COMMA
It feels amazing because you get to learn that skill from one of the best people you know.  ·  Small details make a big difference because without them you do not have the whole project.
YOU WROTE (Q11)
“Why, because if you do have recognizable logo then if people see your probuct…”
Why, because is speech, not a sentence: it has no who and no doing word before the because. And probuct, pratice, follwed and sat at the kitchen are all one read-back from correct.

Where commas go, and where they do not:

KEEPWe took our time, so the pie came out perfect.
Two whole sentences joined by so: comma before so.
KEEPWhile the pies baked, we sat at the kitchen table.
An opening part gets a comma after it.
NO COMMAGrandma Addie won because she followed every step carefully.
Because joins on its own.
NO COMMALogos feel familiar, because we see them every day.
The comma before because. Delete it.
Which sentence has its commas right?
AOnly one student drew the logo correctly, because our brains drop the details.
BOnly one student drew the logo correctly because our brains drop the details.
Which sentence has its commas right?
AThe logo felt familiar, so the shoppers trusted the brand.
BThe shoppers trusted the brand because, the logo felt familiar.
Which sentence is ready to submit?
APeople recognize the product because they have seen the logo in practice a thousand times.
BPeople recognize the probuct, because they have seen the logo in pratice a thousand times.
PRACTICE IT IN YOUR ALPHAWRITE LESSONS
Every because: a letter before it, a letter after it. Then one read-back of the paragraph, finger on each word, before you submit.
