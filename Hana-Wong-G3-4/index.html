Make It a Real Question

G3.4 Q3 scored 0/2 because the answer wasn't a question at all

Hana, when a prompt asks you to rewrite a sentence as a question (also called an interrogative), your answer has to be an actual question — starting with a question word like Is, Does, Did, Can, How, Why and ending with a question mark.

YOU WROTE (G3.4 Q3) — scored 0/2
Prompt: "Rewrite this sentence as an interrogative: The geode felt like a perfect souvenir."

Your answer: "The geode, a perfect souvenir, was within her reach."
Problem: this is a statement with an appositive. No question word, no question mark. The prompt asked for a question.
WHAT FULL CREDIT LOOKS LIKE
"Did the geode feel like a perfect souvenir?"
— or —
"Does the geode feel like a perfect souvenir?"

Both start with a question word (Did / Does) and end with a question mark.

You also had a smaller slip on G3.3 Q1 where you wrote "Did Anna name the seal sally?" (lowercase sally — should be capital Sally). The question form was right, the capitalization was the issue.

Which version is a real interrogative sentence?

B Did the geode feel like a perfect souvenir?
THE INTERROGATIVE FORMULA
Step 1. Start with a question word: Is, Are, Was, Were, Do, Does, Did, Can, Will, How, Why, What.
Step 2. Flip the verb and the subject. "Layla saw a butterfly" becomes "Did Layla see a butterfly?"
Step 3. End with a question mark. Not a period.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Q3 was worth 2 points. A 15-second check — "did I start with a question word and end with ?" — would have caught this. Add that check to every proofread pass.

Spaces After Periods

Your Q11 paragraphs have sentences running into each other

Hana, here is something specific that keeps showing up in your paragraphs: when you end one sentence and start the next, the period and the next sentence get jammed together with no space. It makes the paragraph look like a run-on even when the sentences themselves are fine. Three real examples:

FROM G3.1 Q11 — paragraph
"Firstly,soil help veggies and fruits grow,which we use as food.Secondly,groundhogs burrow in it."
Missing space after almost every comma and period.
FROM G3.2 Q11 — paragraph
"The camel libary has affected Garissa by bringing books to the people there.Kids know have books in school."
Period with no space before “Kids” (and “know” should be “now”).
FROM G3.4 Q11 — your most recent paragraph
"Modern sports developed first in Britain starting during the 1700s.There were rules to keep games fair and less complicated also,there were leaques..."
Period with no space before “There” — and a run-on right after.
Which version is correctly spaced?
A Modern sports began in the 1700s.There were rules to keep games fair,and there were leagues.
B Modern sports began in the 1700s. There were rules to keep games fair, and there were leagues.
THE RULE
After every period (.) and every comma (,) there must be a single space before the next word. No exceptions.

✗ "games fair,there were leagues"
✓ "games fair, there were leagues" (space after the comma)
✗ "during the 1700s.There were rules"
✓ "during the 1700s. There were rules" (space after the period)
PROOFREAD TIP
When you re-read, slide your finger under each line. Every time you see a period or comma, check that there is a space before the next letter. This is one of the fastest fixes in the whole review.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Missing spaces make a paragraph look like one big run-on. The grader sees this as a punctuation/conventions error and drops points. This is worth 1–2 points on every Q11 — and it is the easiest fix of them all.
