The Third Layer: Evidence + Explanation

Your body paragraphs have claims and evidence but stop short of explanation
🔎
The pattern: Your body paragraphs follow the structure: claim → evidence. But they stop before the explanation: the sentence that says "This proves..." or "This demonstrates..." or "This connects to the thesis because..." That missing layer is what separates a 16/20 essay from a 19-20/20 essay.
THE THREE-LAYER BODY PARAGRAPH
1. Claim: State what this paragraph will prove.
2. Evidence: A specific detail, quote, or fact from the passage.
3. Explanation: What does this evidence prove? How does it support your thesis?

You consistently do layers 1 and 2. Adding layer 3 is the unlock.

Using the Wilma Rudolph passage
THREE-LAYER EXAMPLE
Claim: "Wilma Rudolph's perseverance is most clearly shown in how she responded to childhood illness."

Evidence: "According to the passage, Rudolph contracted polio at age four and was told she might never walk normally again. Despite this, she spent years doing physical therapy exercises every day until she could walk without braces by age twelve."

Explanation: "This demonstrates that Rudolph's success was not simply natural talent but the result of years of deliberate effort against overwhelming odds. Her refusal to accept the doctor's prognosis reveals a mindset that would later carry her to three Olympic gold medals."
Which body paragraph is fully developed with all three layers?
A"Rudolph showed perseverance in sports. She won three gold medals at the 1960 Olympics despite having polio as a child."
B"Rudolph's response to childhood illness reveals her extraordinary perseverance. She contracted polio at four and doctors said she might never walk normally, yet she did daily therapy until she was brace-free at twelve. This proves that her Olympic success was built on years of determined effort, not natural ability alone."

Connect Every Paragraph to Your Thesis

The final sentence of each body paragraph should loop back

James Finn, at the G6 level, a high-scoring essay does not just present evidence. It connects every piece of evidence back to the central argument. The final sentence of each body paragraph should explicitly link to your thesis.

THE LOOP-BACK SENTENCE
After your evidence + explanation, add one sentence that says:

• "This connects to [thesis] because..."
• "This is another example of how [thesis main claim]..."
• "Without this [evidence], [thesis] would not have been possible because..."

This shows the grading feedback that you are not just listing facts. You are building an argument.
Thesis: "Wilma Rudolph's story proves that perseverance can overcome any obstacle." Which ending sentence best connects back to this thesis?
A"This shows that Rudolph worked hard and eventually became a champion."
B"Her years of therapy prove that perseverance, not physical advantage, was the force that turned an impossible prognosis into Olympic glory."
