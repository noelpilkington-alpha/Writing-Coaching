Question 3 Fix — Combining with Conjunctions

Let’s review this together.

You scored 1/2 on this question.

The question asked:

“Combine the following sentences using a conjunction: The books had golden spines. The books had colorful pages.”

Which sentence correctly combines the two ideas with a conjunction?
A The books had golden spines also, the books had colorful pages.
B The books had golden spines, and they had colorful pages.
YOU WROTE
“The books had golden spines also, the books had colorful pages.”

You had the right idea — you wanted to connect the two sentences. But “also” is not a conjunction. Conjunctions are connecting words like and, but, or, so, and yet.

CORRECTED VERSION
“The books had golden spines, and they had colorful pages.”
THE CONJUNCTION CHECKLIST
When a question says “use a conjunction,” pick from this list:
• and = adds information
• but = shows contrast
• or = gives a choice
• so = shows a result
• yet = shows something unexpected

Put a comma before the conjunction when joining two complete sentences.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Conjunctions are the building blocks of longer, more complex sentences. This same skill is what makes your paragraph sentences flow together instead of sounding choppy.

Questions 4 & 5 Fix — Appositives

Let’s review this together.

You scored 1/2 on Question 4 and 1/2 on Question 5.

🔎 What is an appositive? An appositive is a short phrase set off by commas that renames or gives extra information about a noun. Think of it as a “nickname phrase” tucked into the middle of a sentence.
Which sentence uses an appositive correctly?
A The books are still popular, they are great books, and they are known for their golden spines.
B The books, a beloved children's series, are still popular and known for their golden spines.
QUESTION 4 — YOU WROTE
“The books are still popular, they are great books, and they are known for their golden spines.”

Your sentence combined the ideas, but it didn’t use an appositive. An appositive is a renaming phrase set off by commas, placed right after the noun.

WITH AN APPOSITIVE
“The books, a beloved children’s series, are still popular and known for their golden spines.”
QUESTION 5 — YOU WROTE
“The appositive is ,a best-selling story.”

You correctly identified the appositive phrase, but the formatting was off. The appositive in the sentence is: “a best-selling story” (without the comma in front). Good eye for spotting it!

HOW TO BUILD AN APPOSITIVE
Step 1: Find the noun you want to rename. (Example: “The books”)
Step 2: Write a short phrase that tells more about it. (Example: “a beloved children’s series”)
Step 3: Tuck it in right after the noun, with commas on both sides.

Pattern: Noun, appositive phrase, rest of the sentence.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Appositives make your sentences richer without making them run-on. When you use appositives in your paragraph, it shows that you can pack in extra detail smoothly — and that earns higher scores.
