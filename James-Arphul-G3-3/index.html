THE FIX: SLOW READ-BACK
After you finish writing, read your answer one word at a time, pointing at each word with your finger or pencil. Say each word in your head. When you get to a long word like "because" or "basically," pause and check: does it look right? If you are not sure, sound it out syllable by syllable:

• be-cause (not "be-cas-ue")
• child-hood (not "chil-hood")
• bas-ic-al-ly (not "basic-a-ly")

Also watch for names from the passage. If the passage says Sally, your answer must also say Sally, not "Saly" or "sally."

Punctuation: Comma Placement

No comma right after "because" or "since"

James, you have a habit of placing a comma right after words like "because" and "since." That comma does not belong there. Look at the pattern:

Q5 — 1 out of 2
"Since, there was no sign of seals..."
Q6 — 2 out of 3
"...because, the seal was just a baby..."
Q7 — 2 out of 3
"...becasue, the lesson of the story is to be kind."

In all three cases, the comma breaks the sentence in the wrong place. Words like "because" and "since" are connectors. They join two ideas together. Putting a comma right after them chops the connection.

THE RULE
Never put a comma directly after because, since, although, or while.

Wrong: "I helped the seal because, it was a baby."
Right: "I helped the seal because it was a baby."

Wrong: "Since, there was no sign of seals..."
Right: "Since there was no sign of seals..."

Quick check: If you have written "because" or "since," look at the very next character. Is it a comma? Delete it.
CORRECTED VERSIONS
• "Since there was no sign of seals, the family placed Sally in a fish box and motored into the bay."
• "I would have helped the seal because the seal was just a baby and had a lot of life to live."
• "...freed Sally because the lesson of the story is to be kind."

Q11 — 16 out of 20. Your paragraph needs more from the passage.

James, your Q11 paragraph about Satoshi Tajiri and Pokemon scored 16 out of 20. You have the right idea, but the marker wants to see specific details from the passage and then a sentence that explains what that detail shows.

You state the connection (childhood influenced Pokemon), which is great. But you need to add a specific fact from the article and then explain what it shows. Right now the paragraph stays general.

THE FORMULA: DETAIL + "THIS SHOWS..."
Step 1: Give a specific detail from the passage (with a page clue like "the article says" or "according to the text").
Step 2: Follow it with "This shows..." to explain why that detail matters.

Example:
"The article explains that Tajiri spent hours catching insects near rice paddies as a child. This shows that his love for collecting living creatures became the foundation for catching and collecting Pokemon in the game."

MODEL PARAGRAPH (19–20/20 level)

Satoshi Tajiri's childhood experiences shaped the creation of Pokemon in important ways. For example, the article explains that Tajiri spent hours as a child catching insects and exploring ponds near his home. This shows that his love for collecting creatures in nature became the foundation for the idea of catching and collecting Pokemon in the game. Another example is that Tajiri was fascinated by connecting two Game Boys with a link cable, imagining insects crawling between the devices. This shows that his curiosity about technology combined with his childhood hobby to create the trading feature that made Pokemon special. That is how Satoshi Tajiri's childhood directly led to one of the most popular games in the world.
