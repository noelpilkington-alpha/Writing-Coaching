Comma Splices: Two Sentences, Not One

A comma alone can't join two complete sentences

Lavender, look at this pattern — in every test, two complete thoughts end up glued together with just a comma. A comma alone is not strong enough to hold two full sentences. You need a period, or a word like because, but, so, and. Here are three real examples:

FROM G4.2 Q7 (sentence writing)
"One change I see at the start of spring, is more plants growning."
The comma after "spring" is not needed — this is one sentence, not two.
FROM G4.3 Q8 (sentence writing)
"A unique flower I have seen is a hibiscus flower, I think the hibiscus flower is unique because it has many different colors."
Two full sentences joined with just a comma. Needs a period or "because".
FROM G4.4 Q11 (paragraph)
"At first may people made fun of him but, as the palace started to come together thay were supprised."
"but" belongs between the two ideas, not with a comma after it. Also: "may" should be "many", "thay" should be "they".
Which version fixes the comma splice correctly?
A The hibiscus flower is unique, it has many different colors.
B The hibiscus flower is unique because it has many different colors.
THE RULE
A comma is not a period. If the words on both sides of a comma are each a complete sentence, the comma alone is not enough.

Three ways to fix a comma splice:
1. Change the comma to a period and start a new sentence.
2. Add a joining word after the comma: and, but, so, because.
3. Rewrite as one idea (cut one of the repeated parts).

✗ "I like the hibiscus, it has many colors."
✓ "I like the hibiscus. It has many colors."
✓ "I like the hibiscus, because it has many colors."
THE "TWO SENTENCES" TEST
Cover the comma with your finger. Read each side by itself. If both halves work as full sentences on their own, you have a comma splice — use a period or add a joining word.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Comma splices show up in every Q11 paragraph you write. Q11 is worth 20 out of 45 total points. Cleaning up the comma splices is worth 1–2 points per test on its own — and it makes every sentence read clearer.
