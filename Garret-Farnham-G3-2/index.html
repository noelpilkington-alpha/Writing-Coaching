Error #1: Explain What the Event Shows

Q5 — "fans are loyal" does not explain WHAT the event reveals

Garret, on Q5 the question asked what the Teddy Bear Toss shows about the fans. You wrote that "fans are loyal," which is a general trait but does not explain what the specific event of throwing teddy bears reveals about them.

Q5 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2 weighted points)
"The event shows that fans are loyal."

The problem: "Loyal" describes fans in general, but the question asks what this specific event (throwing stuffed animals to donate to children) shows about them. The action of giving away stuffed animals shows generosity or kindness, not loyalty.

A STRONGER ANSWER
"The Teddy Bear Toss shows that the fans are generous because they buy stuffed animals with their own money and throw them onto the ice so children in need can have them. This detail matters because it proves the fans care about people beyond just the game."
THE FIX: CONNECT THE ACTION TO THE TRAIT
When a question asks "What does this event show about [someone]?":

Step 1: Name the specific action (fans throw stuffed animals to donate).
Step 2: Name the trait that action reveals (generous, caring).
Step 3: Explain why the action reveals that trait (they spend their own money to help strangers).

The trait must match the action. Throwing toys to help children = generous. It does not show "loyal."
The question asks: "What does the Teddy Bear Toss show about the fans?" Which answer earns full points?
A "The event shows that fans are loyal to their team and love hockey."
B "The Teddy Bear Toss shows that fans are generous and caring because they buy stuffed animals with their own money and give them away so children in need can have something to hold."
WHY THIS MATTERS
Q5 is worth 2 weighted points. You earned 1 out of 2. The fix: make sure the trait you name actually matches the action described. Giving things away = generous. Sticking with a team through losses = loyal. Match the trait to what actually happened.

Error #2: Spelling in Your Paragraph

Q11 — "pepole" and "librarys" cost points

Garret, your paragraph had two spelling errors that cost you points: "pepole" (should be people) and "librarys" (should be libraries).

SPELLING ERRORS IN Q11
"pepole" → people
"librarys" → libraries
HOW TO CATCH THESE
"People": This is a common word that just needs to be memorized. The trick: "peo-ple." Say it in two chunks: PEO + PLE. Not "pep-ole."

"Libraries": When a word ends in "y" and you want to make it plural, drop the "y" and add "-ies." Library → libraries. Story → stories. City → cities. The rule: if the letter before "y" is a consonant, change "y" to "ies."
Which is the correct plural?
A "librarys"
B "libraries"
THE PROOFREAD PASS
After writing your paragraph, read it back slowly. If a word looks strange (like "pepole" or "librarys"), try rewriting it. Sound it out: "pee-pull" → "people." "li-BRAR-ees" → "libraries." A 15-second read-back catches both of these.
