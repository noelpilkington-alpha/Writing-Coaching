Appositive = Renaming, Not a List (Q3)

What an appositive actually is — and isn't

Joan, Q3 asked you to add an appositive that gives more information about Peter Tabichi. You gave more information about him — but you did it by adding a list of extra verbs, not by renaming him. That is why Q3 came in at 0.5 out of 1.

YOU WROTE (Q3)
"Peter Tabichi is from Kenya, Africa, teaches high school science, and helps other people for their edication."
The extra part is a list of actions Peter does, not a noun phrase renaming him. That makes it extra information, but not an appositive.
WHAT AN APPOSITIVE IS
An appositive is a noun phrase that renames the person or thing right next to it. It answers the question “who or what is this person?” in a new way.

The form: Subject, a/the [noun phrase that renames the subject], rest of sentence.

Example: Peter Tabichi, a science teacher from Kenya, won the Global Teacher Prize.
→ “a science teacher from Kenya” renames Peter Tabichi. It is a noun phrase, not a list of verbs.
A BETTER Q3 (JOAN'S IDEAS, AS AN APPOSITIVE)
"Peter Tabichi, a high school science teacher from Kenya, Africa, teaches students and helps other people with their education."
Same information about Peter. But now “a high school science teacher from Kenya, Africa” is a noun phrase that renames him — that is an appositive.
The prompt says: Rewrite the sentence to add an appositive that gives more information about Cody.
Which answer adds a real appositive?
A Cody plays soccer, runs fast, and scores goals for his team.
B Cody, a nervous fourth-grader on the soccer team, watched the mud fight start.
THE APPOSITIVE CHECK
Before you finish a Q3 answer, ask yourself these two questions:

1. Can I drop the part between the commas and still have a complete sentence? If yes, it is an appositive.
2. Is the part I added a noun phrase (a/the/an + noun), not a list of verbs? If yes, it is an appositive.

Example check: "Peter Tabichi, a science teacher from Kenya, won the prize." Drop the middle → "Peter Tabichi won the prize." Still complete. And “a science teacher from Kenya” starts with “a” + noun. Both tests pass.
WHY THIS MATTERS
Q3 was worth 1 point and came in at 0.5. Knowing an appositive is a noun phrase that renames (not a list of actions) is a one-second move that recovers that half point — and the same rule protects you on every future appositive question.
