The Strategy: Outline, Check, Write, Proofread

The Single Paragraph Outline you learned in AlphaWrite, used on your October 21 countdown item
THE FOUR STEPS, EVERY PARAGRAPH
1. Outline first. A Single Paragraph Outline, the way AlphaWrite taught it: a topic sentence, supporting details as short notes, a concluding sentence. 2. Check the outline against the prompt. Count the prompt’s parts. Does the topic sentence answer every part? Does every part have a note? Can you point to each note in the passage? 3. Write the paragraph from the outline. Each note becomes a sentence in your words, and after each one you add why it matters: because, so, which means. 4. Proofread. Every part answered, nothing copied word for word, the rushed words checked.
THE ITEM (OCTOBER 21, ITEM 4): THE QUESTION, AND THE PASSAGE IN SHORT
Question: How do families create birthday countdowns, and why do they use them? The passage: paper chains with one link torn off each morning, small chalkboards with a new number each day, pocket charts filled with tiny notes and treats, and the reasons: a countdown builds anticipation, gives kids counting practice, and makes each day before the party memorable.
STEP 1: THE OUTLINE
Topic sentence: Families make birthday countdowns with simple tools so the wait becomes part of the fun.
Detail 1: paper chain, one link torn off each morning
Detail 2: chalkboard, new number each day, or pocket chart with notes and treats
Detail 3 (the why): builds anticipation, counting practice, each day memorable
Concluding sentence: A countdown turns the waiting into part of the celebration.
STEP 2: THE CHECK AGAINST THE PROMPT
The prompt has two parts: how they create them, and why. Topic sentence: tools (how) and fun (why), both there. Notes: details 1 and 2 are the how, detail 3 is the why. Every note points to a sentence in the passage. The outline passes the check.
Run the same check on what you actually wrote that day, and it fails at once: Many families do this because many kids can feel sad is a why with no how. The check is where the five points were lost.
STEP 3: THE PARAGRAPH FROM THE OUTLINE
Families make birthday countdowns with simple tools so the wait becomes part of the fun. Some families hang a paper chain and tear one link off every morning, so the child can see the days getting shorter. Others write a new number on a small chalkboard each day or hang a pocket chart filled with tiny notes and treats, which turns each day into a small surprise. Families do this because a countdown builds anticipation and gives kids practice counting. In the end, a countdown turns the waiting into part of the celebration.
Every fact is the passage’s. The so or which after each fact is your own thinking about why it matters. No sentence is the passage’s sentence. Both parts of the question are answered, and the ending answers it again.
