THE 3-STEP Q11 PLAN (15 MINUTES)
Step 1 — Plan (3 min). Read the prompt twice. Write 2 or 3 details from the article on scratch paper. You do not write the paragraph yet — you just list what you will say.

Step 2 — Write (10 min). Start with a topic sentence that answers the question. Then write 2 or 3 detail sentences. End with a closing sentence.

Step 3 — Check (2 min). Read your paragraph. Does every sentence start with a capital letter? Does every sentence end with a period? Did you use at least 2 details from the story?

Read Each Question All the Way Through

Some questions ask you to do something specific — catch those clues

Mateo, a few times across your tests, the question asked for something specific and the answer did something different. These points are easy to earn once you slow down to catch what the question is really asking.

FROM G3.3 Q1 — most recent test
Prompt: Change this sentence into a question: Anna named the seal Sally.
You wrote: "Anna,"
The prompt said "change into a question." Your answer is not a question.
FROM G3.2 Q3
Prompt: Rewrite this as a question: This fun event sells out every year.
You wrote: "The Hershey Bears host a special event every year called the Teddy Bear Toss."
A good sentence, but it is a statement, not a question. It needs to end with ?

Good news — this is a simple fix. When the question says "write a question" or "rewrite as a question," your answer MUST end with a ? question mark.

THE 3 QUESTION-WORD STARTERS
To turn a statement into a question, start with one of these words:

Who named the seal? → Who did Anna name the seal after?
What → What did Anna name the seal?
Does / Did / Is → Did Anna name the seal Sally?

Every one ends with a ?
Which is a proper question rewrite of "Anna named the seal Sally"?
A Anna named the seal Sally after her grandmother.
B What did Anna name the seal?
THE "MARK THE JOB" TRICK
Before you answer any question, look for the action word in the prompt. Circle it in your head. Common ones:

• Rewrite as a question → ends with ?
• Combine → make one sentence out of two
• Add an appositive → add a word group with commas (like "Sally, a seal,")
• Explain → give a reason, often using "because"

Match your answer to the action word. That is how you earn the points.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
You already got Q3 (appositive) correct on G3.3 because you matched the action word. Doing that on every question is worth 2–4 extra points per test with no new skills needed.

MODEL PARAGRAPH (what 15/20 looks like)

Satoshi Tajiri's childhood had a big influence on the creation of Pokemon. When Tajiri was young, he loved going into the woods to collect bugs and small creatures. He also enjoyed exploring nature and finding things other kids did not notice. These childhood hobbies show up in Pokemon because the game is all about exploring, catching, and collecting different creatures. In the end, Tajiri turned what he loved as a kid into a game millions of people play today.

The 4-Part Paragraph Shape
TOPIC State your main idea. Turn the prompt into a sentence.
DETAIL 1 One specific thing from the article that supports your idea
DETAIL 2 A second specific thing from the article
CLOSING Wrap back to your main idea in new words
Which answer is the shape of a Q11 paragraph?
A One sentence that mentions the main idea.
B Topic sentence + detail 1 + detail 2 + closing (4 or more sentences total).
START WITH THE PROMPT
The easiest way to write a topic sentence is to turn the prompt into a sentence. The prompt was "Explain how Satoshi Tajiri's childhood influenced Pokemon." Your topic sentence becomes: "Satoshi Tajiri's childhood influenced Pokemon in big ways."

Now all you have to do is add 2 details from the article and a closing. The hardest part is already done.
