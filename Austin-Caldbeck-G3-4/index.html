The Comma Goes Before the Joining Word

Questions 2 and 4 — one small habit to flip
🔍 Pattern alert: On this test the comma landed right after the joining word four different times (Q2, Q4, Q6, Q8). The ideas were correct every time — it is the same small comma slip repeating.

Austin, your sentences are good. You just put the comma in the wrong spot when you join two ideas. Here is what that looked like on this test:

YOU WROTE (Q2)
"Layla took out her camera since, she saw a butterfly."
The comma landed right after "since." That is the wrong spot.
YOU WROTE (Q4)
"Layla wanted to keep the geode but, she remembered the guide's rule."
Same slip — the comma is after "but" instead of before it.
Which sentence puts the comma in the right place?
A Layla wanted to keep the geode but, she remembered the guide's rule.
B Layla wanted to keep the geode, but she remembered the guide's rule.
THE RULE
When a joining word like and, but, or so connects two complete ideas, the comma goes before the joining word:

✓ "Layla wanted to keep the geode, but she remembered the guide's rule."
✗ "Layla wanted to keep the geode but, she remembered the guide's rule."

With a word like since at the start, the comma goes after the first idea, never right after "since":

✓ "Since she saw a butterfly, Layla took out her camera."
✗ "Layla took out her camera since, she saw a butterfly."

Explain What Your Evidence Shows

Question 11 — you have the facts, now add the meaning
🔍 Pattern alert: Your paragraph earned 13 of 20. The content is strong — you lost points because the details are stated as facts without explaining what they show.

Austin, your paragraph on how modern sports developed had real facts in it — golf going from rich people to normal people, rules changing over time. The fix is to add one short sentence after each detail that explains what it means. Here is a piece of what you wrote:

YOU WROTE (Q11)
"First, most games like golf were played by rich people. Over time, normal people began playing sports too and rules kept on changing over and over."
Good facts — but it stops at the fact. What does it show?
Which one earns the higher paragraph score?
A Most games like golf were played by rich people. Over time, normal people began playing too.
B Most games like golf were played by rich people, but over time normal people began playing too. This shows that sports grew from a few wealthy players into something everyone could enjoy.
THE "SO WHAT?" TRICK
After you write a detail from the passage, stop and ask yourself: "So what? Why does this matter?" Your answer to that question is your next sentence. Start it with "This shows..." or "This means..."

Detail: Normal people began playing golf too.
So what? → "This shows that sports became open to everyone, not just the rich."
