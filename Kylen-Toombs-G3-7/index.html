Your #1 Error: Run-On Sentences

Two Q1–10 answers on G3.7 lost points to run-ons

Kylen, on G3.7 your biggest point loss happened on short-answer questions that asked you to combine sentences. When you combine, you are supposed to make one clean sentence with a joining word. Instead, two ideas got mashed together with no punctuation. Look at both examples:

FROM G3.7 Q2 — COMBINE SENTENCES, SCORED 0.5/2
Prompt: "Combine sentences 2 and 3 into one clear sentence."
You wrote: "Stores fill big boxes with bright orange pumpkins people decorate their homes with them."
Two complete thoughts jammed together. Needs a period or a joining word like "and."
FROM G3.7 Q8 — WRITE ONE SENTENCE, SCORED 0.33/3
Prompt: "Write one sentence about something that changes as the pumpkin grows and explain why it changes."
You wrote: "The small green pumpkins grow larger each week in the fall they turn orange and stop growing."
Three ideas in one sentence with no stops.
FROM G3.7 Q3 — EXPAND SENTENCE, SCORED 1/2
You wrote: "A pumpkin seed is flat and white inside its hard coat is everything needed to begin a new plant."
Same pattern — two full thoughts with no punctuation between them.
From G3.7 Q2 — which version correctly combines the two sentences?
A Stores fill big boxes with bright orange pumpkins people decorate their homes with them.
B Stores fill big boxes with bright orange pumpkins, and people decorate their homes with them.
THE RULE
When a prompt says "combine" or "write one sentence," you still need to keep ONE complete idea per sentence. If you have two complete thoughts, connect them with a joining word: and, but, so, because.

Two ways to fix a run-on:
1. Add a period and start a new sentence.
2. Add a joining word with a comma.

✗ "Stores fill big boxes with pumpkins people decorate their homes."
✓ "Stores fill big boxes with pumpkins, and people decorate their homes."
THE "PAUSE TEST"
Read each sentence out loud in your head. If you naturally pause in the middle, that is where a period or a joining word belongs. Your brain knows where sentences end — let it tell you.

G3.7 Q6 — FRAGMENT STARTING WITH "BECAUSE" (SCORED 0.33/3)
Prompt: "Write one sentence explaining why pumpkin seeds need warm soil to sprout."
You wrote: "Because if the weather is very dry the seeds may need a little water to help them sprout."
Starting with "Because" makes it a fragment. A complete sentence needs a subject doing something first.
From G3.7 Q4 — which sentence actually includes an appositive about the bees?
A Bees buzz from flower to flower to collect pollen.
B Bees, small pollen-carrying insects, buzz from flower to flower.
WHAT IS AN APPOSITIVE?
An appositive is a renaming noun phrase set off by commas. It tells you more about the noun by giving it another name.

✓ "Bees, small pollen-carrying insects, buzz from flower to flower." (renames bees)
✓ "The pumpkin, a round orange fruit, grew in the garden." (renames pumpkin)
✓ "Maya, my best friend, loves pumpkins." (renames Maya)
THREE CHECKS BEFORE YOU SUBMIT A SHORT ANSWER
1. Is my answer about the right thing from the passage (pumpkins, not me)?
2. Did I do what the prompt named (appositive? because? complete sentence?)?
3. Does my sentence start with a subject doing something (not "Because")?

These three checks take 10 seconds per question. Across the whole test, that is one extra minute — and it saves 3+ points every time.
THE REWRITE TRICK FOR Q1-STYLE PROMPTS
When a prompt says "rewrite this fragment as a complete sentence," ask: who or what is the passage about? Then write the sentence about that thing, not about you. On G3.7, the passage was about pumpkins — so "pumpkins resting on the ground" would have earned full credit.
