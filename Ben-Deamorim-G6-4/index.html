THE 4-STEP PROCESS FOR Q11 (USE ALL OF IT)

POLISHED 18/20 VERSION (your ideas, the three moves applied)
INTRODUCTION — Around the world, people speak many different languages, and even in the United States many people speak more than one language at home. As schools try to teach languages to students of every age, experts have studied what actually works. Students learn new languages most effectively through three methods: immersion classes, AI apps, and starting young when their brains are still developing.

BODY 1 — First, immersion is one of the most powerful ways children learn. In an immersion class, teachers use the new language all day long, and students try to communicate with classmates just by hearing it. The passage also describes pen pal programs that connect students with other countries. This shows that learning happens fastest when students are surrounded by the language every day, because they are forced to use it instead of just memorizing it.

BODY 2 — Another effective method is AI-powered apps. The passage explains that these apps personalize lessons and let students practice just 10 minutes a day for a full year. They also use rewards and competition to keep learners coming back. This proves that the most effective tools are the ones students actually stick with — small daily habits beat long, occasional lessons every time.

BODY 3 — Finally, starting young matters. Experts say learning a second language strengthens memory and improves problem-solving, and young children pick up new sounds more easily because their brains are still developing. This matters because a head start gives young learners years of practice before the language becomes harder to master.

CONCLUSION — In conclusion, immersion, AI apps, and starting early are the three methods that make language learning stick. Experts still debate which age is best, but what matters most is the combination — daily practice, full-language environments, and an early start give every student a real chance to connect with the world.
