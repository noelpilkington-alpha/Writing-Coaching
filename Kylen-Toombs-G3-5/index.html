One Idea Per Sentence

Run-on sentences show up in every paragraph you write

Kylen, look at this pattern — in every Question 11 paragraph, two or three complete thoughts end up mashed together into one sentence. Here are three real examples:

FROM G3.3 Q11 (paragraph)

Sentence runs straight into the next idea without a period.
FROM G3.5 Q11 (your most recent)
"plants and animales began to cover it sponges and seaweed grew and fish began to visit."
Three ideas in one sentence with no stops.
Which version fixes the run-on correctly?
A Plants and animals began to cover it sponges and seaweed grew and fish began to visit.
B Plants and animals began to cover the sculptures. Sponges and seaweed grew, and fish began to visit.
THE RULE
Every sentence should have ONE complete idea. If you read a sentence out loud and it feels like you need to take a breath in the middle, it is probably a run-on.

Two ways to fix it:
1. Add a period and start a new sentence.
2. Add a joining word: and, but, so, because.

✗ "The books had golden spines the books had colorful pages."
✓ "The books had golden spines, and the books had colorful pages." (G3.5 Q3 — you already got this one right!)
THE "PAUSE TEST"
Read each sentence out loud in your head. If you naturally pause in the middle of a sentence, that is where a period or conjunction belongs. Your brain knows where sentences end — let it tell you.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Run-ons are the biggest reason your paragraphs score 75–80% instead of 90%+. Q11 is worth 20 out of 45 total points. Cleaning up the run-ons is worth 2–3 points per test on its own.

MODEL POLISHED PARAGRAPH (19–20/20 level)

Jason chose to place his sculptures under the sea because he wants to protect coral reefs. For example, plants and animals began to cover the sculptures, and sponges and seaweed grew around them. This shows that the sculptures are becoming a safe home for ocean life. Also, each year many swimmers visit the reefs to see the artwork up close. This shows that his sculptures help people care about the ocean by giving them a reason to visit. In the end, Jason's underwater art protects coral reefs while teaching swimmers to love the sea.

The 5-Part Paragraph (Your Next Level)
