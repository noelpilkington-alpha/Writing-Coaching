Fix 1: No Comma After Because

Three points, one habit, in Q1, Q4, Q6 and Q8
THE RULE: THE COMMA TEST
A comma never goes right after because, and never between a subject and its verb. The check: cover the comma and read the words on each side. If both sides could stand alone as sentences, the comma may stay with a joining word beside it. If either side cannot stand alone, delete the comma. Because dogs are my favorite animals cannot stand alone, so no comma goes in front of it or after it.

The same comma, four times:

YOU WROTE
“I would feel happiness because, dogs are my favorite animals.”   “…are parks because, parks are public places…”   “…safe and happy because, resturants are for people…”   “My family, has two dogs…”
Three commas after because, one between the subject My family and its verb has. The ideas were right every time. The comma cost the conventions point.
WITH THE COMMA TEST APPLIED
I would feel happiness because dogs are my favorite animals.  ·  One place where dogs should be allowed is a park because parks are public places with a lot of space.  ·  My family has two dogs.

Where commas do go, and where they do not:

KEEPDogs might growl or bite, so it is best to leave them at home.
Cover the comma: both sides stand alone, and so is beside it.
KEEPIf a dog walked up to me, I would feel happy.
An opening if part gets a comma after it, before the main part.
NO COMMAI would feel happy because dogs are my favorite animals.
The because part cannot stand alone. Nothing goes between because and its reason.
NO COMMASome people, are allergic to dogs.
A subject and its verb are never separated by a single comma.

Fix 2: An Appositive Renames with a Noun

Q1 asked for one. The answer was two sentences and a comma
THE RULE: A NOUN BETWEEN THE COMMAS
An appositive is a noun phrase that renames the noun right before it, set off by commas: My family has two dogs, Lucy and Rocket. The check: cover everything outside the commas and read what is left. Does it start with a noun that could stand in for the thing before it? If what is left is a whole sentence with its own verb, you have joined two sentences, not renamed a noun.
YOU WROTE (Q1, 1 OUT OF 2)
“My family, has two dogs, their names are Lucy and Rocket.” A SECOND SENTENCE, NOT AN APPOSITIVE
Cover the outside: their names are Lucy and Rocket has its own verb. That is sentence 2 again, held on with a comma. The two names alone were the appositive.
WITH THE APPOSITIVE
My family has two dogs, APPOSITIVELucy and Rocket.
Or in the middle: My family’s two dogs, Lucy and Rocket, go almost everywhere with us.
