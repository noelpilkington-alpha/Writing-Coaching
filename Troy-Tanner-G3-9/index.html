#2 Fix: An Appositive Has No Verb of Its Own

Q1 — the third shape you have tried, and the one rule that covers all three

Question 1 asked you to combine two sentences using an appositive:

“A colony has one leader.”  +  “She is the queen bee.”
QUESTION 1 — 1 OUT OF 2
“A colony has one leader, she is the queen bee.”

Read the part after the comma on its own: “she is the queen bee.” That is a complete sentence — it has its own subject and its own verb. So what you built is two whole sentences held together by a comma, which a comma cannot do. If you wanted two sentences, the fix would be a period. But the task wanted one sentence with an appositive, and there is a shorter way to get there.

THE APPOSITIVE VERSION — TWO WORDS SHORTER
“A colony has one leader, the queen bee.”
Drop “she is.” Now the queen bee simply renames leader. No verb of its own — it borrows the sentence it sits inside.

This is the third card of yours to cover appositives, so it is worth seeing all three attempts at once, because they are three different shapes with one thing in common:

Three tests, three shapes
G3.3“A girl, who loves to swim, named sally…” — a describing clause with a verb inside it
G3.5“The Poky Little Puppy, a best-selling story.” — a real appositive, but the sentence around it never got a verb at all
G3.9“…one leader, she is the queen bee.” — a second whole sentence after the comma
THE ONE RULE
[noun], [naming phrase], [rest of the sentence]. The naming phrase has no verb of its own, and the sentence around it does. Two checks: cover the naming phrase — is what is left still a sentence? Then look inside the naming phrase — is there a verb? If there is, it is not an appositive yet.
Which sentence uses an appositive?
AA colony has one leader, she is the queen bee.
BA colony has one leader, the queen bee.

Start with the first sentence, because it is the best one you have opened a paragraph with. “The oak tree is important because it provides shelter to squirrels and birds” — that is a claim with a reason attached, on topic, in position one. Compare it to April’s “Soil is a home to many insects.” This is a different writer.

So where are the six points? Sentences two and three are facts — the lifespan, the acorn, the squirrels burying and forgetting. Good facts, straight from the passage. But each one is put down and left there. The question asked why the oak matters to animals and nature, and the squirrel fact is actually your best answer to the nature half — forgotten acorns become new oaks — except you never say so. The reader has to work it out. When the reader does the explaining, the reader gets the points.

The read-back list is short and lives entirely in this paragraph: hundereds → hundreds, a acorn → an acorn, forgot → forget (the rest of the sentence is in present tense), burried → buried, and the the.

Tap to see your paragraph on the 16 rung and above
Your sentence, your two facts — three sentences added
YOUR PARAGRAPH, DEVELOPED

“The oak tree is important to animals and nature because it provides shelter to squirrels and birds and keeps new trees coming. Squirrels and birds make their homes in its branches, which means one oak is a whole neighborhood of animals, not just a tree. The oak tree can live for hundreds of years, and it all started with a tiny seed called an acorn. Squirrels will bury acorns and then forget where they buried the nuts, and over time the acorn will grow into a mighty oak tree. That means the squirrels are planting the next forest without even knowing it, so the animals and the trees keep each other going. A tree that shelters animals and gets planted by them is important to all of nature.”

Your topic sentence is still first. Both of your facts are still there, in your order and nearly your words. What got added: one “which means” sentence after the first fact, one “that means” sentence after the second, and a closing line. Six sentences. That is the 16 rung and probably the 18.

SO WHAT?
Three added sentences are worth two to four points here — and you need three to pass. Nothing else on the test is worth that much per sentence. Which is the real reason the paragraph should get the most time of anything you do.
