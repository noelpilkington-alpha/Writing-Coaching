Fix 1: Two Sentences Never Share One Comma

Five points on this test, twelve across three. One habit
THE RULE: COVER THE COMMA. IF BOTH SIDES ARE WHOLE SENTENCES, THE COMMA CANNOT HOLD THEM
A comma on its own cannot join two complete sentences. You have three choices: a period and a capital letter; a joining word after the comma (, and / , so / , but); or because or so with no comma at all. The check: put your finger over the comma. Read the left side alone. Read the right side alone. If each one could end with a period, the comma is holding two sentences, and it has to change.

Your four short answers, with the comma covered:

Q4, 1 OUT OF 2
“Anna and Jim ran to the beach, they had found a baby seal stuck in a fishnet.” TWO SENTENCES, ONE COMMA
Left side: Anna and Jim ran to the beach. Right side: They had found a baby seal. Both whole. Fix: Anna and Jim ran to the beach, where they found a baby seal stuck in a fishnet.

Fix 2: An Appositive Renames, and the Job the Instruction Names

Q3 for the third test running, and Q5
THE RULE: READ THE JOB WORD, THEN DO EXACTLY THAT JOB
Each editing question names a job: add an appositive, write the best word to complete the sentence, combine. One point is for doing that job; a good sentence doing a different job earns nothing for it. An appositive is a noun phrase that renames the noun beside it, inside commas: Sally, the baby seal, swam. The check: swap the appositive in for the name. The baby seal swam to the other seals is still a sentence, so it passes. For a blank, the job is one word in the blank, and the rest of the sentence stays exactly as printed.
YOU WROTE (Q3, 1 OUT OF 2)
“Then, Sally swam over to the other seals.” A TRANSITION, NOT AN APPOSITIVE
A clean sentence with a time word added. Nothing renames Sally.
THE APPOSITIVE
Sally, the baby seal, swam over to the other seals.
A noun phrase, inside commas, right after the name it renames. Swap test passes.
YOU WROTE (G4.1 Q3, 1 OUT OF 2)
“Peter Tabichi is from Kenya, Africa, Peter teaches high school science and is known for his dedication to his teaching.” NO APPOSITIVE, AND A COMMA HOLDING TWO SENTENCES
The fix does both jobs at once: Peter Tabichi, a high school science teacher, is from Kenya, Africa.
YOU WROTE (G3.1 Q1, 1 OUT OF 2)
“My family has two dogs, their names are Lucy and Rocket.” NO APPOSITIVE, AND THE SAME COMMA
Drop their names are and the names become the appositive: My family has two dogs, Lucy and Rocket.
YOU WROTE (Q5, 1 OUT OF 2)
“Later, with no sign of the seals, the family placed Sally in a fish box and motored into the bay.” THE JOB WAS ONE WORD IN THE BLANK
The instruction said write the best word to complete the sentence: one joining word, with the printed sentence kept. Since there was no sign of the seals… or Because there was no sign…
