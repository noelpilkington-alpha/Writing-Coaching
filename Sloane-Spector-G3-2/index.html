#1 Focus: The Four Sentence Types

Q4 — the one short answer that slipped

Sloane, on Q4 you were asked for the sentence type of “Workers spent over 30 minutes clearing the ice.” You answered Imperative — but that sentence simply states a fact and ends with a period, so it is declarative.

YOU WROTE (G3.2 Q4)
Sentence: “Workers spent over 30 minutes clearing the ice.”
Your answer: Imperative
Correct answer: Declarative — it states a fact and ends with a period. An imperative gives a command, like “Clear the ice.”
The Four Sentence Types
STATES Declarative — states a fact. Ends with a period. “Workers cleared the ice.”
ASKS Interrogative — asks a question. Ends with a question mark. “Who cleared the ice?”
FEELS Exclamatory — shows strong feeling. Ends with an exclamation point. “What a mess the ice was!”
TELLS Imperative — gives a command. “Clear the ice.”
Which sentence is declarative (states a fact and ends with a period)?
A Clear the ice before the game starts.
B Workers spent over 30 minutes clearing the ice.
THE QUICK TEST
Ask yourself what the sentence does:

• Does it tell you a fact and end with a period? → Declarative
• Does it ask something and end with “?” → Interrogative
• Does it shout a feeling and end with “!” → Exclamatory
• Does it boss someone to do something? → Imperative
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
This was the one short answer that slipped on G3.2. Knowing the four types cold means that point is yours next time — and it is a quick, sure win.
