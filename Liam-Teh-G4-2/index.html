One Idea Per Sentence

Comma splices are costing you points on Q6–Q8 and Q11

Liam, look at this pattern — when you write longer answers, two complete thoughts end up joined with just a comma. That is called a comma splice, and it turns a "Correct" score into "Partial." Here are real examples from your G4.1 test:

FROM G4.1 Q6 (scored 0.67/3)
"I once faced a challenge before which made my learning difficult, the challenge was unkind peers, and that made learning with peer help hard."
Two complete thoughts joined with just a comma. Needs a period or "because."
FROM G4.1 Q7 (scored 0.67/3)
"One lesson from Peter Tabichi's story applies to my own life is his dedication to do good, what I did was donating a lot of toys to charity."
Comma splice again — two complete thoughts jammed together with only a comma.
Which version fixes the comma splice correctly?
A One lesson from his story applies to my life is his dedication, what I did was donate toys to charity.
B One lesson from his story that applies to my life is his dedication to do good. Inspired by that, I donated a lot of toys to charity.
THE RULE
Every sentence should have ONE complete idea. If you read a sentence out loud and you naturally want to stop in the middle, that is where a period belongs — not a comma.

Three ways to fix a comma splice:
1. Replace the comma with a period and start a new sentence.
2. Add a joining word after the comma: and, but, so, because.
3. Rewrite to make one thought depend on the other using because, when, or since.

✗ "It made learning difficult, the challenge was unkind peers."
✓ "It made learning difficult because the challenge was unkind peers."
THE "PAUSE TEST"
Read each sentence out loud in your head. If you need a real stop in the middle — not just a short pause — a comma is not enough. Your brain knows where sentences end; let it tell you.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
On G4.1, three of your "explanation" questions (Q6, Q7, Q8) scored 0.67/3 instead of 3/3 — almost all because of comma splices. That is about 1 full point lost per test just from this one habit. Fix it and you are already at 88%+.
