Error #1: What a True Appositive Is

Q3 asked for an appositive — you joined with "and" instead

Alyan, Q3 asked you to combine two sentences using an appositive. You combined them with the word "and," which makes a fine sentence — but it is not an appositive, so the question could not give full credit.

Q3 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2 weighted points)
"Porcupines are rodents, and they are slow movers of the forest."

The problem: Joining with "and" creates a compound sentence — two separate ideas linked together. An appositive does something different: it renames the noun with a noun phrase, set off by commas, right next to the noun.

CORRECT APPOSITIVE
"Porcupines, the slow movers of the forest, are rodents."

Now "the slow movers of the forest" renames "Porcupines." It sits right after the noun, between two commas, and tells the reader more about what porcupines are — no "and" needed.

THE APPOSITIVE PATTERN
Noun, [renaming phrase], rest of sentence.

The renaming phrase is a noun phrase that names the same thing again. It comes right after the noun, between two commas. If you reach for "and," stop — that joins two ideas; it does not rename one.
Which sentence uses a true appositive?
A "The porcupine is a rodent, and it has sharp quills."
B "The porcupine, a sharp-quilled rodent, climbs trees at night."
WHY THIS MATTERS
When a question says "combine using an appositive," it is testing this exact pattern: Noun, [renaming phrase], rest of sentence. Spot the word "appositive," reach for commas instead of "and," and this point is yours.

Error #2: Subject-Verb & Pronoun Agreement

Q6 — the subject and verb need to match

Alyan, on Q6 your idea was right, but the subject and verb did not match. When the subject is singular ("a porcupine"), the verb needs the -s ending ("lifts," "feels"), and the pronoun has to match too.

Q6 — YOUR ANSWER (2 out of 3 weighted points)
"A porcupine lift its quills when it feel scared because it is scared and wants to protect themself."

The problem: "A porcupine" is one porcupine, so the verbs need to be "lifts" and "feels," and the pronoun should be "itself" (one porcupine = itself). Also, "scared because it is scared" repeats the same idea — the second half can name a real reason instead.

CORRECT AGREEMENT
"A porcupine lifts its quills when it feels scared because the sharp quills protect itself from danger."
THE AGREEMENT RULE
One subject takes the -s verb. A plural subject takes a plural pronoun.

• One porcupine → "A porcupine lifts… it feels… it protects itself."
• Many porcupines → "Porcupines lift… they feel… they protect themselves."

Before submitting, find the subject, then check the verb matches it.
Which sentence has matching subject and verb?
A "A porcupine lift its quills when it feel scared."
B "A porcupine lifts its quills when it feels scared."
WHY THIS MATTERS
This same pattern showed up in your paragraph opener too ("Porcupines are amazing… the way it protects itself"). Find the subject, match the verb and the pronoun, and you protect points across the whole test.
