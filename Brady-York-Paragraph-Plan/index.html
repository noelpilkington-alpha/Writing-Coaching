YOUR FACTS, WITH THE WHY AFTER EACH
New research shows that horses communicate with their faces and their eyes, and that they can learn to tell us what they want. For thousands of years people gave horses orders and the conversation went one way, so horses learned to watch us far more closely than we imagined. Horses are very visual animals, which is why their faces carry so much: researchers at Sussex found seventeen expressions, more than dogs or chimpanzees. Horses read our faces too, because they turned to look at angry photos with their left eye and their hearts beat faster. In Norway, horses learned symbols for blanket on and blanket off and used them to ask, which means the conversation is finally starting to run both ways.
