Step 1: The Prompt Is Your Outline

Three parts in the prompt, three body paragraphs, same order
THE RULE: THE PROMPT IS THE OUTLINE
Every grade-6 essay prompt names three things to explain. Those are your three body paragraphs, in that order. Before you write a sentence, copy the three parts into the box as three topic sentences, put a thesis above them and a conclusion line below. The check: count the parts the prompt names. Count your body paragraphs. Same number, same order.

Here is the G6.10 prompt, with its three parts pulled out:

THE PROMPT SAID
Explain how problems with the mail led to new solutions. Use information from the passage to explain PART 1why people refused letters before stamps existed and why the Penny Black fixed that, PART 2why the Penny Black created a new problem and how post offices solved it, and PART 3why later changes to stamps solved other problems.
THE PLAN, TYPED INTO THE BOX FIRST
THESISEach problem with the mail led to a new solution, and each solution made sending a letter easier for ordinary people.
BODY 1 TOPIC SENTENCEThe first problem was the price of a letter, and the Penny Black fixed it.
BODY 2 TOPIC SENTENCEHowever, the Penny Black created a problem of its own, and post offices solved it by changing the stamp’s colour.
BODY 3 TOPIC SENTENCEFinally, later changes to the stamp solved smaller problems that came up every day.
CONCLUSIONThe thesis again, in new words.
Five lines. Two minutes. Now the essay writes itself downwards from this, and the structure points, five of the twenty, are already safe.

The introduction and the conclusion have a shape too, and it is the same shape reversed:

INTRODUCTION: GENERAL, SPECIFIC, THESIS
GENERAL STATEMENTMost inventions begin with a problem that annoys enough people. SPECIFIC STATEMENTIn Britain before 1840, the problem was the mail: letters cost so much that people refused to accept them. THESISThe postage stamp solved that problem, then solved the new problems it created, and each fix made sending a letter easier for ordinary people.
CONCLUSION: THESIS IN NEW WORDS, SPECIFIC, GENERAL
THESIS, IN NEW WORDSEvery problem with the mail pushed someone to fix it, and every fix made a letter cheaper, safer or easier to use. SPECIFIC STATEMENTThe penny price brought people back to the post office, the red stamp stopped cheating, and the new edges made stamps simple to use. GENERAL STATEMENTThat is how most useful inventions grow: one problem, one solution, and then the next.
NOT A THESISIn this essay I am going to explain three problems with the mail and how they were solved.
An announcement. It promises to explain and explains nothing. A thesis states the answer.

Step 2: A Fact, then Why It Worked

The five evidence points, and where your 16s lost them
THE RULE: A FACT, THEN WHY IT WORKED
Five of the twenty points are for evidence. For each problem, give a detail from the passage, then explain why the solution worked, not just what it was. The check: every detail passes the trace test: can you point to the sentence in the passage that says it? And after every detail comes a sentence that starts This shows why. Two facts and two This shows in each body paragraph.

Step 4: The Last Six Points

Strong sentences, then a read-back
THE RULE: VARY THE OPENERS, THEN READ IT BACK
Three points are for strong sentences: clear, formal, and not all built the same way. Three points are for editing. The check: look at the first word of every sentence in a body paragraph.
