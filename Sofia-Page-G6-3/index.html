Work The 4-Step Essay Process

The essay is 2/3 of the test. It deserves the full process.

Sofia, the G6 test is built so that the essay is 20 of 30 total points — two-thirds of the test. A strong essay requires a full process, not a single pass.

G6.2 — full process Essay: 16/20 83%
G6.3 — skipped revise Essay: 13/20 77%

The gap between those two tests is the revise step. Here is what a full essay process looks like:

Step 1
Read
Read the passage twice. Underline the main claim and two facts.
Step 2
Plan
Jot a 5-paragraph outline. Thesis + 3 body points + close.
Step 3
Draft
Write each paragraph. Use a fact from the passage in every body paragraph.
Step 4
Revise
Reread. Check spelling, transitions, and that each point has evidence.
Which approach to a 20-point essay earns a higher score?
A Read the passage once, start writing, submit when the page feels full.
B Read twice, jot a quick outline, draft each paragraph, reread to catch errors and add missing evidence.
WHAT THE REVISE STEP CATCHES
On G6.3, you spelled "beneficial" as "benificial". A single reread would catch that. A revise pass also catches:

• A body paragraph with no fact from the passage
• A thesis statement that does not match what you actually wrote
• Two paragraphs that accidentally make the same point
• A missing transition between paragraphs

Each of these is worth 1–2 points. Revising catches three of them in five minutes.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
On G6.2 you worked the process and scored 16/20 on the essay. On G6.3 you skipped revise and scored 13/20. Same skill level. Different process. The fix is not new learning — it is using the process you already used on G6.2.

Q11 Essay — Use Real Evidence

"Support each point with facts from the passage" is 5 of 20 points.

Sofia, the G6.3 essay rubric breaks down like this:

Essay Rubric (Q11 G6.3)
5 pts 5 clear paragraphs (intro + 3 body + conclusion)
5 pts Use evidence and explain it — facts from the passage + explanation
4 pts Stay organized and on topic
3 pts Use strong, varied sentences
3 pts Edit your writing (spelling, punctuation, grammar)

The two rubric lines where G6.3 slipped are the ones in orange. Evidence + editing. Together they are worth 8 of 20 points. That is the whole difference between 13/20 and 20/20.

Which body paragraph sentence earns the evidence points?
A Citizen scientists help monarchs by studying them and sharing what they learn.
B According to the passage, citizen scientists tag butterflies, count caterpillars, and record sightings online, which gives researchers the data they need to track where monarch populations are shrinking.
THE EVIDENCE PATTERN
Every body paragraph should have this shape:

1. Topic sentence — state the point of the paragraph.
2. Evidence — "According to the passage..." or "The author writes..." followed by a fact.
3. Explanation — "This shows..." or "This matters because..." followed by what the fact proves.
4. Wrap — a sentence that connects the point back to your thesis.
SPEED TIP
While you read the passage, underline or jot down three facts you might use in your essay. When you get to the body paragraphs, each fact has a home. You never have to flip back through the passage wondering what to cite.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
The rubric line "use evidence and explain it" is 5 points. Missing this one line pulls your essay score from the 90s down into the 70s. Adding one real fact to each body paragraph solves it completely.

Q11 Essay — Edit Your Writing

3 points for spelling, punctuation, and grammar. All catchable on a reread.

Sofia, the "Edit your writing" rubric line is worth 3 points. It rewards clean spelling, punctuation, and grammar across the whole essay. That is a full letter grade sitting in the final minutes of the test.

A SPELLING ERROR THAT SHOWED UP IN YOUR G6.2 ESSAY
"high-speed trains are a benificial and necessary innovation..."
Should be: beneficial (b-e-n-e-f-i-c-i-a-l)

A 5-minute reread catches words like this almost every time. Here is what to look for on your edit pass:

Which editing pass is most likely to catch the points you are losing?
A Skim the essay once at normal reading speed, looking for anything that feels off.
B Read each paragraph aloud in your head. Pause at every period. Check any word longer than 6 letters.
YOUR EDIT CHECKLIST
A 5-minute edit pass should cover:

Spelling: Any word longer than 6 letters — does it look right? If not, change it.
End marks: Every sentence ends with a period, question mark, or exclamation point.
Capitalization: First letter of every sentence. Proper nouns.
Run-ons: If a sentence feels too long to read in one breath, split it into two.
Thesis match: Does the conclusion actually match what the intro promised?
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
The "Edit your writing" line is 3 points. Combine that with the 5 points for evidence, and the edit+evidence pass is worth 8 points on the essay alone. Do both and your 77% jumps into the 90s.

The 5-Paragraph Essay Map

The structure the rubric is grading against.

Sofia, the G6 essay rubric rewards a specific shape. Once you see the shape, you can build any essay in 30 minutes of drafting. Here is the map:

The 5-Paragraph Essay
INTRO Hook + background + thesis (the 3 reasons you will prove)
BODY 1 Reason 1 topic sentence + evidence + explanation + wrap
BODY 2 Reason 2 topic sentence + evidence + explanation + wrap
BODY 3 Reason 3 topic sentence + evidence + explanation + wrap
CLOSE Restate the thesis in new words + big-picture takeaway
Which intro sets up a stronger 5-paragraph essay?
A Monarch butterflies are beautiful insects that migrate thousands of miles every year. Many people care about monarchs.
B Monarch butterflies are in trouble, but people around the world are stepping up to help. Families are planting milkweed, citizen scientists are tagging butterflies, and communities are protecting overwintering forests — three practical ways the passage says we are making a difference.
THE 3-POINT THESIS FORMULA
Your thesis sentence should name three reasons or three ways or three causes. This gives your essay its shape and signals to the grader that you have 3 body paragraphs coming.

Template: "X is true because of A, B, and C."
Example: "Animated movies teach children important life lessons through showing teamwork, modeling bravery, and exploring emotions in safe ways."
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
The rubric gives 5 points for 5 clear paragraphs and 4 points for staying organized and on topic. That is 9 of 20 points just for the shape. The 3-point thesis guarantees both.
