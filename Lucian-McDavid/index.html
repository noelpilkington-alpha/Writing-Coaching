SUBORDINATING CONJUNCTIONS TO MEMORIZE
These words go at the beginning and show a relationship (cause, time, contrast):

"Would Have" — Not "Would Of"

Q6 & Q9 — Fix a common grammar mistake and develop your answers

Q6 — "Would of" is not correct English:

YOU WROTE
I would of helped the seal... because I wouldn't want to see the seal stay in there trap.

"Would of" sounds right when you say it out loud, but it's always "would have" (or "would've"). Also, "there" should be "their" — the trap belongs to the fishermen.

would of → would have (or would've)
could of → could have (or could've)
should of → should have (or should've)
Tap to see the improved version
Read the explanation above first
FIXED
I would have helped the seal because I wouldn't want to see it stay in their trap.
WHY THIS MISTAKE HAPPENS
When you say "would've" out loud, it sounds like "would of." But "of" is a preposition — it can't come after "would." The correct word is always have.

Quick check: Every time you write "of" after would/could/should, change it to have.

THE RULE: ANSWER + BECAUSE + DETAIL
Whenever a question asks for your opinion, follow this pattern:
1. Give your answer: "I would ask for help from experts."
2. Add "because": "...because they know how to handle wild animals."
3. Add one more detail: "I would not try to do it myself because I might hurt the seal."

Two or three sentences is always better than one.

Build Your Paragraph

Q11 — You need more sentences and the TS → D → W plan

Your paragraph scored 16/20. You have smart ideas and a nice writing style, but you only wrote 3 sentences. A G3 paragraph needs much more development.

YOUR PARAGRAPH
Tajiri's love for catching bugs as a kid, one day influenced him to make a video game about catching creatures called Pokemon. The game became very popular over time, and now it even has a card game, a movie, an app, and even a TV show. Now Pokemon is a very famous game and millions of people play it.

Issue 1: Only 3 sentences — needs more development

Your paragraph jumps from "he loved bugs" straight to "the game became popular." You're missing the middle — the details about how his childhood led to creating the game, and why each detail matters.

Issue 2: Unnecessary comma

"as a kid, one day influenced" → "as a kid one day influenced" (no comma needed)

Issue 3: "even" used twice

You wrote "it even has a card game... and even a TV show." Try to avoid repeating the same word in the same sentence.

Here's the G3 paragraph plan — aim for 6-8 sentences:

Paragraph Plan — TS → D1 → W → D2 → W → D3 → W → CS
TS = Topic Sentence  •  D = Detail from the article  •  W = Why it matters  •  CS = Concluding Sentence
TS Tajiri's love for catching bugs as a kid led him to create Pokémon.
D1 As a child, Tajiri spent hours catching bugs — his friends called him "Dr. Bug."
W This shows how much he loved collecting creatures, which became the main idea behind Pokémon.
D2 When he grew up, he remembered his childhood and wanted to turn it into a video game.
W This explains why he combined catching creatures with video games to make something new.
D3 Pokémon became huge — with cards, TV shows, movies, apps, and more.
W This shows that one kid's hobby turned into something millions of people enjoy.
CS Tajiri's childhood love of nature led him to create one of the most popular games in the world.
THE KEY: ADD "WHY IT MATTERS" AFTER EACH DETAIL
You already have a great topic sentence. The next step is to slow down and add details from the article with explanations. After each detail, ask yourself: "So what? Why does this show that his childhood influenced Pokémon?" That answer is your "W" sentence. Then end with a concluding sentence. Aim for 6-8 sentences instead of 3.
