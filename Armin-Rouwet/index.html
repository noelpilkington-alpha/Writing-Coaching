Q4: Turning a Sentence into a Question

Statements TELL — Questions ASK

The question asked you to change this sentence into a question:

Original Sentence
Jordan asked his classmates to help organize the shelves.
YOU WROTE
Jordan asked if his classmates would help organize the shelves.

This is still a statement. It tells us what Jordan asked. A question asks the reader something directly and ends with a question mark.

See the difference:

STATEMENT (WHAT YOU WROTE)
"Jordan asked if his classmates would help organize the shelves." — This tells us something. It's a statement about what Jordan asked.
QUESTION (WHAT THE ANSWER SHOULD BE)
"Did Jordan ask his classmates to help organize the shelves?" — This asks the reader something directly.
THE QUESTION TEST
Read your sentence out loud. Are you telling someone something or asking them something? A question needs the reader to answer "yes" or "no" (or give information). If your sentence just reports what happened, it's still a statement.

More examples — move the helper word to the front:

She went to the store. → Did she go to the store?
The dog is sleeping. → Is the dog sleeping?

Pattern: Move the helper word (did, is, was, could, would) to the front of the sentence.

Q7: Check Your Pronouns

Who are you writing about?

Q7 asked: "How did Ms. Bright feel when the students helped?"

YOU WROTE
He felt happy because the students made him feel welcome.

Two things to fix:

1. Ms. Bright is a woman — use "She" and "her," not "He" and "him." The title "Ms." tells you it's a woman.

2. The feeling doesn't match the question. The question asks how Ms. Bright felt when students helped — she would feel grateful or thankful, not "welcome." ("Welcome" is what a new person feels.)

BETTER
She felt grateful because the students helped clean up the library without being asked.
THE PRONOUN CHECK
Before you write he/she/him/her, go back and check: Who am I writing about? Is this person a boy/man or a girl/woman?

• Ms. or Mrs. = woman → she / her
• Mr. = man → he / him

This is an easy fix that can save you points!

Also: Read the question carefully. "How did Ms. Bright feel when students helped?" → The feeling should be about receiving help (grateful, thankful, happy) — not about feeling welcome.
