Q8 Fix — "Then" vs "Than"

Can you pick the right word? Then we will explain why.
📈 "Then" and "than" are words that sound alike but mean different things. This is a common mix-up — once you learn the trick, you will catch it every time.

You wrote a sentence about listening to books versus reading them. Which word fits when you are comparing two things?

Tap the sentence that uses the right word for a comparison:
A "I would rather listen to a book then read it, because that is faster."
B "I would rather listen to a book than read it, because that is faster."
YOU WROTE
I would rather listen to a book then read it, because that is faster.
THEN = time / order "First this, then that."
THAN = comparing "Bigger than, rather than"
MEMORY TRICK
THAN has an A — just like compArison.
Whenever you are comparing two things (rather X than Y, bigger than, more than), use than.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Using the wrong word can change what your sentence means. "I would rather listen to a book then read it" sounds like you want to do both — listen first, then read. But you meant you prefer listening over reading. The right word makes your meaning clear.

Q10 Fix — Capitalize "I" + "Too" vs "To"

Can you spot both errors? Then we will fix them.
📈 These are two small but important rules. "I" is always a capital letter, and "to," "too," and "two" each have their own meaning. Let's make sure you know which is which!

Your sentence has two small mistakes. Can you find the one with the capital letter error?

Tap the sentence that correctly capitalizes "I":
A "...because i'm too old for them."
B "...because I'm too old for them."
YOU WROTE
I don't think I would like reading a Little Golden Book, because i'm to old for them.

Fix 1: Always capitalize "I"

i'm → I'm

The letter "I" is always a capital letter when it means yourself — even inside shortened words like I'm, I'll, I've, and I'd.

Fix 2: "To" vs "Too"

to old → too old
TO vs TOO vs TWO
To = direction or purpose ("go to the store," "want to read")
Too = also OR way too much ("too old," "me too")
Two = the number 2

Quick test: If you can replace the word with "very" or "also," use too.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Grading feedback checks for correct spelling and capitalization. These two small fixes — capitalizing "I" and using "too" instead of "to" — are easy points you can pick up every time.

Q2 & Q9 Fix — Remove Extra Commas

Can you find where the commas do not belong?
📈 You added commas in two places where they are not needed. This is part of a pattern — you sometimes put commas between words that should stay together. Let's fix that habit!

One of these sentences has a comma that should not be there. Can you tell which one?

Tap the sentence that has the WRONG comma:
A "The Poky Little Puppy, was very popular."
B "The Poky Little Puppy was very popular."

Q2 — No comma between the subject and the verb

YOU WROTE
The Poky Little Puppy, was very popular.
FIXED
The Poky Little Puppy was very popular.

Never put a comma between the subject ("The Poky Little Puppy") and its verb ("was"). They belong together.

Q9 — No comma before "because" here

YOU WROTE
One reason the Little Golden Books became so popular is, because they were so cheap.
FIXED
One reason the Little Golden Books became so popular is because they were so cheap.

When "because" directly explains the main idea, you do not need a comma before it.

COMMA QUICK RULES
1. Never put a comma between the subject and its verb.
2. Do not put a comma right before "because" when it explains the main idea.
3. When you are not sure, read the sentence out loud. If you do not naturally pause there, skip the comma.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Extra commas break up your sentence where it should flow smoothly. They make it harder for the reader to follow your idea — and they cost you points.

How to plan your paragraph before writing
Before you start writing, jot down: Topic, Detail 1, Why, Detail 2, Why, Detail 3, Why, Ending. This takes 30 seconds and it makes sure you write enough sentences and explain why each detail matters. Planning first is the single best thing you can do to raise your paragraph score.
