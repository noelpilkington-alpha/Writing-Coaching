THE RULE: WHO, WHAT THEY DO, THEN BECAUSE
A whole sentence has a who (I, dogs, a dog park) and a doing word (would feel, should be, like). Then comes because and the reason. Three points: one for a whole sentence, one for the because, one for spelling and the period. The check: cover the question and read your answer alone. Does it make sense by itself? Does it have a because?

THE RULE: THE ANSWER, A FACT, THIS SHOWS, AN ENDING
The first sentence answers the question and says how: POINT Soil is important to Earth HOW because it feeds plants and gives animals a home. Then a DETAIL · SHOW: one fact from the article, something you can put your finger on. Then a DETAIL · SO WHAT: This shows… what the fact proves. Then a CONCLUDING SENTENCE that puts it together. Four sentences at least. Six is better. The check: count your sentences. Fewer than four, keep going.

THE RULE: SAY THE THING, A COMMA, THEN ITS OTHER NAME
An appositive gives something another name, right after it, with a comma: My family has two dogs, Lucy and Rocket. The names are the other name for the dogs. The check: cover the words after the comma. The sentence should still be whole. Then read the covered words: are they another name for the thing before the comma?
