How Appositives Work

Q4 — You can spot them; now learn to write them

You identified an appositive perfectly on Q5 — great job! But on Q4, you struggled to write one. Let's look at why:

YOU WROTE (Q4)
The book are still popular, so fun to read, are known for their golden spines.

Two issues here: "book" should be "books", and "so fun to read" is an opinion — it doesn't describe what the books are. An appositive renames or describes the noun.

WITH A PROPER APPOSITIVE
The books, known for their golden spines, are still popular.

"Known for their golden spines" tells the reader something factual about the books — that's what an appositive does.

THE APPOSITIVE PATTERN
Noun, [what it is or what it's known for], rest of sentence.

Think of it like a whisper to the reader: "The books — oh, by the way, they're known for their golden spines — are still popular."

The appositive should describe what the noun IS, not your opinion about it:
• "so fun to read" = opinion (not an appositive)
• "known for their golden spines" = description (appositive)
