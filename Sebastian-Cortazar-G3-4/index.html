#2: Capitalize Names

A person's name always starts with a capital letter
🔍 Pattern alert: On Q8 and Q9 you wrote the name layla with a lowercase l. A person's name is a proper noun, so it always starts with a capital letter. This is an objective rule — a grader will always count a lowercase name as an error.

Sebastian, here are your real slips. The idea in each answer is fine — the only fix is giving the name Layla the capital L it needs.

YOU WROTE (Q8)
"The most important moment was when layla chose to do the right thing."
CORRECTED
"The most important moment was when Layla chose to do the right thing."
YOU WROTE (Q9)
"Nature helped layla decide to do the right thing."
CORRECTED
"Nature helped Layla decide to do the right thing."
THE RULE
Capitalize every person's name. Just like the names Layla, Anna, and Sebastian — a person's name always starts with a capital letter, every single time it appears. The same goes for the names of places, like Britain.
Which sentence is correct?
A In the story, layla chose to protect nature.
B In the story, Layla chose to protect nature.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
This is a tiny fix, but it is objective — a grader will always count a lowercase name as an error. Catching it on your read-back keeps points that are otherwise easy to lose. Whenever a person's name appears in your answer, check that it starts with a capital letter.
