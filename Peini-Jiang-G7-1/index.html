Step 2 Error: Sentence Type (Q2)

You picked Interrogative when the answer was Declarative

Peini, on Q2 you were asked to identify the type of sentence. You selected Interrogative, but the correct answer was Declarative. This cost you 1 point.

🔎 Step 2 check: "What skill is this question asking me to use?" Answer: identifying sentence types. A declarative sentence makes a statement and ends with a period. An interrogative sentence asks a question and ends with a question mark.
THE QUICK CHECK
When a question asks you to identify sentence type, look at two things:

1. What does the sentence do? (States something? Asks something? Gives a command? Shows strong emotion?)
2. What punctuation ends it? (Period = declarative or imperative. Question mark = interrogative. Exclamation mark = exclamatory.)

If the sentence ends with a period and makes a statement about a fact or opinion, it is declarative. It does not matter if the topic involves a question or curiosity. The sentence itself must literally ask a question to be interrogative.
Which sentence is interrogative?
A The team wondered whether the solar panels would generate enough electricity for the whole building.
B Will the solar panels generate enough electricity for the whole building?
THE RULE
A sentence is only interrogative if it directly asks a question (question structure + question mark). Sentences that talk about questions or curiosity are still declarative if they end with a period.

Step 1 Error: Conjunction Logic (Q3)

Cause-vs-effect order matters

Peini, on Q3 you chose the wrong conjunction because the cause and effect were in the wrong order in your answer. This is a Step 1 error: reading the sentence more carefully the second time reveals which clause is the cause and which is the effect.

🔎 How conjunction logic works: Conjunctions like "because," "so," "since," and "therefore" connect a cause to an effect. But they put them in different orders. "Because" introduces the cause. "So" introduces the effect. If you pick the wrong one, the logic of the sentence flips.
THE FIX: LABEL CAUSE AND EFFECT FIRST
Before choosing a conjunction, label the two halves of the sentence:

• Which half is the reason (cause)?
• Which half is the result (effect)?

Then pick the conjunction that matches:
• "because" → what follows is the CAUSE
• "so" → what follows is the EFFECT
• "since" → what follows is the CAUSE
• "therefore" → what follows is the EFFECT
The solar panels reduced the school's electricity bill. Which conjunction correctly shows cause and effect?
A The electricity bill dropped because the solar panels generated clean energy throughout the summer.
B The solar panels generated clean energy because the electricity bill dropped significantly.
STEP 1 IN ACTION
Reading the sentence twice and labeling "cause" and "effect" takes five seconds. On Q3, that five seconds would have earned you 1 point back. Combined with Q2, following Steps 1 and 2 carefully gives you a perfect 10/10 on the multiple choice section.

Step 5 Error: Essay Mechanics

"it's" vs "its" + incomplete opening sentence

Peini, your essay earned 14 out of 20. Two mechanical issues cost you points that Step 5 (proofread using the rubric) would catch:

ISSUE 1: "IT'S" vs "ITS"
You wrote it's when you meant its (possessive). "It's" means "it is." "Its" means "belonging to it."
THE QUICK TEST
Every time you write "it's," try replacing it with "it is." If the sentence still makes sense, keep the apostrophe. If it does not make sense, remove the apostrophe and write "its."

• "The school improved its energy use." → "The school improved it is energy use" = does NOT make sense. So: no apostrophe.
• "It's a great project." → "It is a great project" = makes sense. So: keep the apostrophe.
ISSUE 2: INCOMPLETE OPENING SENTENCE
Your essay begins with a sentence fragment that does not form a complete thought. A complete sentence needs a subject and a verb that together express a finished idea.
STEP 5 IN ACTION
When proofreading your essay, check one category at a time:

Pass 1: Does every sentence have a subject AND a verb AND express a complete thought?
Pass 2: Are apostrophes correct? ("it's" = "it is"; "its" = possessive)
Pass 3: Does the essay answer the prompt's actual question with enough development and explanation?
Which sentence uses "its/it's" correctly?
A The project increased it's efficiency by using solar panels on the roof.
B The project increased its efficiency by using solar panels on the roof.
WHY THIS MATTERS
Mechanical errors like "it's/its" and sentence fragments are easy points to lose. They have nothing to do with your ideas or knowledge. Step 5 (proofread using the rubric, one category at a time) catches these automatically. Build the habit now and these points come back on every future test.

Your Essay Needs More Development

14/20 → 18+ means more explanation after every piece of evidence

Peini, your essay has good ideas and engages with the Solar Panel Project passage. But it stays at 14/20 because you present evidence without fully explaining what it means and why it matters. This is a Step 4 issue: when writing your response, every piece of evidence needs 1 to 2 sentences of development after it.

THE D+E PATTERN
After you state a fact or piece of evidence, add:

D (Development): Explain what the evidence means in your own words.
E (Explanation): Connect it back to the essay's main argument. Why does this evidence matter?

Without D+E: "The school saved money on electricity." (states a fact, stops)
With D+E: "The school saved money on electricity. This means the solar panels paid for themselves within a few years, proving that renewable energy is not just good for the environment but also makes financial sense for schools with limited budgets."
