1. Read the fact in the passage.
2. Cover the passage with your hand.

It doesn’t have to sound fancy. It just has to sound like you.

7 What If I’m Stuck?

If you don’t know how to start:

Use the Question Flip. Write the question words, add “because,” and finish with the first thing that comes to mind. You can fix it later. Don’t open the passage yet.

If the grader keeps saying “copied”:

Do not look at the passage while you write. Read it first. Then cover it up. Write your whole paragraph from memory. It does not have to be perfect—it just has to be yours.

If you can’t think of what to say about a detail:

Ask yourself: “Why would someone care about this?” or “What does this remind me of?” Your answer is the explanation.

8 Quick Spelling & Grammar Fix

Your Conventions score was 3 out of 5. You need a 4. Here is the grammar mistake the grader found:

Parallel structure — making a list match:

✗ You wrote: “Golden Retrievers have soft fur, friendly faces, being gentle, and good with children.”
(The list mixes nouns with a verb phrase. “Being gentle” doesn’t match the other items.)
Which version fixes the list so all items match?
A “Golden Retrievers have soft fur, friendly faces, gentle personalities, and good behavior with children.”
B “Golden Retrievers have soft fur, friendly faces, being gentle, and they are good with children.”
List rule: When you write a list, make every item the same kind of word. If you start with nouns (things), keep using nouns. If you start with actions, keep using actions.

Check: Read each item in your list. Do they all sound the same kind? If one sounds different, change it to match the others.
The good news: This was your only grammar mistake. Fix it and your Conventions score goes from 3 to 4 or 5. Read your sentences out loud before submitting—if something sounds off, it probably is.
Coach tip: If Dominic writes a list, have him read each item separately: “soft fur” — thing. “Friendly faces” — thing. “Being gentle” — wait, that’s an action, not a thing. Can you make it a thing? “Gentle personalities” — yes!
