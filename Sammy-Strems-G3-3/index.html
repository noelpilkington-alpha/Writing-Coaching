Question 7 Fix — Watch for Typos

A small spelling mistake cost you a point

Question 7 asked: "What do you think was the most meaningful moment in the story? Explain why in one sentence."

You had a great answer, but one small word was misspelled. Can you spot which sentence has the error?

Tap the sentence that has a spelling mistake:
A When they release Sally withe other seals, because it shows a moment of connection.
B When they release Sally with the other seals, because it shows a moment of connection.
WHAT YOU WROTE
"When they release Sally withe other seals, because it shows a moment of connection."
CORRECTED VERSION
"When they release Sally with the other seals, because it shows a moment of connection."

Your ideas were perfect here — a really thoughtful answer about connection. The only issue was "withe" instead of "with the" (two separate words). That small typo is what cost you the point.

SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Small typos like running two words together can change how your writing is scored. When you finish writing, read your sentence back to yourself slowly. If two words look stuck together, add a space.

Question 10 Fix — Clear Sentences

Saying something once, clearly, is better than repeating it

Question 10 asked: "What lesson does this story teach about kindness or responsibility?"

Your answer had the right idea, but the sentence got tangled up. Can you pick the clearer version?

Tap the sentence that reads more clearly:
A It teaches to always be alwsays take action in a responible way and to be kind to marine life.
B It teaches us to always take action in a responsible way and to be kind to marine life.
WHAT YOU WROTE
"It teaches to always be alwsays take action in a responible way and to be kind to marine life."
CLEANER VERSION
"It teaches us to always take action in a responsible way and to be kind to marine life."

Two things happened here:

  • Repeated idea: "always be" and "always take action" got mixed together. You only need to say it once: "always take action."
  • Spelling: "alwsays" should be "always" and "responible" should be "responsible."
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
When you write quickly, your brain sometimes starts one sentence and switches to another halfway through. That is totally normal! The fix is simple: after you write a sentence, read it back quietly and ask, "Did I say the same thing twice?" If so, pick the version you like better and cross out the other.

Question 11 Fix — Your Paragraph

Question 11 is worth 20 points — almost half the test. Let's make yours stronger.
📈 This is part of a pattern across your last 3 tests. The structure of your writing is the consistent difference-maker.

Your paragraph about how Satoshi Tajiri's childhood led to creating Pokémon had good ideas — but it can be stronger with better structure. Every strong paragraph follows the same building blocks:

Paragraph Building Blocks
Topic Sentence Answers the prompt directly — tells the reader what the paragraph is about
Detail + Why A fact from the article + explain why it matters
Detail + Why Another fact + explain why it matters
Closing Wraps up the main idea in different words

Now look at what you wrote. Can you spot which topic sentence answers the prompt better?

The prompt asked: "Explain how Tajiri's childhood led to creating Pokémon." Which topic sentence answers that?
A "Tajiri is a child who had great interest in bugs as a child."
B "Satoshi Tajiri's childhood love of collecting bugs and playing video games led him to create Pokémon."

Here is what you wrote, with the building blocks labeled:

[Topic Sentence — doesn't answer the prompt directly] "Tajiri is a child who had great intrest in bugs as a child."

[Detail — but no explanation of WHY it matters] "His intrest brought him ideas when he was in college. He was studying to make games."

[Detail — but no explanation of WHY it matters] "As looked into other people's games he wasn't quite fond of them. He was intrested in creating a better one."

[Detail — good connection!] "So he used his intrest of bugs to create a game we all know and love today. Pokemon!"

[Closing — too vague] "His intrest led to fuffiling his career. That shows us we can too."

What you did well: You told the story of how Tajiri went from bugs to games to Pokémon. That's the right content!

What to fix:

  • Topic sentence: Start by answering the prompt — "Tajiri's childhood love of collecting bugs and playing video games led him to create Pokémon."
  • Detail + Why: After each fact, explain why it matters. For example: "He didn't like the games that existed, so he decided to make his own."
  • Closing: Wrap up with a sentence that connects back to the prompt — "Tajiri's childhood hobbies turned into one of the most popular games in the world."

STRONGER VERSION USING THE BUILDING BLOCKS

Spelling to fix: You also spelled "interest" as "intrest" five times. Remember: inter + est = interest (like international + best).

fuffiling → fulfilling
SO WHAT? WHY STRUCTURE MATTERS MORE THAN SPELLING
A strong topic sentence, details with explanations, and a good closing sentence are worth more points than perfect spelling. Fix the structure first — then proofread for spelling.
