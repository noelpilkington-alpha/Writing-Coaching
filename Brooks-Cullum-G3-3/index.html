DETAIL One specific thing from the article — quoted OR paraphrased. A name, a nickname, a thing he did.

EVIDENCE — QUOTED OR PARAPHRASED
You do NOT have to copy exact words from the article. You can retell the details in your own words — that's called paraphrasing, and it earns the same credit. What matters is that the detail is real and from the passage, not a general claim.

Satoshi Tajiri's childhood shaped the creation of Pokemon in a huge way. When Tajiri was young, he loved collecting bugs so much that kids called him "Dr. Bug". Your Personalized CUPS Check

U Understand the prompt: On G3.3 Question 1 the prompt asked you to "change this sentence into a question." You wrote: "Did Anna name the seal Sally." — but a question needs a question mark (?), not a period. Always check: what type of sentence did the prompt ask for?

The prompt says: "Change this sentence into a question: Anna named the seal Sally." Which answer is correct?

B Did Anna name the seal Sally?
THE APPOSITIVE COMMA RULE
An appositive is a noun phrase that renames another noun. It needs commas on BOTH sides — like a sandwich.

Right: Sally, a seal, swam to the other seals.
Wrong: Sally, a seal swam to the other seals. (missing second comma)

Think of the commas like bookends — one on each side holds the renaming phrase.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
These small errors — missing question marks, missing appositive commas, spelling — are easy to find on a proofread. But you can't find them if you submit in 14 minutes. Slow down, reread, and fix them. These are the easiest points on the whole test.
