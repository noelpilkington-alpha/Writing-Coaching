Grammar: "Would Have"

Q7 — Fixing a common verb mistake

On Q7, you had a great idea but a grammar error tripped you up:

YOU WROTE
I would shown the geode to a guide so no one steals and it satys in a safe place.

The problem is "would shown" — that's not how the verb works. You need either:

would shown → would show
would shown → would have shown
FIXED
I would have shown the geode to a guide so no one steals it and it stays in a safe place.
THE RULE
After "would", you use either:

• would + base verb → "I would show"
• would have + past participle → "I would have shown"

Never put "would" directly before a past participle like "shown," "done," or "gone" without "have" in between.

Also notice: you wrote "satys" — that should be "stays." Always re-read your answer to catch spelling errors like this.

Run-On Sentences

Splitting two ideas that got smashed together

Your paragraph has a run-on sentence — two complete ideas stuck together without proper punctuation:

YOUR RUN-ON

This is actually two ideas:

• Idea 1: Rules were the first big change.

• Idea 2: When rules were set, nobody could cheat.

FIXED (TWO SENTENCES)

You also started a sentence with "And":

FRAGMENT
And sports slowly gained much popularity thoughout the world.
FIXED
Over time, sports slowly gained much popularity throughout the world.
HOW TO CATCH RUN-ONS
Read your paragraph out loud. If you run out of breath or hear two ideas smashed together, you probably need a period. Also, avoid starting sentences with "And" in formal writing — use transitions like "Over time," "As a result," or "Because of this" instead.

Capitalization Rules

Proper nouns and sentence starts need capital letters

Your paragraph had several capitalization mistakes. Here are the rules you need to remember:

1. Always capitalize proper nouns (names of specific places, people, events):

britain → Britain

Countries, cities, and specific places are always capitalized.

2. Be consistent with historical terms:

industrial Revolution (inconsistent) → Industrial Revolution

If it's the name of a specific historical event, both words get capitalized.

3. Always capitalize the first word of a sentence:

then sports became... → Then sports became...

Every sentence must start with a capital letter — no exceptions.

CAPITALIZATION CHECKLIST
Before you hand in your writing, scan for these three things:

• Names of places — countries, cities, continents (Britain, Greece, America)
• Historical events — capitalize all important words (Industrial Revolution, World War II)
• Sentence starts — every sentence begins with a capital letter
