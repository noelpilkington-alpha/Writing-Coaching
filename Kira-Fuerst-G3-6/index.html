Error #1: Keep the Same Meaning

Step 5 catch: your converted sentence changed the meaning

Kira, Q1 asked you to turn a fragment into a complete sentence. You wrote a complete sentence, but it slightly changed the original meaning. That cost you 1 point.

THE RULE
When you convert a fragment into a sentence, the meaning must stay the same. Add words to complete the thought, but do not change what the original fragment was saying.

Read both versions back to back:
• Original fragment: [what it said]
• Your sentence: [what you wrote]
• Same meaning? If not, adjust.
Fragment: "The porcupine's sharp quills protecting it from predators." Which conversion keeps the same meaning?
A"The porcupine's sharp quills hurt other animals that come close."
B"The porcupine's sharp quills protect it from predators."

Error #2: Word Order in Unscramble

Step 5 catch: "Its tail swishes the porcupine" has reversed subject/object

Kira, on Q5 you unscrambled the words into a sentence, but the word order put the wrong thing as the subject.

Q5 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2 weighted points)
"Its tail swishes the porcupine."

The problem: This says the tail swishes the porcupine (the tail is doing something to the porcupine). The correct meaning is: the porcupine swishes its tail (the porcupine is the one acting).

CORRECT WORD ORDER
"The porcupine swishes its tail."
THE UNSCRAMBLE CHECK
After you unscramble, ask:
• Who or what is doing the action? That is the subject. It goes first.
• What is the action? That is the verb. It goes after the subject.
• What is being acted on? That is the object. It goes last.

"The porcupine [subject] swishes [verb] its tail [object]."
Which unscrambled sentence has the correct word order?
A"Sharp quills protect from the porcupine's body."
B"The porcupine's body is protected by sharp quills."

Your Proofread Focus: Check Meaning

Read your answer back and compare to the original

Kira, your errors on Q1 and Q5 were not about missing knowledge. They were about not reading your answer back to check if it still means the right thing. Your proofread focus should be:

THE MEANING CHECK (STEP 5)
After writing your answer on sentence-structure questions:

1. Read the original (the fragment, the scrambled words, the prompt).
2. Read your answer.
3. Ask: "Does my answer mean the same thing?"
4. Ask: "Is the right thing doing the right action?"

If the meaning shifted or the subject/object flipped, fix it before moving on.

This takes 10 seconds per question. On Q1, it would have caught the meaning change. On Q5, it would have caught "Its tail swishes the porcupine" (which says the tail is acting on the porcupine, not the other way around).
