When expanding a kernel, ask: WHEN? WHERE? HOW?

Q9 Fix — "While" vs. "Although"

Both show contrast, but they work differently

Both "while" and "although" connect contrasting ideas, but they signal different relationships:

Word What It Does When to Use It
While Shows two ideas existing at the same time When presenting two equal sides of a topic or debate
Although Concedes one point to support the other When one idea is more important — "Yes, this is true, BUT..."

The question presented two equal opinions about language learning. Neither opinion was being dismissed — both were valid viewpoints existing side by side.

YOUR ANSWER
You chose "Although" — which implies one viewpoint is being conceded (given up) to support the other.
Tap to see the correct answer
Are both viewpoints equal, or is one being dismissed?
CORRECT ANSWER
"While" is the right choice because it shows two viewpoints coexisting equally — neither is being dismissed or conceded.

Example with "While": "While some experts believe early immersion is best, others think children benefit more from structured lessons." (Both views are presented equally.)

Example with "Although": "Although early immersion has some drawbacks, the benefits clearly outweigh them." (One side is conceded to support the other.)

QUICK TEST
Ask: Are both sides equal, or am I giving up one to support the other?
• Both sides equal → While
• One side wins, one side concedes → Although

The Body Paragraph Pattern

How to build a strong body paragraph every time

Every body paragraph in a G6+ essay should follow this pattern:

Body Paragraph Formula
TS Topic Sentence — states the paragraph's main point
E1 Evidence 1 — a specific fact, quote, or example from the passage
X1 Explanation 1 — explain why that evidence matters
E2 Evidence 2 — a second fact or example from the passage
X2 Explanation 2 — explain why that evidence matters
CS Concluding Sentence — wraps up the paragraph's main idea

Here's an example BP1 about immersion classes using your ideas:

Review the formula above first
EXAMPLE: BP ABOUT IMMERSION CLASSES
[TS] One of the most effective ways to learn a new language is through immersion classes.
[E1] In immersion classes, the new language is used throughout the entire lesson.
[X1] This constant exposure helps children pick up new sounds and words naturally.
[E2] Students must use the language for every activity, not just during practice time.
[X2] This builds real fluency because students learn to think in the new language.
[CS] Immersion classes work because they surround students with the language all day long.
THE KEY RULE
Every E (evidence) must be followed by an X (explanation). Evidence without explanation is just a list of facts. The explanation is where you show the reader why that evidence supports your point. Ask yourself: "So what? Why does this matter?"

WHY "LETS THEM HAVE" (NO "TO")
"Let" is a special verb. After "let," you use the bare verb (no "to"):
• "She lets them have a break." (correct)
• "She lets them to have a break." (incorrect)

Compare with "allow," which does use "to": "She allows them to have a break."
3-MINUTE PROOFREADING RULE
Save the last 3 minutes of your test for proofreading. Read your essay backward (last sentence first) — when you read forward, your brain auto-corrects errors. Reading backward forces you to see each sentence on its own.
