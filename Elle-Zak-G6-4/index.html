Question 11 — Spelling & Grammar

Errors that cost you points
Which sentence is grammatically correct?
A "Studies have shown that young children can communicate with people from other countries."
B "Studies have showed that young children can communicate to people from other countries."

Elle, your writing is mostly clean, but there are a few grammar errors to fix. Here is every one:

GRAMMAR ERRORS

1. "Studies have showed that young children" (Body 1)

2. "they can communicate to people from other countries" (Body 1)

3. "students that know more than one language" (Body 2)

4. "are hands on activities" (Body 3)

CORRECTED

1. "Studies have shown that young children" (showed is simple past; with "have" you need the past participle shown)

2. "they can communicate with people from other countries" (you communicate with someone, not to them)

3. "students who know more than one language" (use who for people, that for things)

4. "are hands-on activities" (when two words act as one describing word before a noun, connect them with a hyphen)

STYLE NOTE

"strengthen memory/improve problem-solving skills" (Introduction)

Using a slash (/) is informal. In an essay, write it out: "strengthen memory and improve problem-solving skills."

PROOFREADING TIP
Before you submit, read your essay out loud. Three things to check:
1. Does each sentence sound right when you say it? ("communicate to" sounds off; "communicate with" sounds natural)
2. When you use "have" + a verb, is it the right form? (have showed → have shown, have went → have gone)
3. Are compound describing words hyphenated? (hands-on, well-known, long-term)

Question 11 — Strengthen Your Evidence

How to turn good evidence into great evidence
Which paragraph uses evidence from the passage effectively?
A "Students can also have fun while learning a new language. Many classrooms continue their daily activities and just use a different language. Also, many activities for learning a new language are hands on activities."
B "Students can also have fun while learning a new language. The passage explains that many classrooms turn everyday activities like songs, games, and group work into language lessons. This means students do not even realize they are learning because the activities feel like play."

You used ideas from the passage — that is good. But your evidence is vague, and Body 2 and Body 3 need more development and explanation.

VAGUE EVIDENCE

Your essay refers to "studies" and "many classrooms" but never gives specific details from the passage. You can use a direct quote (the exact words in quotation marks) or paraphrase (restate the idea in your own words) — but either way, the details need to be specific and you need to explain what they show.

Without specific evidence and explanation, grading feedback cannot tell whether you used the passage — it looks like you are writing from general knowledge.

THIN BODY PARAGRAPHS

Body 2 (memory and problem-solving) has only 2 sentences. It makes a claim but doesn't explain how or why knowing multiple languages helps memory. What does the passage say specifically?

Body 3 (fun while learning) says "many classrooms" and "many activities" without giving a single specific example. What kind of activities? What does the passage describe?

BODY 2 REWRITTEN WITH SPECIFIC EVIDENCE + EXPLANATION

"Learning a new language can also help with memory and improve problem-solving skills. According to the passage, students who speak more than one language often score higher on tests that measure memory and critical thinking. This makes sense because when you learn a new language, your brain has to constantly switch between two sets of words and rules, which strengthens your ability to focus and solve problems."

THE EVIDENCE SANDWICH
Every piece of evidence should follow this pattern:

1. Set it up: Write your topic sentence that introduces the point.
2. Give evidence: Use specific details from the passage. You can quote the exact words (in quotation marks) or paraphrase (restate the idea in your own words). Either way, introduce it with "According to the passage," "The article explains," or "The author describes."
3. Explain it: Tell the reader why this evidence matters. Don't just restate your thesis — explain what makes this specific example meaningful.
SO WHAT?
Your essay structure is already solid — five paragraphs, clear thesis, good transitions. The difference between 15/20 and 20/20 is: (1) fixing the 4 grammar errors, (2) using specific evidence from the passage (quoted or paraphrased), and (3) developing Body 2 and Body 3 with deeper explanations. All three are within reach.
