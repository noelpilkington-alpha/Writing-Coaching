Connectors and Capitalization

Q2 and Q5 — two quick fixes worth 2 points
Q2 (1/2) — CAPITALIZATION + PASSAGE DETAIL
You wrote "Freed" with a capital "F" in the middle of a sentence. Only capitalize the first word of a sentence or a proper noun (a name). Also, the detail you included did not match what the passage actually said.
CAPITALIZATION RULE
Only capitalize a word if it is:
• The first word of a sentence
• A proper noun (a specific name: "Sally," "Texas," "Monday")

"Freed" is not a name and not the start of a sentence, so it should be lowercase: "freed."
Q5 (1/2) — WRONG CONNECTOR
You used "But" where the question needed "Since." The second idea gives a reason for the first, so you need "Since" or "Because" (not "But").
CONNECTOR RULE
• "Because" / "Since" = the second idea explains why.
• "But" / "However" = the second idea contrasts with the first.
• "So" / "Therefore" = the second idea is a result.

Ask: "Does the second part explain why?" If yes, use "since" or "because."
Which connector fits? "_____ the weather was bad, the game was canceled."
ABut the weather was bad, the game was canceled.
BSince the weather was bad, the game was canceled.
QUICK CHECK
Before picking a connector, ask: "Does the second part explain WHY?" If yes, use "since" or "because." If it shows the opposite, use "but."

Appositives: Q3

A renaming phrase, not just repeating the noun

Finley, Q3 asked you to combine two sentences using an appositive. You wrote something like "Sally the seal" — just putting the noun next to the name without commas or a full renaming phrase.

Q3 (1/2) — NOT AN APPOSITIVE
"Sally the seal" is not an appositive. An appositive needs to be a full phrase set off by commas that could replace the noun.
CORRECT APPOSITIVE
"Sally, a young seal who lives near the shore, swam through the cold water."

The phrase "a young seal who lives near the shore" renames Sally. It is between commas. You could swap "Sally" for the whole phrase and the sentence still works.
APPOSITIVE CHECKLIST
1. Is it between commas? (Required)
2. Does it rename the noun before it? (Required)
3. Could I replace the noun with this phrase and the sentence still makes sense? (Test it)

If all three are yes, you have a correct appositive.

SHORT VS DEVELOPED
Short (12/20 level): "Dogs are good pets. They are loyal. They are fun."

Developed (16+/20 level): "Dogs are good pets because they are loyal companions. For example, dogs greet their owners happily every day, which shows they care about their family. Another reason dogs make good pets is that they encourage exercise because owners need to walk them regularly. That is why dogs are one of the best animals to have as a pet."
