THE RULE: A FACT IS A SENTENCE YOU CAN PUT YOUR FINGER ON IN THE ARTICLE
The paragraph is scored on the article’s facts. A true thing you already know about sports earns nothing, because the grader cannot find it on the page. So: read the article twice, put your finger on two sentences that answer the question, and write them as your two DETAIL · SHOW facts. After each one, DETAIL · SO WHAT: This shows plus what the fact proves. The check: for each fact in your paragraph, can you point to where the article says it? If not, it is not a fact for this test.

Fix 2: Finish the Because

Q9 says nature guided Layla and stops. Q10 names a rule and stops
THE RULE: THE REASON HAS TO SAY WHAT, OR WHY, NOT JUST THAT
The third point on a one-sentence answer is for a reason that adds something. Guided her to do the right thing names a direction; guided her to leave the geode in the park says what. Take only pictures names a rule; so the park stays the same for the next visitor says why. The check: cover the first half of your sentence. Does what is left tell the reader something new?
YOU WROTE (Q9, 2 OUT OF 3)
“When she was deciding if she was going to take it or not to take the geode, it felt like the wind and the birds where guiding her to do the right thing.” GUIDED HER TO WHAT? AND WHERE FOR WERE
The breeze and the birds are the right detail. The right thing does not say what it was: showing the geode to the guide instead of keeping it.
FINISHED
The breeze and the birds seemed to guide Layla to show the geode to the guide instead of keeping it, because they reminded her that it belonged in the park.
YOU WROTE (Q10, 2 OUT OF 3)
“Take only pictures and, dont take anything that is part of nature.” A RULE, NO WHY
The question asked why the rule is important. And the comma goes before and, not after, and don’t takes an apostrophe.
FINISHED
People should take only pictures and leave nature where it is, because every rock and flower taken is one the next visitor never sees.
