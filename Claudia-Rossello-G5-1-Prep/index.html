Watch: Subject-Verb Agreement

Your recurring error from G4.1 and G4.2 — appears on BOTH tests
🔍
This is your #1 recurring pattern. On G4.2 Q7, you wrote "One change...are the chirping sounds" when it should be "One change...is the chirping sound." The same type of error appeared on G4.1. When extra phrases sit between the subject and verb, it is easy to lose track of which word the verb must match.
G4.2 Q7 — SUBJECT-VERB MISMATCH
You wrote: "One change in nature that only happens in spring are the chirping sounds of birds returning."
The subject is "One change" (singular). The phrase "in nature that only happens in spring" is extra information between the subject and verb. The verb must match "One change" — which is singular — so it should be "is," not "are."
CORRECTED — VERB MATCHES THE SUBJECT
✓ "One change in nature that only happens in spring is the chirping sound of birds returning."

"One change" = singular subject. "Is" = singular verb. They match. The extra phrase ("in nature that only happens in spring") does not change the subject.
THE "SKIP THE MIDDLE" TRICK
When a sentence has extra phrases between the subject and verb, mentally mentally skip the middle:

"One change in nature that only happens in spring _____ the chirping sound."

Now ask: "One change _____ the chirping sound." IS or ARE?
One change is. Done.

This works every time. Find the real subject, mentally skip the extra phrases, and match the verb to the subject.

YOUR PROOFREAD STEP FOR THIS
On G5.1, after writing any sentence with a long subject phrase, do the cross-out test: find the subject, ignore the extra phrases, and check if the verb matches. This takes 5 seconds and prevents the exact error that has appeared on your last two tests.

Your Paragraph Strategy for G5.1

The rubric expects deeper D+E at G5 — here is exactly how to deliver it

Claudia, your G4.2 paragraph scored 17/20. That is solid, but at G5.1 the rubric expects more development — specifically, an explanation sentence after each supporting detail. Here is how to build that into your paragraph using the Single Paragraph Outline in the AlphaTest scratchpad.

Single Paragraph Outline — T.S. / S.D. / S.D. / C.S.
T.S. Answer the question in one clear sentence. This is your claim.
S.D. 1 Detail from the passage + explanation: "According to the article, [fact]. This shows that [meaning]."
S.D. 2 Second detail from the passage + explanation: "The author also states [fact]. This matters because [reason]."
C.S. Closing sentence: restate the claim in new words or explain the bigger meaning.
🔍
The D+E pattern at G5: Detail alone = "According to the article, birds migrate in spring." Detail + Explanation = "According to the article, birds migrate in spring. This shows that even animals respond to seasonal changes, which is why the author calls spring a time of renewal." The bold part is the explanation layer the G5 rubric expects.
D+E STARTERS YOU CAN USE
After naming a detail from the passage, use one of these to launch your explanation:

"This shows that..."
"This matters because..."
"This is important because..."
"In other words,..."

Pick one. Write one sentence. That is your explanation. Each supporting detail in your outline should have this second sentence built in.

MODEL G5 PARAGRAPH (full-credit level)

[T.S.] The author believes that community gardens benefit neighborhoods in multiple ways. [S.D. 1] According to the article, community gardens provide fresh vegetables to families who might not otherwise have access to healthy food. This matters because it means the garden is solving a real problem — not just growing plants, but improving people's diets and health. [S.D. 2] The author also explains that neighbors who work in the garden together form stronger friendships. This shows that the garden's value goes beyond food — it builds connections between people who might never have spoken otherwise. [C.S.] Community gardens benefit neighborhoods by meeting both physical and social needs at the same time.

Clear T.S. Two details, each with an explanation sentence (in bold). Closing sentence wraps back to the claim. This is the depth G5 expects.

SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Your G4.2 paragraph already had good structure. The only difference at G5 is that each supporting detail needs a "This shows..." or "This matters because..." sentence after it. Add that one layer and your paragraph score moves from 17/20 toward full credit.
