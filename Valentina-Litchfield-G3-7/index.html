Your #2 Error: Combining Sentences & Appositives

On Q2–Q4, you often leave out a piece of the original sentence

Valentina, this is a smaller pattern but it shows up on almost every test. When a question asks you to combine two sentences or add an appositive, you sometimes drop part of the meaning instead of keeping everything the prompt gave you.

FROM G3.6 Q3 (appositive — scored 0.5/2)
Prompt: Combine using an appositive. 17. Porcupines are rodents. 18. They are the slow movers of the forest.
You wrote: "Porcupines are slow moving rodents."
You combined the idea, but without an appositive. The appositive version keeps "of the forest" as extra info set off by commas.
FROM G3.7 Q2 (combine — scored 0.75/2)
Prompt: Combine sentences 2 and 3 into one clear sentence.
You wrote: "Stoes fill big boxes with bright orange pumpkins so people can decorate their homes with them."
Good combining! But “stoes” should be “stores” — spelling slip on the first word.
FROM G3.7 Q3 (add detail — scored 0.5/2)
Prompt: Expand sentence 5 with another detail from the passage. 5. A pumpkin seed is flat and white.
You wrote: "A pumpkin seed is small, white, and flat."
You rearranged the same 3 words the prompt already had. The question wanted a NEW detail from the passage — like "pointed at one end" or "about the size of a fingernail."
THE RULE FOR "COMBINE" AND "APPOSITIVE" QUESTIONS
Step 1: Read BOTH original sentences fully. Underline every important word.
Step 2: Your answer must include every important word from both sentences.
Step 3: For an appositive, use commas to set off the extra info. Example: Porcupines, the slow movers of the forest, are rodents.
Step 4: For "add a detail," the new detail must come from the passage — not from rearranging the words already in the prompt.
From G3.6 Q3 — which version uses an appositive correctly?
A Porcupines are slow moving rodents of the forest.
B Porcupines, the slow movers of the forest, are rodents.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Q2, Q3, and Q4 are worth 2 points each. Losing half a point on two of them costs you 1 full point per test. That is another piece of the jump from 88% to 90%+.
