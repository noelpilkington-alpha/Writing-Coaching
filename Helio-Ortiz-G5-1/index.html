#1 Focus: Break the Comma Chain

Q6, Q10, and your paragraph — one habit, big payoff

Helio, here is the one habit costing you the most points: chaining three, four, even five complete thoughts together with commas. A comma cannot hold two complete thoughts together. See it in your own answers from this test:

▶ Tap to see the chain from G5.1 Q6
From G5.1 Q6 — 2 out of 3
"When I was in one of my old schools, I broke a marker, I felt bad because my teacher bought the entire class new school supplies, I felt guilty and I went to tell my teacher my mistake."
One clear sentence
"When I was at my old school, I broke a marker and felt so guilty that I went to admit my mistake."
▶ Tap to see the chain from G5.1 Q10
From G5.1 Q10 — 2 out of 3
"I would try to fix my mistake, if I make it worse then it was already, then I would go tell the adult, I would apoligize, say it was my mistake, and promise that I would never do it again."
Broken into clear sentences
"I would try to fix my mistake first. If I could not, I would tell the adult, apologize, and promise to never do it again."
THE RULE
A comma cannot hold two complete thoughts together. When a thought is complete, end it with a period and start a new sentence — or join it to ONE more thought with a connector (and, but, so, because). At most one joined pair per sentence. Never a chain of commas.
Which version is punctuated correctly?
AI broke a marker at my old school. I felt so guilty that I went to admit my mistake.
BI broke a marker at my old school, I felt guilty, I went to admit my mistake.
YOUR MOVE
When you finish a thought, stop. Period. New sentence. If two thoughts truly belong together, join that one pair with and, but, so, or because — then stop again. Both Q6 and Q10 were 2-out-of-3 answers that this one habit can turn into 3s.

THE SHAPE OF A FULL-CREDIT ANSWER
Answer the question, then attach your best reason with because, so, or for example. One sentence, one clear pairing, done. A longer chain does not earn more points — a clearer sentence does.

REMEMBER
One clear sentence with a reason beats a chain every time. Do not pile on commas to say more — pick your best reason and attach it with because, so, or for example.
