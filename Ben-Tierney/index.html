Q1: Keep the Same Meaning

Skill: Rewrite a statement as a question

The question: Rewrite this sentence as a question: "The books were sold in department stores."

YOU WROTE
Why did the sell the books in department stores?

Two things went wrong here:

  • You changed it into a "why" question, which changes what the sentence is about. The original sentence is just saying where the books were sold — not why.
  • Small typo: the sell → they sell
CORRECT VERSION
Were the books sold in department stores?
TIP
When you rewrite a sentence as a question, keep the same idea — just flip the word order. Don't add new words like "why" or "how." The question should have the same meaning as the original sentence.

Q4: What Is an Appositive?

Skill: Add an appositive phrase to a sentence

The question: Add an appositive to: "The books are still popular."

YOU WROTE
The books are still popular, the best selling book being The Poky Little Puppy, they are mainly known for the golden spines.

Your sentence was too long and added extra information that wasn't asked for. An appositive is a short extra detail placed inside commas that tells more about a noun.

The pattern: noun , extra detail , rest of sentence.

CORRECT VERSION
The books, known for their golden spines, are still popular.
TIP
Keep your appositive short — just a few words between the commas. It should give one extra detail about the noun right before it. Don't try to add too many ideas into one sentence.

The Paragraph Plan (SPO)

Use this plan every time you write a paragraph on the test

Here is a model paragraph for Q11 using the SPO plan. Notice how each part has a job:

MODEL PARAGRAPH
[Topic Sentence] Jason chose to place his sculptures under the sea to protect coral reefs and create a new kind of museum experience. [SD1 + why it matters] Each year, many swimmers visit the coral reefs and accidentally hurt the coral and animals. Jason's statues give people a different place to explore, which keeps the reefs safe. [SD2 + why it matters] An underwater museum is also very unusual. People can snorkel, scuba dive, or ride a glass-bottom boat to see the art, which makes it an exciting experience that attracts many visitors. [Closing Sentence] By placing his art underwater, Jason found a creative way to protect nature and share something amazing with the world.
THE SPO PATTERN
Topic Sentence: Answer the question
Detail 1: First reason + why it matters
Detail 2: Second reason + why it matters
Closing Sentence: Restate your main idea in new words

This pattern works for every paragraph question on the test!
