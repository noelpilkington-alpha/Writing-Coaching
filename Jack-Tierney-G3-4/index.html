Fix: Question Sentences & Spelling

Q3 — Interrogative form + spelling fixes

Q3 asked you to change a statement into a question (interrogative sentence). Your version was close but had two issues:

YOU WROTE
Did the geode not feel like the perfect souvenir.

Two problems: the awkward "not" changes the meaning, and you used a period instead of a question mark. When changing a statement to a question, just flip the word order — don't add negation.

Tap to see the improved version
Read the explanation above first
BETTER
Did the geode feel like a perfect souvenir?
HOW TO FORM A QUESTION
Step 1: Move the helping verb (did, was, is, can) to the front.
Step 2: Keep the meaning the same — don't add "not."
Step 3: Always end with a question mark (?).

Statement: The geode felt like a perfect souvenir.
Question: Did the geode feel like a perfect souvenir?
More examples
Statement: Layla saw a butterfly.
Question: Did Layla see a butterfly?

Statement: The crystals were sparkling.
Question: Were the crystals sparkling?

You also had a couple of spelling slips across the test:

"braking" → breaking
"there free time" → their free time
QUICK SPELLING CHECK
"braking" means pressing the brakes on a car. "Breaking" means not following a rule.
"There" is a place. "Their" means belonging to them. Try replacing it with "belonging to them" — if it makes sense, use their.

Build Your Paragraph

Q11 — Good start, but you need more development

Your paragraph scored 14/20. You had ideas about how modern sports developed, but you only wrote 3 sentences. A G3 paragraph needs more details and explanations.

YOUR PARAGRAPH
Modern sports grew in the 1700s, because factory work was boring and repeated, but sports gave them something to do in there free time. over time schools started to give students fields and training. All in all, this made sports popular all over the world.

Good things: you answered the prompt and tried to use evidence. But there are issues:

  • "there" should be "their"
  • "over" starts a new sentence but isn't capitalized
  • Only 3 sentences — you need more specific evidence and explanations
  • The conclusion ("this made sports popular") is too brief — restate your main point

Here's the G3 paragraph plan you should follow:

Paragraph Plan — TS → D1 → W → D2 → W → D3 → W → CS
TS = Topic Sentence  •  D = Detail from the article  •  W = Why it matters  •  CS = Concluding Sentence
TS Modern sports became popular because people in the 1700s needed fun activities outside of work.
D1 Factory workers had long, boring days doing the same tasks over and over.
W This shows that people needed something exciting in their free time, which is why sports grew so quickly.
D2 Over time, schools started giving students fields and training for organized sports.
W This shows that sports became so important that even schools made them part of students' lives.
D3 Rules were created so that everyone could play the same way, no matter where they lived.
W This shows that having official rules helped sports spread to new places and become popular worldwide.
CS In conclusion, modern sports grew because bored workers, supportive schools, and official rules all helped turn simple games into a worldwide tradition.
THE KEY: ADD "WHY IT MATTERS" AFTER EACH DETAIL
You already know how to find details in the article. The next step is to explain why each detail matters to the main point. After each detail, ask yourself: "So what? Why does this show that sports became popular?" That answer is your "W" sentence. Also, always end with a concluding sentence that wraps up your main point.
