Q1: Keep the Same Meaning

Skill: Change a sentence into a question

The question: Change this sentence into a question: "Anna named the seal Sally."

YOU WROTE
Why did Anna name the seal sally.

Two things to fix:

1. You turned it into a "why" question — but the original sentence just says Anna named the seal Sally. It doesn't ask why. When you change the type, keep the same meaning.

2. "Sally" is a name, so it needs a capital S. And questions end with a question mark (?), not a period.

CORRECT VERSION
Did Anna name the seal Sally?
THE RULE
When you change a sentence to a question, don't change what it's about. Just flip the words around:

Anna named the seal Sally. → Did Anna name the seal Sally?

Check: Does my question ask the same thing the sentence said? If yes, you're good!

Q11: Add More Details from the Article

Use the paragraph plan to make it longer and stronger

You mentioned catching bugs — great! But the article has more details you could use. Here's a stronger version using the paragraph plan:

STRONGER PARAGRAPH

Topic Sentence: Satoshi Tajiri's childhood love of collecting bugs inspired him to create Pokémon.

Detail 1: The article says that as a boy, Tajiri loved exploring nature and spent hours catching insects near his home. This gave him the idea for a game where players could collect and trade creatures.

Detail 2: The article also explains that Tajiri turned his childhood hobby into a video game, and it became popular around the world. This shows that his experiences as a kid directly shaped what Pokémon became.

Closing Sentence: Because of his childhood love of bugs, millions of people now enjoy Pokémon.

THE PARAGRAPH PLAN
Topic Sentence: Answer the question
Detail 1: One fact from the article + why it matters
Detail 2: Another fact from the article + why it matters
Closing Sentence: Wrap up your main idea

That's at least 5 sentences. Use this plan every time!
