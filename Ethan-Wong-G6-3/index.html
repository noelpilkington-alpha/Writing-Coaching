The #1 Unlock: Break Your Essay Into Paragraphs

Your essay was one block — and it already contains every paragraph you need

Here is the single biggest thing on this whole page. Your G6.3 essay was written as one big block of text. The rubric asks for a five-paragraph shape: an intro with a thesis, body paragraphs, and a conclusion. Here is that target shape:

The G6 Expository Essay Shape
INTRO Set up the topic + a thesis (the main claim: people help monarchs in several ways)
BODY 1 First way people help + evidence + "this shows..."
BODY 2 Second way people help + evidence + "this shows..."
BODY 3 A third way (room to grow — even one more reason strengthens the essay)
CLOSE Restate the main idea + a final thought

Now the exciting part: you already wrote almost all of these paragraphs — they were just crammed into one block. Here is your own essay, split apart into where each piece belongs:

Your G6.3 Essay, Split Into 5 Paragraphs
INTRO Monarchs are beautiful but endangered — so people are helping them survive in several ways (your thesis).
BODY 1 Why they are endangered: land is cleared for farms and houses, destroying their food — this shows why they need help.
BODY 2 Citizen scientists tag butterflies, count caterpillars, and record sightings — this helps scientists track where monarchs are dying.
BODY 3 Many people plant milkweed, the monarchs' favorite food — this gives them a food source while migrating.
CLOSE In conclusion, there are many ways to help — planting milkweed and keeping track of the butterflies.

See it? That is a complete intro, three body paragraphs (why they are endangered, citizen scientists, and planting milkweed), and a conclusion — and you already wrote almost every word of it. You had all these ideas in one block; splitting the "why they are endangered" part into its own paragraph turns your two reasons into a full five-paragraph essay.

YOUR SAME ESSAY — SPLIT INTO 5 PARAGRAPHS

[Intro + thesis] Monarch butterflies are some of the most beautiful and fascinating creatures, evolving from little green caterpillars to two-winged flying creatures. However, every year there are fewer and fewer monarch butterflies, making them endangered. Because of this, people across North America are helping monarch butterflies survive in several different ways.

[Body 1 — Why they are endangered] One reason monarchs are in trouble is habitat loss. People clearing land for farms and houses has destroyed the monarchs' main source of food. This shows why the butterflies need help — without action, they lose the plants they depend on to survive.

[Body 2 — Citizen scientists] One way people help is by becoming citizen scientists. Thousands of people tag butterflies with small stickers, count caterpillars, and record monarch sightings online. This helps scientists track how many butterflies there are and what places in the world they are dying in.

[Body 3 — Planting milkweed] Another way people help is by planting milkweed plants, which are the butterflies' favorite food. This gives monarchs a source of food while they migrate, so more of them can complete their long journey.

[Conclusion] In conclusion, there are many ways of helping butterflies, from planting milkweed plants to keeping track of how many caterpillars and butterflies there are. Together, these efforts give monarchs a real chance to survive.

What changed: Nothing about your ideas. The same content is organized into an intro, three body paragraphs (why they are endangered, citizen scientists, and planting milkweed), and a conclusion, and the spelling is fixed (beautiful, fascinating, stickers). That is the difference between one block and a full 5-paragraph essay.
