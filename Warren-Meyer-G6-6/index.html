#1 Focus: Passage Evidence Beats Invented Scenes

The 5-point evidence bucket — where your 3 points live

The prompt asked you to explain three ways therapy dogs help students — and to support each way with evidence from the passage. Here is your third paragraph, exactly as you wrote it:

Your Paragraph 3:
"Next, dogs can make reading fun. First, if you are reading a boring book, a dog might give a fun one to you. Also,if you are getting tired of reading, the dog might play with you. Last, the dog might make you laugh if you are reading a sad book. In summary, dogs can make reading a book with them fun."

Every example in this paragraph is a scene you invented — notice how each one says "might." They are charming, but the passage never says any of them. On an evidence-from-the-passage prompt, an example the passage does not contain earns nothing in the 5-point evidence bucket — no matter how good it sounds.

Here is the frustrating part: the passage handed you real evidence you never used.

What the passage actually offered
FACT Students who read to therapy dogs often improve their reading skills
FACT Attendance improved on days the therapy dog visited
QUOTE Dr. Sarah Mitchell, a school counselor, explains that dogs can help lower stress
FACT Students pay better attention, behave more calmly, and feel more connected to school

Your Paragraph 2 (dogs do not judge) got this right — the passage really does say dogs do not judge or correct mistakes, which helps nervous readers feel comfortable. That paragraph is passage-aligned. The other two ran on invented scenes.

THE TRACE TEST
Before an example goes into your essay, ask: "Can I point to the sentence in the passage that says this?"
• If YES — use it, then add one sentence explaining what it shows.
• If NO — it does not go in, no matter how fun it is.
Which sentence would earn evidence points on this prompt?
A "The passage says students who read to therapy dogs often improve their reading skills, which shows the dogs are not just fun to be around — they help build real readers."
B "If you are getting tired of reading, the dog might play with you until you feel like reading again."
YOUR ACTION STEP
On your next essay, pick your three ways from the passage first, and pull one traceable fact for each before you draft. Fact from the passage + one sentence of what it shows, in every body paragraph — that is the full 5-point evidence bucket.

#2 Focus: Make the Ending Earn Its Points

One summary per essay — and it reinforces, never invents

Warren, count the summaries in your essay. Every single paragraph ends with its own "In summary…" — four mini-summaries before the real conclusion even starts. Then the actual conclusion is one sentence:

YOUR CONCLUSION (PARAGRAPH 5)
"In conclusion, dogs are cute, frendly compainions who are now being used in schools."

Two problems, two fixes:

FIX 1 — ONLY THE CONCLUSION SUMMARIZES
A body paragraph does not need its own summary. It makes its point, then flows into the next paragraph — your transitions ("Next…", "Last…") already do the connecting. Save the summarizing for the one place it belongs: the essay's conclusion.
FIX 2 — A CONCLUSION NEVER INTRODUCES NEW IDEAS
"Cute" and "companions" are brand-new ideas — no body paragraph argued either one. Meanwhile your three real ways (dogs do not judge, dogs make reading fun, dogs keep listening) never get mentioned. A conclusion's job is to restate your main idea and your three ways in fresh words — it reinforces what the essay proved, and nothing else.

A REINFORCING CONCLUSION
"In conclusion, therapy dogs belong in schools for the three reasons this essay showed: they do not judge readers who make mistakes, they make reading time something students enjoy, and they keep listening from the first page to the last. That is why more and more schools are opening their doors to them."
Which conclusion earns its points?
A "In conclusion, dogs are amazing animals. Someday they might even learn to fetch library books, and every school should also get a cat."
B "In conclusion, dogs help students in three ways: they do not judge mistakes, they make reading fun, and they listen all the way through."
THE ENDING RULE
One summary per essay — the conclusion. And the conclusion restates your main idea plus your three ways in fresh words. Quick check: if a sentence in your conclusion cannot point back to one of your body paragraphs, cut it.
