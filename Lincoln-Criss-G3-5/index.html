Q2 Fix — Unscramble Sentences

Can you spot the problem? Then we will break it down.
📈 This is the first time you have had an Unscramble Sentences question, so there is no pattern to worry about — just a small slip to learn from.

You were given these words to unscramble into a sentence:

puppy / little / poky / the / popular / very / was

Which sentence is put together correctly?
A "The Poky Little Puppy was was very popular."
B "The Poky Little Puppy was very popular."

Here is what happened:

WHAT YOU WROTE
"The Poky Little Puppy was was very popular."
CORRECT VERSION
"The Poky Little Puppy was very popular."

You had all the right words in the right order — the only mistake was using "was" twice instead of once. The word list only had one "was," so each word should appear exactly one time.

HOW TO CATCH THIS
After you unscramble a sentence, read it out loud (or mouth the words quietly). When you hear "was was," it sounds wrong immediately. A quick re-read catches this kind of slip every time.
Why reading out loud helps
Your eyes can skip over repeated words when reading silently — your brain fills in what it expects to see. But when you say the words out loud, your ears catch the extra word right away. This works for repeated words, missing words, and awkward phrasing.
"SO WHAT?" — WHY THIS MATTERS
Repeated words make sentences confusing to read. Even when you know all the right words, a small slip like this can cost you points. The fix is simple: read your answer one more time before moving on.

Q11 Fix — Add More Detail to Your Paragraph

Your ideas are good — they just need more support.
📈 Your paragraph scores across 5 tests: 85%, 85%, 85%, 70%, 80%. You dipped on G3.4 but came back up. The pattern each time: good ideas, but not enough detail to fully support them.

The prompt asked you to explain why Jason deCaires Taylor chose to place his sculptures under the sea. Here is what you wrote:

"Have you ever seen the artist Jason deCairs Taylor life sized statues? The thing that makes them the most amazing is that they are underwater, and have sea life surviving on them! There are two of them, and both are in the Caribbean sea, to protect the coral reefs and animals living there. All in all, Jason deCairs Taylor is a pretty cool guy, and a friend of wildlife."

Which version of a body sentence gives the reader more useful information?

Tap the version that better explains WHY the sculptures are underwater:
A "The thing that makes them the most amazing is that they are underwater, and have sea life surviving on them!"
B "Jason placed his sculptures underwater because too many tourists were damaging the natural coral reefs, and he wanted to give them something else to explore."

Your paragraph has good pieces, but it needs more detail from the passage to fully answer the prompt. Here is what was strong and what was missing:

STRONGER PARAGRAPH EXAMPLE
[Topic Sentence] Jason deCaires Taylor chose to place his sculptures under the sea to help protect coral reefs.
[Detail + Why] Too many tourists were visiting the natural reefs, which was damaging them. By creating underwater museums, Jason gave tourists something new to explore so the real reefs could recover.
[More Detail] The sculptures are made of special material that sea life can grow on, turning them into new artificial reefs over time.
[Closing] Jason's underwater museums show that art can help protect nature, not just look beautiful.
THE "WHY + HOW" RULE
When a prompt says "explain why," your paragraph needs to answer two questions:
• WHY did it happen? (the reason)
• HOW does it work? (the details)

Your paragraph told us what the sculptures are, but the prompt wanted why they were put underwater. Always go back to the prompt and check: did I actually answer the question?
See the difference between "what" and "why"
What: "The sculptures are underwater and have sea life on them." (describes the sculptures)
Why: "Jason put them underwater because tourists were damaging the natural reefs, and he wanted to create new places for sea life to grow." (answers the question)
"SO WHAT?" — WHY THIS MATTERS
You already know how to write a paragraph with a beginning, middle, and end — your structure is solid. The next step is making sure you pull specific details from the passage and connect them to the prompt. That is what turns an 80% paragraph into a 90%+ paragraph.
