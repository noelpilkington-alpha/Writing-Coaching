Q1 Appositives — Partial 3 Tests in a Row

Your only consistent rubric gap. Here is exactly how to close it.

Kaiden, Q1 on every test asks you to add an appositive. You have scored Partial (0.5) on all three. Here is what you wrote each time:

FROM G5.1 Q1 (Partial 0.5 — no wait, scored 1.0)
"Today, Ms. Kessler, a brown eyed, brown haired teacher that works at my school, wasn't at her desk."
This one earned full credit. Good appositive — it adds specific details about Ms. Kessler.
FROM G5.2 Q1 (Partial 0.5)
"Grandma Addie, my grandma that bakes pies with me, has came second place in the fair 3 years in a row."
Problem: "my grandma" is circular — you're calling Grandma Addie "my grandma." An appositive should add NEW info, not restate the relationship.
FROM G5.3 Q1 (Partial 0.5) — your most recent
"Every summer, the Thompsons, Kim's family, camped at Willow State Park."
Problem: "Kim's family" is circular again — the Thompsons ARE Kim's family. The appositive doesn't add new detail about them.
THE APPOSITIVE RULE
An appositive is a noun phrase right next to another noun that gives new information about it. The test is simple: if you deleted the appositive, would the reader lose something interesting?

✗ "The Thompsons, Kim's family, camped..." (circular — you already know they're Kim's family)
✓ "The Thompsons, a family who had camped together every summer for a decade, camped..." (new info about them)
Which appositive would earn full credit on G5.3 Q1?
A Every summer, the Thompsons, Kim's family, camped at Willow State Park.
B Every summer, the Thompsons, a family of outdoor enthusiasts who loved the quiet of the woods, camped at Willow State Park.
The Appositive Checklist
STEP 1 Find the noun you're describing (Grandma Addie, the Thompsons, Ms. Kessler)
STEP 2 Ask: what is one new thing about them? (a job, a trait, an expertise, a background)
STEP 3 Write it as a noun phrase with commas: ", a ____ who ____,"
STEP 4 Delete-test: if you remove it, does the reader lose something interesting? If yes, done.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Q1 is worth 2 points on every test. Getting Partial every time means losing about 1 point. Over a year of tests, that is 5–10 total points. Master this one move and you never lose a Q1 again.

THE "i BEFORE e" TRAP
Several of your slips follow the same pattern: believe, field, efficiency, receive. The old rule is "i before e except after c." For field and believe, that is i before e (f-ie-ld, bel-ie-ve). For efficiency and receive, they come after c, so it flips to e before i (r-ec-ei-ve).
Which sentence is fully polished?
A The auther shows VR field trips are better then regular ones because of their effeciency.
B The author shows VR field trips are better than regular ones because of their efficiency.
THE "READ BACKWARDS" TRICK
Read each paragraph one word at a time, from the last word to the first. Your brain stops auto-completing what it expects to see and actually reads each word. Misspellings jump out. Takes 60 seconds per paragraph.

One Idea Per Sentence

Comma splices still sneak in on your longer answers

Kaiden, the big overpacking problem from G5.2 is mostly gone — great work. But a few comma splices still slipped through on your longer G5.3 answers. A comma splice is when two complete sentences are joined by only a comma (they need a period OR a joining word like "and" or "because").

FROM G5.3 Q6 (Partial 0.67)
"Changing plans can still lead to fun because of an open mind and creative ideas, in the story Kim came up with a creative and open minded idea to camp in the backyard, this showed us that a change in plan does not mean you can not have fun."
Three complete thoughts joined by commas. Needs periods or "and" to separate them.
FROM G5.3 Q11 (paragraph)
"The text includes studies that show that people remember more when they experience something instead of only reading it showing it's increase in attention and learning."
The word "showing" joins two ideas awkwardly. Cleaner: end the first thought with a period and start fresh with "This shows..."
Which version fixes the comma splice correctly?
A Changing plans can still lead to fun, in the story Kim camped in the backyard, this shows that a change in plan can still be fun.
B Changing plans can still lead to fun. In the story, Kim camped in the backyard. This shows that a change in plan can still be fun.
THE COMMA SPLICE TEST
Look at each comma in your answer. Ask: is there a complete sentence on both sides of the comma?

If YES — you need either:
• A period (two sentences)
• A joining word after the comma: and, but, so, because

✗ "The pie was ready, we took it out of the oven." (splice)
✓ "The pie was ready. We took it out of the oven." (period)
✓ "The pie was ready, so we took it out of the oven." (joining word)
THE "PAUSE TEST"
Read each sentence out loud in your head. If you take a big pause in the middle and then start a whole new idea, that is where a period belongs. Your brain already knows where sentences end — let it tell you.
