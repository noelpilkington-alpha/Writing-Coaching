Step 2: Keep ALL Ideas When Combining

Q1 — 1 out of 2 weighted points

Emma, Q1 asked you to combine the ideas in two sentences: "Roadsides and hills take on a whole new look" and "Meadows do too."

Q1 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2)
"Then, roadsides and meadows begin having a new look."

The problem: The original sentences mention three places: roadsides, hills, and meadows. Your combined sentence only kept two of them. "Hills" was lost entirely.

THE FIX: COUNT BEFORE YOU COMBINE
Before writing your combined sentence, count the ideas in both original sentences.

• Sentence 1: "Roadsides and hills take on a whole new look" → 2 places
• Sentence 2: "Meadows do too" → 1 more place
• Total: 3 places (roadsides, hills, meadows)

Your combined sentence must include all 3.
A STRONGER ANSWER
"Then, roadsides, hills, and meadows all begin to take on a whole new look."

Notice how the stronger answer keeps all three places. It also keeps the original idea of "a whole new look" instead of shortening it to just "a new look."

Combine: "Roadsides and hills take on a whole new look. Meadows do too." Which answer keeps ALL ideas?
A Roadsides and meadows start to look different in the spring.
B Roadsides, hills, and meadows all take on a whole new look.
CHECKLIST FOR COMBINING
• Count the ideas in both sentences BEFORE writing
• Write your combined sentence
• Count the ideas in your answer — does the number match?
• If not, find what you dropped and add it back

Step 2: Complete vs Rewrite

Q3 — 1 out of 2 weighted points

Emma, Q3 gave you an incomplete sentence that starts with a subordinating conjunction: "Since bluebonnets grow best in sunny places." The prompt asked you to complete it.

Q3 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2)
"The best place that bluebonnets can grow is in sunny weather."

The problem: You rewrote the sentence from scratch instead of completing it. The word "Since" is gone. The original structure is gone. The prompt said "complete," which means keep the beginning and add an ending.

THE FIX: READ THE PROMPT WORD-BY-WORD
"Complete" = Keep the original beginning. Add words after it to finish the thought.

"Rewrite" = Start fresh. Say the same idea in new words.

These are different instructions. Q3 said "complete," so you keep "Since bluebonnets grow best in sunny places" and add an independent clause after it.
A STRONGER ANSWER
"Since bluebonnets grow best in sunny places, they are often found along open roadsides and in wide meadows."

Notice how the stronger answer keeps the entire original fragment ("Since bluebonnets grow best in sunny places") and then adds a comma followed by a complete thought. That is what "complete" means.

The prompt says: "Complete this sentence: 'Since bluebonnets grow best in sunny places.'" Which answer follows the instruction?
A Since bluebonnets grow best in sunny places, gardeners should plant them where there is plenty of light.
B Bluebonnets need lots of sunlight to grow well.
QUICK CHECK
After you write your answer, ask: "Does my answer still start with the original words?" If the prompt said "complete" and your answer does not include the original sentence, you accidentally rewrote instead of completing.

Detail + Explanation for Q11

Q11 — 16 out of 20 — strong hook, needs more development

Emma, your paragraph opening is strong: "When someone hears the word 'robot' they may think of science fiction books or imaginary books, but robots are more help..." That hook grabs the reader. The issue is what comes after.

Your opening: "When someone hears the word 'robot' they may think of science fiction books or imaginary books, but robots are more help..."

Two things to fix:

  • Redundancy: "Science fiction books" and "imaginary books" mean the same thing. Pick one or replace the second with a different example (like "movies").
  • Development: After your topic sentence, you need D+E pairs. Each supporting detail (D) needs an explanation (E) that says "This shows..." or "This means..."
THE D+E PATTERN
Every supporting detail needs an explanation:

Detail: A specific fact or example from the passage.
Explanation: A sentence starting with "This shows..." or "This means..." that tells the reader WHY that detail matters.

Two D+E pairs make a fully developed paragraph.

When someone hears the word "robot," they may think of science fiction movies or futuristic stories, but robots are more helpful in real life than most people realize. For example, the passage explains that robots can perform surgery with more precision than human hands. This shows that robots are not just entertainment but are saving lives in hospitals. Another detail from the passage is that robots help explore dangerous places like deep oceans and outer space. This shows that robots allow scientists to study places that would be too risky for people to visit. That is why robots are more useful in real life than the imaginary versions people see in stories.

Your Q11 Paragraph Plan

Single Paragraph Outline: T.S. / S.D. / S.D. / C.S.

Emma, before you write Q11 on the next test, plan your paragraph using this outline. Think of it as your scratchpad before writing.

Single Paragraph Outline
T.S. Topic Sentence: State your main idea. Answer the prompt directly. (Your hook on G4.2 was strong here.)
S.D. Supporting Detail 1: A specific fact or example from the passage + "This shows..." explanation.
S.D. Supporting Detail 2: A second fact or example from the passage + "This shows..." explanation.
C.S. Concluding Sentence: Restate your main idea in different words. Wrap up the paragraph.

How this helps: On G4.2, your paragraph had a strong topic sentence but was underdeveloped after that. Planning two S.D. rows before you write makes sure you include enough detail and explanation to earn full points.
