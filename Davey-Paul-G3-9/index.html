Your #2 Leak: Appositive Construction

Q3 or Q4 every test. You get the idea — you just miss the commas.

Davey, appositives come up on almost every test (usually Q3 or Q4). You understand what they are — you can even spot them correctly (G3.5 Q5 you nailed it). But when you write your own, 4 of your 9 tests show the same structural slip: the appositive is there, but it is not fenced in by two commas the way it needs to be.

Here is what happened on four different tests:

FROM G3.1 Q1 — 0/2
Prompt: "Rewrite sentences 1 and 2 as one sentence that includes an appositive."
You wrote: "My family has dogs, we have two, their names are Lucy and Rocket."
(Three separate clauses — no appositive structure at all.)
FROM G3.5 Q4 — 0/2
Prompt: "Combine the ideas by using an appositive."
You wrote: "The books, are still popular they are known for their golden spines, are still very popular."
(Repeated the main clause twice — the appositive never actually got inserted.)
FROM G3.6 Q3 — 0.5/2
Prompt: "Combine sentences 17 and 18 using an appositive."
You wrote: "They are slow movers, porcupines are rodents of the forest."
(Two clauses stuck together with a comma — neither one is renaming the other.)
FROM G3.9 Q1 — 0.5/2 (most recent)
Prompt: "Combine Sentence 3 and Sentence 4 into one sentence using an appositive."
You wrote: "The queen bee, the biggest bee in the colony, is the only leader of the colony."
(Structure is great! But it dropped the original word "colony" from sentence 3 — grading looked for the word to be preserved.)
Prompt: "Combine using an appositive — The porcupines are rodents. They are the slow movers of the forest." Which rewrite uses an appositive correctly?
A Porcupines are rodents, they are the slow movers of the forest.
B Porcupines, the slow movers of the forest, are rodents.
THE APPOSITIVE RULE (YOUR 3-STEP CHECK)
An appositive is a describing phrase that renames a noun. It sits right next to the noun and is fenced in by two commas.

1. Find the noun you want to describe. (e.g. porcupines)
2. Right after it, add a comma and a describing phrase that renames the noun. (e.g. , the slow movers of the forest,)
3. Close with another comma, then finish the sentence. (e.g. are rodents.)

Pattern: Noun , describing phrase , rest of sentence.
CORRECT REWRITES FOR YOUR 4 PROMPTS
• G3.1: "My family has two dogs, Lucy and Rocket, who we love."
• G3.5: "The books, known for their golden spines, are still popular."
• G3.6: "Porcupines, the slow movers of the forest, are rodents."
• G3.9: "A colony has one leader, the queen bee." (appositive at the end — one comma only when it ends the sentence)
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Q3/Q4 appositive questions are worth 2 points each. You have averaged 0.5 out of 2 on these when you write them (not when you identify them). That is 1.5 points per test going uncollected. Lock the pattern — noun, describing phrase, rest — and you claim another 3 percentage points on every test.

POLISHED VERSION (19–20 out of 20 level)

Oak trees are super important for nature and humans. Oak trees provide shelter for many animals including birds, squirrels, and bugs. This shows that oak trees act like apartment buildings for the forest. These trees can also grow super big, which means they can clean a lot of air. In addition, you can make things out of these trees such as tools, houses, and desks. In conclusion, oak trees are super important, and that is why you should care for them.

The Next-Level Paragraph Formula
TOPIC State your central idea (you already do this!)
DETAIL 1 Specific detail from the passage
SHOWS "This shows..." sentence — explain what detail 1 means
DETAIL 2 A second specific detail from the passage
DETAIL 3 A third detail (optional but strong)
CLOSING Connect back to your central idea AND give a reason to care
PROOF Proofread for spelling (provide, shelter, conclusion, awesome)
