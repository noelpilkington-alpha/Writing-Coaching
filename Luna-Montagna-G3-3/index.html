Your #1 Priority: Build a Full Paragraph

Q11 — 11 out of 20 — only one sentence written
🔍
What happened on Q11: You wrote only one run-on sentence. A full-credit paragraph needs 4 to 5 complete sentences: a topic sentence, evidence from the passage, an explanation of what the evidence shows, and a closing sentence. One sentence cannot earn full marks no matter how good it is.

Luna, your paragraph on Q11 was just one long sentence that tried to say everything at once. The ideas are in your head, but they need to come out as separate sentences in a clear order.

THE 4-SENTENCE MINIMUM
For Q11, aim for at least 4 sentences:

Sentence 1: State your main idea (topic sentence).
Sentence 2: Give one detail or example from the passage.
Sentence 3: Explain what that detail shows or why it matters.
Sentence 4: Wrap up with a closing sentence that connects back to your main idea.

Each sentence ends with a period. Each sentence starts with a capital letter. That structure alone can move your Q11 score significantly.
WHAT A 4-SENTENCE PARAGRAPH LOOKS LIKE

1. Collecting things can help people become more creative. 2. For example, the article says that some people who collected cards started designing their own versions. 3. This shows that collecting gave them ideas and made them want to create something new. 4. That is how a simple hobby can lead to real skills.

Which is a complete paragraph that would earn more credit on Q11?

B Collecting things can help people become more creative. For example, the article says that some collectors started designing their own versions. This shows that collecting gave them ideas and made them want to create. That is how a simple hobby can lead to real skills.
