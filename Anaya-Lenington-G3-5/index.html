Change the Sentence Type

Your #1 recurring miss — now 5 tests in a row
📈
This is the same pattern we flagged on your G3.4 card — and it showed up again on G3.5 Q1. Every single test, a "change the sentence type" prompt has cost you half a point. That's real, and it's fixable with one rule.

Anaya, on G3.5 Question 1, the prompt was: "Rewrite this sentence as a question: The books were sold in department stores." You got the question mark right! But the verb slipped.

G3.5 Q1 — WHAT YOU WROTE
"Where books sold in dapartment stores?"
(You typed "Where" — that's a place word, like "Where are you going?" The original sentence said "The books were sold." To turn it into a question, you swap "were" to the front: "Were the books sold...?")
"Were" vs "Where" — Two Different Words
were "Were" is a verb (past tense of "are"). It talks about what happened.
The books were sold. → Were the books sold?
where "Where" is about a place. It asks at what location.
Where are the books? Where did you go?
How to Turn a Statement Into a Question
1 Find the "be" verb in the original sentence: is, are, was, were, am.
2 Move it to the front. "The books were sold" → "Were the books sold?"
3 Add a question mark at the end. (You already did this — nice.)
The prompt says: "Rewrite as a question: The books were sold in department stores." Which answer is correct?
A Where books sold in department stores?
B Were the books sold in department stores?
YOUR LAST 5 TESTS — THE FULL-CREDIT VERSIONS
• G3.1 Q2 (change to declarative): "There is only one place we do not take our dogs."
• G3.2 Q3 (change to exclamatory): "What an amazing and fun event this is!"
• G3.3 Q1 (change to question): "Should I name her Sally?"
• G3.4 Q3 (change to interrogative): "Isn't the geode a perfect souvenir?"
• G3.5 Q1 (change to question): "Were the books sold in department stores?"
YOUR 3-STEP TRICK
Step 1: Read the prompt's verb. "Change to a question" → need ?. "Change to a declarative" → need .. "Change to an exclamatory" → need !.

Step 2: Find the "be" verb in the original (is, are, was, were, am). Move it to the front.

Step 3: Keep the rest of the sentence the same. Don't swap "were" for "where" — those are different words.
