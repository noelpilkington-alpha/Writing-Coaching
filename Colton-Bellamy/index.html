Q2 Fix — Know Your Sentence Types

You picked Imperative — correct answer was Declarative

The question asked: "What type of sentence is this?"

"Some residents worried about the costs of maintaining the panels."

Here are the four sentence types:

Declarative
Makes a statement. Ends with a period.
Interrogative
Asks a question. Ends with ?
Imperative
Gives a command. Ends with . or !
Exclamatory
Shows strong feeling. Ends with !

"Some residents worried about the costs..." is a statement about what happened. No one is being told to do anything, so it is not imperative.

QUICK TEST
Is someone being told to do something?
Yes → Imperative ("Close the door." "Study harder.")
No → It's probably Declarative (a statement of fact or opinion).

Essay Fix — No Personal Opinion in Expository Writing

Expository means EXPLAIN using facts — not "I think"

Expository writing explains a topic using facts and evidence from the passage. It is not about what you personally believe. Watch for these phrases and remove them:

I personally think I believe I think In my opinion
YOUR ESSAY
"Still, I personally think solar panels are one of the most important things to have in a village, city, or house because it gives you reliable energy."

FIXED
"Solar panels are one of the most important additions to any community because they provide reliable, low-cost energy."

Notice the fix: remove "I personally think" and state it as a fact. The evidence from the passage supports the claim — you don't need to say "I think."

Also watch for "you" throughout your essay. Expository writing uses third person (the village, residents, people) — not second person ("you").

THE RULE
In expository writing, never use "I think," "I believe," or "I personally think." State your points as facts and let the evidence support them. Replace "you" with specific nouns (residents, the community, people).

Essay Fix — Stick to the Passage

Don't add information that isn't in the text

In an expository essay, every fact should come from the passage. If it's not in the text, don't include it. Here are two examples from your essay:

Solar panels collect energy from sunlight, not "from the air." The passage doesn't describe it this way, and the claim is inaccurate.

The Body Paragraph Pattern

How to build a strong body paragraph every time

Every body paragraph in a G7 essay should follow this pattern:

Body Paragraph Formula
TS Topic Sentence — states the paragraph's main point
E1 Evidence 1 — a specific fact or detail from the passage
X1 Explanation 1 — explain why that evidence matters
E2 Evidence 2 — a second fact or detail from the passage
X2 Explanation 2 — explain why that evidence matters
CS Closing Sentence — wraps up the paragraph's main idea

Here's what a body paragraph about medical benefits would look like with this pattern:

Tap to see the example paragraph
Study the pattern above first
EXAMPLE BODY PARAGRAPH
[TS] The solar panels greatly improved the village's medical clinic.
[E1] The clinic could now store vaccines safely overnight.
[X1] This meant medicine stayed effective, protecting more people.
[E2] Doctors and nurses could work longer hours.
[X2] Patients no longer had to rush to get care before dark.
[CS] Reliable power made the clinic a safer place for everyone.
THE KEY RULE
Every E (evidence) must be followed by an X (explanation). Evidence without explanation is just a list of facts. The explanation shows the reader why that evidence supports your point. Ask yourself: "So what? Why does this matter?"
