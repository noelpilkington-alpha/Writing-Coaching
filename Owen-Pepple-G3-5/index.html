What Is an Appositive? (Q4)

Q4 — 1 out of 2

Owen, Q4 asked you to combine two ideas using an appositive. An appositive is a short naming phrase tucked into a sentence between commas that gives extra information about a noun. Here is what happened:

FROM G3.5 Q4 — 1 OUT OF 2
Prompt: Combine these ideas using an appositive: "The books are still popular. They are best sellers."
You wrote: "The books, are best sellers, are known for thier golden spines."
The commas break the sentence apart here. "The books, are best sellers, are known for their golden spines" doesn't read correctly because the appositive phrase isn't tucked in properly. Also: "thier" → "their".
What an Appositive Looks Like
PATTERN [Noun], [naming phrase], [rest of sentence].
EXAMPLE The books, popular best sellers, are known for their golden spines.
TEST IT Remove the phrase between commas. Does the sentence still make sense? "The books are known for their golden spines." — Yes! That means the appositive is correct.

The appositive must be a naming phrase (no verb of its own). It renames or describes the noun right before it. If you remove it, the sentence should still be complete.
