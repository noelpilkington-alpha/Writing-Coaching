YOUR 15-MINUTE REVISE PASS
Minute 1–5 — Thesis & topic sentences. Read your thesis out loud. If you stumble, rewrite it simpler. Then check that each body paragraph opens with a clear topic sentence that echoes the thesis.

Minute 6–10 — Evidence explanation. Find every piece of evidence in your essay (anything from the passage). After each one, check: did you write a sentence starting with “This shows...,” “This means...,” or “This proves...”? If not, add one.

Minute 11–15 — Spelling, commas, and sentence variety. Read the whole essay out loud. Any word that looks off, fix it. Any sentence that runs long, split it or add a comma + conjunction.

Your 2 Missed Multiple Choice Questions

G6.2 Q2 & Q3 — both fixable in one minute

Ava, on G6.2 you missed exactly two multiple-choice questions. Here is each one, what the right answer is, and how to recognize it next time.

ERROR 1 — FROM G6.2 Q2 (sentence types)
Question: What type of sentence is this? “Did you enjoy the animated movie last night?”
Your pick: did not identify it as interrogative.
THE RULE — THE 4 SENTENCE TYPES
Every sentence is one of four types. Match the ending punctuation to the type:

Declarative — makes a statement. Ends with .
  “Animated movies teach life lessons.”
Interrogative — asks a question. Ends with ?
  “Did you enjoy the animated movie last night?”
Imperative — gives a command. Usually ends with .
  “Watch the movie tonight.”
Exclamatory — shows strong feeling. Ends with !
  “That movie was amazing!”
From G6.2 Q2 — which sentence is interrogative?
A Animated movies often teach children important life lessons.
B Did you enjoy the animated movie last night?
ERROR 2 — FROM G6.2 Q3 (logical conjunctions)
Question: Which sentence combines the ideas using a logical conjunction?
“Animated movies are fun. They also include valuable lessons.”
Your pick: a combined sentence, but the conjunction didn't match the meaning.
THE RULE — MATCH THE CONJUNCTION TO THE MEANING
When you combine two sentences, the joining word has to match the relationship between them:

and = adds a second idea of the same type (fun AND lessons)
but / however = shows a contrast (fun BUT also serious)
because / so = cause and effect (fun BECAUSE of the colors)
or = gives a choice (fun OR boring)

For the G6.2 Q3 stimulus, both ideas are positive — movies are fun AND they also have lessons. That is an additive relationship, so the right conjunction is and, not but or because.
From G6.2 Q3 — which combination uses a LOGICAL conjunction?
A Animated movies are fun, but they also include valuable lessons.
B Animated movies are fun, and they also include valuable lessons.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
These two questions cost you 2 points on G6.2. Both are one-minute fixes with a simple rule: match the ending punctuation to the sentence type, and match the conjunction to the meaning. Those 2 points would have moved your score from 83 to 90.

MODEL BODY PARAGRAPH (5/5 evidence-and-explanation level)

First, animated movies teach children about kindness through the way characters treat each other. For example, in many animated films the main character learns to help others instead of thinking only about themselves. This shows that children who watch these movies see kindness modeled in action, not just described, which makes the lesson easier to remember. In addition, animated movies often end with the kind character being rewarded in some way. This proves to young viewers that choosing to be kind is worth it, even when it is hard. By pairing a likable character with a clear lesson, animated movies give children a safe way to practice recognizing kindness before they need to show it in their own lives.

WHAT THIS MODEL DOES THAT YOURS CAN DO NEXT TIME
• Two pieces of evidence (“characters learn to help” and “kind character rewarded”)
• Each piece of evidence is followed by a “This shows...” or “This proves...” explanation
• A closing sentence that ties the paragraph back to the thesis
• Everything stays focused on one main idea (kindness)
