What an Appositive IS (and What It Isn't)

Your #1 structural error — 3 of 4 tests

Kira, your #1 structural error across 4 tests is the appositive question. You reach for conjunctions, adverbs, or adjectives when the prompt asks for an appositive. Let's look at the pattern:

FROM G3.1 Q1 — APPOSITIVE TASK
"My family has two dogs and their names are Lucy and Rocket."
(You used the conjunction "and" — this is a compound sentence, not an appositive. An appositive version: "My family has two dogs, Lucy and Rocket.")
FROM G3.3 Q3 — APPOSITIVE TASK
"Sally excitedly swam to the other seals."
(You added the adverb "excitedly" — but adverbs aren’t appositives. An appositive version: "Sally, the playful young seal, swam to the other seals.")
FROM G3.4 Q1 — EXPAND THE VERB
"Layla looked at the bright and blooming flower."
(You added adjectives to the flower — but the prompt asked you to change the verb. "Looked at" could become "gazed at" or "studied.")
What Is an Appositive?
DEF An appositive is a noun phrase that renames another noun right next to it, usually set off by commas.
My dog, Lucy, loves the beach. → "Lucy" renames "my dog" — that's an appositive.
NOT Not an appositive: adding "and" to join another sentence. That's a compound sentence.
NOT Not an appositive: adding an adverb like "excitedly" or "quickly." Those describe actions, not nouns.
NOT Not an appositive: adding adjectives like "bright" or "tall" in front of a noun. Those are just describing words — not a renaming phrase.
The prompt says: "Expand this sentence using an appositive: My brother rides a bike." Which answer is an appositive?
A My brother quickly rides a bike.
B My brother, a fifth grader who loves races, rides a bike.
YOUR TEST PROMPTS, REWRITTEN WITH APPOSITIVES
• G3.1 Q1: "My family has two dogs, Lucy and Rocket," (name-renaming appositive)
• G3.3 Q3: "Sally, the playful young seal, swam to the other seals." (description-renaming appositive)
• G3.4 Q1: Use a stronger verb: "Layla gazed at the flower." (different task — verb change, not appositive)
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
You hit this on 3 of 4 tests. The pattern: when the prompt says "appositive," write a noun phrase set off by commas that renames another noun. No "and," no adverbs, no adjectives. One clear rule, worth 0.5–1 point per test.
