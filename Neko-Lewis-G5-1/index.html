Error #1: "As" Creates the Wrong Meaning

Step 5 catch: "as" means simultaneous, but you needed causation

Neko, on Q3 you wrote a sentence using "as" to connect two ideas. The problem is that "as" makes the two events sound like they happened at the same time (simultaneous). But the question wanted you to show that one event caused the other.

Q3 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2 weighted points)
"I stood up straight, as my backpack knocked the vase off the shelf."

The problem: "As" here means "at the same time." It says you stood up straight simultaneously with the backpack knocking the vase. But the intended meaning is that standing up straight caused the backpack to knock the vase. You need a causation connector.

CORRECT: CAUSATION CONNECTOR
"I stood up straight, and my backpack knocked the vase off the shelf."

Or even clearer: "When I stood up straight, my backpack knocked the vase off the shelf."
CONNECTOR MEANINGS
• "as" = at the same time (simultaneous)
• "when" = at that moment (trigger + result)
• "and" = then this also happened (sequence)
• "so" = therefore (direct cause)

For a cause-and-effect relationship, use "when," "and," or "so." Avoid "as" unless you truly mean simultaneous actions.

A"I stood up straight while my backpack knocked the vase off the shelf."
B"When I stood up straight, my backpack knocked the vase off the shelf."

Error #2: State the Answer Explicitly

Q6 and Q9: engaging stories that do not directly answer the question

Neko, your writing voice is a strength. You tell engaging stories. But on Q6 and Q9, the story did not explicitly state what the question asked for.

🔎
Q6 (2/3): The question asked you to describe a time you admitted a mistake. You wrote a long, engaging story, but you never explicitly said what the mistake was that you admitted. The grading feedback needs to see the mistake named directly.

Q9 (2/3): Good answer but generic. The question asked for specifics and you gave a general response.
THE FIX: ANSWER FIRST, THEN STORY
For questions that ask "describe a time when..." or "give an example of...":

1. First sentence: Directly answer the question. Name the thing.
2. Then: Tell the story as support.
3. Last sentence: Explain why it matters.

Example structure: "One time I admitted a mistake was when I [name the mistake]. [Tell what happened]. This shows that [explanation]."
The question asks: "Describe a time you admitted a mistake." Which answer earns full points?
A"I was in the kitchen and something happened and then I told my mom about it and she said it was okay."
B"One time I admitted a mistake was when I accidentally broke my mom's favorite mug. I could have hidden it, but instead I told her it was my fault. This showed me that being honest is more important than avoiding trouble."
