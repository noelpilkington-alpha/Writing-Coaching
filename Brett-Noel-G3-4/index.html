Develop the Paragraph with Specific Details

Question 11 — this is your #1 opportunity

Brett, the Question 11 article was "Sports and Society" — about how modern sports developed and became popular. Here is the paragraph you wrote:

YOU WROTE (G3.4 Q11, paragraph scored 13/20)
"Sports are popular games that are played all over the world. Sports developed rules over time. There are scores and records that have been set over time in many different types of sports. Sports have developed over time."

The bones of a paragraph are here — a topic sentence and a closing. But it stays general: it tells us sports "developed over time" three times without ever explaining HOW they developed or WHY that made them popular. That repeating is what held the score at 65%.

Which detail would earn more credit on this paragraph?
A Sports have changed and developed over time in many ways.
B Sports created clear rules so that every game was fair, which made people want to watch and play them.
THE RULE: SPECIFIC, THEN SO WHAT
A strong detail does two things:
1. It names a specific fact from the article (a rule, a score or record, a stadium, a team).
2. It explains HOW that fact answers the question — here, how it helped sports become popular.

General lines like "sports developed over time" do neither. Trade each one for a real fact plus its reason.

MODEL PARAGRAPH (article-grounded, higher-scoring)

Modern sports became popular as they developed over many years. First, sports created clear rules so that every game was fair, which made people trust the results and want to play. This shows that rules helped sports grow because fans knew what to expect. Sports also began keeping scores and records, so people could compare players and celebrate the best ones. This shows that records gave fans a reason to follow their favorite teams. Big teams and stadiums let huge crowds watch together, making each game feel exciting. In the end, rules, records, and teams all worked together to turn sports into something people everywhere wanted to be a part of.

SEE THE DIFFERENCE
The model uses three specific details — rules, scores and records, and teams and stadiums — and after each one it explains why that helped sports become popular. Not one sentence repeats. That is how a 65% paragraph becomes a strong one.

Do the Exact Edit the Question Asks

Question 1 — a small habit worth real points

Brett, Question 1 asked you to make the verb stronger in a sentence about Layla. Here is what you wrote:

YOU WROTE (G3.4 Q1, scored 1/2)
"Layla looked at the flower and took a picture of it."

You added a nice extra action ("took a picture"), but the question asked you to strengthen the verb — and the verb is still the weak word "looked." The task was to swap "looked" for a more vivid verb, not to add a new sentence part. Doing the exact edit asked is what earns full credit.

The question asks for a STRONGER verb. Which answer does that?
A Layla looked at the flower very carefully and closely.
B Layla studied the flower closely.
THE RULE: CHANGE THE EXACT WORD ASKED
When a question says "make the verb stronger," find the action word and swap it for a more vivid one:

looked → studied, admired, gazed at, examined
walked → marched, wandered, hurried
said → whispered, shouted, explained

Don't keep the weak verb and add more words. Replace the verb itself.
YOUR SENTENCE, FIXED
"Layla studied the flower and took a picture of it."
One stronger verb, and the question is fully answered.
