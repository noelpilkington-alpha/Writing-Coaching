The Detail + Explanation Pattern

Q11 — 16 out of 20 (same pattern as G3.3)
🔎 This is the same gap from your last test. When you write a paragraph, you state your ideas clearly, but you stop one step short. You give the what but not the so what.

Here is what the graders look for in a strong paragraph:

THE TWO-PART MOVE
Every strong supporting sentence has two parts:

1. A detail from the passage (a fact, event, or quote)
2. What it shows (why that detail matters to your point)

Most students at 16/20 do part 1 well but skip part 2. They give the detail and move on without explaining what it proves.
Which supporting sentence uses both a passage detail AND explains what it shows?
A "Layla found a geode on the nature trail."
B "Layla found a geode on the nature trail, which shows that paying attention to small things can lead to surprising discoveries."
THE "WHICH SHOWS THAT" TRICK
After you write a detail from the passage, add the words "which shows that..." or "this proves that..." and finish the sentence. That forces you to explain WHY the detail matters.

You do not have to keep those exact words in your final answer. But using them while drafting guarantees you include the explanation part every time.

18-20/20 VERSION (DETAIL + EXPLANATION)
"Nature trails are good for kids because they turn ordinary walks into learning experiences. For example, Layla found a geode on the trail, which shows that paying close attention can lead to surprising discoveries. She also noticed different plants and birds, which helped her understand how many living things share the same space. Nature trails teach kids about the world by letting them experience it firsthand instead of just reading about it."

See the difference? Same details, but every one is followed by an explanation of what it shows. The ending also wraps up the idea instead of just restating the topic.

YOUR FORMULA FOR EACH SUPPORTING SENTENCE
Detail (what happened in the passage) + Explanation (what it shows about your main idea)

Aim for at least 2 supporting sentences that follow this pattern. That alone will push you from 16 to 18+.

Proofread Before You Finish

Catch small errors that cost easy points

Nico, your grammar on the short-answer questions is strong (24/25). But when you write a longer paragraph, spelling and small errors can sneak in because you are focused on your ideas. A quick proofread at the end catches those free points.

THE 30-SECOND PROOFREAD
Before you call your paragraph done, read it back one time slowly and check three things:

1. Spelling — Do any words look wrong? Sound them out.
2. Capitals — Does every sentence start with a capital letter? Are names capitalized?
3. Periods — Does every sentence end with a period? Did you accidentally put a period in the middle of a thought (the fragment pattern from G3.3)?
Which sentence has a spelling or capitalization error?
A "Layla liked the trail becuase she found interesting things."
B "Layla liked the trail because she found interesting things."
WORDS TO WATCH
Some words are easy to misspell when you are writing fast. Keep an eye on:

• because (not "becuase" or "becuse")
• different (not "diffrent")
• interesting (not "intresting")
• through (not "threw" when you mean passing through)

If a word looks off, slow down and sound out each syllable.

BEFORE YOU FINISH YOUR PARAGRAPH

If you can answer yes to all three, you are in the 18-20 range.
