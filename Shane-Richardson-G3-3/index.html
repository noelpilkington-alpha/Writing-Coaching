Fix 2: Facts You Can Point To

Q11: four true sentences, none of them from the article
THE RULE: THE POINT, THE HOW, THEN FACTS FROM THE PAGE
The first sentence answers the question (POINT) and names the two reasons the paragraph will give (HOW). Then each DETAIL · SHOW is a fact you can put your finger on in the article: a name, a number, a quote. After each, a DETAIL · SO WHAT: This shows… Then an ending. The check: for each sentence in the middle, point to the words in the article. If you cannot, it is a gist, not a detail, and gists do not earn the points.

Fix 3: An Appositive Sits Between Commas

Q3: the right idea, with the commas in the wrong place
THE RULE: A NOUN PHRASE THAT RENAMES, WITH A COMMA ON EACH SIDE
An appositive is a noun phrase that gives the thing another name, and it sits between commas: Sally, the baby seal, swam to the other seals. The check: cover everything between the commas. The sentence left behind must still be whole. Then read what you covered: does it start with a noun that could stand in for the thing before it? One comma before, one after.
YOU WROTE (Q3, 1 OUT OF 2)
“Sally the seal, swan to the other seals.” COMMA ON THE WRONG SIDE
The seal is the right kind of phrase, a noun that renames Sally. But the only comma sits after it, cutting the subject from its verb, and swan is a bird. Cover the phrase: Sally swam to the other seals. The phrase needs a comma on each side.
WITH THE COMMAS IN PLACE
Sally, APPOSITIVEthe baby seal, swam to the other seals.
Cover the middle: Sally swam to the other seals. Whole. Read the middle: the baby seal, a noun that could stand in for Sally.

Three appositives from the story, and one that is not:

APPOSITIVEThe pup, a baby seal tangled in fishnet, would not leave the shallow water.

Fix 4: Joining Words That Lean

Q5: the blank needed one. Q7: one was there with nothing to lean on
THE RULE: SINCE, WHEN, BECAUSE, AFTER START A PART THAT CANNOT STAND ALONE
Some joining words start a leaning part: since, when, because, after, while, although. A leaning part needs a main part to lean on. When the leaning part comes first, a comma separates them: Since there was no sign of the seals, the family placed Sally in a fish box. The check: if a sentence starts with one of these words, find the main part after the comma. If there is no main part, the sentence is a fragment.
YOU WROTE (Q5, 0 OUT OF 2)
“Sadly, there was no sign of the seals, the family placed Sally in a fish box and motored into the bay.” A FEELING WORD, NOT A JOINING WORD
The blank sits in front of a part that has to lean on the main part. Sadly tells how someone felt and joins nothing, so the sentence becomes two sentences on one comma. Since, When, After or Because all do the job.
WITH A JOINING WORD
SINCESince there was no sign of the seals, the family placed Sally in a fish box and motored into the bay.
YOU WROTE (Q7, 1 OUT OF 3)
“When they found the seal beucase it was very sad.” TWO LEANING PARTS, NO MAIN PART
A when-part and a because-part, and nothing for either to lean on. Cover the question and read it: it floats.
WITH A MAIN PART
