Step 5: Subject-Verb Agreement

Q1 lost a point because “was” should be “were”

Jidechi, on Q1 you wrote a sentence with the wrong verb form. The subject is plural but the verb is singular.

Q1 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2 weighted points)
"Where was the books sold?"

The problem: "Books" is plural (more than one book). Plural subjects need "were," not "was."

FIXED
"Where were the books sold?"
THE RULE: SUBJECT-VERB AGREEMENT
Singular subject (one thing) → use "was" or "is"
• "The book was sold at the store."

Plural subject (more than one) → use "were" or "are"
• "The books were sold at the store."

How to check: Find the subject of your sentence. Is it one thing or more than one? Then make sure the verb matches.
The subject is "books" (plural). Which verb is correct?
A "Where was the books sold?" — use "was" because it sounds natural.
B "Where were the books sold?" — use "were" because "books" is plural.
WHY THIS MATTERS
Q1 is worth 2 weighted points. You earned 1 instead of 2 because of the was/were error. The fix: find your subject, count it (one or more than one), and pick the matching verb.

Step 2: Appositive Structure

Q4 lost a point because the appositive got mixed with a conjunction

Jidechi, on Q4 you were asked to write a sentence with an appositive. Your answer mixed the appositive structure with a conjunction, which broke the format.

Q4 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2 weighted points)
"The book are still popular, Little Golden Book, because families love reading them."

The problems:

  • The appositive "Little Golden Book" is placed after "popular" instead of after the noun it renames ("The book").
  • "are" should be "is" (singular: "The book is...")
  • The appositive got mixed together with "because," creating a confusing structure.
CORRECT APPOSITIVE STRUCTURE
"The book, a Little Golden Book with a golden spine, is still popular with families today."
APPOSITIVE FORMULA
Noun + comma + renaming phrase + comma + rest of sentence

• "The book, a Little Golden Book with colorful covers, is still popular."

The appositive (the part between commas) renames or describes the noun right before it. It does not include "because" or any new ideas. It just gives more information about the noun.
Which sentence uses the appositive correctly?
A The book are still popular, Little Golden Book, because families love them.
B The book, a Little Golden Book with a golden spine, is still popular with families today.
THE PAYOFF
Q4 is worth 2 weighted points. You earned 1. The fix: keep your appositive simple. It goes right after the noun, between two commas, and only renames or describes that noun. Do not mix in "because" or other ideas.

How to use "because" without creating a fragment
FRAGMENT (from Q10)
"I think the books are popular. Because families love reading them together."
FIXED (one complete sentence)
"I think the books are popular because families love reading them together."

The rule: "Because" connects a reason to a main idea. It belongs inside a sentence, not at the start of a new one. If you see "Because" starting a sentence after a period, remove the period and make it one sentence.
