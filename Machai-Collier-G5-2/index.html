TWO PATTERNS TO WATCH
your / you're
• your = belongs to you ("your idea", "your book")
• you're = you are ("you're trying", "you're here")
• Quick test: if you can say "you are" in the sentence, use you're.

Possessive apostrophe — when something belongs to someone, add 's:
• everyone's favorite (belongs to everyone)
• Grandma's pie (belongs to Grandma)
• my friend's house (belongs to my friend)

THE FINAL READ-THROUGH
When you finish a question, before you move on, do this one thing:

1. Read your sentence out loud in your head, one word at a time.
2. Point at each word (in your head). Does it look right? Is it spelled right?
3. For every your, ask: could I say "you are" instead? If yes, it should be you're.
4. For every compound word (like hard work or every day), ask: is this one word or two?

When the Question Says "Revise"

Q5 asked for a bigger change than you made

Q5 was the single lowest score on the sentence section (0.5/1). The prompt asked you to revise sentence 17 to make it clearer and more effective. Here is what happened:

The question asked
Revise sentence 17 to make it clearer and more effective.
WHAT YOU WROTE
We were both amazed to see our apple pie got first place!

This is a fine sentence — but it is very close to the original. The grader is looking for a real revision: new word choices, stronger verbs, more detail, maybe a different sentence shape. When a prompt says "revise for clarity and effectiveness," it is inviting you to rewrite, not just polish.

THE "REVISE" MOVE — ADD ONE OF THESE
When you see the word revise, try to add at least one of these to your rewrite:

• A stronger verb (swap "got" for "won" or "earned")
• A sensory detail (what did you see, hear, feel?)
• A specific noun ("blue ribbon" instead of "first place")
• A different sentence shape (start with a phrase, combine two ideas)
The prompt: "Revise sentence 17 to make it clearer and more effective." Which version is the stronger revision?
A We were both amazed that our apple pie won first place!
B When the judges announced our apple pie had won the blue ribbon, Grandma Addie and I beamed with pride.
Revising Sentence 17 — a stronger version
START We were both amazed to see our apple pie got first place!
VERB Swap got for won or earned
NOUN Swap first place for the blue ribbon
SHAPE Start with a phrase: "When the judges announced..."
RESULT "When the judges announced our apple pie had won the blue ribbon, Grandma Addie and I beamed with pride."
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Revision questions are worth the same as every other sentence question, but they reward you for doing more than a tiny tweak. A half-credit answer comes from a half-revision. Full credit comes from actually rewriting the sentence to make it stronger — and you already have the word-choice range to do that.

When making a business, people often work hard on making a recognizable logo. This is because a recognizable logo can make your business easier to remember and helps customers find you quickly. A recognizable logo also allows people to tell the difference between your business and someone else's. To sum it up, having a recognizable logo can help your business grow in many ways.
