Question 5 Fix — Connecting Words

Let's review this together.

You scored 0/2 on this question.

The question asked:

"Write the best word to complete the sentence below: ___ there was no sign of the seals, the family placed Sally in a fish box and motored into the bay."

Which word best completes this sentence?
A Although there was no sign of the seals, the family placed Sally in a fish box and motored into the bay.
B However, there was no sign of the seals, the family placed Sally in a fish box and motored into the bay.
YOU WROTE
"However,"
PICKING THE RIGHT CONNECTING WORD
Ask yourself what relationship the ideas have:
• Because / Since = explains WHY
• Although / Even though = shows something unexpected
• When / After / Before = tells WHEN
• If = shows a condition
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
The right connecting word makes the relationship between ideas crystal clear. Using the wrong one can change the meaning of your whole sentence.

Question 11 — Your Paragraph

From Collecting to Designing: The Story of Pokémon — 17.0/20 points
Which central idea sentence is stronger?
A The rescue of Sally the seal shows that families can make a difference when they help wildlife.
B Families can help animals.
Strong: 17.0/20 6 sentences 76 words

Austin, this is solid writing. You told Satoshi's story clearly and used details from the passage. Let's look at how each sentence works.

TOPIC (could be more specific) Satoshi Tajiri enjoyed collecting bugs as a child.

DETAIL 1 Later, as he grew older, he liked to play video games.

DETAIL 2 However, he did not like most games and thought they were boring.

DETAIL 3 He studied how to make games, and used his love for catching bugs to help make the game.

DETAIL 4 He created a game where you can find and catch creatures called Pokemon.

CLOSING (could connect back to the main idea) He wanted players to feel the same way he did when he caught bugs.
WHAT YOU DID WELL
You have clear details in the right order: bugs → video games → not liking most games → studying how to make games → creating Pokémon. That is strong storytelling. You also used the transition word "However" to show contrast.
TWO THINGS TO IMPROVE
1. Your topic sentence needs a central idea. Right now it says what Satoshi liked, but it doesn't tell the reader what the paragraph is about. A stronger version: "Satoshi Tajiri's childhood love of collecting bugs led him to create one of the world's most popular video games."

2. Your closing sentence should wrap up the main point instead of adding one more detail. Try: "Satoshi turned a simple childhood hobby into a game that millions of people enjoy."

Question 11 — Stronger Writing

How to push from 17/20 to 20/20
Which paragraph uses stronger evidence?
A Anna and Jim found a seal. They helped it. The seal got better and they let it go.
B Anna and Jim rescued a baby seal tangled in a fishing net. According to the passage, "they carefully cut away the net and placed Sally in a fish box." This shows the family acted quickly and gently to save the seal.

Your paragraph is clean and well-organized. The 3 points you lost come from two things: no specific evidence from the passage (quoted or paraphrased), and explanations that don't go deep enough.

NO SPECIFIC EVIDENCE

You mentioned facts from the passage (bugs, video games, creating Pokémon), but you didn't use specific details from the passage (quoted or paraphrased) and explain what they show. Grading feedback needs to see that you are pulling evidence from the text and connecting it to your point.

DETAIL 3 REWRITTEN WITH SPECIFIC EVIDENCE

"According to the passage, Satoshi ‘studied computer science and learned how to design games.’ He used his love for catching bugs to create a game where players could do the same thing."

Note: This example uses a direct quote, but you could also paraphrase — for example: "The passage explains that Satoshi went on to study computer science so he could learn to build his own games." Either way works, as long as you include specific details and explain what they show.

THE EVIDENCE SANDWICH
1. Set it up: Write a sentence that introduces your evidence.
2. Evidence: Use specific details from the passage (quoted or paraphrased).
3. Explain: Tell the reader why this evidence matters or what it shows.
SO WHAT?
