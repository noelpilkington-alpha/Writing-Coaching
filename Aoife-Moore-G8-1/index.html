Step 6: Paragraph Logic — Your #1 Error

Q3, Q6, Q9 all lost to the same mistake
🔎 Pattern alert: Three multiple-choice questions on G8.1 all test the same skill: identifying which sentence is the topic sentence or which sentence does not belong in a paragraph. You missed all three. The same error appeared on G7.1 (Q7). This is a recurring pattern.

Q6 (0/1): The question asked which sentence best introduces the paragraph. You picked a sentence that supports the main idea rather than the one that states it. The topic sentence is the one that makes a claim the rest of the paragraph proves.

Q3 (0/1): You chose an answer with the wrong conjunction logic. The sentence needed to show a cause-and-effect relationship, but the option you picked showed a contrast relationship.

Q9 (0/1): The question asked which sentence does not belong. You picked a sentence that does belong because it supports the paragraph's main idea. The sentence that does not belong introduces a new topic unrelated to the paragraph's focus.

THE FIX: RE-READ THE PARAGRAPH, THEN ASK ONE QUESTION
Before answering any paragraph-logic question, go back to the passage and re-read the entire paragraph the question refers to. Then ask yourself:

• "What is this paragraph mostly about?"

The topic sentence is the one that answers that question directly. Supporting details explain or prove it. A sentence that "does not belong" introduces a different topic entirely.
A paragraph is about the benefits of the new lakeside library for local students. Which sentence does NOT belong?
A Students will have access to quiet study rooms that were not available in the old building.
B The construction project took fourteen months longer than originally planned due to supply chain delays.

Step 4: Essay Development — The D+E Pattern

Q11 scored 16/20 — development is the gap

Aoife, your essay earned 16 out of 20. Your structure is sound and your ideas are clear. But your body paragraphs present evidence without fully explaining why that evidence matters. This is what the rubric calls "development" and "elaboration."

THE D+E PATTERN
After every piece of evidence (a fact, a quote, or a detail from the passage), add one sentence that explains:

• Why does this matter?
• How does this connect to my thesis?
• What does this show about the bigger idea?

Evidence without explanation = a list of facts. Evidence with explanation = a developed argument.
YOUR PATTERN (EVIDENCE WITHOUT ELABORATION)
"The library will have a community room for events. It will also have a children's reading area on the second floor."
DEVELOPED VERSION (EVIDENCE + ELABORATION)
"The library will have a community room for events, which means local organizations will finally have a free space to hold meetings and workshops. It will also have a children's reading area on the second floor, giving younger students a dedicated space where they can explore books without competing with adult patrons for seating."

Notice how the developed version adds one sentence of explanation after each fact. The facts are the same. The difference is explaining what they mean.

Which version shows proper development after the evidence?
A "The new library has large windows. It also has solar panels on the roof. The building uses recycled materials."
B "The new library has large windows facing the lake. This design choice allows natural light to fill every floor, which reduces electricity costs and creates a calmer environment for readers."
