THE RULE
A comma marks a real pause between two parts of a sentence. It never goes between a word and the thing it directly connects to — so never between to and its verb, and never between finally and the verb it describes. Read the sentence out loud: if you would not naturally pause there, the comma does not belong there.
Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
AMy favorite thing to do with a pet is to, play catch with them.
BMy favorite thing to do with a pet is to play catch with them.

What is working, and it is a lot. Your opening sentence states the central idea cleanly. Then you build an actual chain of consequences: no proper soil means no plants, no plants means no food, no food means animals starve, and that means starvation worldwide. Following a cause all the way to its end like that is a genuinely advanced move, and it is why this scored 18.

The two points you lost. The idea “plants would not be able to grow” appears twice, once in your third sentence and again in your fifth. Cutting the repeat frees up room. Beyond that it is just the two stray commas and “is need”, which should be “is needed”.

YOUR PARAGRAPH, POLISHED (20/20 territory)

“Soil is important to Earth in a multitude of ways. To start off, soil is needed for proper plant life to grow. Without the correct type of soil, plants cannot take root or take in the nutrients they need. That leads to a second problem. Without plants, we would have no food. Different animals eat plants to survive and grow, so a world without healthy soil would eventually lead to the starvation of many animals. That would finally cause starvation around the world.”

Every link in your chain survived. The repeated clause became a new piece of information (“cannot take root or take in the nutrients they need”), and the two commas came out.
