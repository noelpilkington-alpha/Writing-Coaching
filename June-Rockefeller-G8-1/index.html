#1 Focus: The Umbrella Test

Q6 — choosing the sentence that introduces a paragraph

You went 8 for 10 on multiple choice, and both misses — Q6 and Q9 — test the same skill: paragraph logic. A paragraph has one controlling topic; the topic sentence names it, and every detail must belong to it. Here is the first miss.

Q6 asked: Which sentence would best introduce a paragraph about new activities at the library?

YOU CHOSE (WRONG)
"Neighborhoods across the lake enjoyed the lively activities at the library."

Why it fails as an introduction: this sentence reacts to the activities — it tells us who enjoyed them — instead of introducing them. The paragraph is supposed to describe the activities themselves, and those details cannot sit under a sentence about the neighborhoods' reaction.

CORRECT ANSWER
"From music performances to workshops, the library became a lively community space."

Why it works: it names the category — the activities themselves — and sets the scope for everything that follows. A sentence about music performances fits under it. A sentence about workshops fits under it. Every detail has a home.

THE UMBRELLA TEST
A topic sentence is the umbrella — every other sentence in the paragraph must fit under it. Before you pick, test each option: "Could all of the paragraph's details sit under this sentence?" If the option describes a reaction, a single detail, or a side effect, it belongs inside the paragraph — not on top of it.
Which sentence could introduce a paragraph about new study spaces at the library?
A "Students loved the bright reading corner by the windows."
B "From quiet reading corners to group study rooms, the library now offers a space for every kind of learner."
HERE IS THE ENCOURAGING PART
You already use this skill perfectly in your own writing — every body paragraph in your G8.1 essay opens with a topic sentence that all its details fit under. Q6 was just the reading-side version of a skill you own on the writing side. Turn the umbrella test around and those points come home.

#2 Focus: Does It Belong?

Q9 — the same umbrella skill, run in reverse

Q9 is the umbrella test flipped around: instead of picking the umbrella, you find the sentence that doesn't fit under it.

Q9 asked: Which sentence does NOT belong in a paragraph about programs at the library?

YOU CHOSE (WRONG)
"Monthly talks drew people from all over the city."

Why that one belongs: monthly talks are a library program — they fit squarely under the paragraph's controlling topic, so they stay.

CORRECT ANSWER
"Weekend markets sold fresh bread, fruit, and crafts."

Why it doesn't belong: the markets are commerce on the dock — something happening near the library, not a program the library runs. It is easy to see why they felt on-topic: your own essay mentions the weekend markets as part of the library's transformation. But "near the library" is not the same as "a library program."

NAME THE TOPIC FIRST
Before testing any option, say the paragraph's controlling topic in your own words: "programs the library runs." Then test each sentence against it: "Is this actually a programs item, or just something near the library?" The sentence whose subject sits outside the topic is the one that goes.
Which sentence does NOT belong in a paragraph about ways to travel to the library?
A "A water taxi carries visitors across the lake every half hour."
B "Local artists hang colorful paintings on the library's wooden walls."
ONE SKILL, TWO POINTS
Q6 and Q9 are the same move in two directions: name the controlling topic, then test every sentence against it. Pick the umbrella that covers all the details; remove the sentence the umbrella cannot cover. Master this one check and both of your missed points come back.
