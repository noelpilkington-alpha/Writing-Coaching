Q3: "But" vs "Because" — Logical Conjunctions

Your only MCQ miss — understanding the relationship between ideas

The original sentences were:

Original Sentences
"Animated movies are fun. They also include valuable lessons."

These are two separate qualities — the movies are fun AND they have lessons. The word "also" signals that the second idea adds to the first.

YOU CHOSE ("BECAUSE")
"Animated movies are fun because they include valuable lessons."

"Because" means the movies are fun for the reason that they have lessons. That changes the logical relationship — it makes the lessons the cause of the fun, which isn't what the original sentences say.

Read the explanation above first
CORRECT ANSWER ("BUT")
"Animated movies are fun but they include valuable lessons."

"But" keeps both ideas as connected but distinct qualities: fun BUT ALSO educational.

Conjunction What it means Example
because Cause and effect (A happens because of B) "I stayed home because it rained."
but Contrast or addition of a different quality "It rained, but we still had fun."
TIP
Look for signal words in the original. "Also" tells you the second idea is being added, not used as a reason. When you see "also," the combined sentence needs a conjunction that connects two separate ideas — like "but," "and," or "yet" — not "because."
