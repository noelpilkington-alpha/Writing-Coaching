Run-On Sentences (Q3)

Q3 — 1 out of 2 — the appositive is great, but watch the run-on
💪
Good news first: You used an appositive correctly ("small creatures covered in quills"). That is an advanced skill. The issue is what happens after "rodents."
FROM G3.6 Q3 — 1 OUT OF 2
"Porcupines, small creatures covered in quills, are rodents they are the slow movers of the forest."
After "rodents," a new idea starts ("they are the slow movers") but there is no period or conjunction separating them. That makes it a run-on.
How to Fix This Run-On
RUN-ON ...are rodents they are the slow movers of the forest.
FIX 1 ...are rodents. They are the slow movers of the forest.
FIX 2 ...are rodents, and they are the slow movers of the forest.
THE RULE
When one complete thought ends and another begins, you need something between them: a period, or a comma + conjunction (and, but, so). Without that separator, you have a run-on sentence.
Which version correctly fixes the run-on?
A Porcupines, small creatures covered in quills, are rodents, they are the slow movers of the forest.
B Porcupines, small creatures covered in quills, are rodents. They are the slow movers of the forest.
YOUR REREAD CHECK
When you reread, look for spots where a new subject appears right after another word with no punctuation between them. If you see "[word] they" or "[word] it" or "[word] he" in the middle of a sentence, you probably need a period before the pronoun.

Possessives (Q8)

Q8 — 2 out of 3 — showing ownership with an apostrophe

Ryan, on Q8 your answer was strong — you described what happens when another animal gets too close to a porcupine. But one punctuation mark was missing:

FROM G3.6 Q8 — 2 OUT OF 3
"The other animal will get poked by the porcupines quills."
The quills belong to the porcupine. When something belongs to someone or something, you need an apostrophe: "porcupine's quills."
The Possessive Rule
MISSING the porcupines quills (looks like plural, not possessive)
CORRECT the porcupine's quills (apostrophe shows the quills belong to it)
HOW TO DECIDE: PLURAL OR POSSESSIVE?
Ask yourself: Does the next word belong to this thing?
Plural (more than one): "I saw three porcupines." — no apostrophe needed.
Possessive (belongs to): "The porcupine's quills are sharp." — apostrophe before the s.
Which sentence uses the apostrophe correctly?
A The porcupines tail is covered in sharp quills.
B The porcupine's tail is covered in sharp quills.
YOUR REREAD CHECK
Whenever you write a noun followed by another noun, pause and ask: does the second noun belong to the first? If yes, add 's to the first noun. "The dog bone" becomes "the dog's bone." "The porcupines quills" becomes "the porcupine's quills."
