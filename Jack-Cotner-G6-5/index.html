Picking the Right Logical Connector

All 3 of your multiple-choice misses were this ONE type of question

Jack, here is a remarkable fact. Across 5 tests you missed 3 multiple-choice questions out of 50. All 3 were about picking the right word to connect two ideas — words like because, so, and, unless. Let us walk through each one.

⚑ The pattern: Each time, you picked a connector that was close but not quite right for the relationship between the two ideas. The cure is to ask one question first: "What relationship do these two ideas actually have?"
From G6.2 Q3
Which sentence combines these ideas with a logical conjunction? Sentence 1: "Animated movies are fun." Sentence 2: "They also include valuable lessons."
Your pick: Animated movies are fun because they include valuable lessons.
Correct: Animated movies are fun and they include valuable lessons.
Why: The word "also" in sentence 2 is the clue. The relationship is ADDITION (fun + valuable), not cause/effect. "Because" would mean the lessons CAUSE the fun — that is not what the sentences say. The right connector for "also" is and.
From G6.3 Q3
Choose the sentence that correctly uses "because."
Your pick: Carter planted milkweed, because it attracts monarch butterflies.
Correct: Carter planted milkweed because it attracts monarch butterflies.
Why: When "because" joins two halves of a sentence in the middle (not at the start), no comma before it. "Because" itself is the connector — it does the joining by itself. A comma would only be correct if the "because" clause came first: "Because it attracts monarch butterflies, Carter planted milkweed."
From G6.3 Q9
Which transition best connects these two sentences? Sentence 1: "Fewer butterflies are surviving." Sentence 2: "People are taking action to protect them."
Your pick: Unless fewer butterflies are surviving, people are taking action to protect them.
Correct: Fewer butterflies are surviving, so people are taking action to protect them. (or: "therefore")
Why: "Unless" means only if NOT — it is a condition. But the relationship here is CAUSE → RESPONSE: the butterfly problem is causing the action. The right connector is so or therefore.

YOUR G6.1 ESSAY, LEVELED UP TO 0.90

Wilma Rudolph overcame huge obstacles to become an Olympic champion. As a child, she had polio and had to wear a leg brace for years, which made it hard for her to walk. This shows that Wilma's path to athletics started from the hardest possible place — she could not even walk normally, let alone run. Later, she trained with determination and won three gold medals at the 1960 Olympics. This means her determination was not optional; it was the only way someone with her starting point could reach the Olympics at all. Wilma's story shows the power of hard work.
