Question 3 Fix — Writing Appositives

Let's review this together.

You scored 0/2 on this question.

The question asked you to rewrite a sentence by adding an appositive — a short phrase set off by commas that gives extra information about a noun.

Original: "Sally swam to the other seals."

Which sentence correctly adds an appositive?
A Sally swan to the seals.
B Sally, a tiny gray seal pup, swam to the other seals.
YOU WROTE
"Sally swan to the seals."

There are two issues here: the sentence doesn't include an appositive, and "swan" should be spelled "swam".

HOW TO WRITE AN APPOSITIVE
An appositive is a short phrase right after a noun that tells us more about it. It is always set off by commas:
• Step 1: Pick the noun you want to describe (Sally).
• Step 2: Think of a short phrase that identifies or describes that noun (a tiny gray seal pup).
• Step 3: Put it right after the noun with commas on both sides: Sally, a tiny gray seal pup, swam to the other seals.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Appositive questions appear on every writing test and are worth 2 points each. Learning this one skill means 2 more points every time.

Question 5 Fix — Connecting Words

Let's review this together.

You scored 0/2 on this question.

The question asked:

"Write the best word to complete the sentence below: ___ there was no sign of the seals, the family placed Sally in a fish box and motored into the bay."

Which connecting word best completes this sentence?
A Sad there was no sign of the seals, the family placed Sally in a fish box and motored into the bay.
B Since there was no sign of the seals, the family placed Sally in a fish box and motored into the bay.
YOU WROTE
"Sad"

The word "Sad" is a feeling word, not a connecting word. The sentence needs a word that connects two ideas together and shows their relationship.

PICKING THE RIGHT CONNECTING WORD
Ask yourself what relationship the ideas have:
• Because / Since = explains WHY something happened
• Although / Even though = shows something unexpected
• When / After / Before = tells WHEN something happened
• If = shows a condition
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Connecting words are worth 2 points on this test. The key is to ask yourself: "What is the relationship between these two ideas?" That question will guide you to the right word every time.

THE THREE-PART ANSWER
For any question that asks for your opinion or explanation:
• Part 1: Restate the question in your answer.
• Part 2: State your answer or opinion clearly.
• Part 3: Explain WHY using "because" — this is where the points are.

Question 11 — Your Paragraph

From Collecting to Designing: The Story of Pokémon — 14/20 points
Which central idea sentence is stronger for a paragraph about Pokémon?
A Pokémon is very popular and you can trade and play with them.
B Satoshi Tajiri turned his childhood love of catching bugs into one of the most popular video games ever made.
Needs Work: 14/20 No clear central idea sentence Run-on sentence

Ansel, you had some good ideas in your paragraph about Pokémon. Let's look at what worked and what to fix.

TOPIC (missing — no clear central idea) Your paragraph jumps into details without first telling the reader what the main point is.

DETAILS You included information about Pokémon — good! But some sentences run together without proper punctuation.

SPELLING You wrote remebered — the correct spelling is remembered.
WHAT YOU DID WELL
You picked a topic you know about and tried to include details. That shows you understand the basic idea of writing a paragraph with supporting information.
THREE THINGS TO IMPROVE
1. Start with a central idea sentence. Tell the reader what the whole paragraph is about in your very first sentence. Example: "Satoshi Tajiri turned his love of catching bugs into one of the world's most popular video games."

2. Fix the run-on sentence. When two complete ideas are in the same sentence, separate them with a period or connect them with a word like "and," "but," or "so."

3. Check your spelling. Read your paragraph slowly one more time before turning it in. Words like "remembered" are easy to catch on a second look.
