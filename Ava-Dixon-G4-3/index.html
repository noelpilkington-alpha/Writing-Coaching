#2 Fix: An Appositive Is a Name, Not a Clause

Q3 — a good sentence that used the wrong tool, plus one point on Q8

Question 3 asked you to rewrite “He asked his mom for some lettuce” with an appositive that gives more information about Noah’s mom.

Q3 — 1 OUT OF 2
“He asked his mom, who loves vegetables, for some lettuce.”

This is a perfectly good sentence, and it does tell us something about the mom — which is why it kept a point. But “who loves vegetables” is a clause: it has its own verb, loves. An appositive is a naming phrase — a noun that renames the person, with no verb of its own.

THE APPOSITIVE VERSION
“He asked his mom, a vegetable lover, for some lettuce.”
Same information, but now it is a name for her — “a vegetable lover” — not a sentence about her. No verb inside the commas.
THE CHECK
Look inside the commas. If there is a verb in there, it is a clause, not an appositive. Swap “who loves vegetables” for “a vegetable lover,” “who gardens” for “the family gardener.” The test always wants the noun version when it says appositive.

And the other point, Q8. The task was to describe one way a plant or animal changes as it grows. You wrote: “One way a plant or animal changes as it grows is getting bigger.” True of everything alive — which is the problem. It describes nothing in particular. Pick one real change and show it: “A tadpole loses its tail and grows legs as it turns into a frog.” Now the reader can picture it. Your Q9 already does this perfectly; Q8 just needed the same specificity.

The task: add an appositive about Noah’s mom. Which sentence uses one?

YOUR PARAGRAPH, DEVELOPED

“Yacouba’s ideas and character helped him find a way to grow plants in dry land. First, he had the idea to dig zai holes, small pits that trap the rain, after learning from people who had planted trees that way. The article explains that the holes hold water in the ground, so seeds can grow where the land used to be too dry. Also, his character helped him keep going when nobody believed in him. When someone shouted, “Do you really think those seeds will grow without water?” he planted anyway, and that determination is what let him stay hopeful about his idea. Overall, a smart method and the stubbornness to keep using it are how Yacouba turned dry land green.”

Your frame is completely intact — same first sentence, same First, same Also, same Overall. Two facts from the article went into the two middle slots, each followed by what it shows. And the conclusion now says something the opening did not, because there was finally something to conclude.
