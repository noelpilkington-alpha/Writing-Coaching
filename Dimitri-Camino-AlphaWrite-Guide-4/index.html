Apostrophes

Missing letter, or owner. Nothing else
THE RULE
An apostrophe marks a missing letter or an owner. can’t, wasn’t, it’s: a letter is missing. Sam’s bike: Sam owns it. A plural noun, more than one of something, gets none: goldendoodles, knives. The check: can you say the missing letters back (can not, it is)? Apostrophe. Does one thing own the next? Apostrophe. Is it a plural noun, more than one of something, with nothing owned? None. The owner words its, his, hers never take one.

Capitals and Spaces

Two small rules the read-back catches in a second
THE RULES
Names, months and titles get a capital. Alexander, Batman, August, the Odyssey. The check: is it the name of one particular person, place, month or thing? Capital.
A comma hugs the word before it, then one space. No space before a comma, one space after.
