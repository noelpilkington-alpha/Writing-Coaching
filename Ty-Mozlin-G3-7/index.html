Questions 8 & 10 — Explain WHY

Two sentence-writing questions where your answer stopped one step early

You scored 2/3 on Q8 and 2/3 on Q10 — 2 points off on its own.

💡
The recurring pattern: when a question asks you to explain why, you give the what but stop before the because. This also cost you on G3.4 Q6 and G3.5 Q10.

Question 8: "Write one sentence about something that changes as the pumpkin grows and explain why it changes."

YOU WROTE
"One thing is that pumpkins turn from green to orange."

This tells us what changes (green to orange) but not why it changes. The prompt asked for both.

Tap to see the 3-point version
What + because + why
FULL-CREDIT VERSION
"Pumpkins turn from green to orange because they are ripening in the sun and the orange color shows they are ready to be picked."

Question 10: "Write one sentence explaining why someone might save pumpkin seeds to plant in spring."

YOU WROTE
"Because the person may want to grow more pumpkins."

This is a sentence fragment — it starts with "Because" but never gives a complete sentence. Fragments lose points even when the idea is right.

Which answer gets full credit on Q10?
A Because the person may want to grow more pumpkins.
B Someone might save pumpkin seeds to plant in spring because the seeds will grow into new pumpkin plants that make food for their family.
THE 3-POINT SENTENCE FORMULA
When a question says "explain why":
  1. Restate the prompt as a full sentence ("Pumpkins turn from green to orange...")
  2. Add "because" or "so that" or "in order to"
  3. Give the real reason (what from the passage explains it)
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Every Q6–Q10 question is worth 1 point on this test. Two "explain why" questions that slip from 0.67 to 1.0 adds 0.66 points, which pushes 88% to 90%.
