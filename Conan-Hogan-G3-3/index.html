Error #1: Fix Comma Splices

Step 5 catch: two sentences joined by only a comma

Conan, on Q4 you joined two complete sentences with just a comma. That is called a comma splice, and it costs points.

Q4 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2 weighted points)
"The family ran to the beach, found a seal lying on the rocks."

The problem: "The family ran to the beach" is a complete sentence. "Found a seal lying on the rocks" is another action. A comma alone cannot connect them properly.

Two ways to fix a comma splice:

  • Use "and": "The family ran to the beach and found a seal lying on the rocks."
  • Use a period: "The family ran to the beach. They found a seal lying on the rocks."
THE COMMA SPLICE TEST
Read the words before the comma. Are they a complete sentence? Now read the words after the comma. Are they also a complete sentence?

If both sides are complete sentences, you cannot join them with just a comma. Use a period, or add "and" / "but" / "because" after the comma.
Which sentence correctly combines these two ideas?
A"Sally was cold, she needed help."
B"Sally was cold, so she needed help."
WHY THIS MATTERS
Comma splices appear on almost every test. Q4 is worth 2 points, and you lost 1 point here. The fix is always the same: add a connecting word or use a period.

Error #2: Complete Your Sentences

Step 5 catch: a sentence fragment is not a full thought

Conan, on Q7 you wrote a fragment. A fragment is a group of words that looks like a sentence but is missing something (usually the main action).

Q7 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 3 weighted points)
"When the family helped Sally."

The problem: This starts with "When," which makes it a dependent clause. It sets up an idea but never finishes it. A reader asks: "When the family helped Sally... what happened?"

COMPLETE SENTENCE
"When the family helped Sally, they showed that ordinary people can make a difference for animals in need."
THE FRAGMENT TEST
Read your sentence out loud. Does it answer: Who did what?

• "When the family helped Sally." → Who did what? We do not know! Fragment.
• "When the family helped Sally, they showed compassion." → Who? They. Did what? Showed compassion. Complete!

Words that start fragments: When, Because, If, Although, While, Since, After. If your sentence starts with one of these, make sure the second half finishes the thought.
Which is a complete sentence?
A"Because Sally was alone on the beach."
B"Because Sally was alone on the beach, the family decided to help her."
WHY THIS MATTERS
Q7 is worth 3 weighted points and you earned only 1. The fragment meant the grading feedback could not give full credit because your answer did not express a complete idea. Finishing the sentence would have earned 2 or 3 points.

Error #3: "Would Have" and Cause Connectors

Q5 connector + Q9 grammar fix

Conan, two more patterns cost you points on this test:

Q9 — YOUR ANSWER (2 out of 3 weighted points)
"I would of helped Sally because she needed someone."

The fix: "Would of" is never correct. The correct phrase is "would have". It sounds like "would of" when spoken quickly, but in writing it is always "would have" (or "would've").

Q5 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2 weighted points)
"Later, the family called the rescue center."

The fix: The question asked for a cause-and-effect connector (WHY). "Later" shows time order (WHEN). The correct connector is "Since" or "Because."

CORRECTED VERSIONS
Q9: "I would have helped Sally because she needed someone."
Q5: "Since Sally was injured, the family called the rescue center."
TWO QUICK RULES
1. Never write "would of." It is always "would have," "could have," "should have."

2. Time vs. Cause connectors:
• WHEN something happened: Later, Then, After, Next
• WHY something happened: Since, Because, So, Therefore

Ask yourself: "Is the question asking WHEN or WHY?"
Which sentence is correct?
A"The family could of called earlier."
B"The family could have called earlier."
WHY THIS MATTERS
These two fixes together are worth 2 points. "Would have" is a rule you can memorize today. The connector rule (time vs. cause) applies to every test.
