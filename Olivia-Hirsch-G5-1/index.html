THE "ONE IDEA" RULE
Before you write a comma or the word "and" in the middle of a sentence, ask: "Am I adding a new idea?" If yes, put a period and start a new sentence.

✓ "I broke the machine. I was nervous. I told the truth." ← one idea per sentence

Short sentences are never wrong. Long sentences are risky. When in doubt, use a period.

Step 5: "Broken" not "Broke"

G5.1 Q6 — 1 out of 3 · G5.1 Q10 — 2 out of 3
🔍
Recurring error: On both Q6 and Q10, you wrote "broke" where the correct word is "broken." This is the past participle — the form you use after "had," "have," or "accidentally." It appeared twice on this test, so it is a pattern worth fixing.
G5.1 Q6 — PAST PARTICIPLE ERROR
You wrote: "I had accidentally broke my teacher's machine..."
After "had," the correct form is "broken." Think of it this way: "I broke it" (simple past) vs. "I had broken it" (past participle with helper word).
G5.1 Q10 — SAME ERROR AGAIN
You wrote: "If I had accidentally broke something that belonged to an adult..."
Same pattern — "had" + "broke" should be "had" + "broken."
THE RULE: "HAD" OR "HAVE" = USE "BROKEN"
✓ "I had broken my teacher's machine."
✓ "If I had accidentally broken something..."
✓ "I have broken things before."

✗ "I had broke" ← never correct after "had"

Simple past (no helper word): "I broke the vase." ← this is fine.
With a helper word (had/have/was): "I had broken the vase." ← use "broken."
Which sentence uses the correct form?
A She realized she had broken the window by accident.
B She realized she had broke the window by accident.
THE HELPER-WORD TEST
When proofreading, look for "had," "have," or "was" before a verb. If you see one of those words followed by "broke," change it to "broken."

had + broke ➜ had broken
have + broke ➜ have broken
was + broke ➜ was broken

This applies to other verbs too: "had went" should be "had gone," "had ate" should be "had eaten." The pattern is the same — helper word + past participle.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
This error appeared on two separate questions on G5.1. Each time, it cost partial credit. Fixing this one grammar pattern across your test could recover 1–2 points with no extra thinking — just a proofread scan for "had + broke."
