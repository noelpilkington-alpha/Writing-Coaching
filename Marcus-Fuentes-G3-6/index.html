THE RULE
"Because" is a subordinating conjunction — a word that JOINS the two parts of a sentence. Never put a comma right after it.

✗ "Dogs bite because, they are nervous."
✓ "Dogs bite because they are nervous."
WHEN DOES "BECAUSE" GET A COMMA NEARBY?
Only when "because" starts the sentence. Then the comma goes at the END of the because-clause, not after "because" itself:

✓ "Because they are nervous, dogs bite."
✗ "Because, they are nervous dogs bite."

Rule of thumb: Never put a comma right after the word "because." Period.
Pick the correctly punctuated sentence.
A The fans cheered because, their team won.
B The fans cheered because their team won.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
You did this 10 times across 6 tests. Fixing it is a one-word deletion — on your proofread pass, just search your answer for the string "because," and delete every comma. That's it. Minutes of effort, multiple points per test.

THE RULE FOR REWRITES
Change the STRUCTURE, not the meaning.

1. Start with a question word: Does, Did, Is, Are, Can, Why, How, Were, Was.
2. Keep all the original content. Don't add or remove any information.
3. Keep proper nouns capitalized (Anna stays Anna, Sally stays Sally).
4. End with a ? every time. An interrogative MUST end with a question mark.
CORRECT REWRITES FOR YOUR 4 PROMPTS
• "Anna named the seal Sally." → "Did Anna name the seal Sally?"
• "The geode felt like a perfect souvenir." → "Did the geode feel like a perfect souvenir?"
• "The books were sold in department stores." → "Were the books sold in department stores?"
• "Quills protect porcupines from danger." → "Do quills protect porcupines from danger?"
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
You've scored 50% on Question 3 every test. That's the easiest 1 full point per test to reclaim. Four rules, a few seconds of checking. Do not leave this on the table.

MODEL POLISHED PARAGRAPH (19–20/20 level)

The porcupine, one of the most interesting animals on the planet, can protect itself in many ways! For example, its quills can puncture a predator if the predator ignores the porcupine's warning. This shows that the porcupine's body is built for defense — it doesn't even need to run away. Moreover, the porcupine's tail helps alert predators to stay away. This gives the predator a chance to back off before getting hurt — a warning, not a surprise. Because of these natural defenses, the porcupine can survive in the forest without being fast or strong.

The 5-Part Paragraph (Your Next Level)
TOPIC State your central idea (you already do this!)
DETAIL 1 Specific detail from the passage
SHOWS "This shows..." — explain what detail 1 means
DETAIL 2 A second specific detail from the passage
SHOWS "This shows..." — explain what detail 2 means
CLOSING Connect back to your central idea
