Question 9 Fix — Complete Sentences

Let's review this together.

You scored 1.0/3 on this question — you lost 2.0 points.

The question asked:

"Why does the Downwind family work together before each powwow? Write one sentence that explains the reason."

Which answer is a complete sentence that uses passage details?
A Because they need to tell each other something.
B The Downwind family works together before each powwow because they need to prepare their traditional outfits, such as rolling metal cones for the jingle dress.
YOU WROTE
"Because they need to tell eachother somthing."

Luke, three problems here:

  • Fragment: Starting with "Because" and stopping creates an incomplete sentence. It leaves the reader waiting for the rest.
  • Not from the passage: The passage does not say they need to "tell each other something." The real reason is they prepare their outfits together.
  • Spelling: eachother → each other, somthing → something
STRONGER VERSION
"The Downwind family works together before each powwow because they need to prepare their traditional outfits, such as rolling metal cones for the jingle dress."
HOW TO WRITE A COMPLETE ANSWER
Step 1: Restate the question in your answer ("The Downwind family works together because...").
Step 2: Give a reason from the passage — not your own guess.
Step 3: Make sure it is a complete sentence (subject + verb + complete thought).
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?

Question 5 Fix — Changing a Statement into a Question

Let's review this together.

You scored 1.0/2 on this question — you lost 1.0 point.

The question asked:

"Rewrite the following sentence as a question: The shawl dance began when shawls replaced the blankets young women once wore."

Which question keeps the original meaning?
A When did Shawl begin to dance?
B Did the shawl dance begin when shawls replaced the blankets young women once wore?
YOU WROTE
"When did Shawl begin to dance?"

Luke, you turned "shawl dance" into a person named "Shawl." That changes the meaning completely. The sentence is about the shawl dance (a type of dance), not a person named Shawl.

STRONGER VERSIONS

"Did the shawl dance begin when shawls replaced the blankets young women once wore?"

"When did the shawl dance begin?"

TURNING A STATEMENT INTO A QUESTION
Step 1: Read the original sentence carefully. Identify the subject and the key information.
Step 2: Put a question word at the start (Did, When, How, Why) or flip the subject and verb.
Step 3: Make sure your question is about the same thing as the original sentence.
Step 4: End with a question mark.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
When you change the meaning of the sentence, you lose credit even if you wrote a grammatically correct question. Always check: does my question match what the original sentence was saying?
