Error: Wrong Conjunction

Step 2 skill: identifying the correct logical relationship

Lincoln, Q5 asked you to identify the correct conjunction to connect two ideas, and you chose the wrong one. This is about identifying the logical relationship between the ideas.

CONJUNCTION RELATIONSHIPS
• Addition: and, also, furthermore (adding similar ideas)
• Contrast: but, however, although (opposing ideas)
• Cause/Effect: because, since, therefore, so (one causes the other)
• Condition: if, unless, provided that (one depends on the other)

Before choosing a conjunction, ask: "What is the relationship between these two ideas? Are they similar, opposite, or does one cause the other?"
"The new bridge would reduce commute times _____ it would also bring more traffic to local streets." Which conjunction fits?
A"because" — showing that reduced commute causes more traffic
B"but" — showing the contrast between a benefit and a drawback
THE FIX
Before selecting a conjunction, label the relationship: addition, contrast, cause/effect, or condition. Then pick a conjunction that matches that label. On this test, the two ideas had opposite outcomes (benefit vs. drawback), so you needed a contrast conjunction.

THE THREE-LAYER BODY PARAGRAPH
1. Claim: One sentence stating what this paragraph proves.
2. Evidence: 2-3 sentences with specific details from the passage.
3. Explanation: 1-2 sentences explaining what the evidence proves and how it supports your thesis.

You consistently do layers 1 and 2. Layer 3 is the unlock that moves you from 15 to 18+.

Connect Evidence to Thesis

The final sentence of each body paragraph should loop back to your central argument

Lincoln, at the G8 level, high-scoring essays do not just present evidence. They build an argument. Every body paragraph should end with a sentence that explicitly connects back to the thesis.

THE LOOP-BACK TECHNIQUE
After your evidence + explanation, add:
• "This demonstrates [thesis claim] because..."
• "Without this [benefit/factor], [thesis] would not hold because..."
• "This evidence reinforces the argument that [restate thesis in different words]..."

This transforms your essay from a list of points into a cohesive argument.
