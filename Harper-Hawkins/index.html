What's an Appositive?

Deep dive on Q3 — the comma-description-comma pattern

An appositive is a short description tucked inside a sentence, set off by commas. Here's the pattern:

THE APPOSITIVE PATTERN
Name, [short description], rest of sentence.

Think of it like a whisper: "Porcupines — oh, by the way, they're the slow movers of the forest — are rodents."

Your Q3 example:

Original sentences
Porcupines are rodents. They are the slow movers of the forest.
Tap to see the improved version
Read the explanation above first
WITH AN APPOSITIVE
Porcupines, the slow movers of the forest, are rodents.

More examples from the passage:

  • Quills, sharp spines covering the porcupine's body, protect it from predators.
  • The porcupine, a shy rodent, lives mostly in trees.
HOW TO SPOT IT
Look for the comma, description, comma pattern. That's always the appositive. When you see a question that says "add an appositive," just tuck a short description between two commas right after the noun.
