Your 6-Step Test Process

The same steps that earned you 98% on your best test

Ty, the best tests you have ever taken followed the same rhythm. Use these six steps every time — Step 5 is the one that catches almost every point in this whole review.

1
Read the passage carefully. Re-read it in the panel above before you start. Know what it is really saying.
2
Answer Questions 1–10. For each one, write a complete sentence — and on Questions 6–10, make sure your sentence includes a reason or example.
3
Plan your paragraph. Type a quick outline at the top of the answer box: your main idea, two details, and a closing. Then write below it.
4
Write the paragraph. Use your outline. Add a "this shows..." sentence after a detail to explain why it matters, then delete the outline before you submit.
5
Proofread — spelling sweep. Read every answer slowly. Any word that looks strange (like facatory or throns) is probably misspelled. Sound it out and fix it. This is the step that wins back the most points.
6
Check end marks and capitals. Every sentence ends with a period, question mark, or exclamation point, and starts with a capital letter.
WHY THIS WORKS FOR YOU
Your ideas already earn most of the points. Steps 5 and 6 are where the last few points live. Give the test the time it needs — don't race, don't pad — and save a few minutes at the end for the spelling sweep.

One complete sentence that answers AND gives a reason
💡 Pattern alert: On Q6 and Q10 your sentences were correct but short. A single sentence that adds a reason or example earns the full 3 points.

FROM G4.2 Q6 (earned 2 of 3 points)

Write one complete sentence that does two jobs at once:

1. Answer the question.
2. Add a reason or example with a joining word like because, so, or since.

✓ "During fall, I like going trick-or-treating on Halloween because I love dressing up and getting candy with my friends."
