ELABORATION RULE
Good elaboration always answers "how?" or "why?" with a specific detail. If a sentence just makes a general claim without explaining anything, it's not elaboration — it's just another opinion. Look for the word "because" or a concrete detail from the passage.

Differentiate Your Body Paragraphs

P2 and P4 overlap too much

Your thesis promises three distinct points: history, connecting islanders, and culture. But look at what P2 and P4 actually cover:

Paragraph Topic Sentence What It Actually Explains
P2 El Silbo's ties to the island's history How islanders keep a tradition alive → preservation of heritage
P4 El Silbo connects people to their culture How a cultural practice is valued and appreciated → preservation of heritage

"History" and "culture" are too similar when applied to the same tradition. Both paragraphs end up explaining that El Silbo is a valued heritage practice — just with different quotes.

HOW TO FIX THIS
Replace one of the overlapping topics with a genuinely different angle. For example:

• P2: History — how El Silbo has survived for centuries and is now taught in schools (preservation over time)
• P3: Practical connection — how whistling across ravines lets people communicate in daily life (physical/practical use)
• P4: Cultural identity — how learning El Silbo gives islanders pride and a sense of shared belonging (emotional/identity)

Each paragraph now covers a different type of connection — historical, practical, emotional — so no two paragraphs say the same thing.

All three now use the -ing form: keeping, connecting, helping.

The Parallel Structure Rule
WRONG "I like swimming, running, and to bike."
RIGHT "I like swimming, running, and biking."
WRONG "She reads quickly, writes clearly, and speaking well."
RIGHT "She reads quickly, writes clearly, and speaks well."
QUICK CHECK
Whenever you write a list of three or more items, read each item on its own after the lead-in word. If one doesn't fit the same pattern, fix it. "by keeping... by connecting... by helps" — that last one clearly doesn't work.

The Body Paragraph Pattern

How to build a strong body paragraph every time

Every body paragraph in a G7 essay should follow this pattern:

Body Paragraph Formula
TS Topic Sentence — states the paragraph's main point
E1 Evidence 1 — a specific fact, quote, or example from the passage
X1 Explanation 1 — explain why that evidence matters
E2 Evidence 2 — a second fact or example from the passage
X2 Explanation 2 — explain why that evidence matters
CS Concluding Sentence — wraps up the paragraph's main idea

Here's what a strong BP about El Silbo's practical value looks like with this pattern:

EXAMPLE BODY PARAGRAPH
[TS] El Silbo connected islanders to one another by serving as a practical communication tool across La Gomera's difficult terrain. [E1] The passage explains that whistling saved time because people did not have to walk around the deep ravines to deliver messages. [X1] This shows that El Silbo was not just a tradition — it was a necessity that solved a real problem the island's geography created. [E2] According to the passage, El Silbo can be heard for miles, allowing people to share important information quickly from peak to peak. [X2] As a result, the language created a network of communication that kept the entire community connected, even when they were physically separated by valleys. [CS] By making communication possible across difficult terrain, El Silbo became an essential bond between the people of La Gomera.

Notice how this is clearly about practical communication — not history or culture. Each body paragraph needs its own distinct angle so they don't overlap.

THE KEY RULE
Every E (evidence) must be followed by an X (explanation). Evidence without explanation is just a list of facts. The explanation is where you show the reader why that evidence supports your point. Ask yourself: "So what? Why does this matter?"
