THE FOUR CAPITALIZATION RULES
1. Names of people: Layla, Cooper, Jim

Spelling — Five Words to Learn

Questions 1, 8, 10, and 11

Which group has all the words spelled correctly?
A agressively, insted, visting, industrain, Facotory
B aggressively, instead, visiting, industrial, factory

Here is each word and what went wrong:

agressively → aggressively (double g)
insted → instead (missing "ea")
visting → visiting (missing "i")
industrain → industrial (ends in "-ial" not "-ain")
Facotory → Factory (extra "o" removed)
SPELLING STRATEGY: SAY IT SLOWLY
When you are not sure how to spell a word, say it out loud slowly and listen to each sound:
• ag-gres-sive-ly — you can hear the double "g" if you slow down
• in-stead — you can hear the "ea" sound
• vis-it-ing — it comes from "visit" + "ing"

Another trick: if the word comes from a shorter word you know, build from that. "Visit" becomes "visit-ing." "Factory" comes from "fact" + "ory."
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Spelling errors cost you points on Question 1 (where "aggressively" was misspelled) and make your essay harder to read. On grading feedback, clean spelling shows that you took time to check your work. A quick re-read at the end of the test can catch most of these.

WHAT YOU WROTE (QUESTION 3)

CORRECTED

You wrote a great question! The words are perfect. You just forgot the question mark at the end. That one small symbol cost you a full point.

THE QUESTION MARK RULE
If a sentence starts with a question word (Did, Does, Is, Was, Can, Will, What, Why, How), it must end with a question mark.

Quick check: Read your sentence out loud. Does your voice go up at the end? If yes, it needs a ? not a .

Question 11 (your essay): You had several run-on sentences — places where two complete ideas were joined without proper punctuation.

RUN-ON EXAMPLE FROM YOUR ESSAY

FIXED

When two complete ideas are jammed together without a period or connecting word, the reader cannot tell where one thought ends and the next begins.

Question 1 Fix — Stronger Kernel Expansion

Adding details that paint a picture

You scored 1/2 on this question — you lost 1 point.

The question asked you to expand this kernel sentence by adding details: "Layla looked at the flower."

Which expansion adds more meaningful detail?
A Layla looked at the flower aggressively.
B Along the trail, Layla knelt down and looked closely at a bright purple wildflower.
WHAT YOU WROTE
"Layla looked at the flower agressively."

Two problems here: First, "aggressively" does not really make sense with looking at a flower — it is a strange detail. Second, adding just one word is not enough for a strong expansion. The question is looking for details that help the reader picture the scene.

STRONGER EXPANSIONS
• "Along the trail, Layla knelt down and looked closely at a bright purple wildflower."
• "Layla stopped walking and carefully looked at the tiny flower growing between the rocks."
• "During the nature walk, Layla bent down to look at a beautiful yellow flower near the stream."
HOW TO EXPAND A KERNEL SENTENCE
Ask yourself these questions about the kernel sentence:
• Where? (along the trail, near the stream, in the field)
• When? (during the walk, after finding the geode, in the afternoon)
• How? (carefully, closely, with excitement)
• What kind? (bright purple wildflower, tiny flower, beautiful yellow flower)

A good expansion answers at least two of these questions.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Kernel expansion is worth 2 points, and you only got 1. The difference between 1 and 2 points is adding details that make sense and help the reader see what is happening. Think of it as painting a picture with words instead of just tacking on one extra word.
