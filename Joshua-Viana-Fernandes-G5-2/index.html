THE "DOES THIS LOOK RIGHT?" CHECK
After you write an answer, scan for any word that looks unusual. If you are not sure about a spelling:

1. Sound it out slowly: "ec-sta-tic" has a "c" before the "s." "Busi-ness" goes b-u-s-i-n-e-s-s.
2. Try the word a different way in your head. If "shiney" looks odd, try "shiny." Which one looks more natural?
3. When adding "-y" to a word ending in "e," drop the "e": shine → shiny, noise → noisy.

Your #2 Target: Fix Run-on Sentences

Q6 — two complete thoughts need a period or conjunction
Q6 — YOUR ANSWER (2 out of 3)
"The author uses logos to show how they can connect to people and make them feel something a good logo tells a story without using words."

The problem: This is a run-on sentence. Two complete thoughts are running together without any punctuation between them:

  • Thought 1: "The author uses logos to show how they can connect to people and make them feel something."
  • Thought 2: "A good logo tells a story without using words."

They need a period between them, or a conjunction like "because" to connect them.

TWO CLEAN FIXES
Period: "The author uses logos to show how they can connect to people and make them feel something. A good logo tells a story without using words."

Conjunction: "The author uses logos to show how they can connect to people and make them feel something, because a good logo tells a story without using words."
Which version fixes the run-on from Q6?
A The author uses logos to show how they connect to people and make them feel something a good logo can tell a story without words.
B The author uses logos to show how they connect to people and make them feel something. A good logo tells a story without using words.
THE RUN-ON CHECK
After you write a long sentence, ask: "Does this sentence have two complete thoughts?"

A complete thought has its own subject and verb and makes sense on its own. If you find two of them running together, add a period or a connecting word (and, but, so, because).

Run-on: "...make them feel something a good logo tells a story..."
Fixed: "...make them feel something. A good logo tells a story..."

POLISHED VERSION (19–20 out of 20 level)

A good logo helps a business stand out from others. According to the article "What Can We See in a Logo?" logos use colors, shapes, and symbols to communicate ideas quickly. For example, a fast food logo might use bright red and yellow because those colors make people feel hungry and excited. This shows that logos are designed carefully to create a specific feeling in customers. In contrast, a nature brand might use green and brown to suggest health and the outdoors, which proves that different industries choose different visual messages. Companies spend a lot of time choosing the right logo because it is often the first thing customers notice, and first impressions can decide whether someone buys or walks away.
