WHY THIS HAPPENS — AND HOW TO STOP IT
When a prompt feels hard, your brain wants to write about something easier. That’s normal! But on the test, writing about the easier topic = ZERO points.

The 3-question Prompt-Read:
1. What is the topic of this paragraph? (One specific thing.)
2. What does the prompt want me to prove or explain about that topic?
3. What 2 facts from the passage can I use?

Answer those THREE questions before writing a single word.
Prompt: "Explain the lesson we learn from Ferdinand Cheval’s story." Which opening stays on-prompt?
A My favorite sport is soccer and I play it every weekend.
B Ferdinand Cheval teaches us that patience and believing in yourself can build something amazing.
THE "FIRST-SENTENCE TEST"
Before you finish writing your paragraph, read ONLY your first sentence out loud in your head. Ask:

"Does this sentence name the SAME topic as the prompt?"

If yes: keep going. If no: your paragraph is off-prompt — fix the first sentence before doing anything else.

(“Because,” is a fragment — no main clause. Needs: “Because they can get water from the ground, they survive in the desert.”)
THE RULE — ONE CONNECTOR PER PAIR
Connecting words like because, so, and, but, however, therefore each do one job. Don’t stack them.

✗ "It rained, so because of the rain, we stayed inside." (two connectors)
✓ "It rained, so we stayed inside." (one connector)
✓ "Because it rained, we stayed inside." (one connector)
Which sentence uses only ONE connector (no run-on)?
A Because the seeds were dry, so he had to water them.
B Because the seeds were dry, he had to water them.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Run-ons and double connectors are partial-credit errors. You have had 3+ instances across your tests. On your proofread pass, look for "because" AND "so" in the same sentence — if you see both, delete one.

MODEL POLISHED PARAGRAPH (19–20/20)

The camel is built for the desert. For example, it has a hump that stores fat for energy when food is scarce. This means the camel can go weeks without eating, which is crucial in a place with so little food. In addition, the camel has thick eyelashes that protect its eyes from sand during storms. This shows that even small features of the camel’s body are adapted to desert life. That is why camels can live where most animals cannot.

Your G4.6 Formula — Run It Every Time
TOPIC Name the topic in one sentence (pull the exact words from the prompt)
FACT 1 "For example," + first fact from the passage
MEANS "This means..." — explain WHY the fact matters
FACT 2 "In addition," + second fact from the passage
SHOWS "This shows..." — explain the second fact
CLOSE Tie back to the topic
