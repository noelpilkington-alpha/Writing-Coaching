Period, Not Comma

A comma can't hold two complete sentences together
🔍 Pattern alert: On G4.3 this same error showed up on Questions 3, 7, and 9 — two complete sentences joined by just a comma. Same fix every time: use a period.

David, here are two real answers from your G4.3 test. Read them and find the problem first:

Which version is correct?
A Noah asked his mom for some lettuce, she looked confused.
B Noah asked his mom for some lettuce. She looked confused.
YOU WROTE (G4.3 Q3, earned 1 of 2 points)
"Noah asked his mom for some lettuce, she looked confused."
YOU WROTE (G4.3 Q9, earned 2 of 3 points)
"One day my friend asked me for some dog food, I was confused until he told me that it was for his dog."
THE RULE
Two complete sentences can NOT be joined by just a comma. You have two clean fixes:

1. Use a period and start a new sentence.
2. Add a joining word after the comma: and, but, so, because.

✗ "He asked me for dog food, I was confused."
✓ "He asked me for dog food. I was confused." — or —
✓ "He asked me for dog food, so I was confused."
WHAT Q3 ACTUALLY WANTED: AN APPOSITIVE
Q3 asked you to add an appositive — a few words that rename a noun, set off with commas, right next to it. An appositive does NOT add a second sentence; it gives more info about the same noun.

✓ "Noah asked his mom, a curious gardener, for some lettuce."
Here "a curious gardener" renames "mom." The commas wrap around the rename — they are not joining two sentences. That is the difference: an appositive renames a noun; a comma alone can't join two complete thoughts.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
This one habit cost you points on three questions on G4.3 alone. It is also what keeps a strong paragraph from scoring even higher. Fix the comma splices and you are knocking on the door of 90%.

MODEL POLISHED PARAGRAPH (19–20/20 level)
