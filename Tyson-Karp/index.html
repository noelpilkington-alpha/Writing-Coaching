SPELLING CHECK STRATEGY
After writing each answer, read it word by word — not sentence by sentence. When you read sentences, your brain auto-corrects errors. But when you point to each word individually, you'll catch typos like "trudgesd" and look-alikes like "exited" vs. "excited." Pay extra attention to words that look similar but mean different things.

Expand Brief Answers

Two issues: (1) "Trudges" should be "trudged" (past tense to match the story). (2) "He couldn't go camping" is correct but very brief. The question asks you to explain why Jaylen acted this way — you could add more about how he felt or what he was looking forward to.

THE "BECAUSE" EXPANSION RULE
When a question asks you to expand a sentence with "because," your explanation should be specific and detailed. Instead of "because he couldn't go camping" (5 words), add context: What was he looking forward to? How did it make him feel? Why was it important? Aim for at least 10-15 words after "because" to fully develop your explanation.

Sharpen Your Paragraph

Q11 — 18/20. Good structure, needs more specific evidence

Your paragraph scored 18/20. You have a clear topic sentence, good structure, and solid ideas. To reach 20/20, your evidence needs to be more specific.

YOUR PARAGRAPH
VR can be good for students in many ways. First, VR can let students travel to moments in time where they weren't even alive. It lets them get a different perspective on history. VR headsets may be expensive, but they are much better than field trips because field trips can cost a lot more money than VR headsets. Also, VR helps people see places without going there. For example someone in a wheelchair could climb a mountain. Finally, VR would be very good for students because it makes everything easier and better for everyone and helps students learn faster.

Your structure follows the plan well. Here's what to sharpen:

  • Your topic sentence is vague: "good for students in many ways" — name those ways.
  • Your last sentence is vague: "makes everything easier and better" — be specific about what gets easier.
  • Add quotes or specific details from the article to strengthen your evidence.
Paragraph Plan — TS → D1 → W → D2 → W → D3 → W → CS
TS = Topic Sentence • D = Detail from the article • W = Why it matters • CS = Concluding Sentence
TS Be specific: "VR field trips are a good idea for schools because they make learning exciting, save money, and help every student participate."
D1 VR lets students travel to moments in time. (Good!)
W It gives them a different perspective on history. (Good!)
D2 VR headsets are cheaper long-term than field trips. (Good point!)
W Add why: Schools can reuse headsets for many virtual trips instead of paying for buses each time.
D3 Someone in a wheelchair could climb a mountain. (Great specific example!)
W This shows VR makes learning accessible for students who can't go on physical trips.
CS Be specific: "For all these reasons, VR field trips are a smart investment that makes learning exciting and fair for every student."
THE KEY: REPLACE VAGUE WORDS WITH SPECIFICS
You already have the right structure and good ideas. The upgrade is replacing vague phrases like "good for students in many ways" and "makes everything easier and better" with specific details. Name the ways. Explain what gets easier. Quote the article when you can. Your wheelchair example is already excellent — do more of that!
