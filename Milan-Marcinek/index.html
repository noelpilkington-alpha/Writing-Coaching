Q3 Fix — Choosing the Right Conjunction

The relationship between ideas determines the conjunction

The question asked: "Which sentence combines the ideas using a logical conjunction?"

The two ideas: "Animated movies are fun." + "They also include valuable lessons."

These are two different ideas. Being fun and having lessons are not the same thing — lessons are an unexpected addition to something fun. You need a conjunction that shows contrast or surprise.

YOUR ANSWER
"Animated movies are fun because they include valuable lessons."

"Because" means the lessons cause the fun. But movies aren't fun because of their lessons — they're fun and they also happen to include lessons. That's a contrast.

Conjunction What It Means Example
because Cause → Effect (one thing causes the other) "I stayed inside because it was raining."
but Contrast / Unexpected addition "The movie is short but it tells a powerful story."
so Result (one thing leads to the other) "It was raining, so I stayed inside."
Tap to see the correct answer
Study the table above first
CORRECT ANSWER
"Animated movies are fun, but they also include valuable lessons."
THE RULE
Before choosing a conjunction, ask: What is the relationship?
• Does one idea cause the other? → because
• Does one idea contrast or add something unexpected? → but
• Does one idea result from the other? → so

The Body Paragraph Pattern

How to build a strong body paragraph every time

Every body paragraph in a G6+ essay should follow this pattern:

Body Paragraph Formula
TS Topic Sentence — states the paragraph's main point
E1 Evidence 1 — a specific fact, quote, or example from the passage
X1 Explanation 1 — explain why that evidence matters
E2 Evidence 2 — a second fact or example from the passage
X2 Explanation 2 — explain why that evidence matters
CS Concluding Sentence — wraps up the paragraph's main idea

EXAMPLE: BP ABOUT COURAGE
[TS] Animated movies help children learn about courage.
[E1] In Brave, Merida stands up for what she believes in, even when others disagree.
[X1] This shows kids that being brave means doing what's right, not what's easy.
[E2] Many animated characters face scary problems but find their courage and try again.
[X2] This inspires young viewers to keep trying, even when things get hard.
[CS] Through these stories, children learn that courage is about facing challenges, not avoiding them.
THE KEY RULE
Every E (evidence) must be followed by an X (explanation). Evidence without explanation is just a list of facts. The explanation is where you show the reader why that evidence supports your point. Ask yourself: "So what? Why does this matter?"
