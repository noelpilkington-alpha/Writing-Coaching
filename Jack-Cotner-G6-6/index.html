THE "PLUG IT IN" METHOD FOR CONNECTOR QUESTIONS
When you see a question with connector options, read the full sentence with each option plugged in — do not just scan. Ask one question: does the logic actually work?

because → the part after it gives a reason. ("He was tired because he ran a mile.")
so → the part after it is the result. ("He ran a mile, so he was tired.")
but → the part after it is a contrast. ("He ran a mile, but he was not tired.")
and → the part after it is an addition. ("He ran a mile and drank water.")
unless → the part after it is a condition. ("He will run, unless it rains.")
Pick the sentence that uses "because" correctly.
A The community planted trees because the river was cleaner.
B The community planted trees because the water was polluted.

YOUR G6.1 WILMA ESSAY, LEVELED UP TO 0.90+

Wilma Rudolph overcame huge obstacles to become an Olympic champion. As a child, she had polio and had to wear a leg brace for years, which made it hard for her to walk. This shows that Wilma's path to athletics started from the hardest possible place — she could not even walk normally, let alone run competitively. It was the only way someone with her starting point could reach the Olympics at all. Wilma's story proves the power of hard work and shows that starting conditions do not decide where a person finishes.
