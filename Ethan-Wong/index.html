Q4: What Is an Appositive?

Skill: Add an appositive to a sentence

The question asked you to add an appositive. An appositive is extra information set off by commas that renames or describes a noun.

YOU WROTE
In small towns all over Texas, many people attend bluebonnet festivals with friends and family.

You rearranged the sentence and added "with friends and family," but there's no appositive here. Nothing renames or gives extra detail about a noun between commas.

Tap to see the improved version
Read the explanation above first
WITH AN APPOSITIVE
Many people attend bluebonnet festivals, cheerful spring celebrations, in small towns all over Texas.

See how "cheerful spring celebrations" is tucked between commas? It renames "bluebonnet festivals" — that's the appositive!

THE PATTERN
Noun, extra detail that renames it, rest of sentence.

Examples:
• My dog, a golden retriever, loves to swim.
• Texas, the Lone Star State, has beautiful wildflowers.

Look for a noun you can say more about, then tuck the extra detail between two commas.
