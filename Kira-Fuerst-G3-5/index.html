Kira, the appositive question has cost you points two tests in a row. The twist: when asked to identify an appositive on Q5 of G3.5, you got it right perfectly. But when asked to write one yourself on Q4, the pattern slips into a comma splice. Here is what that looks like:

THE APPOSITIVE PATTERN (YOU ALREADY RECOGNIZE THIS!)
An appositive is a phrase that renames a noun, set off by commas. You identified this perfectly on G3.5 Q5:

The Poky Little Puppy, a best-selling story, became the most popular picture book of all time.

"A best-selling story" is the appositive — it renames The Poky Little Puppy. Now you just have to write one yourself.
THE APPOSITIVE RECIPE FOR Q4
When Q4 says "combine using an appositive":

1. Find the noun the second sentence describes. (Sentence 1: "The books are still popular." Sentence 2: "They are known for their golden spines.")
2. Pull just the renaming phrase from sentence 2. ("known for their golden spines")
3. Drop it into sentence 1 right after the noun, with commas:
    "The books, known for their golden spines, are still popular."
Which sentence correctly combines these using an appositive?
Sentence 1: My cat is very old. Sentence 2: My cat is named Whiskers.
A My cat is very old, my cat is named Whiskers.
B My cat, Whiskers, is very old.

Jason deCaires Taylor decided to place his sculptures under the sea for two main reasons. First, he knew that plants and animals would cover them, which would give sea life a new home. THE TOPIC-SENTENCE TRICK
When your topic sentence says "two reasons" or "three ways" or "many reasons," your brain has to keep writing until you have covered all of them. It is a built-in guardrail against stopping too early.
