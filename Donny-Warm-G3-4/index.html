Step 5: Catching Run-On Sentences

Q9 lost a point because two sentences were jammed together

Donny, on Q9 you wrote a sentence that was actually two sentences pushed together without proper punctuation. This is called a run-on. Here is what you wrote:

Q9 — YOUR ANSWER (2 out of 3)
Your answer ran two ideas together without separating them properly, and it was also too brief to earn full points.
HOW TO SPOT A RUN-ON
Read your sentence out loud in your head. If you hear two complete ideas with no pause word between them, you have a run-on.

The fix: Split them into two sentences with a period. Or connect them with "because," "and," "but," or "so."
Which sentence is a run-on?
A Layla found the geode she picked it up carefully.
B Layla found the geode and she picked it up carefully.
YOUR PROOFREAD CHECK
During Step 5 (proofread), read each sentence and ask: "Does this have two complete ideas with nothing connecting them?" If yes, add a period between them or use a connecting word (and, but, so, because).
