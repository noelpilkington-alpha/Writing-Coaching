Your #1 Win: Capital Letters for Names

This one fix could earn you real points on every test

Alisha, the clearest pattern across your answers is names and special words written with lowercase letters. Look at this:

FROM Q2 — adding details

Days of the week (Tuesday) always need a capital letter.
FROM Q5 — choosing the right word
You wrote: "Interestingly there was no sign of the seals, the family placed sally in a fish box..."
Sally is the seal's name — it needs a capital S every single time.
FROM Q11 — the paragraph
You wrote: "Around the word many people play pokemon... A boy named Satoshi Tajiri invented pokemon."
Pokemon is the name of the game, so it needs a capital P.
THE CAPITAL-LETTER RULE
A word needs a capital letter when it is a name. Names include:

• People: Anna, Jim, Sally, Satoshi Tajiri
• Places: Austin, Texas, Alpha School
• Days & months: Tuesday, January, Friday
• Special things: Pokemon, Pokeball (the name of the game or item)

If you could point at it and say "that exact one," it probably needs a capital.

B On Monday, Dad helped the seal named Sally.
THE "POINT AND NAME" CHECK
Before you finish any answer, read back through and look for any name. If you wrote a person, a place, a day, or the name of a game or story, make sure the first letter is capital.

Your #2 Win: "were" vs. "where"

Two words, two meanings — here is how to pick the right one

Alisha, this little word mix-up showed up in two of your answers. Both times you wrote "were" when you needed "where." Good news: there is a simple trick to tell them apart.

FROM Q4 — combining sentences
You wrote: "Anna and Jim ran to the beach were they found a baby seal in a fishnet."
You needed the place word "where" (where they found the seal).
FROM Q10 — explaining the lesson
You wrote: "The story teaches about kindness in the part were Anna and Jim help the seal."
Same thing here — "the part where" means a place or moment in the story.
THE "HERE" TRICK
Here is how to tell them apart:

• Where = a place. It has the word "here" hidden inside it. W-HERE. Use it for "the place where..." or "the spot where..."
• Were = a past-tense helping word. "They were happy." "The kids were playing."

If your sentence is pointing at a spot or a moment, use where.
Which sentence uses the right word?
A We went to the beach were we saw the seal.
B We went to the beach where we saw the seal.
QUICK CHECK BEFORE YOU MOVE ON
Any time you write the word "were," stop and ask yourself: "Am I pointing at a place or a moment?"

• If yes → you need where (with the H).
• If no → "were" is probably right. "They were kind." "The seals were swimming."
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
On Q4 and Q10, this one swap was part of why you got partial credit instead of full credit. Two questions fixed = almost one full extra point on your sentence score. Over three tests, that adds up fast.

MODEL PARAGRAPH (what 18/20 looks like)

Satoshi Tajiri's childhood had a big influence on the creation of Pokemon. When Tajiri was young, he loved going into the woods near his home to collect bugs and small creatures. He also enjoyed exploring nature and finding things that other kids did not notice. These childhood hobbies show up in Pokemon because the game is all about exploring new places, catching creatures, and collecting them. In the end, Tajiri turned what he loved as a kid into a game that millions of people play today.

The 4-Part Paragraph Shape
TOPIC State your main idea. Turn the prompt into a sentence.
DETAIL 1 One specific thing from the article that supports your idea
DETAIL 2 A second specific thing from the article
CLOSING Wrap back to your main idea in new words
The prompt asks about Tajiri's childhood. Which sentence uses a detail from his childhood?
A In Pokemon, you catch creatures in balls called pokeballs.
B As a child, Tajiri loved going into the woods to collect bugs.
MATCH YOUR DETAILS TO THE PROMPT
Before you pick your details, read the prompt one more time and circle the key word. Your prompt said "Tajiri's childhood." So your details need to be about when he was a kid, not about how the game works now.

This one habit will lift your paragraph score every single time.
