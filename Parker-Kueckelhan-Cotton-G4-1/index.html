Proofread: Catch Doubled Words

Q3 lost a point because of a repeated word

Parker, on Q3 you wrote a solid answer about Peter Tabichi, but a doubled word slipped through:

Q3 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2 weighted points)
"Peter Tabichi, a high school school teacher, is from Kenya, Africa."

The problem: The word "school" appears twice in a row. Your brain knew what you meant to write, but your eyes skipped over the repeat. This is one of the most common small errors on writing tests.

FIXED VERSION
"Peter Tabichi, a high school teacher, is from Kenya, Africa."
THE FIX: REREAD YOUR ANSWER ALOUD
After you finish writing an answer, read it back to yourself slowly, word by word. When you say the words aloud (even in your head), your ear catches doubled words that your eyes miss.

Try it: read "high school school teacher" out loud. You hear the stutter immediately.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Explain WHY

Q6 and Q9 both need a "because" sentence

Parker, on two different questions you named a correct answer but stopped before explaining why or how. Here is what happened:

Q6 — YOUR ANSWER (2 out of 3 weighted points)
"One challenge I have faced is reading and writing."

The question asked: What is a challenge you have faced in learning?

The problem: You named the challenge (reading and writing), but you did not explain what made it difficult for you. The question wants to know why it was hard, not just what it was.

A STRONGER ANSWER
"One challenge I have faced is reading and writing because sometimes the words on the page do not make sense right away, and I have to reread sentences multiple times before I understand them."

Now look at Q9:

Q9 — YOUR ANSWER (2 out of 3 weighted points)
"I would donate some to charity and some to church."

The question asked: What would you do with extra money, and why would it help others?

The problem: You said what you would do (donate), but you did not explain why it would help others or how it would make a difference.

A STRONGER ANSWER
"I would donate some to charity and some to church because charities use that money to buy food and clothes for families who need help, and churches can use it to support people in the community."
THE FIX: ADD "BECAUSE"
After you write your answer, ask yourself: "Did I say WHY?"

If your answer stops at what you would do or what the challenge is, add the word "because" and finish the thought. That one extra sentence is usually worth the missing point.

• "One challenge is reading and writing because..."
• "I would donate to charity because..."
The question asks: "What would you do with extra money, and why would it help others?" Which answer earns full points?

B I would donate to a food bank because families who are struggling can get meals they cannot afford on their own, which means fewer kids go hungry.
WHY THIS MATTERS
Q6 and Q9 together are worth 6 weighted points. You earned 4 out of 6. The knowledge is there. You just need to finish the thought with a "because" sentence that explains why or how.

Detail + Explanation

Q11 at 16 out of 20 — add passage detail and explain what it shows

Parker, your paragraph on Q11 earned 16 out of 20. That is solid. You have a clear structure. The points that slipped away were about developing your evidence more fully.

FROM YOUR PARAGRAPH
"One way Cody's perspective changed is when Mud darted past the defenders. In paragraph 4 it says..."

What you did well: You identified a moment where Cody's perspective changed and you pointed to a specific part of the passage.

What would lift the score: After giving the detail from the passage, add a sentence that explains what this shows about how Cody's thinking changed. Also, use "the passage states" instead of "paragraph 4" when referring to the text.

STRONGER PATTERN
"One way Cody's perspective changed is when Mud darted past the defenders. The passage states that Cody watched Mud weave through the other players with ease. This shows that Cody realized Mud was not just a stray dog but a skilled and surprising teammate, which changed how Cody thought about him."
THE TWO-STEP PATTERN FOR Q11
Step 1: Give the detail. Use "the passage states" and share what happened.

Step 2: Explain what it shows. Write a sentence starting with "This shows that..." and explain what the detail reveals about the character, theme, or idea.
Which version develops the evidence more fully?
A The passage states that Mud darted past the defenders and scored a goal. This happened in the middle of the game.
B The passage states that Mud darted past the defenders and scored a goal. This shows that Cody began to see Mud differently, realizing the dog had real talent and was not just getting in the way.
THE PAYOFF
Your paragraph already has structure. To move from 16 to 18+, make sure every piece of evidence is followed by a "This shows that..." sentence. That extra explanation is what separates a good paragraph from a great one.
