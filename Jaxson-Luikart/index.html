Q2: Using the Right Conjunction

"Since" shows a reason — "so" shows a result

The question asked you to combine two sentences using the conjunction "since":

Original Sentences
Layla saw a butterfly. She took out her camera.
YOU WROTE
Layla saw a butter fly, so she took out her camera.

Your sentence makes sense, but you used "so" instead of "since." Those two words do different jobs:

"so" = here's what happened next (result)
"since" = here's the reason why (cause)
WITH "SINCE"
Since Layla saw a butterfly, she took out her camera.

Also notice: "butterfly" is one word, not two. You wrote "butter fly" — easy fix!

HOW TO REMEMBER
"Since" answers why — it gives the reason.
"So" answers what happened next — it gives the result.

When the question tells you which conjunction to use, make sure you use that exact word. Read the directions carefully before you write.

THE "BECAUSE" TRICK
Whenever a question says "explain" or asks "why," use this formula:

You already know how to give great answers — you just need to add one more sentence that tells the reader why. That's where the points are.

Spelling Spotlight

Three words to practice

You had a few spelling mistakes across your test. Here are the ones to watch:

This is one word, not two. Think of it as one creature, one word.

No "e" at the end. The past tense is "developed" (add -ed, not -ed to "develope").

This looks like a quick writing mistake — slow down and reread to catch it.

PROOFREADING TIP
After you finish writing, read your work one sentence at a time, slowly. Point to each word with your finger or your cursor. This helps you catch missing letters, extra spaces, and words that got split in two (like "butter fly"). If a word looks weird, it probably is — try writing it again.
