Your #1 Error: Quoting Instead of Answering

Q6 and Q8 cost you 4 points on G3.8
🔍 Pattern alert: On Q6 and Q8 of G3.8 you wrote a sentence that was mostly a quote from the passage. The prompt asked you to explain — not to point at a quote. Same mistake, back to back. This is the single biggest point leak on this test.

Kylen, the prompt says “Write one sentence that explains...”. That means: your own sentence that says what the lesson or reason is. If your whole answer is a quote with “In sentence 11 it says...” at the front, the grader cannot see YOUR thinking. The passage already wrote those words. Your job is to say what they mean.

FROM G3.8 Q6 — SCORED 1 OF 3 POINTS
Prompt: “Write one sentence that explains why Jordan wanted to help Ms. Bright.”
You wrote: “In sentence 11 and 12 it says ‘Jorden rushed forward. I can help he said’”
That is a quote, not an explanation. The grader wants to know WHY Jordan wanted to help — what kind of person is he, what did he care about?
FROM G3.8 Q8 — SCORED 1 OF 3 POINTS
Prompt: “Write one sentence that explains the lesson the story teaches about kindness.”
You wrote: “In sentence 20 it says ‘Ms Bright watched, suprised and pleased, as her new helpers filed the room with kindness’”
Same pattern. A quote about what Ms. Bright did — not a sentence stating the lesson itself.

THE RULE — “READ THE STORY, WRITE YOUR ANSWER”
When a prompt says “Write one sentence that explains...” or “...the lesson...” or “...why...”, the answer has to be your sentence in your words. The passage gives you clues. You write what the clues add up to.

✗ “In sentence 11 it says ‘I can help.’” (just a quote)
✓ “Jordan wanted to help Ms. Bright because he is the kind of person who notices when someone is struggling and steps in.” (your words + because + clue from the passage)
THE “BECAUSE BRIDGE” TRICK FOR Q6–Q10
Every Q6–Q10 answer can follow one shape:

[Your answer to the prompt] because [a specific clue from the passage in your own words].

Example for Q6: “Jordan wanted to help because he saw Ms. Bright struggling with a heavy bin and he cared about her.”
Example for Q8: “The story teaches that kindness spreads because one helper turned into many.”

This one shape earns full credit on almost every Q6–Q10 prompt.
WHAT A FULL-CREDIT Q6 ANSWER LOOKS LIKE
Compare your G3.8 Q6 to this model:

Prompt: Write one sentence that explains why Jordan wanted to help Ms. Bright.
Full credit: “Jordan wanted to help Ms. Bright because he saw she was carrying too many books and the bin was slipping, and he is the kind of person who steps in when someone needs help.”

Notice: no quotation marks, no “in sentence 11,” no copied line. Just YOUR sentence, with a clear “because” connecting the answer to a clue from the story.

THE “WHO” CHECK FOR APPOSITIVE PROMPTS
Appositive prompts always tell you WHO the appositive should describe. Before you write your answer, circle the name in the prompt in your head:

“...include an appositive to give more information about Ms. Bright.”

Then the renaming phrase has to go next to that name — with commas around it.

✓ Ms. Bright, the librarian, thanked Jordan.
✓ Ms. Bright, a patient librarian, thanked Jordan.
✗ Jordan, the student, was thanked by Ms. Bright. (right grammar, wrong person)
THE NAME-SPELLING FIX
Before you submit any short answer, do a 10-second name check. Look at the passage and confirm the spelling of every character name you used. On G3.8 that was Jordan and Ms. Bright. Small words, easy to copy, easy points.
