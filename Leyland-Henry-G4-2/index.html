Your #1 Repeated Error: Capital "I" Always

This appeared in 4 different answers on this test alone

Leyland, the word "I" is always a capital letter in English. Not just at the start of a sentence, but everywhere. Here are four examples from your G4.2 test:

Q7 — 2 out of 3
"When spring starts i recognize..."
Q8 — 2 out of 3
"In my front yard i have started seeing small white flowers..."
Q9 — 1 out of 3
"At my grandmas house there is a giant tree with a swing set..."
Q10 — 2 out of 3
"During summer, i love to play baseball and have fun with friends with pool partys also sleep overs."
🔎 Pattern: Every time you write "i" by itself as a word, it should be "I". This single fix would have improved your score on Q7, Q8, Q9, and Q10.
THE RULE
The word "I" is always uppercase. It does not matter where it appears in the sentence:

• "When spring starts I recognize..." (middle of sentence)
• "In my front yard I have started seeing..." (middle of sentence)
• "During summer, I love to play baseball..." (after a comma)

Why? "I" is a pronoun that refers to yourself. In English, it is treated like a proper noun and always capitalized.
Which sentence is written correctly?
A During summer, i love to play baseball and have fun with friends.
B During summer, I love to play baseball and have fun with friends.
YOUR EDITING CHECK
Before you submit any writing, scan every sentence for the word "i" by itself. If you see it lowercase, fix it to "I". This one habit will earn you points on almost every question.

Your #2 Error: Spelling Patterns

Three spelling issues that cost you points

Leyland, alongside the capital "I" issue, there are a few spelling patterns that showed up across your test:

Q11 — MISSPELLED WORD
"Humnas benefit from robots..."
Should be Humans. The letters got jumbled.
Q10 — PLURAL SPELLING
"...pool partys also sleep overs."
Words ending in -y change to -ies in the plural: "parties". And "sleepovers" is one word.
Q9 — MISSING APOSTROPHE (POSSESSIVE)
"At my grandmas house..."
The house belongs to your grandma. When something belongs to someone, you add an apostrophe + s: grandma's house.
THREE SPELLING RULES TO REMEMBER
1. Plurals of words ending in -y: Drop the -y, add -ies.
• party → parties
• story → stories
• baby → babies

2. Possessives: When something belongs to someone, add apostrophe + s.
• grandma → grandma's house
• the dog → the dog's toy

3. Compound words: Some words are written as one word, not two.
• sleep + over = sleepover (plural: sleepovers)
• base + ball = baseball
Which sentence has the correct spelling?
A I went to two birthday partys and three sleepovers this summer.
B I went to two birthday parties and three sleepovers this summer.
YOUR EDITING CHECK
After writing, re-read slowly and look for:
• Any word ending in "-ys" (probably should be "-ies")
• Any place where something belongs to someone (add apostrophe + s)
• Any two-word combinations that should be one word

Building a Stronger Paragraph

Q11 scored 11 out of 20 — here is how to develop it further

Leyland, your Q11 paragraph about robots had a clear opening idea, but it needed more sentences and more detail to earn full marks. The passage was "Are People Ready for Robots?" and your paragraph started strong but ran short.

WHAT A STRONG Q11 PARAGRAPH NEEDS
1. A topic sentence that clearly states your main idea.
2. At least two details from the passage that support your idea.
3. Explanation sentences ("This shows..." or "This means...") after each detail.
4. A closing sentence that wraps up your thinking.

Your paragraph had a topic sentence but needed more details from the passage and explanation sentences to fill it out.
The 5-Part Paragraph Structure
TOPIC State your main idea clearly
DETAIL 1 A specific fact or example from the passage
SHOWS "This shows..." — explain why that detail matters
DETAIL 2 A second specific fact from the passage
SHOWS "This shows..." — explain why that detail matters
CLOSING Wrap back to your main idea
Which approach earns more points on Q11?
A Write a topic sentence and one or two short sentences, then stop.
B Write a topic sentence, include two details from the passage with "This shows..." after each one, and end with a closing sentence.
WHY THIS MATTERS
Your Q11 scored 11 out of 20. Adding two passage details with "This shows..." sentences could be worth 5 to 7 more points. That alone would move your overall score from 67% to close to 80%.
