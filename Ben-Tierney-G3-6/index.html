You understood the appositive structure — commas on both sides, tucked inside the sentence. That's good! But "like a rat" isn't an appositive, and it doesn't come from the passage. Sentence 18 in the article calls porcupines "the slow movers of the forest." That's the detail you needed.

BETTER VERSION
Porcupines, the slow movers of the forest, are rodents.
THE APPOSITIVE TRICK
An appositive is a short description placed right after a noun, set off by commas. On the test, the passage always gives you the description to use — you just have to find it.

Pattern: Noun, [description from the passage], rest of sentence.

More examples:
• The porcupine, a prickly rodent, wandered through the woods.
• Quills, sharp needle-like hairs, protect the porcupine from danger.

Before you write, underline the key word in the question. If it says "why," your answer needs a reason. If it says "how," your answer needs steps. Describing what happens is not the same as explaining why it happens.

TS = Topic Sentence  •  D = Detail  •  E = Explain  •  CS = Concluding Sentence

Spelling: Words to Watch

Two patterns to fix before next time

You made some spelling mistakes that showed up more than once. Fixing these will make your writing cleaner and pick up easy points.

1. "there" vs. "their"

You wrote "there tail" — but you meant "their tail". This came up in both Q6 and your paragraph.

there tail → their tail
HOW TO REMEMBER
Their = belongs to them (their tail, their quills).
There = a place (over there, there is a porcupine).

Quick test: Can you replace the word with "belonging to them"? If yes, it's their.

2. "quill" — not "quil"

You wrote "quils" and "quil" — the correct spelling is quill (double L) and quills.

quil / quils → quill / quills

3. "touch" — not "tuch"

tuch → touch
PROOFREADING TIP
After you finish writing, read your paragraph one extra time looking only at spelling. Don't think about ideas — just look at the words. Circle any word that looks funny and try to fix it. Words from the passage (like "quills") are already spelled out for you in the article — use them!
