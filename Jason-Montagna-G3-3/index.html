Question 5 Fix — Subordinating Conjunctions

You needed to fill in a subordinating conjunction — a word that connects a reason or condition to a result.

Which word is a subordinating conjunction that fits in the blank?
A Still
B Since
YOU WROTE
"Still"

You wrote "Still," which is a transition word, not a subordinating conjunction. "Still" means "even so" or "however" — it does not explain why the family put Sally in the box. The sentence needed a word that gives the reason.

WHAT ARE SUBORDINATING CONJUNCTIONS?
Subordinating conjunctions are words that connect a reason, time, or condition to a result. They answer: Why? When? Under what condition?

Common subordinating conjunctions:
• Because — gives a reason
• Since — gives a reason or time
• Although — shows contrast
• When — tells the time
• If — sets a condition
• After / Before / Until / Unless
MEMORY TRICK
Ask yourself: "Does this word answer WHY, WHEN, or UNDER WHAT CONDITION?"
If yes → it is probably a subordinating conjunction.
If it just means "however" or "also" → it is a transition word, not a subordinating conjunction.
CORRECT ANSWER
"Since there was no sign of the seals, the family placed Sally in a fish box and motored into the bay."
(or "Because there was no sign of the seals...")

THE PATTERN: MISSING LETTERS IN VOWEL COMBINATIONS
Both errors involve dropping a letter from the middle of a word:
• meanin_ful → missing the "g" — think: meaning + ful
• beacause → the "e" and "a" got swapped — think: be + cause
QUICK FIX: SAY IT SLOWLY
When you are not sure about a spelling, say the word slowly out loud and listen for each part:
• mean · ing · ful → meaningful
• be · cause → because

Breaking words into smaller parts helps you catch missing letters.
SO WHAT?
These did not cost you points this time, but on longer writing pieces or future tests, misspellings can add up. Getting "because" and "meaningful" locked in now means one less thing to worry about.
