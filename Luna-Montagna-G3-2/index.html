Detail + Explanation

Q11 — how to develop your paragraph with different details

Q11 — 14 out of 20. You wrote about the Camel Mobile Library and said why it matters. But you repeated the same point three times (that it helps kids learn to read and write). To earn more points, add different details from the passage.

Here is the pattern:

Detail + Explanation Pattern
D Detail: A specific fact from the passage (something new each time).
E Explanation: Why that detail matters or what it shows about the topic.

For example, instead of saying "helps kids learn" three different ways, you could add:

DETAIL 1
"The camel carries books to villages that have no libraries."
EXPLANATION
"This is important because without the camel, those children would have no books at all."
DETAIL 2 (DIFFERENT)
"One camel can carry hundreds of books on its back."
EXPLANATION
"This means many children can pick their own book to read each visit."
THE "DID I ALREADY SAY THAT?" CHECK
After you write a sentence, look back at what you already wrote. If your new sentence says the same idea in different words, cross it out and find a new fact from the passage instead.

Paragraph Review

Q11 — 14 out of 20 — "One Book, One Camel"

Here is what you wrote. Notice how all three sentences repeat the same idea:

YOUR PARAGRAPH (Q11)
The Camel Mobile Libery help kid learn how to read and write, because there are no liberys in Kanya. So this helps them learn. This is important, because then the chrildern can still learn how to read and write.

You have a topic sentence and you use "because" well — that is a solid start. But all three sentences say "helps kids learn." Here is how to build on it with different details:

Paragraph Plan — TS → D + E → D + E → CS
TS = Topic Sentence  •  D = Detail  •  E = Explanation  •  CS = Closing Sentence
TS The Camel Mobile Library helps kids in Kenya learn how to read and write.
D There are no libraries in parts of Kenya, so a camel carries books to the villages.
E This is important because without the camel, those children would never see a book.
D The camel visits different villages so many children get a turn to borrow books.
E This means more kids can practice reading at home, not just at the camel stop.
CS The Camel Mobile Library shows that one good idea can change many lives.

See how each E sentence answers "why does this matter?" and each D is a different fact. That is what moves a paragraph from 14 to 17+.
