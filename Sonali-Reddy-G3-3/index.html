Fix 1: Do the Job the Instruction Names

A question is a question. An appositive renames. A type is one word
THE RULE: READ THE INSTRUCTION TWICE, DO THAT JOB FIRST, THEN DECORATE
Each editing question is two points: one for the skill named, one for a clean sentence. A good sentence doing a different job earns the second point only. Question: a helping verb or a question word at the front, the same facts, a question mark. Appositive: a noun phrase right after the noun, inside commas, that renames it. Type: one word. The check: after you type, read the instruction once more and point to the place in your sentence where you did what it says.

THE SAME PARAGRAPH, AT 20
Satoshi Tajiri’s childhood shaped Pokémon in two ways: his love of catching bugs and his love of video games. As a boy he spent hours catching bugs, just as players in his game catch Pokémon, and he hoped they would feel the same joy he felt. This shows that the game is his childhood hobby turned into something other children could play. When he got older he loved video games but thought most were not very good, so he set out to make a better one. This shows that the second half of the idea came from being a player himself. Catching bugs gave him the idea and video games gave him the place to put it, and that is how a boy’s hobby became Pokémon.
