THE RULE
An expository essay explains ideas using the passage. Every body paragraph needs at least one specific detail from the passage — a movie title, a character name, a quote, or a fact. Without passage evidence, you're writing an opinion, not an expository essay.

The Body Paragraph Pattern

How to build a strong body paragraph every time

Every body paragraph in a G6+ essay should follow this pattern:

Body Paragraph Formula
TS Topic Sentence — states the paragraph's main point
E1 Evidence 1 — a specific fact, quote, or example from the passage
X1 Explanation 1 — explain why that evidence matters
E2 Evidence 2 — a second fact or example from the passage
X2 Explanation 2 — explain why that evidence matters
CS Concluding Sentence — wraps up the paragraph's main idea

EXAMPLE BODY PARAGRAPH
[TS] One way animated movies teach children is by showing characters who demonstrate courage and honesty. [E1] According to the passage, in the movie Brave, Merida shows courage when she stands up for what she believes in, even when it goes against what her family wants. [X1] This teaches kids that being brave sometimes means making hard choices, and that standing up for yourself is important even when others disagree. [E2] The passage also explains that characters in animated movies often face difficult situations where they must tell the truth, even when lying would be easier. [X2] By watching these characters choose honesty, children learn that telling the truth builds trust and leads to better outcomes in the long run. [CS] Through stories about courage and honesty, animated movies give children role models who show them how to handle tough situations.
THE KEY RULE
Every E (evidence) must be followed by an X (explanation). Evidence without explanation is just a list of facts. The explanation is where you show the reader why that evidence supports your point. Ask yourself: "So what? Why does this matter?"
