#1 Fix: One Sentence, With an Appositive

Q1 — every word you needed was already in your answer

Question 1 gave you two sentences and asked you to rewrite them as one sentence that includes an appositive.

The two sentences to fold into one:
“My family has two really cute dogs.”
“Their names are Lucy and Rocket.”
QUESTION 1 — 1 OUT OF 2
“My family has 2 really cute dogs. One is named Lucy, and the other is named Rocket.”

Two good things happened here that are worth naming. You kept “really cute” — a detail the original had and most answers drop. And your second sentence is correctly punctuated, with the comma in exactly the right place before “and.”

What the question wanted, though, was one sentence, and this is still two. An appositive is how you get there: a short naming phrase that sits between commas and renames the noun right before it.

THE FIX — USING ONLY WORDS YOU ALREADY WROTE
“My family has two really cute dogs, Lucy and Rocket.”
The names were in your answer already — they just needed to come inside the first sentence, after a comma. You wrote sixteen words; the full-credit answer is nine. Also worth doing: spell two as a word instead of the numeral 2.
THE APPOSITIVE PATTERN, AND HOW TO CHECK IT
[noun], [naming phrase], [rest of the sentence].

The check: take the naming phrase out. If what is left still works on its own, you built it right. “My family has two really cute dogs” — still a complete sentence. Correct.

And a very close cousin, on Question 2. The task was to rewrite sentence 4 — “There is one place we don’t take the dogs” — as a question. You wrote: “Is there a place we don’t take our dogs?” The form is right: you moved the verb to the front, and it is a real question. The only thing that slipped was a single word — the original says one place, and yours says a place. When you rewrite a sentence, every piece of its information has to make the trip.

Task: rewrite these as one sentence that includes an appositive.
