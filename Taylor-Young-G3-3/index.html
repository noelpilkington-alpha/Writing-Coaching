Write Complete Sentences, Not Fragments

Q7 — 1 out of 3 — fragments lose you points

Q7 — 1 out of 3. The question asked you what part of the story was most meaningful and why. You wrote:

YOUR ANSWER (Q7)
"When they helped the seal."

This is a fragment — it is not a complete sentence. It tells us when something happened, but it does not have a subject doing an action or explain why it was meaningful.

What Makes a Complete Sentence
WHO A subject (who or what does something)
DID A verb (what they did)
WHY A complete thought (so the reader understands the full idea)

THE FINGER TEST
After you write an answer, read it out loud. If it sounds like it needs something before or after it to make sense, it is a fragment. Add a subject, a verb, or finish the thought.

Change to a REAL Question

Q1 — 1 out of 2 — you cannot just add "?" to a statement

Q1 — 1 out of 2. The question asked you to turn the statement into a question. You wrote:

YOUR ANSWER (Q1)
"Anna named the seal Sally?"

You added a question mark at the end, which shows you knew it needed to become a question. But just sticking "?" onto a statement does not restructure it into a real question. You need to flip the word order.

How to Turn a Statement Into a Question
1 Find the helper verb (did, was, is, has, can, will).
2 Move it to the front of the sentence.
3 End with ?

Helper verb: Did (past tense of "do")

QUICK CHECK
If your "question" reads the same as the original statement (just with a ? at the end), you have not changed it yet. A real question flips the word order so the helper verb comes first.

Detail + Explanation

For when you attempt Q11 — how to develop your paragraph

When you write your Q11 paragraph next time, each detail from the passage needs an explanation of why it matters. This is the pattern that earns the most points:

Detail + Explanation Pattern
D Detail: A specific fact or event from the passage.
E Explanation: Why that detail matters or what it shows about the topic.

For example, the passage "From Collecting to Designing: The Story of Pokemon" describes how the creator got the idea:

DETAIL
"When Satoshi Tajiri was young, he loved collecting bugs in the fields near his home."
EXPLANATION (WHY IT MATTERS)
"This is important because his love of collecting insects gave him the idea for players to collect creatures in the game."

After you write a fact from the passage, ask: "So what? Why does this matter?" Write that answer as your next sentence.

Sample Paragraph Plan for Q11
TS "The passage explains how Pokemon was created from a simple childhood hobby."
D Tajiri loved collecting bugs as a child.
E This hobby inspired the "catch them all" idea in the game.
D He spent years designing the game before it was finished.
E This shows that creating something great takes patience and hard work.
CS "Because Tajiri never gave up on his idea, Pokemon became one of the biggest games in the world."
