Your #1 Recurring Error: Sentences Need Proper Punctuation

G5.1 Q1, Q2, Q8 — missing periods and missing endings
🔍
The pattern across G5.1: Three separate questions (Q1, Q2, Q8) had punctuation or completion issues. Q1: "Ms" was written without a period — it should be "Ms." Q2: no end period at all. Q8: the answer was cut off before the end. Individually small. Together, about 4 points on a 25-point section.

Lincoln, every complete sentence in English needs three things working in the background: (1) a capital letter at the start, (2) proper punctuation at the end, and (3) no missing words in between. Titles like "Ms." "Mr." "Mrs." "Dr." always have a period after them — they're abbreviations, and that's what the period tells the reader.

THE SLOW READ-BACK
This is the single highest-yield writing habit you can build right now. Here's how:

1. Point at each word with your finger (or your cursor) as you read.
2. Say it out loud in your head, word by word — don't let your eyes skip ahead.
3. If a "word" looks weird, check: are there two real words stuck together? Is a letter missing?
4. Check possessives: does any word need an apostrophe to show ownership?

On G5.1 this habit would have caught "hima hug," "filled in," "near by," and "humans attention" — roughly 1.5 to 2 extra points.
COMMON COMPOUND WORDS (ONE WORD, NOT TWO)
Some words that seem like they should be two are actually one:

✓ nearby (not "near by")
✓ inside (not "in side")
✓ underneath (not "under neath")
✓ everyone (not "every one" — unless you mean each individual one)
✓ somewhere (not "some where")
