Your #1 Error: One Answer, Not a List

Q8 — the question asked for ONE way, but you listed several

Audrey, on Q8 the question asked you to describe one way Yacouba's work changed the desert. You wrote a sentence that ran on, listing multiple changes about frogs instead of focusing on one change and explaining why it matters.

Q8 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 3 weighted points)
"The frogs came back and they got bigger and there were more of them and the land was different because of what he did."

The question asked: Describe one way Yacouba's work changed the environment.

The problem: Your sentence runs on with multiple ideas connected by "and." It lists several changes (frogs came back, got bigger, more of them, land was different) but does not pick one and explain what it shows about Yacouba's impact.

A STRONGER ANSWER
"One way Yacouba's work changed the desert is that frogs returned to the area. This detail shows that the land became wet enough to support animal life again, which means his tree-planting method truly brought the environment back to health."
THE FIX: PICK ONE, THEN EXPLAIN IT
When the question says "one way" or "one reason," your answer should have exactly two parts:

Part 1: Name the one specific detail from the passage.
Part 2: Explain what that detail shows or why it matters.

If you find yourself writing "and...and...and," stop. Pick the strongest detail and explain it instead.
The question asks: "Describe one way Yacouba changed the desert." Which answer earns full points?
A "Yacouba changed the desert because the frogs returned and the trees grew and the soil changed."
B "One way Yacouba changed the desert is that trees grew tall enough to shade the ground. This shows that his method brought enough water back into the soil to support full-grown trees where nothing used to survive."
WHY THIS MATTERS
Q8 is worth 3 weighted points. You earned 1 out of 3. The fix is simple: when you see "one way" in the question, write one sentence naming the detail and one sentence explaining what it shows. Two sentences, full points.

Your Paragraph — Adding Depth

17 out of 20 — good structure, needs more D+E

Audrey, your paragraph on Q11 earned 17 out of 20. Your structure is good and your ideas come through clearly. The 3 points that slipped away were about developing your details further with explanation sentences.

Topic Sentence Detail from Passage Needs "what this shows" Second Detail Needs "why this matters" Conclusion

What you did well: You have a clear topic sentence, you pull details from the passage, and you wrap up with a conclusion. That structure is strong for G4.

What would lift the score: After each detail from the passage, add one more sentence that explains what this shows or why it matters. Right now you name the detail and move on. Adding the explanation turns a 17 into a 19 or 20.

THE DETAIL + EXPLANATION PATTERN
Every time you use a detail from the passage, follow it with a sentence that starts with one of these:

• "This shows that..."
• "This matters because..."
• "This detail is important because..."

Detail: "Yacouba planted trees in tiny holes he dug across the desert."
Explanation: "This shows that he found a simple way to bring water back to the soil, which helped everything else grow."
Which version uses the Detail + Explanation pattern correctly?
A "Yacouba dug small holes and planted trees. Also, the frogs came back to the area after a few years."
B "Yacouba dug small holes to catch rainwater and planted trees inside them. This shows that a simple, patient solution can transform a desert into farmland over time."
YOUR NEXT LEVEL ON Q11
You already have the structure and the details. To move from 17 to 19+, make sure every detail has an explanation sentence after it. Two well-developed points with explanations score higher than three quick points without them.
