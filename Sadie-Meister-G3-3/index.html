Your 6-Step Process

Sadie, here are 6 steps to follow on every writing test. Step 2 is your biggest one — on Questions 1–5, the trick is to figure out exactly what kind of answer the question is asking for, then build that.

THE 6 STEPS
Step 1: Read the question twice. The first read tells you the topic. The second read tells you exactly what to answer.

Step 2: Identify what the question asks for. This is your key step. Ask: does it want me to change a sentence into a question (ends with ?), add an appositive (rename the noun with commas), or start with a joining word like because or when? Match the exact thing it asks for.

Step 3: Plan. Before typing, think: What will I say? Where in the passage did I see it?

Step 4: Write. For Questions 6–10, write one full sentence that answers the question AND gives a reason or example.

Step 5: Proofread using the rubric. Read your answer back. Does it match what the question asked? Is the spelling correct? Does it match the passage?

Step 6: Single Paragraph Outline (for Q11). Before writing your paragraph, plan it:
• Topic Sentence — answers the question directly
• Detail 1 — a reason or example from the passage + explain it
• Detail 2 — a second reason or example + explain it
• Closing Sentence — wraps up your paragraph

Q3 — "Add an appositive" (1 of 2 points)
"Once Sally was released she swam joyfully to the other seals."

The problem: An appositive renames a noun, set off with commas. This sentence is clear, but it never adds a rename. An appositive for Sally would be: Sally, the baby seal, swam to the other seals.

A Sally is the name Anna chose for the seal.

YOUR QUESTION-TYPE CHECKLIST
• "Change into a question" → your answer must end with ?
• "Add an appositive" → rename the noun, set off with commas (Sally, the baby seal,)
• "Use a joining word" (a subordinating conjunction) → start with because, when, since, after, or although — not "Soon"
• "Combine the sentences" → join two ideas into one smooth sentence
