Fix 1: Count the Ways, One Quote Each

Q11: the question said some ways. The paragraph gave one, in a three-sentence quote
THE RULE: THE POINT, THE HOWS, THEN ONE SHORT QUOTE PER HOW
The first sentence answers the question (POINT) and names the ways the paragraph will prove (HOW). Some ways means at least two. Each how gets a DETAIL · SHOW: a fact from the article, with at most a short exact phrase in quotation marks, two to eight words, copied letter for letter. Then a DETAIL · SO WHAT: This shows plus what that fact proves, not just humans benefit. Then an ending that adds the ways up. The check: count the ways in the question, count the hows in your first sentence, count the quotes. The three numbers match.
YOU WROTE (Q11, 13 OUT OF 20)
“According to the text, “In some jobs, robots work with people. In one factory in the Netherland, workers use robot arms to put together parts for machines. With help from the robots, workers can make many more parts each hour than they could alone.” This shows how humans benefit from robots.” NO FIRST SENTENCE; ONE WAY; A COPIED PARAGRAPH; A SO WHAT THAT NAMES NOTHING
The quote is three whole sentences, and two words inside the quotation marks are changed (Netherlands, machine parts). A quote has to be exact or it is not a quote. The This shows repeats the question instead of answering it: which benefit? For whom? The article has four ways on the page and the paragraph used one.

POINTHumans benefit from letting robots do simple tasks HOWin three ways: workers make more, workers stay safer, and people at home get time and comfort back.
Three hows, so three quotes, three This shows, and an ending with three things to add up.

Fix 2: An Appositive Renames with a Noun

Q4 here, Q3 on G3.6: close both times, and neither renamed
THE RULE: A NOUN PHRASE BETWEEN THE COMMAS
An appositive is a noun phrase that renames the noun right before it, set off by commas: bluebonnet festivals, spring celebrations of the state flower, … The check: cover everything outside the commas and read what is left. Does it start with a noun that could stand in for the thing before it? If it starts with to, or, or a verb form, it adds information without renaming, and that is one point, not two.
YOU WROTE (Q4, 1 OUT OF 2)
“Many people attend bluebonnet festivals to appreciate the beauty of the flower in small towns all over Texas.” A TO-PHRASE, NO COMMAS, NOTHING RENAMED
Good information about why people go. But to appreciate tells a purpose; it does not give the festivals another name, and nothing sits between commas.
WITH THE APPOSITIVE
Many people attend bluebonnet festivals, APPOSITIVEspring celebrations of the state flower, in small towns all over Texas.
Cover the outside: spring celebrations of the state flower starts with a noun that could stand in for the festivals.
YOU WROTE (G3.6 Q3, 1 OUT OF 2)
“Porcupines, or rodents, are slow movers of the west.” OR, NOT A RENAME
Nearly there: commas in the right place and a noun inside. Or offers a choice of names instead of giving one. Porcupines, large rodents covered in quills, are slow movers renames.

Fix 3: A Because That Says Something New

Q5: the because repeated the first half of the sentence
THE RULE: THE REASON ADDS A FACT THE FIRST HALF DID NOT HAVE
When an instruction says using facts from the text, finish the sentence, the because-half has to carry a fact from the page that the first half did not already say. The check: cover the first half and read the because-half alone. If it could be the first half again in other words, it is not a reason yet. Then point to it in the article.
YOU WROTE (Q5, 1 OUT OF 2)
“Bluebonnets don’t need much water or fertilizer because they can survive cold Texas weather and don’t need much water or fertilizer.” THE BECAUSE REPEATS THE CLAIM
The second half ends with the first half’s own words. Cold weather is a fact, but it is a second thing they survive, not the reason they need little water. The article gives the reason one sentence later: They’re strong, tough flowers that grow best in sunny places.
WITH A FACT FROM THE TEXT
Bluebonnets don’t need much water or fertilizer BECAUSEbecause they are strong, tough wildflowers that grow best in sunny places.
