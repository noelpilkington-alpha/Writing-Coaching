Fix 1: The Exact Job, the Exact Word

Q5 asked for because, but or so and got a comma. Q4 asked for a question and got a period
THE RULE: THE INSTRUCTION NAMES THE JOB AND OFTEN THE WORD. USE THAT WORD, AND ONLY THAT JOB
One of the two points on every editing question is for the skill named. Combine using because, but, or so means one of those three words has to be in your sentence; a comma or an and is a different job. Change into a question means the same facts, flipped into a question, with a question mark. The check: after you type, read the instruction once more and point to the place in your sentence where you did what it says.
YOU WROTE (Q5, 0 OUT OF 2)
Combine using because, but or so: Bees are also important for nature. They carry pollen from flower to flower. → “Bees are important for nature, they carry pollen from one flower to another.” NONE OF THE THREE WORDS; A COMMA HOLDING TWO SENTENCES
Both ideas are there. The job was the joining word, and the comma also leaves two sentences stuck together, so both points went.
THE WORD THE INSTRUCTION OFFERED
Bees are important for nature because they carry pollen from flower to flower.
The pollen is the reason they matter. Because. Your Q7 did exactly this: Honey is important to bees because it is their food source during winter.
YOU WROTE (Q4, 1 OUT OF 2)
Change into a question: The honey is food for the bees during the winter. → “Did you know that bees eat honey for food during the winter.” A NEW FRAME, AND NO QUESTION MARK
Did you know that wraps the sentence instead of turning it into a question, and the end mark is a period. Your last card said: move the helping verb to the front and keep every idea.
THE SENTENCE, TURNED
Is the honey food for the bees during the winter?
The helping verb to the front, every idea kept, a question mark. Two points.

THE RULE: SUCH AS BELONGS TO THE SENTENCE BEFORE IT
Such as squirrels, birds, and other living creatures is a list, not a sentence: it has no one doing anything. Attach it to the sentence it belongs to with a comma, or give it a subject and a verb. The check: cover everything before Such as. Can what is left stand alone? If not, join it on.
YOU WROTE (Q11, 18 OUT OF 20)
“Oak trees are important for a number of reasons. One, they can provide shleter to many animals. Such as squirrels, birds, and other living creatures. Two, during a hot summer day this oak tree can provide shade for you and your friends. Three, it gives us cleaner air. Such as it takes in the Co2 and makes oxygen for us to have cleaner air. These are why Oak trees are important to nature and animals.” TWO SUCH AS PIECES; ONE SPELLING
The facts are right and from the passage, and the three reasons are in order. The grader’s only note: join the Such as pieces to the sentence before. That is the two points.

A comma and such as, or because, and the piece becomes part of the sentence it explains.
