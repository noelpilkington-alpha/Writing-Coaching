Step 2: Identify the Skill — Q3 Appositive

The question asked for an appositive. You wrote a participial phrase.
🔍
What happened on Q3: The question asked you to add an appositive. You wrote "Confused, she..." which is a participial phrase (it describes what the character is doing or feeling). An appositive renames or identifies the noun — it does not describe an action or feeling.
Q3 — YOUR ANSWER (0 out of 2 weighted points)
"Confused, she looked around the room for her notebook."

Why this is not an appositive: "Confused" describes how she feels (participial phrase). An appositive renames or identifies WHO she is.

WHAT AN APPOSITIVE LOOKS LIKE
"Sarah, the newest student in the class, looked around the room for her notebook."

The bolded part is the appositive. It renames "Sarah" by telling us who she is. It sits between two commas. It could be removed and the sentence still works.
THE QUICK TEST
After you write your "appositive," ask: Does it rename or identify the noun?

• "Confused, she..." → Does "confused" rename who she is? No. It describes how she feels. NOT an appositive.
• "Sarah, the newest student, ..." → Does "the newest student" rename who Sarah is? Yes. APPOSITIVE.
Which sentence contains an appositive?
A Excited about the field trip, Maya packed her bag early.
B Maya, a talented artist, painted the mural on the school wall.
WHY THIS MATTERS
Q3 was worth 2 weighted points and you earned 0. You clearly know how to write sophisticated sentences — a participial phrase is a real grammar move. The issue is simply matching the move to what the question asks. Step 2 (identify the skill) catches this every time.
