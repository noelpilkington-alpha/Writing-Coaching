Fix #2: Run-on Sentences

Q9 — 2 out of 3 weighted points

Ryan, on Q9 you wrote a run-on sentence that piled ideas together without proper separation: "baked the pumpkin seeds to eat" was connected to other ideas without clear sentence boundaries.

THE FIX: ONE IDEA PER SENTENCE
When you have multiple ideas, give each one its own sentence. Then pick one to develop with Detail + Explanation.

Run-on: "They carved pumpkins and baked the seeds to eat and made soup from the rest."

Developed: "The family carved pumpkins for Halloween. They also baked the pumpkin seeds. The passage says roasted seeds are a healthy snack with lots of protein. This shows that pumpkins can be used for food and not just decoration."

Fix #3: Avoid Repetition in Your Paragraph

Q11 — "Learning to cook can help children by..." repeated

Ryan, your paragraph repeated the phrase "Learning to cook can help children by..." multiple times. This made the paragraph feel circular instead of building new ideas. The grader wants to see variety in your sentence starters and different points developed with new details each time.

YOUR PARAGRAPH PATTERN
"Learning to cook can help children by...
Learning to cook can help children by...
Learning to cook can help children by..."

The problem: Repeating the same phrase makes it sound like you are saying the same thing three times. Each sentence should start differently and add a new idea.

VARY YOUR SENTENCE STARTERS
Instead of repeating the same phrase, use different openings:

• "One reason cooking helps children is..."
• "Another benefit is that..."
• "The passage also says..."
• "This matters because..."
• "For example..."

Each new sentence should introduce a different idea or develop the previous one further.
Which paragraph opening avoids repetition?
A"Cooking helps children learn. Cooking helps children be healthy. Cooking helps children be independent."
B"Learning to cook teaches children important skills. For example, the passage says children who cook learn to measure and follow steps. This shows that cooking builds math and reading skills at the same time."
YOUR PARAGRAPH FORMULA
Topic sentence (state main idea once) + Detail from passage + "This shows..." + Second detail + "This matters because..." + Closing sentence. Never repeat the topic sentence word-for-word in the body.
