Each how gets a DETAIL · SHOW, a quote or fact from the story, and a DETAIL · SO WHAT: This shows… Then a CONCLUDING SENTENCE that names the hows and says what they add up to. The check: read the question, then your first sentence. Does it answer, and does it say how many changes you will show? Then count: one quote per how, one This shows per quote.

Fix 2: Use the Word the Instruction Names

Q5: because, but or so. The answer used and
THE RULE: FIND THE WORDS YOU WERE HANDED
Some combine instructions hand you the exact words: using because, but, or so. One of those three has to appear, and it has to show how the ideas are connected: because gives the reason, so gives the result, but gives the contrast. The check: after you write, find the word from the instruction in your sentence. And joins two ideas without saying how they connect, so it is never one of the three.
YOU WROTE (Q5, 1 OUT OF 2)
“His dedication has transformed the school, and due to his efforts, enrollment has doubled, and more students are now attending college.” AND, NOT BECAUSE, BUT OR SO
Every fact kept, correct punctuation, and the one word the instruction asked for is missing. Sentence 14 is the proof of sentence 13, so the link is so, or because turned around.
WITH SO
His dedication has transformed the school, SOso enrollment has doubled and more students are now attending college.
Or: Enrollment has doubled and more students attend college because his dedication has transformed the school.

The three words, each doing its job, and one and:

BECAUSE: THE REASONTabichi used his own salary to buy food because his students were going hungry.
SO: THE RESULTSome students had to walk four miles to school, so he knew the problem was bigger than one classroom.
BUT: THE CONTRASTHe had worked at a private school with ample resources, but the public school had one computer and no reliable internet.
ANDHe used his own salary to buy food, and his students were going hungry.
Two true facts, joined, and the reader has to guess how they connect. Not one of the three words.
Combine using because, but, or so: He taught them to grow drought-resistant crops. The region gets little rain. Which answer does the job?
AHe taught them to grow drought-resistant crops, and the region gets little rain.
BHe taught them to grow drought-resistant crops because the region gets little rain.
Combine using because, but, or so: Cody expected Mud to lose the ball. Mud scored the first goal. Which answer does the job?
ACody expected Mud to lose the ball, but Mud scored the first goal.
BCody expected Mud to lose the ball, and Mud scored the first goal.
PRACTICE IT IN YOUR ALPHAWRITE LESSONS
When an instruction hands you words, one of them goes in your answer. Then ask which job it does: reason, result or contrast.
