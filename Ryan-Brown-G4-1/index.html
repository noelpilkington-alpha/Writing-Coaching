Clean Up: Redundancy and Comma Splices

Q1 and Q5 lost points to mechanical errors
Q1 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2 weighted points)
"Peter Tabichi received an award, the award he received was for being an amazing teacher."

The problem: "received an award" and "the award he received" say the same thing twice. This is redundancy. It makes the sentence confusing and costs you a point.

FIXED VERSION
"Peter Tabichi received an award for being an amazing teacher who used his own salary to help his students."
Q5 — YOUR ANSWER (1.5 out of 2 weighted points)
"Cody practiced kicking the ball every day, he wanted to surprise his coach at the next game."

The problem: This is a comma splice. Two complete sentences are joined with just a comma. Use "because" or a period instead.

FIXED VERSION
"Cody practiced kicking the ball every day because he wanted to surprise his coach at the next game."
Which sentence fixes the comma splice?
A "He scored the winning goal, everyone cheered for him."
B "He scored the winning goal, and everyone cheered for him."
THE QUICK CHECK
After you write a sentence, look for commas in the middle. Read what comes before the comma and what comes after. If both sides could stand alone as complete sentences, you have a comma splice. Fix it by adding "and," "because," "so," or replacing the comma with a period.

Add Specifics: Vague Answers Lose Points

Q7 and Q9 needed more Detail + Explanation

Ryan, on Q7 and Q9 you gave answers that were too short or too general. The grader needs to see a specific detail from the passage and an explanation of what it shows.

Q7 — YOUR ANSWER (2 out of 3 weighted points)
"Peter Tabichi helps his students a lot and that is good."

The problem: "Helps a lot" and "that is good" are vague. The question wants you to name what specifically he does and why it matters.

A STRONGER ANSWER
"Peter Tabichi uses 80% of his own salary to buy supplies for students who cannot afford them. This shows he cares more about his students' education than his own comfort, which is why he won the Global Teacher Prize."
Q9 — YOUR ANSWER (2 out of 3 weighted points)
"Cody surprised everyone."

The problem: Too brief. The question asks you to describe what happened and why it was a surprise.

A STRONGER ANSWER
"Cody surprised everyone at the game by scoring a goal with the kick he had been practicing in secret. This was a surprise because nobody on the team knew he had been training on his own after school."
THE RULE: DETAIL + EXPLANATION
Every answer that is worth 3 weighted points needs:

1. A specific detail — name what happened, who did it, or what was said
2. An explanation — "This shows..." or "This matters because..."

If your answer is only one short sentence, it is probably missing the explanation. Add one more sentence.
