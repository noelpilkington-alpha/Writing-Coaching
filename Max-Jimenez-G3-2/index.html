THE TRICK TO REMEMBER "BECAUSE"
A CAUSE is a reason. When you spell "because," say it out loud: BE + CAUSE. The word CAUSE is hidden right inside it. If you can spell CAUSE, you can spell BECAUSE.
★ ERROR 2 — Answering without a reason: On short-answer questions, you sometimes stop after the main idea and don't explain WHY. That costs you most of the points.
FROM G3.2 Q6 — 33% (1 out of 3 points)
Prompt: "Why do you think the Teddy Bear Toss is popular with fans?"
You wrote: "Because the team is donates the bears to children."

You had the right idea (donating the bears), but the answer was too short and had a small error ("the team is donates"). The question asks WHY it's popular — you needed to say more about what makes it fun or special.

FROM G3.2 Q7 — 33% (1 out of 3 points)
Prompt: "If you could change one thing about the event, what would you change?"
You wrote: "That the teddy bears made the employees do 30 minutes of hard work."

You named what you would change, but you didn't say WHY that change matters. Full-credit answers always include a reason.

Which answer earns full credit for "Why is the Teddy Bear Toss popular with fans?"
A Because the team donates the bears to children.
B The Teddy Bear Toss is popular because it is fun to throw the bears onto the ice, and fans feel good knowing the bears are donated to children who need them.
THE "BECAUSE + REASON" FORMULA
Every short-answer question can be answered like this:

[Answer] + because + [reason from your brain or the passage].

Example: "The event is popular because fans love throwing bears AND the bears go to kids who need them."

If your answer doesn't have the word "because" or "so" somewhere, it probably isn't done yet.

MODEL POLISHED PARAGRAPH (18–20/20 level)

The Camel Mobile Library is an organization in Kenya that helps kids read, and it has changed life for the people of Garissa. First, the library uses camels to carry books to villages that cars cannot reach. This shows that the people of Garissa really want to read, even in far-away places. Second, kids smile and cheer every time the camels arrive with new books. This shows that the library has made reading feel like a happy event, not a chore. In conclusion, the Camel Mobile Library has made the people of Garissa smarter, happier, and more excited about books.
