Polish #2: Appositives on Q4 & Q5

One tool, two questions, 1.5 points back
🔍 Pattern alert: Both Q4 and Q5 asked about appositives. You reached for "but" on Q4 (that is a conjunction, not an appositive) and guessed "became" on Q5 (that is a verb, not an appositive). The skill is brand-new — one minute of teaching unlocks both questions.

Renee, an appositive is a short phrase, set off by commas, that renames a noun right next to it. Think of it as a quick nickname or description tucked inside the sentence.

THE APPOSITIVE FORMULA
Noun, a short phrase that renames it, rest of sentence.

Examples:
• My dog, a noisy beagle, loves walks.
• Mr. Smith, our new coach, is very kind.
• The Eiffel Tower, a famous landmark in Paris, is 300 meters tall.

Notice: you can lift the appositive out and the sentence still works. "My dog loves walks." Still a full sentence. That is the test.

QUICK CHECK: CONJUNCTION vs APPOSITIVE
• Conjunction = joining word ("and," "but," "because," "so"). Joins two full ideas.
• Appositive = short phrase between commas that renames a noun. No verb of its own.

Trick: if the prompt says "use an appositive," your answer must have commas around a short phrase. If you wrote "but" or "and," you used the wrong tool.
