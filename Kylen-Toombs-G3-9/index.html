THE RULE: ECHO THE QUESTION FIRST
When a prompt asks "Why is X important?" or "Why should people do X?" — your answer should echo the question as a statement, THEN add the "because" part.

Prompt: "Why is honey important to the bees?"
✗ Fragment: "Because that is their food during the winter."
✓ Complete: "Honey is important to the bees because it is their food during the winter."

Prompt: "How do bees help plants grow?"
✗ Fragment: "Because the nectar turns into honey..."
✓ Complete: "Bees help plants grow because they carry pollen from flower to flower."
THE "ECHO TRICK" (USE THIS EVERY TIME)
Before you write, read the question and flip it into the start of your answer:

• "Why is X important?" → Start with "X is important because..."
• "Why should people do Y?" → Start with "People should do Y because..."
• "How does X help Y?" → Start with "X helps Y because..."

The question already gave you the start of your sentence. Use it.

Your #2 Error: Appositive Combine

G3.9 Q1 — you used a comma, but that made a run-on

Kylen, on G3.9 Q1 you were asked to combine two sentences using an appositive. You got halfway there — you used a comma and you kept the names in the right order. But the way you combined them turned the sentence into a comma splice (two complete thoughts joined by only a comma). That is a run-on in disguise, and it cost you half the points.

G3.9 Q1 — APPOSITIVE COMBINE (SCORED 0.5/1)
Prompt: "Combine Sentence 3 and Sentence 4 into one sentence using an appositive. Sentence 3: A colony has one leader. Sentence 4: She is the queen bee."
You wrote: "A colony has one leader, she is the queen bee."
"A colony has one leader" is a complete sentence. "She is the queen bee" is ALSO a complete sentence. Joining them with just a comma makes a comma splice.

A true appositive is a renaming noun phrase — not a full second sentence. It drops the "She is" part and just renames the noun with commas around it:

WHAT AN APPOSITIVE LOOKS LIKE
✓ "A colony has one leader, the queen bee." (renames "leader" — ends with a period)
✓ "A colony has one leader, the queen bee, who lays all the eggs."
✓ "The bees, pollen-carrying insects, buzz from flower to flower."

Notice: no "She is" or "He is" — the appositive is just the name or description, set off by commas.
G3.9 Q1 — which sentence correctly uses an appositive?
A A colony has one leader, she is the queen bee.
B A colony has one leader, the queen bee.
THE APPOSITIVE TEST
After you combine with commas, read the part between (or after) the commas. Ask: "Does this part have its own subject and verb?"

• If YES → you made a run-on. Drop the subject and verb, keep only the renaming phrase.
• If NO → it is a real appositive.

✗ "leader, she is the queen bee" ← "she is" = subject + verb → run-on
✓ "leader, the queen bee" ← no subject + verb → appositive
ONE MORE G3.9 SLIP WORTH FIXING
Q3 asked you to combine with "and" or "then":
You wrote: "The bees bring the nectar back to the hive then they turn it into honey." — scored 0.5/1.
That is another comma splice. When you join two full sentences with "then," you need a comma before it OR make them two sentences.

✓ "The bees bring the nectar back to the hive, and then they turn it into honey."
✓ "The bees bring the nectar back to the hive. Then they turn it into honey."
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Q1 and Q3 together cost you 1 full point on G3.9. Both are the same core skill: knowing when a comma is enough and when you need a joining word OR a period. Master this one rule and those points come home every test.
