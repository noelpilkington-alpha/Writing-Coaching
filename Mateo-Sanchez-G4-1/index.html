#1 Focus: What a True Appositive Is

Rename the noun with a noun phrase — not just add a location

Mateo, here is great news first: you already used a true appositive correctly in your Q11 paragraph — "Cody, Mud's friend, plays against him." That is exactly right. "Mud's friend" is a noun phrase that renames Cody. Be proud of that.

On Q3 the task asked you to add an appositive that gives more information about Peter Tabichi. Here is what you wrote:

Your Q3 (1 out of 2 points)
"Peter Tabichi, from Kenya, Africa, teaches high school science."

What happened: "from Kenya, Africa" tells us where he is from. That is a location phrase, not a true appositive. An appositive must rename the noun with another noun phrase — just like you did with "Mud's friend."

A TRUE APPOSITIVE
"Peter Tabichi, a dedicated science instructor, teaches high school science."

See the difference? "A dedicated science instructor" is a noun phrase that renames Peter Tabichi. That is what earns the full points.

Which sentence uses a TRUE appositive to rename the noun?
A"Peter Tabichi, from Kenya, Africa, teaches high school science."
B"Peter Tabichi, a prize-winning educator, teaches high school science."
THE RULE
A true appositive is a noun phrase set off by commas that renames the noun (a prize-winning educator, a dedicated coach, my best friend). A phrase that only tells where, when, or how (from Kenya, after school) is not an appositive. You already nailed this in Q11 — do it on Q3-type tasks too.

#2 Focus: Answer the Question the Prompt Asks

Open by answering the exact question, then use details as evidence

Mateo, your Q11 paragraph is full of vivid, specific detail — that is a real strength. But the prompt asked a precise question: "Explain how the events in the story cause Cody to change his opinions." Your paragraph retells the soccer play-by-play but never directly states how Cody's opinion changed.

The same thing happened on a couple of short answers. On Q6 and Q10 your ideas were there, but the response drifted instead of answering the exact question being asked.

THE FIX (3 PARTS)
1. Topic sentence that answers the exact question (here: name Cody's opinion and that it changed).
2. Details as evidence — your vivid details, used to support the answer.
3. Closing sentence that states the answer (what Cody now thinks).
The prompt asks how Cody's opinion changes. Which opening answers the question?
A"Cody is on the red team and Mud is on the blue team, and they play a soccer match."
B"At first Cody thought Mud was an easy player to beat, but the match changes his opinion completely."
THE RULE
Before you write, underline the question word in the prompt (how? why? what changed?). Your first sentence should answer that exact question, and your last sentence should restate the answer. Details belong in the middle as evidence — not as the whole paragraph.
