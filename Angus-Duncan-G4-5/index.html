Match Your Subject to Your Verb (Q9, Q11)

Singular subject → singular verb. The read-back catches it.

Angus, this is the #1 specific error on your test besides spelling. It showed up on Q9 and again inside your Q11 paragraph. The pattern is the same both times: a subject that needs a singular verb, paired with a plural verb by accident.

YOU WROTE (Q9)
"The Downwind family work together before each powwow to prepare for the uncoming event."
Two things to catch: “family” is a singular subject (one family), so the verb should be “works” — and “uncoming” should be “upcoming.”
YOU WROTE (Q11, sentence 2)
"First of all stories is a great way to keep culture alive."
“Stories” is plural (more than one story), so the verb should be “are,” not “is.”
THE RULE
The subject is who or what the sentence is about. The verb is the action. They have to match in number.

One thing → singular verb: The family works. The story is. My sister dances.
More than one thing → plural verb: The families work. The stories are. My sisters dance.

Tip: even if the group word sounds like many people (family, team, class), treat it as one group → singular verb.
ANGUS'S IDEAS, AGREEMENT FIXED
Q9: "The Downwind family works together before each powwow to prepare for the upcoming event."

Q11: "First of all, stories are a great way to keep culture alive."
Same meaning, same content. Just the verbs matched to the subjects. (And a comma after “First of all” as a bonus.)

THE PROOFREAD MOVE
On your read-back pass, find the subject (who or what) and the verb (the action) in each sentence. Ask: is the subject one thing or more than one thing? Does the verb match? This takes about 5 seconds per sentence once you practice it, and it protects you on every writing test you ever take.
