Question 4 Fix — Changing Sentence Types

Let's review this together.

You scored 0/2 on this question.

The question asked:

"Change sentence 10 into a question: The honey is food for the bees during the winter."

Which one is actually a question?
A During the winter bees use honey as food.
B Is honey food for the bees during the winter?
YOU WROTE
"During the winter bees use honey as food."

Robin, you rewrote the sentence but it is still a statement, not a question. A question needs to ask something and end with a question mark.

POSSIBLE QUESTIONS

"Is honey food for the bees during the winter?"

"What do bees eat during the winter?"

"Why do bees need honey in the winter?"

HOW TO CHANGE A STATEMENT INTO A QUESTION
Step 1: Pick a question word (Is, Do, What, Why, How, When, Where).
Step 2: Flip the subject and verb. "The honey is" becomes "Is the honey..."
Step 3: End with a question mark.
Quick check: Read it out loud — does your voice go up at the end? If so, it is a question.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
This question was worth 2 points and you got 0. Knowing how to flip a statement into a question is a skill that shows up on every writing test. The trick is simple once you know the steps.

Pattern 1: Words ending in "-ful." When you add "ful" to the end of a word (like "use" + "ful" or "help" + "ful"), it is always just one "l" — never two.
🔎
Pattern 2: "There" vs. "their." Use "their" when something belongs to someone ("their roots," "their tradition"). Use "there" for a place ("over there") or "there is/there are."

Question 1 Fix — Writing Appositives

Let's review this together.

You scored 1.0/2 on this question — you lost 1.0 point.

The question asked:

"Combine Sentence 3 and Sentence 4 into one sentence using an appositive."
Sentence 3: A colony has one leader.
Sentence 4: She is the queen bee.

Which sentence correctly uses an appositive?
A A colony's leader is called the queen bee.
B A colony has one leader, the queen bee, who controls the hive.
YOU WROTE
"A colonys leader is called the queen bee."

Robin, your answer makes sense but it is not using an appositive. An appositive is a phrase that renames a noun and is placed right next to it, set off by commas. Also, "colonys" needs an apostrophe: colony's.

CORRECT APPOSITIVE VERSION
"A colony has one leader, the queen bee, who controls the hive."
WHAT IS AN APPOSITIVE?
An appositive is a noun phrase that renames or describes another noun right next to it. It is set off by commas:
• My dog, a golden retriever, loves to swim. ("a golden retriever" renames "my dog")
• The leader, the queen bee, lays all the eggs. ("the queen bee" renames "the leader")
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Appositive questions show up on writing tests regularly. The key is putting the extra information between commas, right next to the noun it describes.
