Your #2 Repeat Error: Sentence Combining

Conjunctions vs. appositives vs. "because" — matching the tool to the job

David, the sentence-combining question is a tricky one because there are three different ways the test can ask you to combine two sentences. Each one uses a different tool. You have missed this on G3.4, G3.5, and G3.6 — usually by using the wrong tool for the job. Here are the three types:

The 3 Combining Tools
AND / BUT Conjunction. Join two equal ideas. "The books had golden spines, and they had colorful pages."
BECAUSE Cause-and-effect. The second idea is the REASON for the first. "They can hold on tightly because they have strong claws."
APPOSITIVE Rename with commas. Set off a phrase that renames the noun. "Porcupines, the slow movers of the forest, are rodents."
FROM G3.5 Q4 — APPOSITIVE (missed)
Sentences: "The books are still popular. They are known for their golden spines."
You wrote: "The books are still popular, they are great books, and they are known for their golden spines."
This adds a new idea ("great books") and uses "and" instead of an appositive. An appositive rephrases the noun.
THE APPOSITIVE VERSION
"The books, known for their golden spines, are still popular."
The phrase between the commas renames "the books." That is an appositive.
Which one is a correct "combine using because" answer?
A Porcupines have strong claws, and they hold on to branches tightly.
B Porcupines can hold on to branches tightly because they have strong claws and rough paw pads.
THE 2-STEP COMBINE MOVE
Step 1 — Read the directions. Look for the keyword: conjunction, because, or appositive. That keyword tells you which tool to use.

Step 2 — Make sure both ideas show up. Before you move on, find sentence 1 in your answer. Find sentence 2 in your answer. If one is missing, add it back.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
The combine question is worth 2 points. Missing it three tests in a row cost you 6 points total. Knowing which tool to grab — conjunction, because, or appositive — is the whole trick.

MODEL POLISHED PARAGRAPH (19–20/20 level)

Porcupines are cool animals and a big part of the ecosystem, but they can poke you if you touch them. Porcupines are covered with 30,000 quills. These quills are long, stiff hairs that protect them. If a porcupine feels threatened, it will lift its quills to scare predators away. This shows that a porcupine's body is built to keep it safe. Porcupines also spend a lot of time in trees because they can find food there. Their long claws and rough paw pads help them climb without getting hurt. This shows that even the porcupine's feet help it stay safe in the wild. In the end, a porcupine's quills, claws, and paw pads work together to protect it from danger.
