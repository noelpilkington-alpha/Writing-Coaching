Proofread: Verb Agreement

Small errors that cost real points

Aaron, on Q8 you wrote a strong opinion. But there is a grammar slip that cost you a point:

YOUR ANSWER — Q8 (2 out of 3)
"I don't think it matter if they named it or not because they aren't going to keep the seal."

The subject here is "it". When the subject is "it" (or "he" or "she"), the verb needs an -s on the end. "It matter" should be "it matters".

THE RULE
When the subject is he, she, or it, add -s to the verb in present tense:
• It matters (not "it matter")
• She runs (not "she run")
• He thinks (not "he think")

Quick check: Read the sentence out loud. If it sounds like something is missing at the end of the verb, add the -s.
CORRECTED VERSION
"I don't think it matters if they named it or not because they aren't going to keep the seal."
Which sentence has correct verb agreement?
A I don't think it matter if they named it or not.
B I don't think it matters if they named it or not.
WHY THIS MATTERS
Your reasoning on Q8 was actually strong. You gave a clear opinion and backed it up with logic ("because they aren't going to keep the seal"). That one missing -s is the only thing that separated you from full marks on this question. A quick re-read would catch it every time.

Your #1 Error: Appositive Structure

Q3 — 1 out of 2 points. Close, but not quite.

Aaron, Q3 asked you to add an appositive to a sentence about Sally the seal. Here is what you wrote:

YOUR ANSWER — Q3 (1 out of 2)
"Sally, a seal who swam to the other seals"

You got 1 out of 2 because what you wrote is a relative clause (it uses "who" and adds a verb), not an appositive. An appositive is a noun phrase that renames the subject. It does not have its own verb.

APPOSITIVE vs. RELATIVE CLAUSE
Appositive (noun phrase, no verb):
• Sally, a young grey seal, swam to the other seals.
• Sally, the smallest pup in the group, swam to the other seals.

Relative clause (has "who/which/that" + verb):
• Sally, a seal who swam to the other seals ← this has a verb ("swam"), so it is a relative clause, not an appositive.

The test: Can you remove it from the sentence and what remains still makes sense? Yes for both. But does it have its own verb? If yes, it is a relative clause. If no, it is an appositive.
FULL-MARK ANSWER
"Sally, a young grey seal, swam to the other seals."
The appositive "a young grey seal" renames Sally. No verb inside. Surrounded by commas.
Which of these adds a true appositive to the sentence?
A Sally, the fastest swimmer in the pod, raced toward the shore.
B Sally, who was the fastest swimmer, raced toward the shore.
HOW TO REMEMBER
When a question asks you to add an appositive, write a noun phrase between the commas. Ask yourself: "Does this chunk have a verb?" If the answer is yes, you have written a relative clause. Remove the verb and turn it into a describing phrase instead.

Your #2 Error: Explain WHY

Q7 and Q8 — strong opinions, missing the reason

Aaron, on Q7 and Q8 you gave good opinions. But both answers stopped one step short of full marks because they did not explain why.

YOUR ANSWER — Q7 (2 out of 3)
"I think the most meaningful moment was when the dad tried to free the pup but she wouldn't leave."

This tells us what you think. The question also wants to know why you think it. Why was that moment meaningful? What does it show about the relationship between the dad and the seal?

FULL-MARK VERSION
"I think the most meaningful moment was when the dad tried to free the pup but she wouldn't leave. This was meaningful because it shows how strong the bond between the seal and the family had become, even though they were trying to let her go."
YOUR ANSWER — Q8 (2 out of 3)
"I don't think it matter if they named it or not because they aren't going to keep the seal."

This one is closer because you did include a "because." But the reasoning stops short. Why does it not matter? What would naming it change? (Also note the verb error "matter" from Card 3.)

THE FORMULA FOR FULL MARKS
Step 1: State your opinion. ("I think...")
Step 2: Give the reason. ("...because...")
Step 3: Explain what that reason means. ("This shows..." or "That is why...")

If your answer only has Step 1, add the "because." If your answer has Steps 1 and 2, push to Step 3 to lock in the full marks.
Which answer would earn full marks on a "Do you think..." question?
A I think the most meaningful moment was when the dad tried to free the pup but she wouldn't leave.
B I think the most meaningful moment was when the dad tried to free the pup but she wouldn't leave, because it shows how strong the bond had become even though they knew they had to let her go.
WHY THIS MATTERS
On Q7 and Q8, you lost a combined 2 points by stopping at the opinion without the full "because + this shows" follow-through. Those 2 points are the difference between 84% and 89%. The thinking is already in your head. You just need to write it down.
