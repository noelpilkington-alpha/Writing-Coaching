Watch Your Homophones

Two sound-alike word mix-ups that cost you points

Your essay has two homophone errors — words that sound the same but are spelled differently and mean different things:

FROM YOUR THESIS
"Students can effectively learn languages though immersion, real communication, and starting at a young age."
FROM YOUR INTRO

The fixes:

though (means "however") → through (means "by way of")
here (means "in this place") → hear (means "to listen")

MEMORY TRICK
hear = your ear hears sound (the word "ear" is inside "hear").
through = going through something (it has the same letters as "thorough" — longer word, going all the way through).

These are tricky because spellcheck won't catch them — both spellings are real words. During proofreading, pause on "though/through" and "here/hear" and ask which meaning you need.

Transition Words: "While" vs. "Although"

Q9 — the one MCQ question you missed

Here's the question you missed:

Q9
Sentence 1: Some experts think older students learn better in structured lessons.
Sentence 2: Others argue that younger children pick up new sounds more easily.

You chose: Although
Correct answer: While

Why "While" is better here:

  • "While" is used when two ideas are being compared at the same time — both are presented as equally valid viewpoints. "While some experts think X, others argue Y" shows a balanced contrast.
  • "Although" is used when one idea is the main point and the other is a concession. "Although older students can learn, younger children learn better" would favor the second idea.

In this question, the passage presents both viewpoints without favoring either one, so "While" is the better fit because it keeps them balanced.

QUICK TEST
Ask: Are both ideas equal, or does one matter more?
Equal → use "While" (balanced contrast)
One matters more → use "Although" (concession + main point)
See examples of each
While (balanced): "While some students prefer reading, others prefer listening."
Although (concession): "Although reading is helpful, listening builds stronger pronunciation skills."

Notice how "although" makes the second idea the winner. "While" keeps them equal.

The Body Paragraph Pattern

You already use this well — here's the formal framework

Every body paragraph in a G6+ essay should follow this pattern. You're already doing most of this naturally — let's name the parts so you can be intentional about it:

Body Paragraph Formula
TS Topic Sentence — states the paragraph's main point
E1 Evidence 1 — a specific fact, quote, or example from the passage
X1 Explanation 1 — explain why that evidence matters
E2 Evidence 2 — a second fact or example from the passage
X2 Explanation 2 — explain why that evidence matters
CS Concluding Sentence — wraps up the paragraph's main idea
