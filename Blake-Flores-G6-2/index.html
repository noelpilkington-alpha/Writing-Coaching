Error #1 — Conjunction Logic

Q3 — 0 out of 1 — picking the word that matches the relationship between ideas

Blake, you got 9 out of 10 multiple choice questions right. The one miss was Q3 — combining two sentences with a conjunction. Here is the item you saw:

The prompt asked you to combine: "Animated movies are fun. They also include valuable lessons."

YOUR ANSWER
"Animated movies are fun because they include valuable lessons."

The problem: "because" means one idea causes the other. It says the lessons are the reason the movies are fun. But the two original sentences are not about cause — they are two separate true things about animated movies.

Which conjunction correctly combines: "Animated movies are fun. They also include valuable lessons."
A Animated movies are fun because they include valuable lessons.
B Animated movies are fun but they include valuable lessons.
THE CONJUNCTION DECISION TREE
Before you pick a conjunction, read the two sentences and ask: what is the actual relationship between them?

• Does one CAUSE the other? → because, so, therefore
• Are they contrasting or surprisingly coexisting? → but, yet, however
• Are they simply both true? → and, also, in addition
• Is one conditional on the other? → if, when, unless

The test: can you say "X happens as a result of Y"? If not, "because" is wrong. On Q3, animated movies are not fun as a result of having lessons. They are fun AND they have lessons. Two separate facts — that means "but" or "and," not "because."
