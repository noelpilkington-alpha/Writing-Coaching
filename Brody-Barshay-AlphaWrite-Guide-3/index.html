A Naming Phrase

Set 3 of 5. You find appositives every time. This set is about building one
THE RULE
An appositive is a naming phrase. It gives the thing another name. The check: cover the noun. Can your phrase stand in its place? It is a naming word with its describing words, often with a, an, the or my in front, and it has no verb inside.

Find the Is A

Which sentence holds the naming phrase, and what happens to the other one
THE RULE
Find the is a. The other sentence stays whole. One of the two sentences says what the thing is: is a, is an, are, was a. The words after it are the naming phrase. Cut It is or They are. The other sentence keeps every word and its verb.

Two of yours:

YOU WROTE, G3.1 QUESTION 1
“My famly has two friendly dogs, their names are Lucy and Rocket.” TWO SENTENCES, ONE COMMA
Two whole sentences glued with a comma. The test calls it a comma splice and scores it zero.

Three Slots

Same phrase, same sentence. Only the commas move
THE RULE
The slot moves the commas, not the words. Beginning: phrase, comma, sentence. Middle: noun, comma, phrase, comma, the rest. End: sentence, comma, phrase. Same naming phrase, same whole sentence, every time.
