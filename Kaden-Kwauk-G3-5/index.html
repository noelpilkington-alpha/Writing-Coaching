#1 Focus: What a True Appositive Is

Question 4 — a comma splice vs. a real appositive

Kaden, Question 4 asked you to combine two sentences using an appositive. Here is what you wrote:

YOU WROTE (G3.5 Q4)
"The books are still popular, they are known for their golden spines."
This joins two complete sentences with only a comma. That is a comma splice, not an appositive.
WHAT A TRUE APPOSITIVE IS
An appositive renames or describes a noun with a phrase placed right next to it, set off by commas. It stays inside one sentence. You already spotted one perfectly on Question 5 — you identified "a best-selling story" as the appositive. Here is one in action:
A TRUE APPOSITIVE (renames the noun)
The Poky Little Puppy, a best-selling story, is still loved today.

Now watch your Question 4 sentence become a real appositive. Keep the same two ideas, but tuck the describing phrase between commas:

YOUR SENTENCE, FIXED
The books, known for their golden spines, are still popular.
Which one is a real appositive, not a comma splice?
A The books are still popular, they are known for their golden spines.
B The books, known for their golden spines, are still popular.
THE RULE
To build an appositive, take the extra fact about your noun and drop it right after the noun, wrapped in commas. Do not join two whole sentences with just a comma — that is a comma splice. Keep it all inside one sentence.

#2 Focus: Lead With Your Answer, Stay On Topic

Question 11 — put the answer first, then build on it

Kaden, the paragraph prompt asked WHY Jason placed his sculptures under the sea. You knew the answer — to protect the coral reefs — but it landed in the middle, after details about how to see the art. Your first sentence is your best chance to answer the question. Put the answer there.

The prompt asks WHY Jason placed his statues under the sea. Which opening answers the question?
A To see Jason's artwork you can scuba, snorkel, or ride a glass bottom boat.
B Jason placed his statues under the sea to protect the coral reefs.
LEAD, THEN DEVELOP, THEN STOP
1. First sentence — answer the exact question. Why did Jason do it? To protect the coral reefs.

2. Next sentences — develop the reason. Real reefs can be damaged when too many divers and swimmers crowd around them, so his statues give people somewhere else to visit.

3. Cut anything that does not support the answer. Lines like "Sam went to visit" and "it was very cool" can go. They do not explain the WHY.

YOUR IDEA, LEADING WITH THE ANSWER

Jason chose to place his statues under the sea to protect the coral reefs. Real reefs can be damaged when too many divers and swimmers crowd around them, so Jason built his statues nearby to draw those visitors away. Over the years, sponges and seaweed grew over the statues and sea creatures moved in, which shows the art is becoming its own safe home for ocean life. By putting his work underwater, Jason gave people something amazing to visit while keeping the coral reefs safe.
