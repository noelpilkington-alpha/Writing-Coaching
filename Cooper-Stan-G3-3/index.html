An appositive is a short naming phrase that goes right after a noun, between commas, and tells the reader more about it. The question wanted you to tell us more about Sally:

THE FULL-MARKS VERSION
“Sally, the baby seal, swam to the other seals.”
The original sentence stays. The highlighted part is the appositive — it tells the reader who Sally is. Even if you were not sure what an appositive was, typing the whole sentence back would have earned a point. One word earned none.

Your Paragraph: A Starter Kit

There is nothing to review this time — so here is how to build one next time

The paragraph question always looks the same. It gives you an article, and it asks you to explain something about it. Here was this one:

“Explain how Satoshi Tajiri’s childhood influenced the creation of Pokémon.”

The article had already given you the answer. Satoshi loved collecting bugs when he was a boy. When he grew up, he made a game where people collect creatures. That is the whole connection, and a paragraph is just that connection said in four or five sentences.

The four-sentence plan — type it in the box, then fill it in
1Answer the question in one sentence, using the question’s own words plus “because”
2One fact from the article about his childhood
3One fact from the article about the game
4Say how they connect — “That is how…”
Tap to see the plan filled in
Five sentences — this is what a full-credit paragraph looks like
A PARAGRAPH FOR THIS QUESTION

“Satoshi Tajiri’s childhood influenced Pokémon because he loved collecting bugs when he was a boy. He would catch insects and study them for hours. When he grew up, he made a game where players catch and collect creatures. The creatures in the game are like the bugs he collected as a kid. That is how a boy who loved bugs grew up to create Pokémon.”

Notice that every sentence in there is a sentence you can already write. Sentence 1 is the question turned around with a because — the move from card 3. Sentences 2 and 3 are facts from the article. Sentences 4 and 5 say how they fit together. There is nothing here you have not already done on Q6, Q8 and Q10.
