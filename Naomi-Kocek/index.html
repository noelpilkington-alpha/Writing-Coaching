Q3 — "Because" vs. "And"

Cause-and-effect vs. parallel actions

The question: Combine these two sentences: "The library kept the timber beams." + "It added shelves of books and reading corners."

YOU CHOSE
"Because the library kept the timber beams, it added shelves of books and reading corners."

This implies that keeping the beams caused them to add shelves. But there's no cause-and-effect here — keeping beams didn't make them add bookshelves. These are two separate things that happened as part of the same renovation.

CORRECT ANSWER
"The library kept the timber beams, and it added shelves of books and reading corners."

These are parallel actions — two things that happened together — connected with "and."

TIP: THE "BECAUSE" TEST
Before using "because," ask yourself: "Did the first thing actually cause the second thing?" Keeping beams didn't cause adding shelves — they're both part of the renovation.

"Because" = one thing caused another: "The library opened, and people came because they wanted to see the new space."
"And" = two things happened together: "The library kept the beams, and it added new shelves."

In your essay, you actually use "because" well! This was just a tricky MCQ where the cause-and-effect framing sounded plausible but didn't hold up logically.

Q9 — Which Sentence Doesn't Belong?

Programs vs. commerce — precise categorization

Paragraph topic: "programs at the library"

YOU CHOSE (as the irrelevant sentence)
"Monthly talks drew people from all over the city."

This actually is about a library program — talks are organized activities, which makes them programs. This sentence belongs in the paragraph.

CORRECT ANSWER (the sentence that doesn't belong)
"Weekend markets sold fresh bread, fruit, and crafts."

Markets are commerce, not library programs. Selling bread and fruit is a commercial activity — not an organized educational or cultural program.

TIP: DEFINE THE CATEGORY PRECISELY
"Programs" means organized activities: talks, classes, storytelling, workshops. A market where people buy food is a commercial activity, not a program. When answering these questions, define the paragraph topic precisely and check each sentence against that definition.

Interestingly, you actually discussed markets in Paragraph 4 of your essay — and correctly placed them in the paragraph about economic impact, not programs. So you understand this distinction in your own writing. The MCQ just required sharper categorization under time pressure.
