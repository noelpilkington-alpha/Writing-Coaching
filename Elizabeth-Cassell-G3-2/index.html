Skill #1: Finish Every Sentence

Q1 lost all points because the answer was not complete

Elizabeth, on Q1 you started writing an answer but did not finish it. An unfinished answer earns zero points, even if the beginning was heading in the right direction.

Q1 — YOUR ANSWER (0 out of 2 weighted points)
(Answer was left unfinished)

The rule: Every answer must be a complete sentence. A complete sentence has a subject (who or what), a verb (what they do), and ends with a period.

WHAT A COMPLETE ANSWER LOOKS LIKE
"The Teddy Bear Toss happens when fans throw stuffed animals onto the ice to donate them to children."
THE COMPLETE SENTENCE CHECK
Before you move to the next question, ask yourself:

1. Does my sentence have a who or what? (subject)
2. Does it say what they do or what happens? (verb)
3. Does it end with a period?

If any of those is missing, your sentence is not finished. Keep writing until all three are there.

The rule: When the question says "What do YOU think?" or "In YOUR opinion," the answer must come from your own brain. You cannot just repeat words from the passage.

EXAMPLE: YOUR OWN OPINION
Question: "What do you think the event shows about the fans?"

Wrong: Copying a sentence from the passage.
Right: "I think the event shows that the fans are kind because they give away their own stuffed animals to help children they do not even know."
THE "MY OWN WORDS" CHECK
Before you submit, ask:

1. Did the question ask what I think?
2. Am I using my own words, not copying from the passage?
3. Is my answer about the right subject? (If it asks about fans, write about fans, not the team.)

Start your answer with "I think..." or "In my opinion..." to remind yourself to use your own ideas.

Skill #3: Write at Least 3 Sentences on Q11

7 out of 20 — only 1 sentence was written

Elizabeth, your paragraph on Q11 earned 7 out of 20 because it was only one sentence long. Q11 is worth 20 points — almost half the test. Writing more sentences is the single biggest way to raise your score.

THE MINIMUM FOR Q11
Write at least 3 sentences:

Sentence 1: Answer the question directly. (Example: "I think the Teddy Bear Toss is a good tradition.")

Sentence 2: Give a detail from the passage that supports your answer. (Example: "In the passage, the fans throw hundreds of stuffed animals every year.")

Sentence 3: Explain what that detail shows or why it matters. (Example: "This shows that many people care about helping children, which is why the tradition keeps going.")

Three sentences. That is the minimum goal. If you can write more, write more. But always get to at least three.

B "I think the Teddy Bear Toss is a kind tradition. The passage says that fans throw thousands of stuffed bears onto the ice every year. This shows that the fans care about helping other people, which is why the event keeps happening."
