Always Write the Paragraph

This is the #1 way to raise your score

Armin, the paragraph question (Q11) is worth 20 out of 45 points — that's almost half the entire test. On this test, you left it blank, which means you lost all 20 points automatically.

THE MOST IMPORTANT RULE
Always write something. Even 2–3 sentences can earn you 10 or more points in partial credit. A blank answer always gets 0.

Here's what even a short attempt could look like. The prompt for Q11 was about "Sports and Society":

EVEN THIS WOULD EARN POINTS
Sports have a big effect on society. One main point is that sports bring people together because fans cheer for the same team. Another main point is that sports teach kids about teamwork.

That's only 3 sentences, and it would earn significant partial credit because it has a topic sentence and two details.

Here's the G3 paragraph plan you should follow:

G3 Expository Paragraph Plan
TS = Topic Sentence • D = Detail from the article • W = Why it matters • CS = Concluding Sentence
TS State your main point — turn the prompt into a sentence.
D1 First detail or example from the passage.
W Explain why this detail matters or what it shows.
D2 Second detail or example from the passage.
W Explain why this detail matters or what it shows.
D3 Third detail or example from the passage.
W Explain why this detail matters or what it shows.
CS Wrap up — restate your main point in different words.
IF YOU'RE RUNNING OUT OF TIME
Write at least a topic sentence and one detail with an explanation. That alone can earn you 8–10 points. Never leave Q11 blank.

Finish the Thought with WHY

Use "because" to complete your explanations

On several questions, you gave a good start but didn't explain why. That's where you lost partial credit. Look at the pattern:

Q8 — What was the most important moment?

YOU WROTE
The most important moment was when the breeze and birds began to guide her.

Good — you picked a moment. But the question asks you to explain why it was important, and you stopped too soon.

WITH THE WHY
The most important moment was when the breeze and birds began to guide her because without them, Layla might have gotten lost on the trail.

Q10 — What lesson does this story teach?

YOU WROTE
They should never take something that belongs to nature.

That's a good lesson — but you need to explain why or connect it to the story.

WITH THE WHY
This story teaches that you should never take something that belongs to nature because Layla learned that the geode should stay where it was so others could enjoy it too.
THE "BECAUSE" TRICK
Whenever a question asks you to "explain" or says "why," use this formula:

[Your answer] + because + [your reason].

This one habit can pick up several extra points on every test.

Grammar & Spelling Fixes

Small errors that cost you points

You had a few grammar and spelling mistakes that are easy to fix once you know the pattern:

Q3 — Spelling: "goof" instead of "good"

YOU WROTE
Did this geode feel like a goof souvenir?

You formed the question correctly — great job on the interrogative sentence! But "goof" should be "good." A quick re-read would catch this.

Q7 — Grammar: "would shown" instead of "would show"

I would shown the geode → I would show the geode

After "would," always use the base form of the verb: would show, would keep, would tell — never "would shown" or "would kept."

Q7 — Spelling: "satys" instead of "stays"

it satys in a safe place → it stays in a safe place

Q9 — Unclear sentence and fragment

YOU WROTE
Because if the Cooper would've taken the geode home it wouldn't be safe.

This is confusing — who is "the Cooper"? Also, starting with "Because" makes this a fragment, not a complete sentence.

CLEARER VERSION
If Layla had taken the geode home, it would not have been safe because it belongs in nature where it can be protected.
QUICK FIX RULES
• After "would," use the base verb: would show, would take, would keep
• Re-read for swapped letters: "satys" → stays, "goof" → good
• Use character names from the passage — don't make up names
• Don't start a sentence with "Because" by itself — make it a full sentence
