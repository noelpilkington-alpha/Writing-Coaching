EXAMPLE 3 OF 3 — FROM G6.4 Q3 (Incorrect)
Prompt: "Choose the sentence that correctly uses 'because':"
You picked: "Students learn new words, because they hear the language in every part of class."
Correct answer: "Students learn new words because they hear the language in every part of class." (no comma)
THE RULE
"Because" is a subordinating conjunction — a word that joins two parts of a sentence. When "because" is in the middle of the sentence, you do NOT put a comma right before or after it.

✗ "I ran, because I was late."
✓ "I ran because I was late."

Exception: When "because" starts the sentence, the comma goes at the END of the because-clause (not around "because" itself):
✓ "Because I was late, I ran."
WHY YOU MIGHT KEEP PICKING THE COMMA VERSION
The comma version sounds like a pause, so it feels natural. But on a standardized writing test, the test is looking for the grammatically correct version, not the most "spoken-like" version. When you see "because" in the middle of a sentence, the correct answer is almost always the one without extra commas.
Which sentence uses "because" correctly?

B Immersion works because students hear the language all day.

Transition Words: "While" vs. "Although"

You picked the wrong transition on G6.4 Q9 — here's how to choose next time

Gardner, on G6.4 Q9 you picked "Although" when the correct answer was "While". These two words look interchangeable, but they aren't. Let's break it down:

FROM G6.4 Q9 (Incorrect)
Prompt: "Which transition best connects these two sentences? Sentence 1: Some experts think older students learn better in structured lessons. Sentence 2: Others argue that younger children pick up new sounds more easily."
You picked: Although
Correct answer: While
THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN "ALTHOUGH" AND "WHILE"
Both show contrast, but they have different shades of meaning:

Although = "even though / in spite of the fact that" — used when one side is the correct / stronger side.
Example: "Although it was raining, we went for a walk." (We won out over the rain.)

While = "at the same time / whereas" — used when you are showing two viewpoints side by side with equal weight.
Example: "While some prefer dogs, others prefer cats." (Both views are equally valid.)

In the G6.4 question, the two sentences presented two expert viewpoints side by side — neither one was declared the correct view. That is exactly when "While" fits.

Which transition best connects two sentences that present two viewpoints with equal weight?
A Although
B While
QUICK TRANSITION CHEAT SHEET
Contrast (two equal views): While, Whereas, On the other hand
Contrast (one side wins / concession): Although, Even though, Despite
Cause: Because, Since, As
Effect: Therefore, So, As a result
Adding information: Also, In addition, Furthermore, Moreover
Example: For instance, For example, Such as
Conclusion: In conclusion, Overall, Ultimately
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
G6 tests Q9 with a transition word question on almost every form. Know the 6 categories above and you'll pick the right word every time. Q9 is 1 free point per test you don't have to lose.

MODEL POLISHED INTRODUCTION (18–20/20 level)

Learning a new language can feel impossible, especially for kids who don't have the attention span for long lessons. BODY 3 Topic sentence + quote from passage + explanation + transition
