THE 5-PARAGRAPH ESSAY STRUCTURE
Every G6+ essay follows this pattern:
5-Paragraph Essay Blueprint
INTRO Hook + background + thesis statement (your main argument in one sentence)
BODY 1 First reason/point + evidence from the passage + explanation
BODY 2 Second reason/point + evidence from the passage + explanation
BODY 3 Third reason/point + evidence from the passage + explanation
CONCLUSION Restate thesis in new words + final thought that wraps back to your main idea

Q5: What Is "Meaningful" Expansion?

The one multiple choice question you missed

You scored 9 out of 10 on multiple choice — impressive! The only one you missed was Q5, which asked about kernel expansion. Let's look at why.

WHAT IS KERNEL EXPANSION?
A kernel sentence is a basic, simple sentence. Expanding it means adding detail that makes the sentence more meaningful — detail that advances the story, explains something important, or adds real information.

Not all detail is equal. Some detail is cosmetic (how something looks) and some is meaningful (what actually happened, why it matters).
YOUR ANSWER (WRONG)
You chose: "Wilma smiled wide at the spectators."
This adds a visual detail (how she smiled) but does not advance the story or add important information.
CORRECT ANSWER
"Wilma finally walked again at age 12 after years of therapy."
This adds meaningful detail: what happened (walked again), when (age 12), and why it matters (years of therapy). It moves the story forward.
Which sentence is a meaningful expansion of the kernel "The dog ran"?
A "The dog ran happily with its tongue out."
B "The dog ran three blocks to chase the thief and cornered him behind the store."
THE RULE FOR KERNEL EXPANSION
When choosing a kernel expansion, ask yourself: "Does this detail tell me something NEW and IMPORTANT about what happened?"

• Meaningful detail = advances the story, explains cause/effect, adds when/where/why
• Cosmetic detail = describes appearance, adds emotion words, decorates without informing

On the test, always pick the option that adds new information about what happened, not just how it looked.

MODEL ESSAY (18–20/20 LEVEL)

Introduction: Wilma Rudolph is one of America's greatest track athletes. Although she faced a life-threatening illness as a young child, she never gave up, and her story proves that perseverance can overcome any obstacle.

Body 1: When Wilma was younger, she got polio and could not move. Her mother helped her do walking exercises every day to strengthen her legs. This shows that even when things seemed impossible, Wilma and her family refused to quit.

Body 2: By the time she was 12, Wilma could finally walk without her leg brace. She joined her school's track team and won so many races that she qualified for the Olympics. This proves that her years of hard work had turned her from someone who could not walk into a world-class runner.

Body 3: During the 1960 Olympics in Rome, Wilma won three gold medals and became the first American woman to win that many in a single Olympics. This was the ultimate reward for her perseverance — she went from being unable to move to standing on the world's biggest stage.

Conclusion: Even after retiring from the Olympics, Wilma teaches children to never give up and reach for their dreams. Her journey from polio to three gold medals shows that with enough perseverance, anything is possible.
