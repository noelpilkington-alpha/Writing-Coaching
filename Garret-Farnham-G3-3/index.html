You consistently add or swap letters: "meaningfull" (extra L), "woul" (missing D), "helthy" (missing A). The fix: after writing, read each word and ask "does this look right?" Words with missing letters look short. Words with extra letters look long.

CORRECTED
"meaningful" (one L at the end) • "would" (ends in -ould) • "healthy" (heal + thy)
THE FIX: SAY IT SLOWLY
After you write a word that has more than one syllable, say it in your head slowly:
• "mean-ing-ful" — you hear ONE "ful" not "full"
• "would" — you hear the D at the end
• "heal-thy" — you hear "heal" which has an A
Which spelling is correct?
A"meaningfull"
B"meaningful"
SPELLING RULE
The suffix "-ful" always has one L: meaningful, helpful, careful, beautiful. If your word ends in "-full" (two L's), remove one.

Step 5: Check the Passage

Q2 (1/2) — wrong tense from the passage

Q2 (1/2): You wrote "frees" (present tense) but the passage says Sally was "freed" (past tense) from a fishnet. You also wrote that someone "put her in the water" but the passage says she was freed from the net — a different action.

STEP 5: CHECK THE PASSAGE
Before you submit an answer about the passage:
1. Go back to the passage
2. Find the sentence that matches your answer
3. Check: did I use the same tense? Did I describe what actually happened?
The passage says "Sally was freed from the fishnet." Which answer correctly uses this fact?
A"The rescuers frees Sally and put her in the water."
B"The rescuers freed Sally from the fishnet so she could swim again."
THE HABIT
Any time your answer refers to something from the passage, take 5 seconds to look back at the passage and confirm you are using the right words and tense. This one habit saves 1 to 2 points per test.
