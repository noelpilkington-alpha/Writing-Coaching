Your #1 Error: Read the Question Type

Q3 asked for a question. You wrote a statement.

Brenna, this is the most important card in this review. On Q3, the question asked you to write an interrogative sentence (a question). Here is what you wrote instead:

YOUR ANSWER — Q3 (0 out of 2)
"The geode was a absolutly perfect souvenir."

This is a declarative sentence (a statement). The question specifically asked for an interrogative, which means it must end with a question mark and ask something. You lost all 2 points because the sentence type was wrong, even though the content was good.

WHAT AN INTERROGATIVE VERSION LOOKS LIKE
"Was the geode an absolutely perfect souvenir?"
This asks a question, uses a question mark, and includes the word "geode" from the prompt. That is what the question was looking for.
THE FOUR SENTENCE TYPES
Declarative = a statement. Ends with a period. "The butterfly landed on the flower."
Interrogative = a question. Ends with a question mark. "Did the butterfly land on the flower?"
Exclamatory = shows strong feeling. Ends with an exclamation mark. "What a beautiful butterfly!"
Imperative = gives a command. "Look at that butterfly."
The question says: "Write an interrogative sentence about Layla's camera." Which answer earns full marks?
A Layla's camera took beautiful pictures of the butterflies.
B Did Layla use her camera to photograph the butterflies?
C What an amazing camera Layla brought on the trip!
YOUR PRE-WRITING STEP
Before you write your answer, circle or underline the sentence type the question asks for. If it says "interrogative," your sentence MUST end with a question mark. If it says "exclamatory," it MUST end with an exclamation mark. Check the punctuation at the end of your sentence before moving on.

Your #2 Error: Verb Tense Consistency

Present verbs slipping into past-tense stories

Brenna, this error showed up twice on G3.4. When the story is told in the past tense, every verb needs to stay in the past. Here are your examples:

Q1 (1 out of 2)
"Layla turn around and looked at the flower."
The story is in the past. "Looked" is past tense, but "turn" is present tense. It should be "turned".
Q9 (2 out of 3)
"Nature help Layla..."
Same pattern. The story already happened. "Help" is present tense. It should be "helped".

Notice the pattern: both times you dropped the -ed ending. The content of your answers was good, but the missing "-ed" cost you points each time.

THE RULE
If the passage is in the past tense (words like "walked," "saw," "found"), every verb you write about that story must also be in the past tense. Check: does your verb end in -ed (or is it an irregular past form like "saw," "found," "ran")?
The story says Layla "walked through the forest and found a geode." Which follow-up sentence has correct verb tense?
A She pick up the geode and put it in her bag.
B She picked up the geode and put it in her bag.
YOUR CHECK
After writing your sentence, read it out loud quietly. Does "Layla turn around" sound right? Or does "Layla turned around" sound right? Your ear will catch it. Trust it.

Use the Exact Conjunction

Q2 asked for "since" — you used "so"

On Q2, the question told you to combine two sentences using the word "since." Here is what you wrote:

YOUR ANSWER — Q2 (1 out of 2)
"Layla saw a butterfly so she took out her camera."

Your sentence makes sense and the content is correct. But the question specifically required the word "since," and you used "so" instead. That cost you a full point.

WHAT THE QUESTION WANTED
"Since Layla saw a butterfly, she took out her camera."
Same idea. Same two parts joined. But using the exact word the question asked for.
THE RULE
When a question says "combine these sentences using the word ___," that exact word must appear in your answer. It is not enough to use a word that means something similar. The directions are the directions.
The question says: "Combine these sentences using the word 'because'." Which answer follows the directions?
A Layla wanted to take a picture so the butterfly was beautiful.
B Layla wanted to take a picture because the butterfly was beautiful.
YOUR CHECK
After writing your combined sentence, scan it for the exact word the question asked you to use. Can you find it? If not, rewrite until that word is there.
