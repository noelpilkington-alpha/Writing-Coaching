The Body Paragraph Pattern

How to build a strong body paragraph every time

Every body paragraph in a G6+ essay should follow this pattern:

Body Paragraph Formula
TS Topic Sentence — states the paragraph's main point
E1 Evidence 1 — a specific fact, quote, or example from the passage
X1 Explanation 1 — explain why that evidence matters
E2 Evidence 2 — a second fact or example from the passage
X2 Explanation 2 — explain why that evidence matters
CS Concluding Sentence — wraps up the paragraph's main idea

Here's what your BP3 (community involvement) would look like with this pattern fully applied:

EXAMPLE BODY PARAGRAPH
[TS] The community's involvement was the final key to restoring the Mithi River. [E1] Scientists invited local families, schools, and shop owners to events where everyone helped pick up plastic and other trash from the riverbanks. [X1] This was important because the cleanup was too large for Earth5R and the scientists alone — they needed the whole community to make a real difference. [E2] As word spread, more and more people joined, and over the next few months the community removed over fourteen tonnes of trash. [X2] The growing turnout showed that once people saw real results, they became motivated to keep helping, which created a cycle of improvement. [CS] By bringing the community together, Earth5R proved that lasting change requires everyone's participation.
THE KEY RULE
Every E (evidence) must be followed by an X (explanation). Evidence without explanation is just a list of facts. The explanation is where you show the reader why that evidence supports your point. Ask yourself: "So what? Why does this matter?"

Spelling & Grammar Errors

Several recurring spelling mistakes to fix

Your essay has multiple spelling errors that cost you points. Here are the ones to learn:

communitys → communities
comming → coming
sighn → sign
becuase → because
unhealthyness → unhealthiness
familys → families
PATTERN TO NOTICE
Two of your errors are the same type: communitys and familys. When a word ends in a consonant + Y, you drop the Y and add -ies to make it plural. Community → communities. Family → families.
See more examples of this rule
city → cities • party → parties • story → stories • country → countries
Exception: If a vowel comes before the Y, just add -s: day → days, key → keys.
GRAMMAR NOTE
