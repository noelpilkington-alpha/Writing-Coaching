#1 Fix: Appositive Placement

Q3 — 1 out of 2 weighted points

Ozzie, Q3 asked you to combine two sentences using an appositive. You had the right pieces, but the word order got scrambled, so the appositive landed in the wrong spot and the sentence did not work.

Q3 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2 weighted points)
“Sharyl, spends hours rolling metal cones by hand, is ajingle-dress dancer.”

The problem: the phrase you put between the commas, “spends hours rolling metal cones by hand,” is an action (it has a verb), not a naming phrase. An appositive is a noun phrase that renames the noun and sits right after it. Here, the naming phrase is “a jingle-dress dancer” — that is what belongs right after “Sharyl.”

APPOSITIVE IN THE RIGHT SPOT
“Sharyl, a jingle-dress dancer, spends hours rolling metal cones by hand.”
THE APPOSITIVE RULE
An appositive:
• Is a noun phrase — it renames the noun (here, tells us what Sharyl is)
• Sits right after that noun, between commas
• The rest of the sentence keeps going normally afterward

Test it: read the sentence without the commas part. “Sharyl spends hours rolling metal cones by hand” should still make sense — and it does. That is how you know the appositive is placed right.
Which sentence places the appositive right after the noun it renames?
ASharyl, spends hours rolling cones, is a jingle-dress dancer.
BSharyl, a jingle-dress dancer, spends hours rolling cones by hand.
WHY THIS MATTERS
Q3 is worth 2 weighted points and you earned 1. You already knew the naming phrase — you just placed it wrong. Put the noun phrase right after the noun, between commas, and you lock in a structure you will see again.

#2 Fix: Answer the Exact Question

Q10, Q8 & Q9 — do what the prompt asks, match subject and verb

Ozzie, Q10 lost the most points here for one reason: it did not answer the exact question. The prompt asked you to compare a special outfit your family wears to the powwow clothing — to show how they are alike. You named an outfit, but you never made the comparison.

Q10 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 3 weighted points)
“One special outfit for my family would be our matching cats outfits that we wear for parties.”

The problem: this names an outfit, but the prompt asked how it is like the powwow clothing. Naming is only half the job — the points come from the comparison.

AN ANSWER THAT COMPARES
