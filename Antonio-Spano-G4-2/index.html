YOUR SPELLING SWEEP
After you finish an answer, do this before you move on:

1. Read the sentence slowly, one word at a time, in your head.
2. On any word longer than about six letters, sound it out by its parts.
• "be-cause" — you hear "cause," so it ends in "-cause," not "-cuse."
• "dan-de-li-on" — you hear "li-on" at the end, not "li-an."
• "ex-o-skel-e-ton" — you hear "ton" at the end, not "ten."
3. If a word looks even a little off, fix it. Trust that feeling — it is almost always right.

A FULLER COMBINE (full marks level)
"When the wildflowers bloom in spring, roadsides, hills, and meadows take on a whole new look."

The sentence is just as smooth, but now it keeps the specific detail — what causes the new look (the wildflowers blooming in spring). That detail is the half point.
You are combining "The bluebonnets bloom in spring." and "They cover the hills with blue." Which combine earns full credit?

THE QUICK CHECK
After you combine, glance back at the original pieces and ask: "Is every detail still in my new sentence?" If a noun, a place, a time, or a describing word disappeared, add it back. Smooth is good — smooth and complete is full credit.
