Q6: Why Were Her Actions Brave?

Skill: Explain why something was brave

The question: Write one sentence that explains why Claudette Colvin's actions were brave.

YOU WROTE

Two things to fix here:

1. Spelling: "qa" should be "a" — likely a typo.

2. The logic: Your sentence says her actions were brave because people treated her as a troublemaker. But being treated badly is what happened after — it's the consequence, not the reason she was brave.

She was brave because she risked being treated badly and did it anyway.

STRONGER VERSION

CAUSE vs CONSEQUENCE
Cause (why it was brave): She did it despite the risk.
Consequence (what happened after): People treated her as a troublemaker.

When a question asks why, give the cause — not what happened next. A good check: does your "because" explain the reason before or the result after?

Q10: Be Specific — Avoid Vague Words

Skill: Explain how a story reminds us about history

The question: Write one sentence that explains how Colvin's story reminds us about history.

YOU WROTE
It talks about how this act changed history.

The idea is there, but the sentence is too vague. Two problems:

  • "It" — what does "it" refer to? The story? The book? The reader doesn't know.
  • "this act" — which act? Refusing to move? Being arrested? Be specific.
STRONGER VERSION
Colvin's story reminds us that ordinary people can change history by standing up for what is right, even when it is scary.
THE RULE
Avoid starting sentences with "It" or "This" unless the reader already knows exactly what you mean. Instead, name the thing directly:

"It talks about..." → "Colvin's story shows..."
"This act changed..." → "Her decision to stay in her seat changed..."
