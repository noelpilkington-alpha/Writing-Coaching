"Would Have," Not "Would Of"

Same mistake on G3.3 AND G3.4 — let's end it

Lucian, this is a sneaky one. "Would of" and "would have" sound the same when you say them fast. But "would of" is never correct in writing. Look at your last two tests:

FROM G3.3 Q6
"I would of helped the seal the same way Anna, Jim, and there family did..."
Should be: would have
FROM G3.4 Q7
"I would of shown it to the guide, because the guide said to only take pictures and leave footprints."
Should be: would have
Which sentence is written correctly?
A I would of shown the geode to the guide.
B I would have shown the geode to the guide.
THE RULE
The word that comes after would, could, or should is always have, never "of." Same for might have, must have.

✗ I would of gone.
✓ I would have gone. (or "I would've gone" when speaking.)

✗ She could of helped.
✓ She could have helped.
THE QUICK TEST
Any time you write "would," "could," or "should," the next word is almost always have. If you see "of" there, change it. Every single time.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
You used "would of" on G3.3 and G3.4. That is at least 1–2 points lost on short answers every test. Fix this one rule and you catch those points back for free — no new skill to learn.

MODEL POLISHED PARAGRAPH (19–20/20 level)

Modern sports became popular because of changes during the Industrial Revolution, when people had more free time and wanted new ways to have fun. For example, sports like cricket and golf began in Britain in the 1700s, and people started writing down rules to stop fights about the games. This shows that sports needed structure to grow beyond small groups. By the late 1800s, British schools gave students fields and training, and young men competed for respect. This shows that sports became a way to build character, not just pass time. Over time, sports spread to many countries and became the popular activity they are today.
