Read the Connector Requirement (Q5)

When the prompt names the words, use THOSE words

Sammy, Q5 on this test asked you to combine two sentences using because, but, or so. Your combine was smooth and your meaning was clear — but you used and instead of one of the required connector words. That is why Q5 came in at 0.5 out of 1.

YOU WROTE (Q5)
"His dedication has tranformed the school, and due to his efforts, enrollment has doubled..."
Two things to catch: spelling (“transformed”), and the connector word. The prompt asked for because/but/so — “and” was not on the list.
THE RULE
When a Q5 prompt says "combine these sentences using because, but, or so," those are the only three connector words that earn credit. Think of the prompt as giving you a menu — you have to pick from the menu.

because — shows a reason (He was tired because he stayed up late.)
but — shows a contrast (She was nervous but she raised her hand.)
so — shows a result (It started raining so we went inside.)
A BETTER COMBINE (SAMMY'S IDEAS, CORRECT CONNECTOR)
"Peter Tabichi's dedication has transformed the school, so enrollment has doubled and students are performing at much higher levels."
Same meaning, same detail. “So” is from the menu (because/but/so) and it correctly shows the result.
The Q5 prompt says: Combine these sentences using because.
Which combine earns full credit?
A Cody was nervous to face Mahender, and Mahender was a strong player.
B Cody was nervous to face Mahender because Mahender was a strong player.
THE PROMPT-READING HABIT
Before you write a Q5 combine, circle the connector words in the prompt with your eyes. If the prompt says because, but, or so, write one of those three words into your combine. If the prompt says because, use because. Matching the prompt word is half the grade.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Q5 was worth 1 point and came in at 0.5. Reading the connector word carefully is a single-second move that recovers that half point — and the same habit protects you on every future combine question.

Q8 — ONE SMALL PUNCTUATION FIX
On Q8 you wrote: "...by sharing with others, for example, I shared a cookie with my friend..."

“By sharing with others” is a complete thought, and “I shared a cookie” is also a complete thought. A comma alone can't hold two complete thoughts together — that's a comma splice.

The fix: Use a period. "...by sharing with others. For example, I shared a cookie with my friend..."
