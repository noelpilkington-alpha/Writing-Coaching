Follow the Instruction Exactly

Q1 — Read what the question asks you to change

Q1 asked you to change the verb to make the action stronger. Let's look at what happened:

Original Sentence
Layla looked at the bright and blooming flowers.
YOU WROTE
Layla looked at the bright and blooming flowers.

You added the adjectives "bright and blooming," which is creative — but the question asked you to change the verb. The verb in this sentence is looked, and it stayed exactly the same.

CHANGED VERB
Layla gazed at the bright and blooming flowers.

Other strong verb options: admired, examined, studied.

HOW TO AVOID THIS
Before you write your answer, underline the key word in the question. If it says "change the verb," find the verb first, then replace it. Adjectives describe nouns (bright flowers). Verbs are the action words (looked, gazed, admired). The question wanted a new action, not a new description.

Explain WHY with "because"

Q8 — Good answer, but missing the reason

Your answer to Q8 had a good idea, but it didn't finish the thought. Look at the difference:

YOU WROTE
The most important part was when Layla decided to show the rock to her guide.

This tells us what the important part was — but the question asked you to explain. That means you need to say why it was important.

WITH THE REASON
The most important part was when Layla decided to show the rock to her guide because that's how she found out it was a geode, which made the whole hike special.

See the difference? The word "because" connects your answer to a reason.

THE "BECAUSE" TRICK
Whenever a question says "explain" or "why," use this formula:

[Your answer] + because + [your reason].

This one small addition turns a partial-credit answer into a full-credit answer. Check your response — if there's no "because," you're probably missing points.

Paragraph: Develop with More Details

Your topic sentence is strong — now build on it

Your paragraph scored 16/20. You started with a good topic sentence, but you only wrote 3 sentences. An expository paragraph needs more development — each detail should be followed by an explanation of why it matters.

Your Paragraph
Modern sports became popular because they were a fun activity to do in your free time. They also became popular because they have leagues and rules which helped create modern sports. On top of all of that, people liked sports because many people had factory work and they found it boring so in their free time doing sports gave people excitment.

You have a solid topic sentence and three reasons — that's a great start. But each reason needs a follow-up sentence that explains why it matters or gives a specific example. Here's what a full plan looks like:

Paragraph Plan — Expository
TS = Topic Sentence  •  D = Detail from the article  •  W = Why it matters  •  CS = Concluding Sentence
TS Modern sports became popular because they were a fun activity to do in your free time.
D1 People enjoyed playing sports after long days at work.
W This helped sports grow because workers needed something exciting to look forward to after boring factory jobs.
D2 Leagues and rules were created so people could compete fairly.
W This made sports more organized and helped them spread to more cities and towns.
D3 People started watching sports, not just playing them.
W This turned sports into entertainment that entire communities could enjoy together.
CS For all of these reasons, modern sports became one of the most popular activities in the world.
THE PATTERN: TS → D1 → W → D2 → W → D3 → W → CS
Every detail (D) gets a follow-up sentence (W) that explains why it matters or what it shows. This is what turns a 3-sentence paragraph into a fully developed one. You already have the details — you just need to add the "why" after each one.
