3. Draft — write 4–6 sentences using your plan.
4. Revise — reread your paragraph. Does every sentence start with a capital? Did you explain each detail?

Explain WHY — Not Just WHAT

Questions 7, 8, and 9 — all three lost points for answers that were too short
🔎 On Q7, Q8, and Q9 you named what you thought, but you did not explain why deeply enough. These are 3-point questions, so a short answer often earns only 1 or 2 points.
Q7 — YOU WROTE (1 of 3 points)
"When Sally finds her family because it is very graceful."

"Graceful" does not explain why that moment was meaningful. What made finding her family the big moment of the story?

Q8 — YOU WROTE (2 of 3 points)

Close, but "special meaning" is vague. What specifically did naming her do for Anna and Jim?

Q9 — YOU WROTE (2 of 3 points)

Good idea, but why? What would that change about the story or about you as a character?

Which answer earns full credit on Q7 ("Most meaningful moment")?
A When Sally finds her family because it is very graceful.
B The most meaningful moment was when Sally found her family again, because it showed that all of Anna and Jim's hard work to save her was worth it and gave Sally a second chance.
THE [OPINION] BECAUSE [SPECIFIC REASON] TEMPLATE
Part 1: State your answer clearly.
Part 2: Use "because" to start your reason.
Part 3: Name a specific thing from the story (not a vague word like "graceful" or "special").

Example for Q8: "Yes, naming the seal Sally was a good idea because it made Anna and Jim feel like Sally was part of their family, which is why they worked so hard to find her a safe home."
