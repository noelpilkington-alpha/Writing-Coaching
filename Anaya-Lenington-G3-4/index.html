Read the Prompt's Verb

Change the Sentence Type — your #1 repeated miss (5 of 5 tests)
🔍
This is a real pattern across your last 5 tests. Every time a prompt asks you to "rewrite as a question" or "change to an exclamation," the answer doesn't quite match. Same miss, different test.

Anaya, let's look at exactly what happened. When a prompt says "Change this sentence into a question" — it wants a question mark at the end. When it says "Change into an exclamation" — it wants an exclamation point. You already know this in your head. The slip is in reading the prompt closely enough to catch which one it wants.

G3.1 Q2 — "CHANGE TO A DECLARATIVE"
"Why is there only one place that we do not take our dogs to?"
(The prompt wanted a statement with a period. You wrote a question.)
G3.2 Q3 — "CHANGE TO AN EXCLAMATION"
"This amazing and fun event sells out every year."
(The prompt wanted an exclamation with a "!". You ended with a period.)
G3.3 Q1 — "CHANGE TO A QUESTION"
"Should i name her Sally?"
(You got the question mark, but you changed the subject of the sentence. The prompt wanted you to turn the given statement into a question.)
G3.4 Q3 — "CHANGE TO AN INTERROGATIVE"
"The geode is a perfect souvenir."
(The prompt wanted a question. You kept it as a statement with a period.)
The 4 Sentence Types — Memorize These
. Declarative = a statement. Ends with a period.
The geode is a perfect souvenir.
? Interrogative = a question. Ends with a question mark.
Isn't the geode a perfect souvenir?
! Exclamatory = strong feeling. Ends with an exclamation point.
What a perfect souvenir the geode is!
cmd Imperative = a command. Usually ends with a period.
Take the geode as a souvenir.
The prompt says: "Rewrite as an interrogative: The geode is a perfect souvenir." Which answer is correct?
A The geode is a perfect souvenir.
B Isn't the geode a perfect souvenir?
YOUR TEST PROMPTS, REWRITTEN WITH THE RIGHT SENTENCE TYPE
• G3.1 Q2 (declarative): "There is only one place we do not take our dogs."
• G3.2 Q3 (exclamatory): "What an amazing and fun event this is!"
• G3.3 Q1 (question, keep the same subject): "Should I name her Sally?" (your sentence was right — just keep the given subject)
• G3.4 Q3 (interrogative): "Isn't the geode a perfect souvenir?"
YOUR 2-STEP TRICK
Step 1: Find the verb in the prompt. It tells you the type. "Rewrite as a declarative" → period. "Rewrite as an interrogative" → question mark. "Rewrite as an exclamatory" → exclamation point.

Step 2: Before you write, circle the ending mark you need (. or ? or !). Then write the sentence to match.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
This is the one miss that has shown up on every test you've taken. It's worth about 1 to 2 points every time. Nailing this single rule — "read the prompt's verb, then match the ending mark" — is the fastest single move to push 84% up toward 90%.

Change the Verb — Not the Adjectives

Adding Details to Sentences (Kernel Expansion) — G3.4 Q1
🎯
When a prompt asks you to swap a weak verb for a stronger one, that means change the action word itself — not the words around it. You did a beautiful job adding adjectives, but the prompt was asking for something different.

Anaya, on G3.4 Question 1, the prompt was: "Rewrite this sentence by changing the verb to a stronger one." You wrote a really pretty sentence, but you changed the wrong part.

WHAT YOU WROTE ON G3.4 Q1
"Layla looked at a beautiful flower with amazing petals."
(You added "beautiful" and "amazing" — those are adjectives. But the verb "looked at" stayed weak. The prompt wanted a stronger verb.)
Strong Verbs That Replace "Looked At"
calm gazed at, studied, admired — careful, thoughtful looking
quick glanced at, peeked at — fast looking
wow marveled at, stared at — amazed looking
The prompt says: "Rewrite by using a stronger verb. Layla looked at a flower." Which answer followed the prompt?
A Layla looked at a beautiful flower with amazing petals.
B Layla gazed at the flower.
A FULL-CREDIT VERSION OF G3.4 Q1
• "Layla gazed at the flower." (simple verb swap)
• "Layla studied the beautiful flower with amazing petals." (verb swap plus your lovely adjectives — best of both)
• "Layla marveled at the flower's petals." (strong verb with a vivid picture)
YOUR 2-STEP TRICK
Step 1: Before you write, underline the verb in the original sentence. ("looked at")

Step 2: Think of one stronger verb that could replace it. Write your new sentence with that verb. Only then add any extra description.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
You clearly have a rich vocabulary — "beautiful" and "amazing" show that. Now channel that richness into verbs too. Strong verbs carry a sentence. "Layla gazed" tells the reader more than "Layla looked at" ever could. This is worth another 0.5 to 1 point on the next test.

MODEL PARAGRAPH (90%+ level)

Sports rules developed over a long time so people could play fair. First, in the 1800s, leaders wrote down the rules for soccer so every team knew what was allowed.
