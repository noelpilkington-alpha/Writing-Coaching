Turn the Fragment Into One Clear Sentence (Q2)

You had the right idea — it just needed tighter commas

Orion, Q2 asked you to expand the fragment "A wildflower with bright blue petals" into a complete sentence. Your expansion did add the missing subject and verb — but you also added two commas around "with bright blue petals" that weren't needed, and the last word (field) was spelled wrong.

YOU WROTE (Q2)
"A wild flower, with bright blue petals, sits in a large feald."
Three things to catch: the extra commas, the spelling of “field,” and “wild flower” is one word (wildflower).
THE RULE FOR Q2 FRAGMENT-TO-SENTENCE
When you expand a fragment, you only need to add a subject (who or what) and a verb (what they do). You do not need to add extra commas around the descriptive part of the original fragment. The phrase "with bright blue petals" is just describing the wildflower — it doesn't need to be set off by commas when it comes right after the noun.

Formula: [The thing] [does what] [where/when].
Example: A wildflower with bright blue petals sits in a large field.
A STRONGER VERSION (ORION'S IDEA, CLEANER GRAMMAR)
"A wildflower with bright blue petals sits in a large field."
Same subject, same verb, same picture. No extra commas. “Wildflower” is one word. “Field” is spelled right.

THE FRAGMENT-FIX HABIT
Before you add commas to a Q2 expansion, ask: "Is this phrase interrupting the main sentence, or is it just describing the noun right next to it?" If it's just describing the noun it sits beside, skip the commas. Your ear will usually tell you — say the sentence out loud in your head and see if it flows without pauses.

YOUR PARAGRAPH — STRETCHED TO 17–19/20

Robots can help humans at the workplace and at home. In some jobs, robots work alongside people. In one factory in the Netherlands, workers use robot arms to put together parts for machines, which lets the workers focus on harder steps that need human judgment. Robots can also be helpful in homes. Some small robots clean floors or answer questions when someone asks about the weather or a recipe. Because robots handle the simple tasks, humans have more time for the work only people can do.

Notice: every idea is yours. Topic sentence, Netherlands factory, cleaning robots, answering questions — all the same. The only additions are one extra sentence of detail per example (why does it help?) and a stronger closing that ties back to the topic. That alone is worth 3–4 points on Q11.

THE “ONE MORE SENTENCE” HABIT
For every example you give in a paragraph, ask yourself one follow-up question: "Why does this matter?" or "What does this show?"

Robots clean floors → so humans don't have to spend time on that task.
Robots work in factories → so workers can focus on harder parts that need human thinking.

That one extra sentence per example is what turns a 14 paragraph into an 18.
