The #1 Growth Area: Develop Your Paragraph

Helio, your Q11 answer had the right idea — but it was one brief, compressed sentence. A paragraph that earns full points develops that idea: it answers the prompt in a clear topic sentence, then adds two or three supporting details with evidence from the passage, and finishes with a closing. The full-paragraph shape — the 18–20/20 structure
TOPIC Answer the prompt in one clear sentence
DETAIL 1 First reason + evidence from the passage
DETAIL 2 Second reason + evidence from the passage
DETAIL 3 A third reason or detail (optional, if you have one)
CLOSE Wrap up your main idea in a new way
WHAT EACH PART DOES
Topic sentence: states your answer to the prompt directly.

Detail sentences: each one gives a separate reason and points to something from the passage that backs it up.

Closing: one sentence that ties the reasons together. Two to three well-developed details turn a single sentence into a real paragraph.

Give Each Idea a Clean Boundary

When two complete ideas run together, give each one a clear ending

Helio, a few times you joined two complete ideas with just a comma. When you have two complete ideas, give each one a clean boundary: either end the first with a period and start a new sentence, or join them with a connecting word like because, so, and, or which.

You wrote (Q11)
"Jason wanted to protect coral reefs with his statues, by placing the statues, swimmers will swim around the statues, letting the coral reefs rest."
With clean boundaries
"Jason wanted to protect coral reefs with his statues. By placing them underwater, he draws swimmers to swim around the statues. This lets the coral reefs rest."

You can also use a connecting word to join ideas smoothly: "Jason placed his statues underwater so that swimmers gather around them, which lets the coral reefs rest."

THE APPOSITIVE FROM Q4
An appositive renames a noun with a noun phrase, set off by commas. Watch where the renaming phrase sits:

You wrote: "The books are still popular, for their unique design, they are known for their golden spines."
Clean appositive: "The books, a beloved classic, are still popular." The phrase a beloved classic renames the books.
Which version gives each complete idea a clean boundary?
AThe statues drew swimmers, the reefs could rest.
BThe statues drew swimmers, so the reefs could rest.
THE FIX
Two complete ideas need a clean boundary: a period and a new sentence, or a connecting word (because, so, and, which). This keeps your strong ideas easy to read.
