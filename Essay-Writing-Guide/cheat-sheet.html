0. Plan first · the hard thinking happens here

PROMPTCount its parts. How she did it and what it shows is two halves, and every body paragraph must serve both.
PLANKey words, not sentences: the thesis (point + three hows), one topic line per body paragraph, two or three facts you can point to under each, the takeaway.
Check: can you point to every component in the plan before the first sentence? Thinking while typing retells the passage in order and breaks the logic.

1. Introduction · three statements, a zoom in

GENERAL STATEMENTA true sentence about the idea, before anyone is named.
SPECIFIC STATEMENTThe passage’s subject, named, with the fact that sets up the essay.
THESIS STATEMENTThe point, and the hows the body paragraphs will prove, in order. A two-half prompt gets both halves here.
MODEL
Most neighborhoods have a place everyone complains about and nobody touches. On Bridge Street, that place was a fenced lot, and the person who walked into it was fourteen-year-old Dani Okafor. Dani turned the lot into a garden by asking instead of waiting, by building the plan out of her neighbors’ answers, and by keeping the garden alive when the land was taken away, and her story shows that leadership is mostly the work of getting other people to show up.
Common miss: the thesis alone, two sentences, no hows. “Many ways” names none.

2. Body paragraphs × 3 · each links back to the thesis

TOPIC SENTENCEOne how from the thesis, said as this body paragraph’s point. Not the first fact.
SUPPORTING DETAILSFrom the passage: a number, a name, a date, or a short exact quote (2 to 8 words, introduced). Or paraphrase, keeping the fact. Put your finger on it on the page.
EXPLANATIONWhy do they matter? What do they tell us? This shows… After each detail, or one synthesized explanation after all of them. The second half of the prompt is answered here, every time.
CONCLUDING SENTENCELands the point of this body paragraph after the proof. Not the topic sentence again, not the next fact.
MODEL, BODY 1
TSDani’s first move was to ask, and to keep asking after the first answer was no. SDWhen the city turned down a park because one would cost about $400,000, she wrote to the lot’s owner instead, and it took eleven emails and four months to get a yes. EXThis shows that the garden began as persistence rather than planting, and it tells us the first thing a leader does: find the person who can say yes. CSBefore a single seed went in, Dani had already done the hardest work of the project with a keyboard.
Common misses: facts, then a stop (no explanation); a copied paragraph as evidence; a fact where the topic sentence goes; body 2 starting before body 1 ends.

3. Conclusion · the introduction, inverted

THESIS, SYNTHESIZEDHow the essay addressed the thesis: what the three body paragraphs add up to. Not the thesis repeated.
SPECIFIC STATEMENTBack to the passage, one concrete image.
GENERAL STATEMENTWhat you want your audience to take away.
MODEL
Asking, listening, and letting go: the three moves that turned a lot into a garden were all ways of getting other people to act. The twelve beds behind the library are tended today by people Dani knocked on doors to meet. The lesson of Bridge Street is that a leader’s job is not to do the work alone but to make it possible for everyone else to join in.
Check: could your first conclusion sentence have been written before the body paragraphs? Then it is the thesis again, not a synthesis.

4. The other seven points · order, sentences, editing

Transitions tell the reader what kind of sentence is coming: time and sequence (first, after that, by the second summer), illustration (for example, the passage says), change of direction (but, however, instead), emphasis (most of all), conclusion (as a result, in the end). One at the start of each body paragraph and the conclusion.
On topic: cover the thesis, read the sentence, ask whether it proves a how. If not, cut it.
Sentences: join two facts with because, but, so, when, although; open some sentences with the joining word; a naming phrase between commas; one short sentence after a long one. Longer is not varied.
Editing: copy the key word of the prompt from the prompt, never from memory. No comma before because. Read the whole essay back once with a finger on each word.
