What Is an Appositive?

Question 4 — You used a conjunction instead of an appositive

You scored 1/2 on this question — partial credit because your sentence was well-written but used the wrong structure.

Which sentence uses an appositive (a renaming phrase set off by commas)?
A The books are still popular and they are known for having beautiful golden spines.
B The Little Golden Books, a series known for beautiful golden spines, are still popular today.
YOU WROTE
"The books are still popular and they are known for having beautiful golden spines."
AN APPOSITIVE VERSION
"The Little Golden Books, a series known for beautiful golden spines, are still popular today."
HOW TO SPOT THE DIFFERENCE
Conjunction: Two separate ideas joined by "and," "but," or "so." Each side could be its own sentence.

Appositive: A renaming phrase that sits right next to a noun and is set off by commas. It gives extra information about that noun without using "and."

Example: "My dog, a golden retriever, loves to swim." The phrase "a golden retriever" renames "my dog."
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Appositives let you pack more information into one smooth sentence instead of connecting two choppy ideas with "and." They make your writing sound more polished and earn full points when the question asks for them.

One Sentence Means One Sentence

Question 8 — You wrote two sentences when one was requested

You scored 2/3 on this question — your ideas were good, but you lost a point for writing two sentences instead of one.

The question says "Write your answer in one sentence." Which response follows that direction?
A I'd rather read it myself because it makes me feel more calm. When I read books with voice I go to sleep more calm and the next day at school I feel better.
B I would rather read a book by myself because it makes me feel calm and helps me sleep better before school the next day.
YOU WROTE (TWO SENTENCES)
"I'd rather read it myself because it makes me feel more calm. When I read books with voice I go to sleep more calm and the next day at school I feel better."
COMBINED INTO ONE SENTENCE
"I would rather read a book by myself because it makes me feel calm and helps me sleep better before school the next day."
HOW TO COMBINE TWO IDEAS INTO ONE SENTENCE
Use connecting words like "because," "which," "so that," or "and" to join your ideas. If you end up with a period in the middle, you have written two sentences instead of one.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
When the directions say "one sentence," grading feedback checks that you followed that rule. Writing two sentences — even with great ideas — costs you a point. Always count your periods before moving on.

The 4-Part Paragraph
CENTRAL IDEA One sentence that tells the reader what the whole paragraph is about.
DETAIL 1 A specific detail from the passage + explain what it shows.
DETAIL 2 A second detail from the passage + explain what it shows.
CLOSING One sentence that wraps up the main point (do not add new information).

Spelling and Grammar Patterns

Small errors that cost points across multiple questions
Which sentence is spelled and punctuated correctly?
A Jasoon thought the the books had to much color.
B Jason thought the books had too much color.

Here are the patterns that showed up across your test:

1 Spelling: "Jasoon" should be "Jason." When you write a name from the passage, look back at the passage to check the spelling. Names from the text should match exactly.
2 "To" versus "too." Use "too" when you mean "more than needed" (too much, too hot) or "also" (me too). Use "to" for direction or actions (to the store, to run).
3 Repeated words: "the the." Always reread your answer once before moving on. Your eyes will catch doubled words if you read slowly.
4 Comma before "because." You wrote: "I wouldn't like a Little Golden Book, because..." In most cases, do not put a comma before "because" when it explains the reason for the first part of the sentence.
5 Subject-verb agreement: "feels" should be "feel." You wrote: "it made the books feels special." The word "books" is plural, so the verb should be "feel" (no "s").
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Each of these small errors can cost you half a point or more. Getting into the habit of rereading your answers once before moving on is the fastest way to catch them.
