Quick Fixes — Proofread Your Opener

Two punctuation fixes that are easy to catch

Aya, your Q11 opening has two small punctuation issues that are easy to fix once you know to look for them.

FROM G3.2 Q11 — YOUR OPENING
"Did you know there was a mobile library in Kenya. The books aren't held by librarians they're held by camels..."
Fix 1: Rhetorical Question Needs a Question Mark
WRONG "Did you know there was a mobile library in Kenya."
RIGHT "Did you know there was a mobile library in Kenya?"
THE RULE
Any sentence that starts with "Did you know," "Have you ever," or "Can you believe" is a question. It needs a question mark at the end, even when it feels like you're telling the reader something.
Fix 2: Run-on Sentence Needs a Period or Connector
WRONG "The books aren't held by librarians they're held by camels."
RIGHT "The books aren't held by librarians. They're held by camels!"
ALSO OK "The books aren't held by librarians, but they're held by camels."
THE RULE
When you have two complete thoughts (each could stand alone as a sentence), you need either a period between them or a comma + a connecting word (like "and," "but," "so"). Without one of those, it's a run-on.
Which version fixes BOTH punctuation errors?
A Did you know there was a mobile library in Kenya. The books aren't held by librarians, but they're held by camels.
B Did you know there was a mobile library in Kenya? The books aren't held by librarians. They're held by camels!
C Did you know there was a mobile library in Kenya? The books aren't held by librarians they're held by camels.
PROOFREAD HABIT
After you finish writing Q11, reread your first two sentences out loud. Ask: "Does every question have a question mark? Does every sentence have its own period?" Catching these before you submit is the easiest way to pick up points.

Fixing Run-on Sentences

Two complete thoughts need something between them

Aya, a run-on sentence happens when you put two complete thoughts next to each other without a period or a connecting word. You did this once in your Q11 opener. Let's make sure you can spot and fix this pattern every time.

How to Fix a Run-on
FIX 1 Add a period. Split it into two sentences.
"The books aren't held by librarians. They're held by camels."
FIX 2 Add a comma + connecting word.
"The books aren't held by librarians, but they're held by camels."
NO Don't just put them together.
"The books aren't held by librarians they're held by camels." (run-on!)
Which sentence is a run-on?
A The camels carry heavy boxes of books. They walk for hours through the desert.
B The camels carry heavy boxes of books they walk for hours through the desert.
C The camels carry heavy boxes of books and walk for hours through the desert.
THE QUICK TEST
Read each sentence in your paragraph. Ask: "Could I split this into two sentences that each make sense on their own?" If yes, and there's no period or connecting word between them, it's a run-on. Add a period or add a comma plus "and," "but," or "so."
