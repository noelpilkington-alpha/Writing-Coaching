Paragraph: Cover More Than One Point

The prompt says "body" — that means more than just quills

Your paragraph scored 16/20. You wrote well about quills and the tail, but the prompt asks how a porcupine's body helps it stay safe. The article describes several body features — your paragraph should cover more than one.

Your Paragraph
A porcupine's body can help porcupines stay safe in many ways. They can swing there tail to shoot quills at predators that try to kill it. Also, they can climb trees to gather food, and hide from predators. These are the reasons why the porcupine can stay safe.

You mention quills and climbing trees, but both are connected to the same idea (defense/escape). A stronger paragraph would cover different body features from the article:

Paragraph Plan — "How a porcupine's body helps it stay safe"
TS = Topic Sentence  •  D = Detail from the article  •  W = Why it matters  •  CS = Concluding Sentence
TS A porcupine's body helps it stay safe from predators in many ways.
D1 Quills: sharp, barbed spines that cover the porcupine's back and tail
W When a predator touches the porcupine, the quills detach and stick in the attacker's skin, causing pain.
D2 Strong claws that let porcupines climb trees
W Climbing helps them escape predators on the ground and find food up high.
D3 A warning display — the porcupine rattles its quills and turns its back
W This warns predators to stay away before anyone gets hurt.
CS From quills to claws to warning signals, the porcupine's body is built for protection.
IMPORTANT: QUILLS DON'T SHOOT!
You wrote that porcupines "shoot quills" — this is a common misconception. Porcupines cannot shoot their quills. Instead, the quills detach on contact. When an animal touches or bumps into the porcupine, the quills come loose and stick in the attacker's skin. On a test, using the correct fact from the article earns more credit.

Spelling: "there" vs. "their"

Two words that sound the same but mean different things

In your paragraph, you wrote "there" when you meant "their" — and you made this mistake twice. Let's fix that pattern.

swing there tail → swing their tail

These two words sound identical, but they mean completely different things:

THE RULE
Their = something that belongs to them. "Their quills are sharp."
There = a place or location. "The porcupine is over there."

Quick test: Can you replace the word with "belonging to them"? If yes, use their.
• "They swing belonging to them tail" — Yes! So it's their.
• "The tree is over belonging to them" — No! So it's there.

You also had two other spelling errors in your paragraph:

tuch → touch
quil → quill

Remember: "quill" has two L's, just like "fill" and "pill."
