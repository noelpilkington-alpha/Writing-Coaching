#1 Growth Area: The Missing Apostrophes

The new pattern on G3.9 — three missed apostrophes in one paragraph

Valentina, here is a pattern that jumped out on G3.9 that was not there as strongly before: contractions and possessives without their apostrophes. On your oak-tree paragraph alone, three words were missing an apostrophe:

FROM G3.9 Q11 — your most recent paragraph
"Oak trees are very important to animals and nature, heres why. Firstly, the oak tree's branches are very wide and provide a good home for birds and other animals. Also, oak trees provide shade and cleaner air for humans and other birds. Thats why oak trees are bery important."
Should be: here's (here is), That's (that is), and very (not bery).
FROM G3.9 Q2 — fragment rewrite
"The bees jobs are to collect sweet nectar from flowers."
Should be: bees' jobs (the jobs that belong to the bees) or bee's job if it is one bee. That missing apostrophe dropped you from 1.0 to 0.75 on this question.
THE APOSTROPHE CHECK (TWO RULES)
Rule 1 — Contractions. If a word is two words squished together, it needs an apostrophe where the missing letters are. "Here is" → "here's." "That is" → "that's." "Cannot" → "can't." If you can expand it back into two words, you need the apostrophe.

Rule 2 — Possessives. If something belongs to someone or something, use an apostrophe. "The bees' jobs" = the jobs belonging to the bees. "The oak tree's branches" = the branches belonging to the tree (and you got this one right!).
Which version of your G3.9 closing earns full credit?
A Thats why oak trees are bery important.
B That's why oak trees are very important.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Three missing apostrophes and one letter typo on G3.9 = about 2–3 points gone. Your paragraph went from 20/20 structure to 16/20 because of these — not because of any missing idea. A 90-second scan of every contraction and every possessive in your paragraph would have caught all three.

#2 Growth Area: Q4 Sentence-to-Question

Match the original meaning when you flip a statement into a question

Q4 on G3.9 asked you to change "The honey is food for the bees during the winter" into a question. You wrote:

FROM G3.9 Q4
"When is the honey food for the bees?"
Scored 0.5 out of 1. The sentence became a question, but the meaning changed. The original is about whether honey is food for bees (or who it is food for) — not when. Also, "during the winter" was cut entirely.

When a prompt says "change this sentence into a question," the trick is to keep all the same information and just rearrange it so it asks instead of tells.

How to Flip a Statement into a Question
STEP 1 Find the verb. Here it is "is." Move it to the front.
STEP 2 Keep every other word from the original in the same order.
STEP 3 Swap the period for a question mark.
RESULT "Is the honey food for the bees during the winter?"
