Your 6-Step Process

90 minutes — how to use your time on G8+ tests
The 6-Step Process for G8 Writing Tests
STEP 1 Read the passage carefully — underline key details, changes, and character reactions (15 min)
STEP 2 Answer multiple choice Q1–Q10 — read all options before choosing. Check connector words carefully. (15 min)
STEP 3 Read the essay prompt — identify exactly what it asks you to argue or explain
STEP 4 Plan your essay — write a quick outline: THESIS + 3 body points. For each body point, note the EVIDENCE you will use AND what it SHOWS. (10 min)
STEP 5 Write your 5 paragraphs — follow your outline. Each body paragraph: point + evidence + explanation of what it shows. (35 min)
STEP 6 Review and revise — re-read your essay. Does the intro state a real thesis? Does each body paragraph explain significance? Does the conclusion connect back? (15 min)
THE KEY STEP FOR YOU: STEP 4 (PLANNING)
Byron, your G8.1 essay has structure — 5 paragraphs, topic sentences, transitions. What it is missing is the explanation layer. The fix happens in the planning step. Before you write, jot down for each body paragraph:

POINT: What am I arguing in this paragraph?
EVIDENCE: What specific detail from the passage supports it?
EXPLANATION: What does this evidence SHOW? Why does it matter?

If you plan the explanation before you write, it ends up in the essay. If you skip the plan, you end up listing facts without analysis — which is exactly what happened on G8.1.

Connector Logic — Q3

Your only multiple choice miss: "because" vs. "and"

Byron, you scored 9/10 on multiple choice — almost perfect. The one you missed was Q3, where you had to choose the right word to combine two sentences. Here is what happened:

YOU CHOSE (WRONG)
"Because the library kept the timber beams, it added shelves of books and reading corners."
CORRECT ANSWER
"The library kept the timber beams, and it added shelves of books and reading corners."

Why "because" was wrong: "Because" means one thing CAUSED another. But keeping timber beams did not CAUSE adding shelves — both things just happened. The relationship is additive ("and"), not causal ("because").

CONNECTOR WORD LOGIC
and Both things happened (addition) because One thing CAUSED another (cause → effect) but / however Two things contrast or oppose each other so / therefore One thing is the RESULT of another

The test: Ask yourself, "Did the first thing MAKE the second thing happen?" If yes, use "because." If they just both happened, use "and." If they oppose each other, use "but."
Which connector correctly joins these two facts about the library?
A "The library hosted movie nights under the stars because monthly seminars drew attention across the city."
B "The library hosted movie nights under the stars, and monthly seminars drew attention across the city."
QUICK CHECK FOR YOUR NEXT TEST
Every time you see "because" in a sentence-combining question, ask: "Did A actually MAKE B happen?" If you cannot explain the cause-and-effect chain, the answer is probably "and" (addition) or "but" (contrast) instead.

Essay Depth: Evidence + Explanation

The #1 move that takes your essay from 75% to 90%

Byron, your essay has the right skeleton — 5 paragraphs, topic sentences, supporting details. Where you lost points is that your body paragraphs state facts without explaining what they show. Look at this body paragraph from your essay:

Your Body Paragraph 2:
"Originally, certain residents were unsure about the lakeside location. They expressed concerns about parking and the cost of maintaining the library. However, when shops and businesses opened nearby, the concerns faded."
Topic Sentence Evidence Transition (However) Missing: What This Shows

The facts are there. The transition is smooth. But there is no sentence explaining what this shows about the library's impact on the community. Watch what happens when we add that layer:

Tap to see the paragraph with explanation added
Same facts + "so what?" sentences
WITH EXPLANATION LAYER ADDED
"Originally, certain residents were unsure about the lakeside location. They expressed concerns about parking and the cost of maintaining the library. This shows that not everyone believed the project would succeed, and the library had to prove its value. However, when shops and businesses opened nearby, the concerns faded. This demonstrates that the library did not just serve readers — it sparked economic growth that won over even its critics."
THE PATTERN: FACT → EXPLANATION
After every key detail from the passage, add a sentence that answers one of these:

• "This shows that..." (what the fact reveals)
• "This matters because..." (why the reader should care)
• "This demonstrates that..." (what it proves about your argument)

You already have the facts. The explanation is the sentence that tells the reader why those facts matter to your argument.
Which body paragraph would earn a higher essay score?
A "Families often explore the children's wing or join storytelling sessions. This demonstrates that the library serves all ages, not just individual readers, making it a true community gathering place."
B "Families often explore the children's wing or join storytelling sessions. Also visitors come by boat sometimes. Which is nice for them."
YOUR ACTION STEP
In your next essay, after you write each body paragraph, re-read it and ask: "Did I explain what this evidence SHOWS about my argument?" If the paragraph only states facts, add one "This shows..." or "This demonstrates..." sentence. That single sentence is worth 2–3 points per body paragraph.

Thesis + Conclusion

Framing the essay: a clear opening, a strong close

Byron, here is your introduction and conclusion from G8.1:

YOUR INTRODUCTION
"What once was a vacant lakeside warehouse quickly turned into a lively library with many features and attractions."
YOUR CONCLUSION
"What was a forsaken warehouse turned into a bustling community of all different people. This reminds us that hard work and planning can completely change the story of a place."

What is working: You have an introduction that sets the scene and a conclusion that wraps up. Good bones. What is missing: Your introduction is a statement, not a thesis. A thesis previews the argument you are about to make — it tells the reader WHAT you will prove. And your conclusion, while nice, is generic because it has no specific thesis to echo back to.

STATEMENT vs. THESIS
Statement (what you wrote): "The warehouse turned into a lively library with many features."
→ This describes what happened, but it does not preview your argument.

Thesis (what earns full credit): "The lakeside warehouse became more than a library — it brought the community together through arts programming, economic growth, and family events."
→ This previews the 3 body points you will prove.

UPGRADED INTRODUCTION WITH THESIS
"What once was a vacant lakeside warehouse quickly became the heart of a community. The library brought people together through inclusive programming, sparked economic growth in the surrounding area, and proved that even skeptics could be won over by a well-planned public space."
UPGRADED CONCLUSION ECHOING THESIS
"From storytelling sessions to summer movie nights, every feature of the library fulfilled the promise of its transformation: a forgotten warehouse became a gathering place that serves readers, families, and businesses alike. The lakeside library demonstrates that a single ambitious project can reshape not just a building, but an entire community's sense of what is possible."
Which introduction contains a real thesis statement?
A "The warehouse became a library. A place with lots of different things. People liked going there because it was fun."
B "The lakeside library transformed its community by attracting diverse visitors, overcoming initial doubts from residents, and evolving into a cultural hub that extends far beyond books."
THE FORMULA
Thesis: [Topic] + [3 reasons/points you will prove in body paragraphs]

Conclusion: Echo the thesis + add a "bigger picture" takeaway

Your introduction sets up the argument. Your conclusion lands it. When both are specific to YOUR essay's points (not generic), you earn full credit on both.
