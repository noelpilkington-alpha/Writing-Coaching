Plan Before You Write

Use a paragraph plan to organize your ideas

In AlphaWrite, you learn to make an outline before you write. On a test, you can do a quick version called a paragraph plan. Here's what your plan for the Pokémon paragraph could look like:

Paragraph Plan — "How Tajiri's childhood influenced Pokémon"
TS = Topic Sentence  •  D = Detail from the article  •  W = Why it matters  •  CS = Concluding Sentence
TS Satoshi Tajiri's love of catching bugs as a kid led him to create Pokémon.
D1 As a child, Tajiri spent hours catching bugs — his friends called him "Dr. Bug."
W This shows how much he loved collecting creatures, which became the main idea behind Pokémon.
D2 When he grew up, he combined his love of bugs with his knowledge of video games to create Pokémon.
W This shows that his childhood hobby directly inspired him to design a game where players collect creatures.
D3 Pokémon became huge — over 150 creatures, plus TV shows, movies, and apps.
W This shows that one kid's hobby turned into something millions of people around the world enjoy.
CS Tajiri's childhood love of bugs turned into one of the most popular games ever created.
THE TOPIC SENTENCE TRICK
Read the prompt, then turn it into your first sentence:

Prompt: "Explain how Satoshi Tajiri's childhood influenced the creation of Pokémon."
Topic sentence: "Satoshi Tajiri's childhood love of catching bugs influenced him to create Pokémon."

You just flip the prompt into a statement. Then your details and explanations come after.

Your Proofreading Checklist

Catch these errors before you hand in your test

You got a perfect editing score, which means you know how to spot errors in other people's writing. Now use that same skill on your own writing. Here are the errors from your paragraph:

0 checked
Check your verb tenses — stay in past tense.
You wrote "he will catch them" — but the story happened in the past. It should be "he would catch them." When writing about something that already happened, use past tense throughout.
Check for missing words.
You wrote "This is how the epic game of Pokemon created" — but the word "was" is missing. It should be "Pokemon was created." Read each sentence out loud. If something sounds off, you're probably missing a word.
Read your writing out loud before submitting.
Both errors above ("will catch" and "Pokemon created") would sound wrong if you read them out loud. Your ear catches things your eyes miss.
Does every detail have an explanation after it?
After each detail from the article, check: did I explain why this matters? If not, add a sentence starting with "This shows..." or "This is important because..."
Did I use specific details from the article?
Check your paragraph for names, numbers, and facts from the passage. If everything sounds general, go back and add specific details like "Dr. Bug" or "over 150 creatures."
