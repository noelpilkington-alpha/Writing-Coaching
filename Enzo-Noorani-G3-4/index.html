THE RULE: TWO FACTS WITH A THIS SHOWS EACH BEAT FIFTEEN FACTS WITH NONE
The grading counts how far each fact is developed, not how many facts there are. The TOPIC SENTENCE answers the question and names two reasons. Each reason gets one DETAIL · SHOW from the article and a DETAIL · SO WHAT: This shows plus what the fact proves. The CONCLUDING SENTENCE adds the two reasons up. The check: after each fact, is the next sentence a This shows, or the next fact?

Fix 2: Do the Job the Instruction Names

Q1 asked for a stronger verb. G3.3 Q3 asked for an appositive
THE RULE: READ THE INSTRUCTION TWICE AND DO EXACTLY THAT JOB
One of the two points on an editing question is for the skill named. Change the verb means the verb changes; adding detail around the same verb is a different job. Add an appositive means a noun phrase in commas that renames; a which clause is a different job. The check: after you type, read the instruction once more and point to the place where you did what it says.

Change the verb to make the action stronger: Layla looked at the flower. → “Layla looked at the special flower with a butterfly on top of it.” MORE DETAIL, SAME VERB
A good detail, and looked at is still the verb. The job was to change it.
THE VERB CHANGED

One stronger verb does the job. Your detail can stay after it: Layla studied the flower with a butterfly on top of it.

An appositive renames a noun with a noun phrase: Sally, the baby seal, swam to the other seals. Swap test: The baby seal swam to the other seals. Passes.
