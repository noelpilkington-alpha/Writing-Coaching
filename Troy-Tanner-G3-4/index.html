THE "THERE vs THEIR" TRICK
There points to a place ("over there"). It has the word "here" hiding inside it.
Their shows ownership ("their car"). It has the word "heir" hiding inside it — and an heir owns things.
They're is short for "they are" ("they're hungry"). The apostrophe replaces the “a.”

One Idea Per Sentence

Run-ons are costing you points on Q4 and Q11 every test

Troy, when two complete thoughts get joined with just "and," your sentence turns into a run-on. It happens most often on Q4 (combining sentences) and Q11 (paragraph). Here are three real examples:

FROM G3.3 Q4 — combining sentences
"Anna and Jim ran to the beach and they found a baby seal in a fishnet."
Two complete ideas joined with just “and.” Needs a comma before “and.”
FROM G3.1 Q4 — combining sentences
"Secondly, dogs might growl or because they might be nervous or tired."
Two connecting words (“or” and “because”) jammed together. Pick one.
FROM G3.4 Q11 — your most recent paragraph
"Modern sports began in the 1700s and quickly took of some of the first of sports were, golf and cricket."
Two thoughts mashed together with no period between them.
Which version fixes the run-on correctly?
A Modern sports began in the 1700s and quickly took off, some of the first sports were golf and cricket.
B Modern sports began in the 1700s and quickly took off. Some of the first sports were golf and cricket.
THE RULE
Every sentence should have ONE complete idea. If you can split a sentence into two separate sentences that both make sense on their own, it is probably a run-on.

Two ways to fix it:
1. Add a period and start a new sentence.
2. Add a comma plus a joining word: and, but, so, because.

✗ "Anna and Jim ran to the beach and they found a baby seal."
✓ "Anna and Jim ran to the beach, and they found a baby seal." (comma before “and”)
✓ "Anna and Jim ran to the beach. They found a baby seal." (two clean sentences)
THE "PAUSE TEST"
Read your sentence out loud in your head. If you naturally want to take a breath in the middle, that is where a period or comma+and belongs.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Q4 is worth 2 points. Q11 is worth 20 points. Together, that is almost half the test. Fixing run-ons saves you 1–2 points on Q4 and 2–3 points on Q11 — every test.

Troy, when a question asks you to rewrite a sentence as a question, your answer has to be an actual question — with a question word at the start (Is, Does, Can, Will, Did, How, Why) and a question mark at the end.

THE INTERROGATIVE FORMULA
Step 1. Start with a question word: Is, Are, Was, Were, Do, Does, Did, Can, Will, How, Why, What.
Step 2. Flip the verb and subject. "Layla saw a butterfly" becomes "Did Layla see a butterfly?"
Step 3. End with a question mark. Not a period.
