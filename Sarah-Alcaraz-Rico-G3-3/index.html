Step 1 Error: Do What the Question Asks

Q1 asked for a question but you rewrote the story

Sarah, on Q1 you scored 0 out of 2. The question said: "Rewrite this sentence as a question." But instead of changing it into a question, you just rewrote the story in your own words. The sentence you wrote was still a statement, not a question.

🔎 Step 2 check: "What skill is this question asking me to use?" Answer: turning a statement into an interrogative sentence. An interrogative sentence asks something and ends with a question mark.
HOW TO TURN A STATEMENT INTO A QUESTION
Statement: "Sally the seal loves swimming in the ocean."

To make it a question:
• Move the verb to the front: "Does Sally the seal love swimming in the ocean?"
• Or use a question word: "What does Sally the seal love doing in the ocean?"

The key sign is: does your sentence end with a question mark and actually ask something?
"Sally the seal plays on the rocks every morning." Which is this sentence rewritten as a question?
A Sally the seal plays on the rocks because she likes to have fun in the morning.
B Does Sally the seal play on the rocks every morning?
THE FIX
Before writing your answer on Q1, check: "Does the question ask me to rewrite it as something specific?" If it says "as a question," your answer MUST end with a question mark and MUST ask something. If your answer does not ask anything, you have not done what the question asked.

Step 2 Error: Connector Choice (Q5)

"When" vs "Since" — choosing the right relationship

Sarah, on Q5 you used "When" as your connector, but the relationship between the sentences needed "Since" (meaning "because"). This is a Step 2 issue: identifying what kind of connection the sentences have.

CONNECTOR RELATIONSHIPS
• "When" = time (something happens at a certain time)
• "Since" or "Because" = reason (one thing causes another)
• "Although" = contrast (something is true even though something else is also true)

Before picking a connector, ask: "What is the relationship between these two ideas? Is one the REASON for the other? Or did one happen at the same TIME as the other?"
Jim loves Pokémon. He plays it every day. Which connector shows the REASON?
A When Jim loves Pokémon, he plays it every day.
B Since Jim loves Pokémon, he plays it every day.
STEP 2 IN ACTION
Before picking a connector, ask: "Is one idea the REASON for the other?" If yes, use "since" or "because." If one idea happened at a certain TIME, use "when." Naming the relationship first makes the connector choice easy.
