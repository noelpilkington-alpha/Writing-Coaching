Q7 — The Sentence Has to Match the TOPIC

Q7 asked which sentence does NOT belong in a paragraph about "Writing changed the way people lived"

Shepperd, this is the harder of your two multiple-choice misses, and it's the one worth slowing down on. The question asked: "Which sentence does NOT belong in a paragraph titled Writing changed the way people lived?"

Here is the paragraph from the passage. Read each sentence and ask: "Is this sentence about how writing changed daily living?"

1.Writing changed the way people lived in important ways. 2.Traders could keep track of deals across great distances. 3.Scholars call this system cuneiform, a word from the Latin term for "wedge-shaped." 4.Rulers could pass written laws that applied to all people.
From G8.7 Q7
Which sentence does NOT belong in a paragraph about how writing changed the way people lived?
Your pick: Sentence 2 — "Traders could keep track of deals across great distances."
Correct: Sentence 3 — "Scholars call this system cuneiform, a word from the Latin term for 'wedge-shaped.'"
Why: Sentence 2 does belong — traders keeping track of deals is a real example of how daily life changed. Sentence 3 is about etymology (where the word "cuneiform" came from), not about how living changed. Same passage, different topic.
⚑ The rule: A sentence belongs in a paragraph if it is about the exact topic of that paragraph — not just connected to the passage somehow. "How writing changed living" and "where the word cuneiform came from" are two different topics, even though they sit near each other.
Which sentence does NOT belong in a paragraph titled "How early scribes held power in society"?
A Because so few people could read, scribes held great power in their governments.
B Both systems took years to learn, so only trained scribes could read and write.
C The oldest known writing system began around 3400 BCE in Mesopotamia.
YOUR 3-STEP Q7 STRATEGY
1. Circle the topic phrase in the question: "changed the way people lived," "scribes held power," "access for residents." Whatever it says.

2. For each answer option, ask one question: "Is this sentence ABOUT that exact topic?" Not near it. Not related to it. ABOUT it.

3. Check whether the question asks DOES or does NOT belong. On Q7 the question was "does NOT" — you need the outlier, not the fit.

Quick note on Q2: You picked Declarative for "Think about how different life would be if no one had ever pressed a pen into clay." The answer is Imperative. Rule: if a sentence starts with a verb giving a command or instruction (Think, Imagine, Consider, Look, Remember), it's imperative. That's it.

SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Q7-style questions show up on every test. Circling the topic phrase before you read the options is a 3-second habit that would have caught this one.
