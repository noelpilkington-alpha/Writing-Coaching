#1 Fix: Answer It, Then Say Why

Q7 and Q10 — two good answers, each missing its second half

Questions 6 to 10 are worth three points each, and the rubric for them is simple: one complete sentence that answers the question and includes a reason or example. You already do this when the question makes it obvious — your Q4 has a clean “because” in it. Two answers stopped before that half arrived. Tap them:

Tap: Question 10
The question said “explain why” — and the why is missing
Q10 — 2 OUT OF 3
“They should always do research before buying a pet no matter what.”
A genuinely good rule — better than most. But the question was “Write one sentence explaining why this rule is important,” and “no matter what” is emphasis, not a reason. “They should always do research before buying a pet, because a pet is a living thing that will depend on them for years.”
Tap: Question 7
Two actions, zero reasons
Q7 — 2 OUT OF 3
“I would ask his owners to settle him down or make him wait outside for a little.”
Sensible and specific — and the question asked what action you would take, so the action is there. What is missing is the why that turns an action into an explanation: “…wait outside for a little, because everyone else in the restaurant is trying to eat in peace.”

Compare those with the one that did include its reason — Q8, where you wrote “if owners can go on vacation why can’t the dog.” That is a real reason, and a good one. It only lost its point to punctuation, not to thinking.

THE HABIT
After you write a three-point answer, look for the word because or so. If it is not there, the sentence is probably half finished. Add the reason to the same sentence — one sentence, not two — and move on.
Question: “What is one rule people should follow when caring for a pet? Write one sentence explaining why this rule is important.” Which answer does what was asked?
AThey should always do research before buying a pet no matter what.
BThey should always do research before buying a pet because a pet will depend on them for years.

Your Paragraph: 15 out of 20

Real reasoning — and a second half that wandered away from the article

The question: Explain the ways that soil is important to Earth — using evidence from the article. Here is what you wrote:

“This is why earths soil is important. Earth’s soil is very important for a ton of reasons it makes plants grow, it holds water and it holds literally everything else. If we didn’t have soil to grow plants we would not have oxygen to breath and humans and so many more animals whoud have never existed. Also if we didnt have soil to hold everything up we would have nothing no mountains, buildings, towns, shops, houses, and so much more wouldnt be there. Thats why Earths soil is important.”
A real cause-and-effect chain Explains, does not just retell Opens and closes on the central idea Second half is not from the article First sentence points at nothing yet Apostrophes and a run-on

The best thing here is the third sentence. Soil grows plants, plants make oxygen, no oxygen means no humans and no animals. That is you explaining why soil matters instead of listing facts about it, and it is the part of this paragraph a grader remembers.

So where are the five points? Mostly the fourth sentence. The prompt asked you to use evidence from the article, and “Digging on Dirt” never says soil holds up mountains, buildings and shops — that came from you, and it is also not quite true. Meanwhile the article was full of details you left on the table: worms and other animals living in the soil, fallen leaves and dead bugs rotting into humus that feeds plants, the different kinds of soil. Your own reasoning is strong enough — it just needs the article’s facts underneath it instead of invented ones.

Two smaller things. The opener, “This is why earth’s soil is important,” points at something you have not said yet — it is a closing sentence sitting in the opening slot. And the second sentence runs three ideas together with no stop after “reasons.”

YOUR PARAGRAPH, DEVELOPED

“Earth’s soil is important for many reasons. It makes plants grow, it holds water, and it is home to many living things. If we didn’t have soil to grow plants, we would not have oxygen to breathe, and humans and so many more animals would never have existed. The article also explains that worms and other animals live in the soil, and that fallen leaves and dead bugs rot into humus, which is food for plants. That means the soil is feeding the plants that feed everything else. That’s why Earth’s soil is important.”

Your third sentence — the chain — is untouched. The invented fourth sentence became two sentences of facts from the article plus one “that means.” The apostrophes came back. Everything else is yours.

SO WHAT?
Reasoning is the hard part and you already have it. The rubric pays for reasoning about the article’s facts. Before you write, pick two details from the passage and build your chain on those. Same thinking, five more points.
