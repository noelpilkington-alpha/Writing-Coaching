Match Evidence to the Claim

Q10 — Elaboration (0/1)

The question asked: Which detail best elaborates on the claim "The bridge brought the city's two halves closer together"?

YOUR ANSWER
D: "Delivery drivers feared delays on narrow blocks."

This answer describes a concern about the bridge — it doesn't show the two halves coming together. It's an additional detail about the bridge, but it doesn't elaborate on the specific claim about connection.

The key word in the claim is "two halves closer together" — so the correct detail must show both sides being connected through a shared experience.

Tap to see the correct answer
Think about which detail shows BOTH sides connecting
CORRECT ANSWER
B: "Parks on both ends became gathering places with gardens, benches, and art."

Notice "both ends" — this directly shows two sides of the bridge becoming shared community spaces. Gardens, benches, and art are things people enjoy together. That's what "two halves closer together" means — shared spaces that bring people from both sides into the same place.

THE SKILL
When a question asks which detail elaborates on a claim, don't just look for any related detail — look for one that directly supports the specific words in the claim. Ask yourself: "Does this detail show the exact thing the claim is talking about?"
See a quick test you can apply
Claim: "The bridge brought the city's two halves closer together."
Test each answer:
• Does "delivery drivers feared delays" show two halves coming together? No — it shows a worry, not a connection.
• Does "parks on both ends became gathering places" show two halves coming together? Yes — "both ends" = two sides, "gathering places" = coming together.

Match the detail to the specific claim, not just to the general topic.

Explain WHY, Not Just WHAT

The main difference between 17/20 and 20/20

Your essay does a strong job describing what happened after the bridge was renovated — cafés filled up, musicians played, events drew visitors. But right now it reads more like a summary of events than an analysis of why those changes mattered.

Your teacher put it clearly: "Try deepening your analysis by explaining why these changes mattered to residents, rather than primarily describing what happened."

Here's the pattern: Evidence → So what? → Why it matters

Example 1:

YOUR VERSION (DESCRIBES WHAT)
"Furthermore, street musicians played musical pieces to those walking around the bridge."
Tap to see the improved version
Notice how the added sentence explains WHY this matters
IMPROVED VERSION (EXPLAINS WHY)
"Furthermore, street musicians began performing for passing crowds, transforming the bridge from a simple crossing into a gathering place where strangers could share an experience together."

What changed? Instead of just stating the fact (musicians played), the sentence now explains the significance — the bridge became a place for spontaneous community connection, not just a way to get across the river.

Example 2:

YOUR VERSION (DESCRIBES WHAT)
"Various cafés were crowded with pedestrians and cyclists taking a break from their journey to enjoy a cup of coffee."
Tap to see the improved version
Notice how the added clause explains the broader significance
IMPROVED VERSION (EXPLAINS WHY)
"Various cafés were crowded with pedestrians and cyclists stopping for coffee, showing that the bridge had created an entirely new neighborhood economy where none had existed before."

What changed? The original states a fact (cafés were crowded). The improved version explains what that fact means — the bridge didn't just attract people, it built a whole new economic ecosystem.

THE BODY PARAGRAPH UPGRADE
Your body paragraphs already follow a strong structure (topic sentence, evidence, closing sentence). To reach the next level, after each piece of evidence, add an explanation using TS → E1 → X1 → E2 → X2 → CS:

E = Evidence (what happened) → X = Explanation (why it matters)

Ask yourself after every piece of evidence: "So what? Why does this matter to the people in the city?" If your next sentence doesn't answer that question, your explanation is missing.
See what your teacher suggested as a model
"By drawing families, tourists, and performers to the same space, the bridge did more than connect two riverbanks — it rebuilt a sense of shared community identity that the city had long been missing."

Notice how this sentence doesn't just describe what happened — it explains the deeper meaning: the bridge rebuilt community identity. That's the level of analysis that pushes an essay from strong to excellent.
