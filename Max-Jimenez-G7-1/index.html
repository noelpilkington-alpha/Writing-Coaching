Sentence Types — Q2

Q2 — 0 out of 1 — Declarative vs. Imperative
🔎 What happened: The question asked you to identify the sentence type of "Some residents worried about..." You picked Imperative, but the correct answer was Declarative. This sentence is a statement of fact — it tells you what the residents felt. It does not give a command.
THE SENTENCE
"Some residents worried about the cost of maintaining solar panels in such a remote area."
THE KEY RULE
A sentence is Imperative only if it gives a command or instruction (like "Close the door" or "Please sit down"). This sentence does not command anyone to do anything — it simply states a fact about what residents felt. If it makes a statement of fact, it is Declarative.
What type of sentence is this? "Many villagers wondered whether the panels would survive the rainy season."
A Interrogative
B Declarative
QUICK TEST FOR NEXT TIME
When a sentence-type question comes up, look at the end punctuation first:

• Ends with . → Declarative (statement) or Imperative (command)
• Ends with ? → Interrogative (question)
• Ends with ! → Exclamatory (strong emotion)

Then check: is it giving a command? If yes, Imperative. If it is just stating information, it is Declarative. Words like "worried," "wondered," or "asked" do not make a sentence imperative — the sentence is simply reporting what someone felt or thought.

Essay Development — Add Depth

Q11 — 14 out of 20 — body paragraphs need more evidence
📊 Why 14 instead of 18: Your essay opens with a good rhetorical question and shows you understand the passage. But each body paragraph makes a general claim without enough specific passage evidence to back it up. A grader needs to see: claim → specific detail from the text → explanation of what that detail proves.
EXAMPLE: A BODY PARAGRAPH THAT STAYS GENERAL
"The solar panels helped the village in many ways. They provided electricity for homes and businesses. This made life better for everyone who lived there."
Which version develops the point with specific passage evidence?
A "The solar panels helped the village in many ways. They gave people electricity that they did not have before. This made life better and helped a lot of people in the community."
B "The solar panels transformed the village's healthcare. According to the passage, the medical clinic could now refrigerate vaccines that previously spoiled in the heat. This meant that children no longer had to travel hours to a distant hospital for basic immunizations — solar energy was directly saving lives in the community."
THE PATTERN: CLAIM → DETAIL → EXPLANATION
Every body paragraph should follow this three-step pattern:

1. Claim — one sentence stating what you will prove
2. Detail — a specific fact, quote, or example from the passage
3. Explanation — 1–2 sentences explaining what that detail shows and why it matters

If your paragraph only has step 1 (the claim), it feels empty. If it has steps 1 and 2, it feels like a list. When you add step 3, the grader sees thinking — and that is where the points are.

The "what this shows" move that earns development points
💡 The move you are missing: You pull details from the passage (good), but you stop there. The grader needs one more sentence: "This shows that..." or "This matters because..." That sentence is where you prove you actually understood the detail, not just copied it.

YOUR SENTENCE STARTERS
After you write a passage detail, use one of these to launch your explanation:

• "This shows that..."
• "This matters because..."
• "This reveals that..."
• "In other words,..."

If you cannot finish the sentence, the detail might not connect to your claim — pick a different one. If you can finish it easily, you just earned 2–3 more points on your essay.
