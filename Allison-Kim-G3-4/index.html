Write Complete Sentences

A complete sentence needs a subject and a verb

Allison, on the questions you did attempt, a pattern shows up: your ideas are there but they are not always in complete sentence form. Here is what that looks like:

Q6 — 1 out of 3
You wrote: "She is a brave and she loves the outdoors"
What went well: You named a character trait ("brave") and added a second idea.
What to fix: "She is a brave" is not quite right. It needs to be "She is brave" (drop the "a"). Also, the sentence is missing a period at the end.
Q6 STRONGER VERSION
She is brave and she loves the outdoors.
Drop the extra "a" and add a period. Now it is a complete sentence.
Q7 — 2 out of 3
You wrote: "Yes because feel like that is the right thing to do"
What went well: You gave an opinion and a reason ("the right thing to do"). Great thinking.
What to fix: The word "I" is missing before "feel." Without it, the sentence has no subject. Also missing a period at the end.
Q7 STRONGER VERSION
Yes, because I feel like that is the right thing to do.
Add "I" before "feel" and end with a period. Now it is complete.
Q10 — 1 out of 3
You wrote: "So you won't get in troble."
What went well: You tried to give a reason.
What to fix: Starting with "So" makes this a fragment. It needs a full sentence before it. Also "troble" should be "trouble."
Q10 STRONGER VERSION
You should follow the rules so you will not get in trouble.
Now it starts with a subject ("You") and has a complete thought.

See the pattern? Every time, the fix is the same: make sure your sentence has a subject (who or what), a verb (what they do), and a period at the end.

Complete Sentence Checklist

DOES WHAT? Add a verb (is, feels, should, loves, etc.)
END IT Put a period at the end of every sentence

WHAT "STRONGER" MEANS
A stronger verb paints a clearer picture. For example: "walked" is okay, but "stomped" or "tiptoed" is stronger because it tells you how someone moved. When the question says "stronger," pick a verb that is more specific.

The Paragraph: What It Is and How to Start

Q11 is worth 20 points — the biggest question on the test

Allison, Question 11 asks you to write a paragraph. This is one block of sentences (usually 3 to 5) that all talk about the same topic. On this test, you left it blank. That is 20 points at zero.

Here is the good news: you do not need a perfect paragraph. Even 2 or 3 sentences earn real points. Look at the difference:

A blank or "?" 0 / 20
One real sentence answering the prompt ~6 / 20
2 or 3 sentences with one detail ~10 / 20
4 sentences with details and a closing ~15 / 20

Just going from blank to 2 or 3 sentences is worth about 10 points. That alone would push your overall score from 18% up to about 40%.

FULLER PARAGRAPH — ABOUT 15/20

Layla is a girl who loves exploring nature. On her walk, she finds a geode, which is a special rock with crystals inside. She also sees a beautiful butterfly and takes a picture of it with her camera. Layla shows that being curious about nature can lead to amazing discoveries.

Topic sentence + two details + closing. Four sentences. That is the full shape.

The Paragraph Shape
TOPIC Turn the prompt into a sentence (your main idea)
DETAIL 1 One thing from the passage that supports your idea
DETAIL 2 A second thing from the passage
CLOSING Wrap back to your main idea in different words
