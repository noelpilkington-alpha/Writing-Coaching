Question 3 Fix — Using "Because" Correctly

You scored 0/1 on this question.

The question asked: Choose the sentence that correctly uses "because."

Which sentence uses "because" correctly?
A "Carter planted milkweed, because it attracts monarch butterflies."
B "Carter planted milkweed because it attracts monarch butterflies."
YOU CHOSE
"Carter planted milkweed, because it attracts monarch butterflies."
THE RULE: NO COMMA BEFORE "BECAUSE"
When "because" comes in the middle of a sentence and gives the reason for something, you do not put a comma before it.

• Correct: "I stayed inside because it was raining."
• Incorrect: "I stayed inside, because it was raining."

Think of it this way: the reason is a direct part of the sentence, not an add-on. You would not write "I ate lunch, because I was hungry" — the reason and the action are one idea.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
"Because" sentences appear in almost every essay you write. If you put a comma before "because" every time, it adds up across the whole essay and costs you points on grammar and punctuation. Getting this one rule right will help on every future test.

Gardner, you wrote 5 clear paragraphs about how people help monarchs. Good structure. Let's look at what to improve.

INTRODUCTION Every fall, millions of monarch butterflies fly thousands of miles from Canada to Mexico. When spring arrives, the new generation flies back north. This is one of the most remarkable events of migration in nature.

BODY 1 (good setup) But lately, fewer monarchs are migrating for a few reasons. First, more farms are plowing land and destroying milkweed, monarch butterflies' favorite food. Second, strong storms are destroying the forest that they take shelter in. For these reasons, scientists worry that the monarchs are dying out.

BODY 2 (vague evidence) But millions of people are helping in many ways. Some people tag butterflies and track them so they can see the population levels. Some people make gardens with milweed and shelter. And some people donate to organizations that help monarchs.

BODY 3 (shifts to "you" — informal tone) There are a few things you can do as well. First, don't destroy monarch shelter or milkweed. Second, make gardens or a shelter for monarchs. Lastly, tell other people to do the same things and why monarchs need help.

CONCLUSION Many people believe that we are the problem, but in reality, we are the problem and also the solution. If we keep working, eventually, maybe in 10 years, the monarch population will return to normal. If everyone helps, even just a bit, we can make big changes.
WHAT YOU DID WELL
You have all 5 paragraphs. Body 1 is your strongest — it explains why monarchs are in trouble with specific reasons (farms destroying milkweed, storms destroying forests). Your conclusion has a strong final thought. Good transitions with "First," "Second," and "Lastly."
ISSUE 1: VAGUE EVIDENCE IN BODY 2
Body 2 says "some people tag butterflies" and "some people make gardens." These ideas come from the passage, but they are vague. Who tags butterflies? The passage calls them citizen scientists. What do they record? Where do they track them? Add specific details from the passage and explain what the evidence shows.
ISSUE 2: AVOID SECOND PERSON ("YOU") IN FORMAL ESSAYS
Body 3 switches to "you": "There are a few things you can do." In a formal essay, avoid speaking directly to the reader. Instead, use:

• "There are a few things people can do."
• "Individuals can also help by..."
• "Communities can take action by..."
