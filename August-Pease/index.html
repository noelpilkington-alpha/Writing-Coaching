Make Your Appositive Accurate

Q1 — The detail must match the noun

Q1 asked you to add an appositive giving more detail about Willow State Park. You added one, but the detail was inaccurate.

YOU WROTE
Every summer, the Thompsons camped at the Willow State Park, a campsite with hiking trails and a bird sanctuary.

The problem: Willow is a state park, not "a campsite." A state park is much bigger than a campsite. Also, "the Willow State Park" should just be "Willow State Park" (no extra "the"). Your appositive structure (commas, placement) is correct, but the description must be accurate.

FIXED
Every summer, the Thompsons camped at Willow State Park, a large nature reserve with hiking trails and a bird sanctuary.
APPOSITIVE ACCURACY CHECK
An appositive renames or describes the noun right before it. Before you write one, ask: "Is my description actually true?" A state park is not a campsite. It could be "a large nature reserve," "a popular outdoor destination," or "a park known for its wildlife." Always match the detail to what the noun really is.

"Might Have," Not "Might Of"

Q9 — A common sound-alike error

On Q9, you explained how Jaylen felt when the trip was canceled. Good idea, but you used "might of" instead of "might have."

YOU WROTE
Jaylen might of felt really sad whenever he heard that the trip was canceled.

"Might of" is not correct English. When people say "might've" out loud, it sounds like "might of," but the real words are might have. This applies to all similar phrases: could have, would have, should have.

Tap to see the improved version
Read the explanation above first
FIXED
Jaylen might have felt really sad when he heard that the trip was canceled.
might of → might have (might've)
could of → could have (could've)
would of → would have (would've)
should of → should have (should've)
HOW TO REMEMBER
"Of" is a preposition (part of something). "Have" is a helping verb. You need the verb, not the preposition. Whenever you write "might of," "could of," "would of," or "should of," replace "of" with have.
