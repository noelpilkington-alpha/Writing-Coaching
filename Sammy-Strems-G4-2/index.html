Your #1 Pattern: Tangled Appositives

Q1, Q2, and Q4 lost points from twisted sentence structure

Sammy, an appositive is extra information you add to a sentence, usually between commas. You are trying to use them, which shows maturity in your writing. But the wording gets tangled when you add them.

Q1 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2 weighted points)
"The bluebonnets, which add a the flowers beautiful touch, bloom in spring."

The problem: The phrase between the commas does not make sense. "Which add a the flowers beautiful touch" has words in the wrong order and an extra "the."

FIXED VERSION
"The bluebonnets, which add a beautiful touch to the landscape, bloom in spring."
Q4 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2 weighted points)
"The festivals, which many people attend to, are held every year to celebrate robots."

The problems: "Attend to" should be just "attend" (you attend an event, you do not attend to it). Also, the comma placement and verb agreement get confused after the appositive.

FIXED VERSION
"The festivals, which many people attend, are held every year to celebrate advances in robotics."
THE FIX: THE REMOVAL TEST
After you write a sentence with an appositive (a phrase between commas), remove that phrase and read the sentence without it. Does it still make sense?

• "The bluebonnets which add a the flowers beautiful touch bloom in spring." → "The bluebonnets bloom in spring." ✓ Good skeleton.
• Now check: does the phrase between the commas make sense on its own? "Which add a the flowers beautiful touch" → No. Rewrite it: "which add a beautiful touch to the landscape."

Fragment and Comma Breaks

Q2 still has a comma breaking the sentence
Q2 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2 weighted points)
"Bluebonnets are Texas's state flower, because they grow wild across the fields and make everything look beautiful."

The problem: When "because" comes in the middle of a sentence (after the main clause), you do not put a comma before it. The comma makes the sentence feel broken.

FIXED VERSION
"Bluebonnets are Texas's state flower because they grow wild across the fields and make everything look beautiful."
Which sentence uses "because" correctly without a comma break?
A "People are excited about robots, because they can make life easier."
B "People are excited about robots because they can make life easier."
THE COMMA-BEFORE-BECAUSE RULE
No comma before "because" when it comes after the main clause:
• "She ran because she was late." ✓
• "She ran, because she was late." ✗

Exception: Use a comma only when "because" introduces a reason that negates the main clause: "I did not go, because I was sick." (This is rare at G4 level. For now, just drop the comma.)

Spelling: "Hear" vs "Here"

Q11 had a homophone error that cost a point

Sammy, in your paragraph you wrote "here" when you meant "hear." These are homophones (words that sound the same but have different meanings and spellings).

YOUR SENTENCE
"Many people want to here about what robots can do."
CORRECT VERSION
"Many people want to hear about what robots can do."
THE QUICK CHECK
Hear = with your ears (has "ear" inside it)
Here = in this place (has "here" like "where" and "there")

Memory trick: "Hear" has an EAR in it. If you are talking about listening, use "hear."
Which sentence uses the correct spelling?
A "The audience gathered to hear the scientist's speech about artificial intelligence."
B "The audience gathered to here the scientist's speech about artificial intelligence."
PROOFREADING TIP
When you use "hear" or "here," pause and ask: am I talking about listening or a place? This one-second check catches the error every time.
