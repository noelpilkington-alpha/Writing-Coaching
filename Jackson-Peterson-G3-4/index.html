Error #1: Keep the Same Meaning

Q3 — 1 out of 2 — you changed what the sentence was asking

Jackson, on Q3 you were asked to convert a statement into a question without changing its meaning. You wrote a question, but it asked about something different.

Q3 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2)
"What kind of rock is a geode?"

The problem: The original sentence was about something specific (like whether geodes are found in certain places, or how geodes form). Your question changes the topic to "what kind of rock" which is a completely different idea.

The rule: When you convert a sentence, the meaning must stay the same. Only the form changes (statement becomes question, or question becomes statement).

THE FIX
Read the original sentence. Identify the main idea. Then turn that exact idea into a question without changing what it is about. If the original says "Geodes are found in dry areas," the question version is "Are geodes found in dry areas?" Not "What kind of rock is a geode?"
The sentence says: "Geodes can take millions of years to form." Which question keeps the same meaning?
A "What are geodes made of?"
B "Can geodes take millions of years to form?"
YOUR NEW RULE
Before you submit a conversion answer, ask yourself: "Does my answer talk about the same thing as the original?" If the original is about how long something takes, your answer must also be about how long something takes. If the topic changed, rewrite it.

Error #2: Write Complete Sentences with Full Explanations

Q5, Q6, Q8, Q9 — fragments and answers that are too brief
🔎 Pattern: On four different questions, you either wrote a fragment (not a complete sentence) or gave an answer that was too short to earn full points. This is the biggest point drain on your test.
Q5 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2) — FRAGMENT
"Because geodes are beautiful inside."

The problem: This is a fragment. It starts with "Because" but has no main clause. A complete sentence needs a subject doing something.

Q8 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 3) — TOO BRIEF
"They are special."

The problem: The question asked you to explain something, but your answer only makes a claim without any reason. "They are special" does not tell us why or how.

Rule 2: After your main idea, add "because" + one specific reason. Do not stop at just the claim. Tell us WHY or HOW.
