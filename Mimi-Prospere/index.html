"To" vs. "Too" — Know the Difference

Q9 — A common mix-up

On Q9, you explained how Jaylen felt about the canceled trip. Great ideas and complex sentence structure, but you used "too" when you meant "to."

YOU WROTE
Because the trip was something the whole family was looking forward too, and because Jaylen did so much work in packing...

"Looking forward to" uses "to" (a preposition showing direction). "Too" means "also" or "excessively." They sound the same but mean different things.

Read the explanation above first
FIXED
Because the trip was something the whole family was looking forward to, and because Jaylen did so much work in packing and organizing, it makes sense he would be devastated when the trip was canceled so suddenly.
looking forward too → looking forward to
I want too go → I want to go

HOW TO TELL THEM APART
"To" = a preposition or part of a verb (going to the store, want to go, looking forward to)
"Too" = means "also" or "too much" (I want to go too; it's too hot)

Quick test: Can you replace the word with "also" or "excessively"? If yes, use "too." If not, use "to."

THE ONE-SENTENCE RULE
When the question says "Write one sentence," keep it focused on one main idea with one supporting reason. You don't need to include every thought you have. Pick your strongest point, state it clearly, and support it with "because." A focused sentence is stronger than a packed one.

One Paragraph, Not Many

Q11 — 18/20. Great ideas, wrong format

Your paragraph scored 18/20 — your ideas and evidence are strong. But the prompt asked for one clear paragraph, and you wrote four separate paragraphs.

WHAT YOU WROTE (SHORTENED)
Paragraph 1: "There are many ways students learn... VR field trips can provide this..."
Paragraph 2: "Virtual-reality, known as VR, allows students to be transported..."
Paragraph 3: "VR field trips are also much safer and simpler..."
Paragraph 4: "For these reasons, I would consider virtual reality to be a worthy investment..."

Each of these paragraphs has great content — strong evidence, good vocabulary, clear reasoning. But on this test, the directions say "Write one clear paragraph." All your ideas should flow together in a single block of text.

Here's how to combine your ideas into one paragraph:

Paragraph Plan — TS → D1 → W → D2 → W → D3 → W → CS
TS = Topic Sentence • D = Detail from the article • W = Why it matters • CS = Concluding Sentence
TS The author shows that VR field trips are a good idea for schools because they improve learning, increase accessibility, and save money.
D1 VR allows students to be transported to places they otherwise could not visit.
W This helps students interact with lessons and remember them better than traditional methods.
D2 VR field trips are safer and simpler, cutting costs like transportation and permission slips.
W This makes learning accessible for everyone, including students with physical disabilities.
D3 Though headsets are expensive, they save money compared to multiple real-world trips.
W This makes VR a cost-effective long-term investment for schools.
CS For all these reasons, VR field trips are a worthy investment that makes learning exciting and fair for every student.
THE KEY: ONE PARAGRAPH = ONE BLOCK OF TEXT
You clearly know how to write an essay with multiple paragraphs — that's an advanced skill! But when the prompt says "one paragraph," keep all your sentences in one continuous block. Use transition words (First, Additionally, Finally) to move between ideas instead of starting new paragraphs. Also note: a few spelling errors ("sutdents," "cancled," "spontanious," "acedemic") — proofread carefully.
