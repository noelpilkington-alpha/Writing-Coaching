Fix Run-On Sentences

Q7 — 2 out of 3

On Q7, you had a comma splice — two complete thoughts joined with just a comma. That's called a run-on sentence.

YOU WROTE
"One time I was in the forest and saw a little plant, when I went up to it I saw that it had four leaves..."

The problem: "I was in the forest and saw a little plant" is a complete thought. "When I went up to it I saw that it had four leaves" is another complete thought. You cannot join them with just a comma.

Two ways to fix it:

Tap to see fixed versions
Think about it first — how would you split it?
FIX 1: USE A PERIOD
"One time I was in the forest and saw a little plant. When I went up to it, I saw that it had four leaves..."
FIX 2: ADD A CONJUNCTION
"One time I was in the forest and saw a little plant, and when I went up to it I saw that it had four leaves..."
THE RULE
If you have two complete thoughts (each could be its own sentence), you need one of these between them:

1. A period. End the first thought. Start a new sentence.
2. A comma + a conjunction (and, but, so). The conjunction connects them properly.

A comma alone is never enough to join two complete thoughts.
How to spot a comma splice
Read the words before the comma. Could they stand alone as a sentence? Now read the words after the comma. Could those stand alone too? If both answers are yes and there is no conjunction, you have a comma splice. Add a period or a conjunction.

Start With a Thesis, Not Meta-Commentary

Q11 — 17 out of 20

Your paragraph has good ideas inside it, but the opening loses points because it tells the reader what you are about to do instead of making your point.

YOU WROTE
"Hi in this analysis I will go over why ideas and character helped him find a way to grow plants..."

What's wrong:

  • "Hi" is too informal for a written paragraph. Do not greet the reader.
  • "In this analysis I will go over why..." is meta-commentary — it describes what you plan to write instead of actually writing it.

What to do instead: Jump straight into your main point. Your topic sentence should state the answer, not announce that you are going to give an answer.

Tap to see a strong thesis opening
Notice: no greetings, no "I will go over"
STRONG TOPIC SENTENCE
"Yacouba's ideas and determination helped him find a way to grow plants in the desert, turning dry land into green fields."

This jumps straight to the point. The reader immediately knows your argument. No greetings, no announcements.

THE TEST: CROSS IT OUT
After writing your first sentence, try crossing out everything before the main idea. If you can remove "Hi" or "In this analysis I will go over why" and the sentence still makes sense — those words should not be there in the first place.

Never start a paragraph with:
• "Hi" or "Hello"
• "In this paragraph I will..."
• "I am going to explain..."

Instead, start with your answer: State the main idea directly.

Paragraph Review

Q11 — 17 out of 20 — Detail + Explanation

Your paragraph scored 17/20. The body has good content about Yacouba's methods. To reach 20/20, your paragraph needs a strong thesis opening (Card 5 showed you that) and the Detail + Explanation pattern throughout.

What is Detail + Explanation?

Every time you give a detail from the passage, follow it with a sentence that explains why that detail matters to your main point.

G4 Paragraph Plan: TS → D + E → D + E → D + E → CS
TS = Topic Sentence  •  D = Detail from the passage  •  E = Explanation (why it matters)  •  CS = Concluding Sentence
TS Yacouba's ideas and determination helped him grow plants in the desert by using a simple, old farming method.
D First, he dug small holes and filled them with manure and leaves, then planted seeds inside.
E This shows that his idea was creative because he used materials that were already available, even in dry land.
D Other villagers laughed at him, but he kept working and did not give up.
E This shows his strong character because he believed in his method even when others doubted him.
D Eventually, the plants grew and many other people started using his method too.
E This proves that his determination paid off and helped his whole community, not just himself.
CS Yacouba's creativity and refusal to quit turned dry desert into green, growing land.
DETAIL + EXPLANATION IN ACTION
After each detail, ask yourself: "Why does this matter to my main point?"
Your answer to that question is your Explanation sentence. It connects the detail back to your topic sentence.

Detail alone: "He dug holes and filled them with manure."
Detail + Explanation: "He dug holes and filled them with manure. This shows his idea was creative because he used simple materials."
