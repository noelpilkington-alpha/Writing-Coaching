THE 90% ESSAY FORMULA
Every body paragraph should have this five-part shape:

1. Topic sentence — states the body paragraph's focus.
2. Evidence — a specific detail from the passage.
3. Explanation — "This shows..." / "This proves..." / "In other words..."
4. Second detail or example — another fact or quote from the passage.
5. Tie-back — one sentence that connects both details back to the thesis.

That shape works for every G6 expository essay. Locking it in moves the 5-point evidence row from 2–3 up to 5/5.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Your essay landed at 15/20. Adding one specific fact from the passage and one "this shows" sentence per body paragraph — three times — is worth +3 to +4 points on the essay and 15 to 20 percentage points on the overall test. 80% goes to 90%+.

Your One Multiple Choice Miss

Q3 — combining two ideas with a logical conjunction

Jude, you went 9 out of 10 on multiple choice. The only miss was Q3, which asked you to combine two sentences using a conjunction that matches the logic of the ideas.

Q3 — What Was Asked
PROMPT Which sentence combines the ideas using a logical conjunction? "Animated movies are fun. They also include valuable lessons."
CORRECT Animated movies are fun but they include valuable lessons.
YOURS Animated movies are fun because they include valuable lessons.

Here is the logic: "because" says one idea causes the other — as in "it is raining because the sky is cloudy." But fun and lessons do not cause each other. They are two separate qualities held together. That is a contrast, so the right connector is "but" — or "and," or "yet." Those connect two parallel ideas without claiming one produced the other.

🧩
Same skill shows up in the essay. Choosing the exact conjunction that matches the logic of two ideas is the same move you make inside every paragraph. "Because" is strong and you use it well for cause/effect — just save it for when one thing actually causes the other.
Which sentence uses the correct conjunction for the logic?
A Animated movies are usually short because they are animated.
B Animated movies are usually short and they are colorful.
THE CONJUNCTION CHEAT SHEET
Match the word to the logic between the two ideas:

because / so — one idea causes the other
but / yet / although — the two ideas contrast
and / also — the two ideas add together
if / when — one idea depends on the other

Before you pick, ask: "Does idea A cause idea B? Contrast it? Add to it?" The answer names the right conjunction.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Getting that one multiple choice question back is worth +1 percentage point — small by itself. But the bigger win is that the same logic question shows up inside your essay every time you join two ideas. Tighten conjunction choice and every paragraph reads stronger.
