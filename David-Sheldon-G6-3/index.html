Error #1: Kernel Expansion

Step 2 skill: identifying the correct sentence expansion

David, Q5 asked you to identify the correct kernel expansion, and you chose the wrong option. This was your only missed question on the multiple-choice section.

WHAT IS KERNEL EXPANSION?
A "kernel sentence" is the simplest version of a sentence (subject + verb + object). Expansion means adding details (who, what, when, where, why, how) without changing the core meaning.

The correct expansion keeps the same subject doing the same action but adds description. If the expansion changes who is doing what, it is not a valid expansion of the original kernel.
Kernel sentence: "The butterflies travel." Which is a valid expansion?
A"Scientists study the butterflies that travel south."
B"The monarch butterflies travel thousands of miles each autumn to reach warmer climates."
THE TEST
Ask: "Is the same person or thing still doing the same action?" If yes, it is a valid expansion. If the subject or action changed, it is not.

Your Essay Needs More Development

12 out of 20 — the ideas are there but underdeveloped

David, your essay earned 12 out of 20. The biggest reason is that your body paragraphs are too short and lack specific evidence from the passage. You make claims but do not support them with details or explain what those details prove.

THE DETAIL + EXPLANATION PATTERN
Every body paragraph needs:
1. Topic sentence (one claim that supports your thesis)
2. Evidence (a specific fact, quote, or detail from the passage)
3. Explanation (what does this evidence prove? why does it matter?)

Think of it as: Claim, Evidence, Explanation. If you skip the evidence or the explanation, the paragraph is underdeveloped.

Spelling: Proofread Your Essay

Step 5 catch: "magestic" should be "majestic"

David, your essay contained the spelling error magestic (correct: majestic). At the G6 level, spelling errors in the essay cost points under the Conventions category of the rubric.

THE FIX: WHISPER-READ YOUR ESSAY
After you finish writing, go back to the first sentence and read every word slowly. When you hit a word that looks unusual, say it syllable by syllable:

• "ma-jes-tic" — you hear "jes" not "ges"

This takes 3 to 5 minutes and catches spelling errors that cost points.
Which sentence has the correct spelling?
A"The magestic monarch butterflies migrate thousands of miles."
B"The majestic monarch butterflies migrate thousands of miles."
WHY THIS MATTERS
Spelling errors in the essay contribute to a lower Conventions score. A clean essay with no spelling errors signals control over language and earns higher marks in that category.

Plan Your Essay Before Writing

Step 3: spend 5 minutes outlining

David, the single biggest improvement you can make is planning your essay before you start writing. Right now your essay reads like a first draft that goes wherever your thoughts take you. An outline keeps you focused.

ESSAY OUTLINE TEMPLATE
Before you write a single sentence, type this in the scratchpad:

Thesis: [Answer the prompt in one sentence with 2-3 reasons]

Body 2: [Second reason + what evidence I will use]
Body 3: [Third reason + what evidence I will use]

Then write your essay following the plan. Each body paragraph develops ONE reason with evidence + explanation.
