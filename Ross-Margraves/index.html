Sharpen Your Thesis

Your introduction needs a roadmap
Your Introduction
Every human being speaks some kind of language. Especialy in the United States, where communities are built of immigrants all speaking different languages. Learning a second language is a very hard thing to do, but children have to develope their own language skills in many ways.

Your thesis (highlighted) is too vague. It says "in many ways" but doesn't tell the reader which ways. A strong thesis previews the three points your body paragraphs will make.

Also, your second sentence is a fragment — it starts with "Especially" but has no main verb.

STRONGER VERSION
Learning a second language is challenging, but children can develop their skills through immersion classes, pen pal programs, and starting at a young age.
THE THESIS FORMULA
[Main idea] + [Point 1], [Point 2], and [Point 3].

This gives your reader a roadmap. They know exactly what's coming in each body paragraph. It also helps you stay organized while you write.

Use Specific Evidence from the Passage

The difference between a good essay and a great one

You referenced the right ideas from the passage (immersion, pen pals, learning young), but your evidence was general instead of specific. The rubric requires you to use facts from the passage and explain what they show.

You wrote (general) What the passage actually says (specific)
"just learning a language through your teacher talking in school" "In immersion classes, students must use the new language all day" — during lessons, group work, and daily conversations
"one kid can send messeges to another kid across the world" Pen pal programs let students "practice reading and writing in the new language" through real communication
"there are studys proving that learning a second language while you're young can strengthen your problem-solving skills" Young children's "brains are still developing" which makes them better at picking up new sounds and patterns
THE EVIDENCE + EXPLANATION PATTERN
Evidence: The passage explains that [specific detail].
Explanation: This shows that [why it matters / how it connects to your point].

Use this pair twice per body paragraph. That's the structure that earns full evidence points.

The Body Paragraph Pattern

Use this exact structure for every body paragraph

Here's how your Body Paragraph 1 (immersion) could look using the full pattern:

STRONGER BODY PARAGRAPH 1

Topic sentence: First, immersion classes are an effective way for children to learn a new language.

Evidence 1: The passage explains that in immersion classes, students use the new language all day during lessons, group work, and conversations.

Explanation: This shows that students learn not just in formal lessons, but by hearing and speaking the language constantly.

Evidence 2: The passage also says that students learn new words because they hear the language in every part of class.

Explanation: This means that constant practice helps children build vocabulary naturally, rather than just memorizing words.

Concluding sentence: Because of this, immersion is one of the most effective methods for language learning.

MEMORIZE THIS STRUCTURE
TS → Topic Sentence
E1 → Evidence from the passage
X1 → Explanation (This shows that...)
E2 → Evidence from the passage
X2 → Explanation (This means that...)
CS → Concluding Sentence

TS → E1 → X1 → E2 → X2 → CS — use it for all three body paragraphs.
