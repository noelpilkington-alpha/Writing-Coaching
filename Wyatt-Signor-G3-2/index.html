Your SPO (Single Paragraph Outline)
TOPIC One sentence that says your main idea.
D1 Supporting Detail 1: A specific fact from the passage + one sentence of your own explanation.
D2 Supporting Detail 2: A different fact from the passage + your explanation.
CLOSE One sentence that wraps up your main idea.
HOW TO USE THE SPO ON TEST DAY
1. Type the four tags (TOPIC, D1, D2, CLOSE) at the top of your answer box.
2. Next to each tag, type a short phrase for what goes there.
3. Write your paragraph below using the outline.
4. Delete the outline before you submit.
The Evidence Sandwich (for your D1 and D2)

When you pull a detail from the passage, make a sandwich:

TOP Set it up: A phrase that tells what the detail is about. ("The passage says...")
FILL The detail: The quote or paraphrased fact from the passage.
BOTTOM Explain it: One sentence in your own words about why it matters.
DO
  • Use your own words most of the time
  • Put quote marks around exact words from the passage
  • Pick a different fact for D1 and D2
  • Explain why the fact matters in your own words
  • Use specifics: names, numbers, places
DO NOT
  • Copy a whole passage sentence without quote marks
  • Drop a fact with no explanation after it
  • Use the same fact twice in D1 and D2
  • Jam two sentences together with only a comma
  • Write only one big run-on sentence

Appositives — Commas on BOTH Sides

G3.1 Q1 scored 0% — time to lock this in

Wyatt, on your G3.1 test, Question 1 asked you to write an appositive. Your answer started with "Hello! My name is Wyatt Signor and I like video games..." — you wrote a self-introduction instead of an appositive. That question scored 0.

G3.2 didn't have an appositive question, so this skill hasn't been re-tested yet. Let's make sure you're ready next time.

What an Appositive IS
DEF An appositive is a noun phrase that renames another noun right next to it, with commas on BOTH sides.
My dog, Buddy, loves the park. → "Buddy" renames "my dog." Commas before AND after.
EX 1 My neighbor, an amazing baker, gave me cookies.
EX 2 Austin, the capital of Texas, has a lot of parks.
EX 3 My favorite book, Diary of a Wimpy Kid, is really funny.
What an Appositive IS NOT
NOT Not an appositive: a whole new sentence joined with "and." That's a compound sentence.
NOT Not an appositive: one adjective like "fast" or "red." Those just describe — they don't rename.
NOT Not an appositive: a self-introduction or a long story about yourself.
NOT Not an appositive: a noun phrase with a comma only on ONE side. Both sides, always.
The prompt: "Expand this sentence using an appositive: My cousin plays soccer." Which answer is a real appositive?
A My cousin plays soccer and he is also really fast.
B My cousin, a kid from Dallas, plays soccer.
YOUR APPOSITIVE CHECK BEFORE YOU SUBMIT
1. Did I add a noun phrase (a word or few words that name a person, place, or thing)?
2. Does the noun phrase rename the noun right before it?
3. Is there a comma BEFORE the noun phrase AND a comma AFTER it?
If all three are YES, you've got an appositive.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Appositive questions show up on writing tests. You got 0 on your first one. The rule is short: a noun phrase that renames another noun, with commas on BOTH sides. Lock this in and a future Q1 appositive goes from 0 to full credit.

Proofread Before You Submit

You had 28 minutes — you have time to proofread

Wyatt, your pacing on G3.2 was solid. 28 minutes is a good, careful pace for this test. That means you have time for one clean pass through your answers before you hit submit. Here's what a proofread pass should catch:

Your Personalized CUPS Check
C Capitalization: Start every sentence with a capital letter, and capitalize names (Wyatt, Austin, Hershey Bears, Camel Mobile Library).
U Usage — apostrophes: its means "belonging to it." it's means "it is." On Q7 you wrote "its only 1 point" — you meant it's. Check every "its" — if you can swap in "it is," use it's.
P Punctuation — comma splices: On Q11 you joined two sentences with just a comma. Two complete thoughts need a period, OR the word "and"/"but"/"so" after the comma. Also: comma after Yes or No at the start of an answer (Q8: "Yes, I would...").
S Spelling — long words: On Q11 you wrote Libary (it's library — "libr-ary") and alot (it's a lot — two words). Long words are where spelling slips hide.
You're proofreading your answer. Which sentence is fully correct?
A The Camel Mobile Libary helps alot of kids and its really special.

YOUR 4-STEP PROOFREAD PASS
1. Read each answer out loud in your head — one sentence at a time.
2. Stop at every "its" — does it mean "it is"? Add the apostrophe.
3. Check long words like library, a lot, really, because.
4. Check your paragraph — are there any commas where a period should go?

Proofread slips cost you a point here, half a point there. Over one test, that's easily 2–3 points. With your 28-minute pace, you have time for a clean proofread pass — and those 2–3 points are yours for free.
