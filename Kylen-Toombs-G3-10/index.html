Your #1 Polish Target: Appositive Combine

G3.10 Q1 — you were close, one small fix gets full credit

Kylen, this is the same appositive pattern that tripped you on G3.9 — but this time you got much closer. You used the right structure idea: two commas, a describing phrase, and no second "she is" or "it is" floating around. The small slip is that you dropped the original subject of the first sentence, so the combined result rewrote things instead of just inserting an appositive.

G3.10 Q1 — APPOSITIVE COMBINE (SCORED 0.5/1)
Prompt: "Combine Sentence 2 and Sentence 3 into one sentence using an appositive. Sentence 2: The first bikes looked very different from bikes today. Sentence 3: Long ago, the first bicycle was made of wood."
You wrote: "the first bikes, primitive machines made of wood, looked very diffrent from bikes today."
Good news: you DID use a true appositive ("primitive machines made of wood") with commas around it. Small misses: (1) lowercase "the" at the start, (2) "diffrent" should be "different", and (3) the phrase is invented rather than taken from Sentence 3's actual words ("made of wood / long ago").

A full-credit appositive combine pulls a noun phrase from Sentence 3, wraps it in commas, and tucks it next to the matching noun in Sentence 2. Same two commas you used — just closer to the source text:

WHAT FULL CREDIT LOOKS LIKE
✓ "The first bikes, made of wood long ago, looked very different from bikes today."
✓ "The first bicycle, a wooden machine, looked very different from bikes today."

Notice: no "she is" or "it was" — the appositive is just the renaming phrase, set off by two commas, capital "T" at the start, and the word "different" spelled out fully.
G3.10 Q1 — which sentence uses an appositive most cleanly?
A the first bikes, primitive machines made of wood, looked very diffrent from bikes today.
B The first bikes, made of wood long ago, looked very different from bikes today.
THE APPOSITIVE 3-STEP
Every time you see a "combine with an appositive" prompt, do this:

1. Pick the sentence with the main action (that one stays mostly the same).
2. From the other sentence, grab the describing phrase (drop "He is / She is / It was").
3. Wrap it in two commas right after the noun it describes.

Example from G3.10:
Main: "The first bikes looked very different from bikes today."
Grabbed phrase (from "the first bicycle was made of wood"): "made of wood"
Combined: "The first bikes, made of wood, looked very different from bikes today."
THE APPOSITIVE TEST
After you combine, read the part between your two commas. Ask: "Does this part have its own subject + verb?"

• If NO → real appositive. ✓
• If YES → it's a run-on. Drop the subject + verb.

✓ "bikes, made of wood, looked..." ← no subject/verb inside the commas → appositive
✗ "bikes, they were made of wood, looked..." ← "they were" = subject + verb → run-on
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Q1 cost you half a point on G3.10 — same pattern that cost you half a point on G3.9. Lock the 3-step in, and this is automatic points on every writing test that has a "combine with appositive" question. That is the first half-point between 86% and 87%.

THE COMMA-BEFORE-SO RULE
When you join two full sentences using because, but, or so, check: does each half work on its own as a sentence?

• "It was hard to balance on this bike." ✓ full sentence
• "Many people fell off and got hurt." ✓ full sentence

Two full sentences + joining word → add a comma before the joining word.
✓ "It was hard to balance, so many people fell off."
✓ "People fell off, but the bike was fun."
✓ "People fell off, because the bike was hard to balance."
THE SPELLING SWEEP (DO THIS LAST 90 SECONDS)

• safer (not "safier") — comparative of "safe"
• different (not "diffrent") — two Fs, one E before -rent
• unbalanced (no hyphen)
• its vs it's — if you can swap in "it is," use it's
• Capital letter at the start of every answer
