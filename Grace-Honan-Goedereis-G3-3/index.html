Your 6-Step Test Plan

The same steps, every test — so nothing gets rushed

Grace, when a paragraph comes out short or an answer drifts off-topic, it usually means there was no plan before the writing started. Here is a steady 6-step routine to use on every question. There is always plenty of test time available — use it.

1
Read the question twice. What is it actually asking you to do? Find the key word (describe, explain, what would you do).
2
Read the passage part you need. Go back and find the exact spot in the story or article that answers it.
3
Plan first. For the paragraph, type a quick outline in the answer box: Topic → Detail 1 → Detail 2 → Closing. Then turn it into sentences.
4
Write a complete answer. For Questions 6–10, write one full sentence that answers the question and gives a reason or example.
5
Check it matches the question. Re-read the question, then your answer. Does your answer truly answer that question?
6
Proofread. Read every answer once more for spelling and to make sure each sentence is complete.
The Single Paragraph Outline (Question 11)
TOPIC One sentence that states the main idea of your paragraph.
DETAIL 1 A specific fact or example from the article.
DETAIL 2 A second specific fact or example from the article.
CLOSING One sentence that wraps up your main idea.
WHY THIS HELPS
Step 3 is the one that lifts your paragraph the most. When you outline first, you never end up with just two sentences — the plan gives you four parts to fill in. Step 5 catches the off-topic answers before you submit.

Build a Full Paragraph

Question 11 is worth 20 of the 45 points — your biggest opportunity

There are good ideas in here — you remembered that he loved bugs. But it is only two sentences, there is no clear topic sentence, no specific detail from the article about his childhood, and the last thought is left unfinished. The outline fixes all of that.

Plan it first — the Pokémon paragraph
TOPIC Satoshi Tajiri's childhood love of collecting led him to create Pokémon.
DETAIL 1 As a kid he loved collecting bugs and exploring outside.
DETAIL 2 That's why in Pokémon you catch and collect creatures.
CLOSING His childhood hobby became a game millions of people play.

MODEL PARAGRAPH (full-credit level)

Satoshi Tajiri's childhood love of collecting is what led him to create Pokémon. When he was a kid, he loved collecting bugs and exploring outside to find new ones. That is exactly why in Pokémon you travel around to catch and collect different creatures. His favorite childhood hobby turned into a fun game that millions of people play today.

Which detail sentence does more for the paragraph?

B As a child he collected bugs, which is why you catch creatures in Pokémon.

Answer the Exact Question

Two questions where the answer drifted off-target

Grace, on a couple of questions your answer went a little off from what was actually being asked. The skill here is simple: re-read the question, then answer THAT exact question in one complete sentence with a reason. Here are the two from G3.3:

FROM G3.3 Q7 (scored 1 out of 3)
"When they all grabed the seal and helped it fight a group of seals."
In the story, the family rescued the baby seal Sally — they didn't fight any seals. This answer describes something that didn't happen, and it isn't a complete sentence.
FROM G3.3 Q9 (scored 1 out of 3)
"If I was Anna I would not wonder if they found her family or not."
The question asked what you would do differently — this says what you would NOT wonder, which doesn't tell the reader an action you'd take.
The question asks: "What would YOU do differently if you were Anna?" Which answer is on-task?
A Anna found the baby seal on the beach and helped it get back to the water.
B If I were Anna, I would call a wildlife rescue first because they know how to help a hurt seal safely.
THE 2-STEP CHECK
Step 1 — Re-read the question. Underline the action word in your head: describe, explain, what would you do differently.

Step 2 — Match your answer to it. Write one complete sentence that does exactly that, plus a reason or example. Then read the question one more time and make sure your sentence answers that question.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
On Q7 and Q9 you lost 4 points together — not because the ideas were wrong, but because the answers drifted off the question. A quick re-read before you submit catches that almost every time.

MODEL SENTENCE (full-credit level)

If you think an animal is hurt, you should go get a parent or another adult, because they can help the animal safely.

Notice: it answers the question, gives a reason ("because they can help safely"), and every word is spelled correctly.

Which answer earns full credit on a Question 6–10 prompt?
A Go get help for the seal.
B You should tell an adult right away, because an adult knows how to get the seal back to safety.
THE FORMULA
Answer + because + reason. One complete sentence. Start with what you'd do or think, add the word because, then give the reason. Read it back to make sure it's a full sentence and every word is spelled right.
