Appositives: Word Order Matters

Q4 had the right idea but scrambled structure

Daveyp, Q4 asked you to combine two sentences using an appositive. You had the right idea — you knew to use commas — but the word order got scrambled.

Q4 (1/2) — SCRAMBLED WORD ORDER
"Festivals, in small towns..., many people attend" — the appositive phrase interrupted the sentence in the wrong place, making the meaning unclear.
APPOSITIVE PATTERN
An appositive renames a noun. It goes right after the noun it renames, between commas:

Pattern: [Noun], [renaming phrase], [rest of sentence].

Example: "The festival, a popular event in small towns, attracts many visitors each year."

The appositive "a popular event in small towns" renames "The festival." It sits right after it. The rest of the sentence continues after the second comma.
CORRECT VERSION
"Festivals, popular events in small towns across the country, bring many people together every summer."

The appositive renames "Festivals" and sits between the commas. The sentence continues smoothly after.
QUICK CHECK AFTER WRITING
After you write an appositive sentence, read it without the phrase between commas. Does the sentence still make sense? If yes, the appositive is in the right place. If no, move it.
