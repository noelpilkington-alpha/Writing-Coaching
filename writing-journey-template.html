Q5 Fix — Add SPECIFIC Details

Can you spot the difference? Then we'll break it down.
📈 You've missed "adding details to sentences" questions on 2 of 3 tests — but you got it right on G6.3, which proves you CAN do this. The pattern each time: picking a vague restatement instead of specific details. This same skill is what makes body paragraphs convincing in your essays.

The question gave you a short sentence (called a "kernel"): "Children hear language."

Your job was to add real detail to make it longer and more informative. Which version does that?

Tap the one that actually adds new information:
A "Children hear language by listening to others speak."
B "Children hear the new language during lessons, group work, and simple daily conversations."

Now here's why this matters:

Vague Expansion (restates the idea) Specific Expansion (adds real detail)
"Children hear language by listening to others speak." "Children hear the new language during lessons, group work, and simple daily conversations."

The specific version answers WHEN and WHERE. That's what makes it an expansion, not a restatement.

THE EXPANSION CHECKLIST
When you need to add detail to a short sentence, run through: WHERE? WHEN? HOW? WHY?
If your longer version doesn't answer at least one of these, you've just said the same thing in different words. Real details add new information the reader didn't already have.
See it in action with another example
Short sentence: "Dogs play."
Restatement: "Dogs play by being playful with things." (same idea, different words)
Expansion: "Dogs play fetch in the backyard every evening after dinner." (WHERE + WHEN)

Q9 Fix — "While" vs. "Although"

Can you pick the right one? Then we'll explain why.
📈 Transition Words started strong (100% on first 3 tests) then slipped on G6.4. You've proven you understand transitions — this one just requires distinguishing between equal contrast and concession.

Two experts have different but equally valid opinions about when children should learn a language. Which word connects their views?

Which word presents both viewpoints as equally valid?
A "Although some experts think older students learn better in structured lessons, others argue younger children pick up new sounds more easily."
B "While some experts think older students learn better in structured lessons, others argue younger children pick up new sounds more easily."
Word What It Does When to Use It
While Shows two ideas existing at the same time When presenting two equal sides of a topic or debate
Although Admits one side is less important to support the other When one idea matters more — "Yes, this is true, BUT the other side wins"

With "While": "While some experts believe early immersion is best, others think children benefit more from structured lessons." (Both views presented equally.)

With "Although": "Although early immersion has some drawbacks, the benefits clearly outweigh them." (One side is being dismissed — "yes, BUT...")

QUICK TEST
Ask: Are both sides equal, or am I giving up one to support the other?
• Both sides equal → While
• One side matters more → Although

The Body Paragraph Pattern

How to build a strong body paragraph every time

Every body paragraph in a G6+ essay should follow this pattern:

Body Paragraph Formula
Topic Sentence States the paragraph's main point
Evidence A specific fact, quote, or example from the passage
Explain why Why does that evidence matter? ("So what?")
More Evidence A second fact or example from the passage
Explain why Why does THIS evidence matter?
Closing Sentence Wraps up the paragraph's main idea

EXAMPLE BODY PARAGRAPH: IMMERSION CLASSES
[Topic Sentence] One of the most effective ways to learn a new language is through immersion classes.
[Evidence] In immersion classes, the new language is used throughout the entire lesson.
[Explain why] This constant exposure helps children pick up new sounds and words naturally, because their brains get used to hearing and using the language all day. (So what? Why does it matter that the language is used all lesson?)
[More Evidence] Students must use the language for every activity, not just during practice time.
[Explain why] This builds real confidence because students learn to think in the new language, rather than translating from their first language every time. (So what? Why does constant use matter?)
[Closing Sentence] Immersion classes work because they surround students with the language all day long.
THE KEY RULE: "SO WHAT?"
Every piece of Evidence must be followed by an Explain why. Evidence without explanation is just a list of facts. After each piece of evidence, ask yourself: "So what? Why does this matter?" — the answer IS your explanation.

Spelling & Grammar Quick Fixes

WHY "LETS THEM HAVE" (NO "TO")
"Let" is a special verb. After "let," you use the verb by itself (no "to"):
• "She lets them have a break." (correct)
• "She lets them to have a break." (incorrect)

Compare with "allow," which does use "to": "She allows them to have a break."
3-MINUTE PROOFREADING RULE
Save the last 3 minutes of your test for proofreading. Read your essay backward (last sentence first) — when you read forward, your brain auto-corrects errors. Reading backward forces you to see each sentence on its own.
