Best-Match Multiple Choice (Q7 & Q10)

The #1 pattern on G7 — picking the tightest option, not just a plausible one

Both Q7 and Q10 asked you to pick the best option out of several that looked reasonable. Here's Q7:

Q7 — topic-sentence matching
Topic: "Benefits for local businesses after solar panels were installed"

You chose: "Businesses could stay open for longer."
Correct: "The extra hours gave customers more time to shop."

Both options mention businesses. So why is the second one tighter? The topic is about benefits for local businesses — meaning what did businesses gain? "Staying open longer" is a change, not a benefit. "Customers had more time to shop" is the actual benefit — it directly means more sales, more income.

Now Q10 — the "does NOT belong" question:

Q10 — eliminate irrelevant
Paragraph topic: Building trust in the project

You chose: "Residents learned energy-saving habits"
Correct: "The panels were installed on every rooftop."

"Residents learned energy-saving habits" actually does build trust — it shows residents engaging with the project. "The panels were installed on every rooftop" is just a physical installation fact — it has nothing to do with trust. That's the one that doesn't belong.

Try one: Which sentence best matches the topic "How the medical clinic helped patients after solar panels were installed"?
A. The clinic's rooftop received new solar panels.
B. Nurses could now treat emergencies safely during the night.
C. The village had a small medical clinic.
Yes. B directly shows patients being helped. A is about installing panels (not helping patients). C just describes the clinic's existence (not what happened after solar).
Re-read the topic: "how the clinic helped patients." A is about installing panels. C just says the clinic exists. Only B shows patients actually being helped.
THE G7 TEST
On every "best match" question, ask: "Does this option directly answer the exact wording of the topic — or is it just related to the topic?" On G6, any related option could score. On G7, only the direct-match option scores. This is the single biggest shift you'll make.

Elaboration: Specific Beats General (Q8)

Q8 is the same skill as Q7 + Q10 — pick the most specific, most direct option

Q8 asked which detail best elaborates the benefits for the medical clinic. You picked a plausible answer, but the correct one was more specific:

Q8 — elaboration
Stem: "Which detail best elaborates on the benefits for the medical clinic?"

You chose: "The clinic could hire more nurses."
Correct: "The clinic now stores vaccines safely overnight."

Why is the vaccine answer better?

  • Direct link to solar panels — storing vaccines overnight requires constant electricity (refrigeration). That's literally what solar panels enabled.
  • "Hiring more nurses" is not directly caused by solar panels — it's a budget decision. The passage never connects solar panels to hiring.
  • "Best elaborates" = the detail that flows directly from the main point. Vaccines need power. Power came from solar. That's a tight causal chain.

Here's the test to apply on G7:

The G7 Best-Match Test
1 Read the exact question wording. What specifically is it asking about? (Benefit? Cause? Match? Doesn't belong?)
2 Eliminate anything "just related." If an option is about the topic but doesn't answer the exact question, cut it.
3 Of the remaining options, pick the one with the tightest direct link. The tightest causal or logical connection wins.
WHY THIS IS A G7 SKILL
On G6 tests, one option was usually obviously wrong and two were obviously right. On G7, three options often look plausible, and you have to find the single tightest match. This is reading comprehension at a new level — and it's what you're practicing right now.

POLISHED Body 1 (elaboration added + spelling fixed)
Solar panels brought many improvements to the village. Children were able to study in the evenings without straining their eyes, which meant they could complete homework and improve their education. Shops were able to stay open later, which boosted local businesses because customers now had more time to shop and spend money. A small medical clinic that once could not operate at night was now able to store vaccines safely and run equipment after dark — a change that meant the clinic could actually save lives during nighttime emergencies.

What changed: each evidence sentence now has an "which meant..." or "because..." tail that explains the impact. That's the move from 15/20 to 17-18/20 — and it's the same skill as the best-match multiple choice questions: connect the specific detail to why it matters.

THE ONE MOVE
After every piece of evidence in a body paragraph, add a sentence that starts with "which meant...", "because...", or "this shows that...". One sentence of elaboration per evidence. That's what separates a G7 essay from a G6 essay.
