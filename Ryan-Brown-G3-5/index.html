Fix Fragments (Q6)

Q6 — 1 out of 3 — "Because" needs a main clause

Ryan, Q6 asked you to explain why the golden spines helped the books stand out. Here is what happened:

FROM G3.5 Q6 — 1 OUT OF 3
"Because nobody else put a golden spine on the books."
This starts with "Because" but has no main clause to finish the thought. It is a fragment — an incomplete sentence. It also does not really explain WHY the spines helped the books stand out.
THE RULE ABOUT "BECAUSE"
"Because" starts a reason. A reason cannot stand alone — it needs a main clause to complete the thought. If your sentence starts with "Because," you must finish it with a comma and then the main idea.
How to Fix a "Because" Fragment
FRAGMENT Because nobody else put a golden spine on the books.
FIXED Because nobody else used golden spines, the books were easy to spot on the shelf.
ALSO OK The golden spines helped the books stand out because no other publisher used that color on their covers.

Quick test: Read your sentence out loud. Does it feel like it is waiting for something more? If yes, it is probably a fragment. Add the main clause.

Which sentence correctly answers "Why did the golden spines help the books stand out?"
A Because nobody else put a golden spine on the books.
B Because no other books had golden spines, customers could find them right away on the shelf.
REMEMBER
Every time you start a sentence with "Because," check: did I finish the thought? "Because [reason], [what happened]." If there is no comma and main clause, you wrote a fragment.

Subject-Verb Agreement (Q3)

Q3 — 1.5 out of 2 — plural subjects need plural pronouns

Ryan, on Q3 you combined two sentences correctly using a conjunction — nice work on the structure. But one pronoun did not match its subject:

FROM G3.5 Q3 — 1.5 OUT OF 2
"The books had golden spines, and it had colorful pages."
The conjunction "and" is correct. But "it" refers to "the books" — which is plural. Plural subjects need "they," not "it."
The Rule
WRONG The books had golden spines, and it had colorful pages.
RIGHT The books had golden spines, and they had colorful pages.
HOW TO CHECK
After you write a pronoun (it, they, he, she), look back at the noun it replaces. Is that noun singular or plural?
Singular (one book) = it, he, she
Plural (books, children, people) = they
Which sentence has correct subject-verb agreement?
A The books had golden spines, and it had colorful pages.
B The books had golden spines, and they had colorful pages.
YOUR QUICK CHECK
After writing any sentence with a pronoun, point back to the noun it replaces. If the noun is plural (ends in -s, means more than one), the pronoun must be they/them/their.
