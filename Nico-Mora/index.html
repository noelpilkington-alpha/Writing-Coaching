THE FRAGMENT RULE
If your sentence starts with Because, Since, Although, When, Before, After, or While, it needs two parts connected by a comma — not a period.

Fragment: Because the Bears scored a goal. Fans tossed bears.
Complete: Because the Bears scored a goal, fans tossed bears onto the ice.

Quick test: If your sentence starts with "Because," look for a comma. If you see a period instead, replace it with a comma and keep going.

TWO RULES FOR ANSWERING QUESTIONS
1. Read the question twice. Does it ask you to identify something (like a sentence type) or to write something? These need different kinds of answers.

2. Finish your thought. If your answer doesn't have a period at the end AND express a complete idea, it's not done. Read it back to yourself: does it answer the question? For Q7, "What I would change" is the beginning — you still need to say what you would change and why.
See the 4 sentence types
Declarative: Makes a statement. Ends with a period. "The ice was covered in bears."
Interrogative: Asks a question. Ends with a question mark. "Did fans toss the bears?"
Exclamatory: Shows strong feeling. Ends with an exclamation mark. "What an amazing event!"
Imperative: Gives a command. "Toss the bears onto the ice."

Develop Your Paragraph

Q11 — You have a start, but you need more

Your paragraph scored 7/20. You had a topic sentence, but you only wrote 2 sentences total. A G3 paragraph needs much more development.

YOUR PARAGRAPH
The Camel Mobile Library has affected the people of Garissa. By making sure they feel like they're in the story.

Your first sentence is a good start — it answers the prompt. But "By making sure they feel like they're in the story" is a fragment (it starts with "By" and can't stand alone), and it's too vague. What story? How does the library help?

Here's the G3 paragraph plan you should follow:

Paragraph Plan — TS → D1 → W → D2 → W → D3 → W → CS
TS = Topic Sentence  •  D = Detail from the article  •  W = Why it matters  •  CS = Concluding Sentence
TS The Camel Mobile Library has greatly affected the people of Garissa by bringing books to their community.
D1 Camels carry boxes of books to villages that have no libraries or schools nearby.
W This matters because without the camels, these people would have no way to read or learn from books.
D2 Children and adults gather around the camels to borrow books and read together.
W This shows that the library brings the community together and makes learning exciting.
D3 The program has helped more children learn to read in an area where many people could not before.
W This shows that the library is changing lives by giving people a skill they will use forever.
CS The Camel Mobile Library has changed the people of Garissa by giving them access to books and the chance to learn.
THE KEY: ADD DETAILS AND EXPLAIN WHY THEY MATTER
You already know how to write a topic sentence — that's a great start! Now you need to add details from the article and explain why each one matters. After each detail, ask yourself: "So what? Why is this important?" That answer is your "W" sentence. Then end with a concluding sentence that restates your main point. Aim for 6-8 sentences total.
