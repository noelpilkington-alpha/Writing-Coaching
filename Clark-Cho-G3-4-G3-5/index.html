#1 Focus: Answer the EXACT Question

Question 11 — your writing is strong, let's point it on target
🔍 Pattern alert: On G3.5 your paragraph scored 12 of 20 — not because the writing was weak, but because it answered a different question than the one asked. This is the single biggest lever you have.

The G3.5 prompt asked: Why did Jason place his sculptures under the sea? Here is part of what you wrote — read it first, it's genuinely good:

YOU WROTE (G3.5 Q11 — paragraph scored 12/20)
"Artist Jason DeCaires Taylor has made more than 500 statues. But you will not find them in a museum above land. His art is under the ocean! Jason made two underwater statue parks. 65 statues are in one park near Grenada. He also built another one in Mexico which has close to 500 statues!"

See how much is right here? Vivid opening, real details, a clear voice. But notice: it tells us what the statues are and where they are — counts, locations, how to see them. It never plainly says WHY Jason put them there. That mismatch is the whole reason a strong-sounding paragraph scored 12/20.

FIND THE QUESTION WORD FIRST
Before you write, find the prompt's question word — WHY, HOW, or WHAT. Then write a first sentence (your central idea) that answers that exact word, and pick details that support it. If the prompt says why, your central idea needs a reason: "...because..."
The prompt asks WHY. Which opening sentence actually answers it?
A Jason made more than 500 statues, and they are in two underwater parks near Grenada and Mexico.
B Jason placed his sculptures under the sea because he wanted the ocean to grow over them and turn the art into a home for sea life.

Here is your real G3.5 opening next to a refocused topic sentence. Same subject — but the second one answers the WHY:

YOUR OPENING (describes what / where)
"Artist Jason DeCaires Taylor has made more than 500 statues. But you will not find them in a museum above land. His art is under the ocean!"
REFOCUSED TOPIC SENTENCE (answers the WHY)
Jason placed his sculptures under the sea because he wanted the ocean to slowly grow over them and turn his art into a living reef that becomes a home for sea life.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Your details did not change — the statues, the parks, the ocean growing over them are all still there. The only change is that the first sentence answers the WHY. Graders check first whether you answered the actual question, so that one move is worth several points on Question 11.

Organize Your Paragraph

G3.4 Question 11 — good facts, now put them in order
🔍 Pattern alert: On G3.4 your paragraph (16/20) had good facts, but it jumped from idea to idea and had some very short, choppy sentences. Putting facts in a clear order and linking them is the fix.

The G3.4 prompt asked you to explain how modern sports developed and became popular. You had real facts — rules, records, factories — but here is how a couple of lines came out:

YOU WROTE (G3.4 Q11 — choppy, jumps around)
"Rules were the big change. When rules were made, sports groups began. Sports use records. Records show points. Factories and machines changed work in Britain. Factory work was boring."

Every fact here is good. The trouble is the order jumps around, and short sentences like "Sports use records. Records show points." feel choppy. Two fixes work together: put your facts in a logical order, and link them with transition words like First, Then, and As a result.

TRANSITION WORDS THAT SHOW ORDER
First → your earliest idea · Then → the next step · As a result → what it caused · Finally → the last idea. These words tell the reader how your facts connect.
Which version explains HOW sports grew in a clear order?
A First, rich people had free time to play. Then, when rules were made, sports groups began. As a result, sports spread and became popular.
B Sports use records. Records show points. Factories changed work. Rules were the big change.

Here are your two choppy lines combined into one smooth sentence — same facts, better flow:

YOU WROTE
"Sports use records. Records show points."
COMBINED & SMOOTH
Sports began to use records that show how many points each team scored.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
You already have the facts a top paragraph needs. Putting them in order and linking them with First / Then / As a result turns a list into an explanation — which is exactly what a "how did this develop" prompt is asking for.

Two Quick Sentence Fixes

G3.4 — stronger verbs and complete sentences

Clark, your short answers are strong — you were nearly perfect on G3.4 and fully perfect on G3.5. Just two G3.4 answers came up short, and both are quick fixes.

✎ Fix 1 — When asked for a stronger verb, change the VERB. On G3.4 Q1 you were asked to make the action stronger. You added a describing word instead of swapping the weak verb.
YOU WROTE (G3.4 Q1 — asked to strengthen the verb)
"Layla looked at the beautiful flower."
STRONGER VERB
"Layla admired the flower."  or  "Layla studied the flower."

Adding "beautiful" describes the flower, but the action word is still the weak "looked." The task is to change the verb — swap "looked" for a stronger action like admired, studied, or gazed at.

✎ Fix 2 — Every answer is a complete sentence. On G3.4 Q8 your answer started with "When..." but had no main part, so it was a fragment, not a full sentence.
YOU WROTE (G3.4 Q8 — a fragment)
"When Layla shows the geode to the guide because that was the right thing to do."
COMPLETE SENTENCE
"The most important moment was when Layla showed the geode to the guide, because that was the right thing to do."
Which answer is a complete sentence?
A When Layla shows the geode to the guide because that was the right thing to do.
B The most important moment was when Layla showed the geode to the guide, because that was the right thing to do.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
These are the last few points hiding in your short answers — and they are easy ones. When a task says "make it stronger," change the verb. And check that every answer is a complete sentence with a subject and a main part. Do that and your short answers stay perfect, like G3.5.
