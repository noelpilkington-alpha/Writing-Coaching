Do the Exact Edit the Question Asks

Question 1 wanted a stronger verb — let's nail it
🎯 Pattern alert: On Question 1 the task was to make the verb stronger. You added a colorful adjective but kept the weak verb "looked." When a question names a specific change, that exact change is what earns the points.

Grace, here is what you wrote on Question 1. The question asked you to make the verb stronger:

YOU WROTE (G3.4, Question 1)
"Layla looked at the bright yellow flower."
You added a great detail (“bright yellow”), but the verb is still “looked” — the exact thing the question asked you to change.
The question says: "Make the verb stronger." Which rewrite does what it asks?
A Layla looked at the bright yellow flower.
B Layla gazed at the yellow flower.
THE RULE: CHANGE THE THING THE QUESTION NAMES
When a question says "make the verb stronger," swap the action word for a more vivid one:

looked → gazed at, studied, admired, examined
walked → marched, wandered, strolled
said → whispered, shouted, explained

Adding an adjective is a nice bonus, but only the stronger verb earns the point on a "stronger verb" question.

SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Before you submit any edit question, read it one more time and ask: "Did I change the exact thing it named?" A verb question needs a new verb. A question question needs a question mark. Matching the exact ask is free points you already know how to earn.

YOUR CLOSING (G3.4, Question 11)

A closing should make a strong statement, not ask the reader a question. Also: “dont” → “don't,” “alot” → “a lot.”
