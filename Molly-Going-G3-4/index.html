FROM G3.4 Q7

FROM G3.4 Q10
"One rule is if you find nature on the ground leave it there, this is important because nature is living to."
"to" should be too (meaning "also"). The "to / too / two" homophone is one of the most common spots to lose a point.
THE TO / TOO / TWO RULE
Use this quick check:
• to = direction or part of a verb ("go to school," "to run")
• too = also or very ("me too," "too loud")
• two = the number 2
Swap test: if you can say "also" instead, it's too. "Nature is living also" works — so it's too.
From G3.4 Q10 — which version earns full credit?
A If you find nature on the ground, leave it there, because nature is living to.
B If you find nature on the ground, leave it there, because nature is living too.
YOUR REVISE-AND-EDIT PASS (STEP 4)
Pass 1 — Hunt single-letter slips. Look at every word. Does anything look off by one letter? "doind" vs "doing." Your fingers moved one key over.

Pass 2 — Hunt homophones. Stop at every to, there, your, its. Do a swap test: "too" = also, "their" = belongs to them, "you're" = you are.

Pass 3 — Read the paragraph out loud in your head. If you have to pause to figure out what a word says, it is probably misspelled.

Q2: Use the Exact Word the Prompt Asks For

Half a point turns on one word

PROMPT — G3.4 Q2
Combine the following sentences using the subordinating conjunction since:
1. Layla saw a butterfly.
2. She took out her camera.
YOUR ANSWER (0.5 / 1)
"Layla saw a butterfly, so she took out her camera."
Your combining was perfect. But the prompt asked for "since" — when the prompt names a specific word, you have to use that exact word.
FULL-CREDIT VERSION (1 / 1)
"Since Layla saw a butterfly, she took out her camera."
Same two sentences combined, but using "since" like the prompt asked. "Since" is a subordinating conjunction, so it usually starts the sentence.
🔍
Key pattern: when a writing prompt gives you a specific word in italics or in bold — like "since," "because," "but," or "so" — you must use that exact word. Your answer can be clever or creative, but the required word has to appear. Read the prompt twice (Step 1) and lock the required word in your head before you draft.
SUBORDINATING CONJUNCTIONS 101
A subordinating conjunction connects two ideas where one depends on the other. Common ones:
• since = because / from the time that
• because = gives a reason
• when / while / after / before = shows time
• if / unless = shows a condition
These often start the first half of a sentence, then a comma, then the second half: Since it rained, we stayed inside.
The prompt says: "Combine using since." Which answer earns full credit?
A Layla saw a butterfly, so she took out her camera.
B Since Layla saw a butterfly, she took out her camera.
