The 4 Sentence Types

Question 4 — match the sentence to its type
🔍 Pattern alert: On Q4 you picked the wrong sentence type — it earned 0 of 2 points. The good news: there are only four types, and once you know the end mark for each, this becomes an easy point.

Brett, here is the question you missed. The sentence was: "Workers spent over 30 minutes clearing the ice." You called it imperative, but that is not right. Let's look at the four types:

Declarative .
Tells you something. A statement that ends with a period.
Interrogative ?
Asks something. A question that ends with a question mark.
Exclamatory !
Shows strong feeling. Ends with an exclamation point.
Imperative .
Gives a command or order. "Clear the ice." Tells someone to do it.
YOU WROTE (G3.2 Q4)
"The anser that I chose imperative."
Two things to fix: the type is wrong (it is declarative, not imperative), and this is not a complete sentence. "anser" should be "answer."
Which sentence is declarative (a statement that ends with a period)?
A Clear the ice right now.
B Workers spent over 30 minutes clearing the ice.
HOW TO TELL DECLARATIVE FROM IMPERATIVE
Both can end with a period, so look at who is doing what:

Declarative tells you a fact about someone: "Workers spent over 30 minutes clearing the ice." (It names who did it.)

Imperative tells you to do something: "Clear the ice." (No name — it is a command to the reader.)

The Q4 sentence names the workers and tells what they did, so it is declarative.
A COMPLETE ANSWER FOR Q4
"The sentence is declarative because it is a statement that tells what the workers did."
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Q4 is worth 2 points, and you got 0. Knowing the four types and their end marks turns this into an easy 2 points every test. Match the type, then write your answer as one complete sentence.

Which answer would earn all 3 points?

THE ANSWER + REASON FORMULA
For every Question 6–10, write one sentence in two parts:

Part 1 — your answer: "I would change the name to Teddy Bear Dream"
Part 2 — the reason, joined with "because": "...because it sounds gentle and fun."

Put them together into one sentence and you have all 3 points. Always ask yourself: did I say why?
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Each of Questions 6–10 is worth 3 points. A bare answer with no reason caps you at 2 of 3. Adding one "because" reason to each is worth up to 5 extra points across the test — and it is the same habit that makes your paragraph stronger.
