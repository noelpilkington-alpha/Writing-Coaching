Subject-Verb Agreement

Q1 — 1 out of 2

You lost 1 point on Q1 because of a subject-verb agreement error. This is a small fix, but it matters on every test.

YOU WROTE
"A study in Seattle showed that music can help people feels less stressed and more relaxed."

The problem: The subject is "people" (plural). After "help people," the verb must be in its base form: "feel" not "feels."

"people feels" → "people feel"
THE RULE: PLURAL SUBJECT = BASE VERB
When the subject is plural (people, dogs, students, they), the verb does not get an "s" ending.

Singular: "The dog feels happy." (one dog = verb gets -s)
Plural: "The dogs feel happy." (many dogs = base verb)

Also, after "help [someone]" the next verb is always in base form:
"Help people feel..." / "Help him feel..." / "Help her understand..."
Quick check to catch this
Find the subject of each verb. Ask: is it one thing or more than one? If it is more than one (people, students, animals), drop the -s from the verb. Read the sentence aloud — "people feels" sounds wrong, and trusting your ear helps catch these.

Detail + Explanation

Q11 — 15 out of 20

Your paragraph scored 15/20. The main issue is development depth. You state a point but do not develop it with enough detail from the passage or explain why it matters.

YOU WROTE
"One lesson the reader can learn about this story is that anyone can do something great if they work hard and don't give..."

This is a good start to a topic sentence, but it needs to be followed by specific details from the passage and explanation sentences that connect those details back to your main point.

What is Detail + Explanation?

Every time you give a detail from the passage, follow it with a sentence that explains why that detail supports your point.

Tap to see a developed example
Think: what detail from "A Legacy of Stone" could you use?
DEVELOPED VERSION
"One lesson the reader can learn from this story is that anyone can do something great if they work hard and do not give up. For example, [character] spent years working on [specific detail from passage] even when others doubted him. This shows that hard work and determination can lead to lasting achievements because he did not quit when things were difficult."

Notice: a specific detail from the passage followed by an explanation of why it matters. This is how you earn full points.

DETAIL + EXPLANATION PATTERN
After each detail, ask yourself: "Why does this matter to my main point?"
Your answer to that question is your Explanation sentence.

Detail alone (weak): "He worked hard for many years."
Detail + Explanation (strong): "He worked hard for many years, even when people laughed at him. This shows that determination matters more than what others think, because his results proved everyone wrong."

Paragraph Review

Q11 — 15 out of 20 — Building a complete answer

Your paragraph needs more sentences. Right now, you have a topic sentence that trails off. A complete G4 paragraph follows this structure:

G4 Paragraph Plan: TS → D + E → D + E → D + E → CS
TS = Topic Sentence • D = Detail from the passage • E = Explanation (why it matters) • CS = Concluding Sentence
TS One lesson from "A Legacy of Stone" is that anyone can achieve something great through hard work and determination.
D In the story, [character] spent years [specific action from passage] despite facing challenges.
E This shows that persistence is more important than quick success, because the results only came after long effort.
D [Another specific detail from the passage about the character's actions or results.]
E This proves that hard work leads to lasting achievements because [connection to main point].
CS The story teaches that great things come from refusing to give up, no matter how long it takes.
