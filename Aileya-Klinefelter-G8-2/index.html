Remove Meta-Commentary

State your thesis directly instead of announcing it

Aileya, your intro opens with an engaging hook and previews three topics. That is great structure. But one habit costs you points:

FROM YOUR G8.2 ESSAY
"Have you wondered how a simple bridge helped change the life in the city? Well, I will be explaining why they change the bridge, how it has affected others, and how the buisness is going."

The phrase "I will be explaining" is called meta-commentary — it tells the reader what you plan to do instead of actually doing it. The grader sees this as filler that weakens your thesis. A strong thesis makes the argument directly.

DIRECT THESIS VERSION
"Have you wondered how a simple bridge helped change the life in the city? The bridge renovation revitalized the neighborhood by attracting new visitors, boosting local businesses, and creating a safer route for residents."

Notice: the direct version still previews three topics, but it makes the argument rather than announcing it. Same information, stronger delivery.

Which thesis is stronger?
A In this essay, I will be discussing why renewable energy is important and how it helps the environment.
B Renewable energy reduces pollution, lowers electricity costs, and creates thousands of new jobs across the country.
THE META-COMMENTARY CHECK
Before you submit, reread your introduction. If you see any of these phrases, delete them and state the argument directly:

• "I will be explaining..."
• "In this essay I will discuss..."
• "I am going to tell you about..."
• "This essay is about..."

Replace with: A sentence that names your argument + previews your body paragraph topics. No announcement needed.

Detail + Explanation

Develop body paragraphs with passage evidence and analysis

Aileya, your essay scored 14/20. A significant chunk of the missing points comes from body paragraphs that name a fact but do not explain what it proves. The grader calls this "evidence depth" — and it is the difference between a 14 and an 18.

Here is the pattern. Right now your body paragraphs look like this:

Your Current Body Paragraph Pattern
TOPIC State the central idea of this paragraph
DETAIL Name a fact from the passage
STOP Move on to next paragraph without explaining

What the grader wants to see:

The Detail + Explanation Pattern (Your Next Level)
TOPIC State the central idea (you already do this)
DETAIL A specific detail from the passage
EXPLAIN "This shows..." or "This matters because..." — what the detail proves
DETAIL A second specific detail from the passage
EXPLAIN "This shows..." — what the second detail proves
CLOSING Wrap back to the paragraph's central idea
The passage says "majority of the city avoided the old bridge, deciding to go on more efficient roads." Which body paragraph sentence earns more essay points?
A Most people in the city avoided the old bridge because there were faster roads they could take instead.
B Most people in the city avoided the old bridge, choosing faster roads instead. This shows that the bridge had completely failed its purpose of connecting the two sides of town, which is exactly why the city decided a renovation was necessary.
THE "SO WHAT?" TEST
After every detail from the passage, imagine the grader asking: "So what? Why does this matter for your thesis?"

Your next sentence answers that question. Detail alone = partial credit. Detail + analysis of that detail = full credit. Skip the analysis and you leave half the evidence points on the table.

Sentence starters that work: "This shows that..." / "This matters because..." / "This proves that..." / "In other words..."

POLISHED VERSION OF YOUR OPENING
"Have you wondered how a simple bridge helped change life in the city? The bridge renovation attracted new visitors, revived struggling businesses, and reconnected a divided neighborhood.
