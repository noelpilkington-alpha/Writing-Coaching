Your #1 MC Pattern: "Which sentence is better?"

Q8 and Q9 were the same kind of question

Jaxon, both of your MC misses on G6.4 (Q8 and Q9) were the same type of question: "Which sentence makes the paragraph better?" These are the questions that ask you to pick the sentence with more specific information, or the transition that shows the right kind of relationship between two ideas.

You got every pure grammar question right. The ones that tripped you up asked you to think about what the sentence does for the paragraph, not just whether it is grammatical.

Q8 — Best Elaboration for an Immersion Paragraph
YOU "Immersion learning is a method that helps people learn language in classrooms." — a definition, not elaboration.
ANSWER "In immersion classes, students must use the new language all day, which helps them learn vocabulary faster." — adds a specific effect.
Q9 — Which transition best connects: "Some experts think older students learn better in structured lessons." / "Others argue that younger children pick up new sounds more easily."
A Additionally, others argue that younger children pick up new sounds more easily.
B While others argue that younger children pick up new sounds more easily.
THE TRANSITION DECISION TREE
Before you pick a transition, ask: do the two sentences agree, disagree, or cause each other?

• Agree / pile on? → Also, Additionally, Furthermore, Moreover
• Disagree / contrast? → However, While, Although, On the other hand
• Cause and effect? → Therefore, As a result, Because, So
• Sequence? → First, Next, Then, Finally

If you pick from the wrong group, the sentence is grammatical but wrong. Always name the relationship first, then pick the transition.
THE ELABORATION TEST (FOR Q8-STYLE QUESTIONS)
When you are asked "which sentence best elaborates on a paragraph about X," ask: does this sentence add a new detail, cause, or effect — or does it just define the topic again?

✗ Define: "Immersion learning is a method that helps people learn language." (you already said that)
✓ Elaborate: "Students must use the new language all day, which helps them learn faster." (adds what happens and why it matters)

Elaboration always pushes the idea forward, not sideways.
