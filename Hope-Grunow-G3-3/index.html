Your Test-Taking Process

6 steps for every question

Before you write anything, follow these steps. They help your brain get organized.

6 Steps for Every Answer
1 Read the question TWICE. The second time, underline the key words.
2 Find the action word. What is it asking you to DO? (explain, change, write, add)
3 Plan: Echo the question. Turn the question into the start of your answer in your head.
4 Write your answer. Start with the echo, then add "because" and your reason.
5 Check: Did I answer the "why" part? If it says "explain," make sure you gave a reason.
6 Re-read your sentence. Does it make sense on its own? Is it a full sentence?
MOST IMPORTANT STEPS FOR YOU
Steps 1 and 3 matter most right now. Reading twice helps you catch what the question really wants. Echoing the question gives you a sentence starter so you never get stuck staring at a blank line.

The Echo Trick (Your Superpower)

You already know this — now make it automatic

Here is the secret: Start every answer by turning the question into the beginning of your sentence. You already do this on your best answers. Now we make it a habit.

THE ECHO TRICK TEMPLATE
[Echo the question] because [your reason].

How it works for Q7 and Q8:
• "The most meaningful moment was when the seal found its family because it shows that animals need their families just like people do."
• "It was not a good idea to name the seal Sally because it might make you too attached and not want to let her go."

Finish the Thought

Q8 — 2 out of 3 — the "because" needs a REAL reason

On Q8, you DID use "because" (great job!). But the reason went in a circle:

YOUR ANSWER (Q8)
"No because I feel like that is not a good name for seals."

This says "it is not a good name" as the reason for "it is not a good name." That is like saying "I do not like it because I do not like it." The reason just repeats the answer.

After "because," ask yourself: WHY specifically? Give a real reason from the passage or from your own thinking.

Tap to see a stronger version
Think: what is a SPECIFIC reason first?
STRONGER VERSION
"No because if you give a wild animal a human name, you might get too attached and not want to set it free when it needs to go back to the ocean."

The reason is specific: naming causes attachment, attachment makes releasing harder. That is a REAL reason, not a circle.

THE "WHY SPECIFICALLY?" CHECK
After you write "because," read your reason out loud. Then ask: "Could someone ask 'why?' again?" If yes, your reason is too vague. Keep going until the reason is specific.

What IS an Appositive?

Q3 — 1 out of 2 — a new word to learn

The question asked you to add an appositive to: "Sally swam to the other seals." You wrote "Sally swam to the other seals kindly." That added a word about HOW she swam, not an appositive.

That is totally okay. This is a new word. Here is what it means:

AN APPOSITIVE IS A NAMING PHRASE BETWEEN COMMAS
It renames the noun right before it. Think of it as a little name tag tucked into the sentence.

Example:

WITH AN APPOSITIVE
"Sally, a baby seal, swam to the other seals."

The phrase "a baby seal" renames Sally. It tells us WHO Sally is.

Quick test: Can you remove it and the sentence still makes sense?

YES "Sally swam to the other seals." ← Still works without it!

More examples:

  • "Jim, Anna's dad, freed the pup."
  • "The beach, a quiet stretch of sand, was empty."
  • "Satoshi Tajiri, the creator of the game, loved bugs as a kid."

Pattern: Noun, [who or what the noun is], rest of sentence.

Your Paragraph — Three Sentences

Q11 — 11 out of 20 — your one sentence is GOOD, it just needs company

Here is what you wrote for Q11:

YOUR ANSWER (Q11)
"It influenced Tajiri because he loved catching bugs when he was little and then when he was older he loved video games."

This is actually a good sentence. It uses echo + because + real detail. The problem? It is only ONE sentence. To earn more points on the paragraph, you need at least three.

Here is your simple plan: write THREE sentences.

1
Echo the prompt.
"Satoshi Tajiri's childhood influenced him to create the game."
2
One fact from the passage.
"When he was little, he loved catching bugs outside."
3
Why it matters.
"This shows that his hobby gave him the idea for a game where you collect creatures."

That is it. Three sentences. Your existing sentence already covers boxes 1 and 2. You just need to add a "why it matters" sentence at the end.

YOUR PARAGRAPH FORMULA
Sentence 1: Echo the prompt (what it is about).
Sentence 2: One fact from the passage.
Sentence 3: Why that fact matters or what it shows.

Three sentences can earn 14–16 out of 20. You are already at 11 with just one sentence. Adding two more is all it takes.
