Fix 2: A Thesis That Names the Prompt’s Parts

And topic sentences where the headings were
THE RULE: THE THESIS NAMES THE PARTS THE PROMPT ASKED FOR, AND EACH BODY PARAGRAPH OPENS WITH ONE OF THEM
The introduction runs general statement, specific statement, thesis, and the thesis names the parts the prompt asked for, in order. The body paragraphs take those parts one each, and each opens with a topic sentence that states the part, not a heading that labels a time. The check: read the prompt, then your thesis. Could a reader list the prompt’s parts from your thesis alone? Does each paragraph start with a sentence, not a label?

Fix 3: After Each Fact, This Shows

The grader’s note, and the five evidence points
THE RULE: AFTER EVERY FACT, A SENTENCE THAT SAYS WHAT IT SHOWS ABOUT THE THESIS
The rubric line: support each point with facts or examples from the passage and explain what they show. Five points. After each date, distance or event comes a sentence that starts This shows and says what the fact proves about the connection the prompt asked for. The check: after each fact, is the next sentence about what it shows, or is it the next thing that happened? A body paragraph that is only next things is a history, not an explanation.
YOURS, G7.3 “ENDANGERED LANGUAGE”
“In the late 1900s, many inhabitants, alarmingly, didn’t know how to whistle. The islands’ government reconginized this, and decided to make the language mandaotory to learn. The outcome was still unclear, but it changed for the better.” TWO FACTS, THEN THE NEXT THING; NO THIS SHOWS
The grader wrote: show more clearly how each fact connects people both to one another and to their past, rather than just stating the fact. The classes are the best fact in the passage for that, and the body paragraph moves on without saying so.
