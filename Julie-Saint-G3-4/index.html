Give Each Idea a Clean Boundary

The extra sentences created comma splices
🔍
What happened: When you tacked an extra sentence onto your answer, you joined two complete ideas with only a comma. That is a comma splice. It happened on Q1 (...glanced at the flower, I support...) and Q9 (...taking it to the guide, I support...). You also have one inside a real answer on Q4 (Layla wanted to keep the geode but, she remeberd...).

Each complete idea needs a clean boundary. You have two good ways to do that:

  • End the sentence with a period and start a new one.
  • Join with a connecting word — because, so, but, and, since.
THE FIX (Q4)
Your answer: "Layla wanted to keep the geode but, she remeberd the guide's rule." Move the comma and put it before the connecting word: "Layla wanted to keep the geode, but she remembered the guide's rule." One clean idea, one clean boundary.

QUICK CHECK
Before a comma, ask: "Is the next part a whole new sentence on its own?" If yes, either use a period and start fresh, or add a connecting word (because / so / but / and) right after the comma.

Do you want to know how modern sports started? Well it started in Britain in the 1700s. Games like golf and cricket were first played by rich people because they had enough free time to play sports. After the rules were set, sports groups began. A league is a group of teams that play each other. Leagues and rules got us closer to modern sports. Modern sports also use scores and records.
