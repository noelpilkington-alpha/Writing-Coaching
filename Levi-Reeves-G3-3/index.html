Step 2: What Is an Appositive?

Q3 (1/2) — you rewrote instead of adding an appositive

Q3 (1/2): The question asked you to add an appositive. You wrote "Sally went and swam," which is just rewriting the sentence. An appositive is a different thing.

WHAT IS AN APPOSITIVE?
An appositive is a phrase that renames or describes the noun next to it. It is set off by commas.

Example: "Sally, a rescued seal, swam back to the ocean."

The bold phrase renames Sally. It tells us WHO Sally is.
Which sentence has an appositive?
A"Sally went and swam in the ocean after she was freed."
B"Sally, a young seal rescued from a fishnet, swam happily back to the ocean."
APPOSITIVE CHECK
After writing your answer, ask: "Did I add a phrase between commas that RENAMES the noun?" If yes, you have an appositive. If you just rewrote the whole sentence, that is not an appositive.

Step 2: Connector Words

Q5 (0/2) — "And" does not show WHY

Q5 (0/2): You chose "And" as the connector, but the sentence needed "Since" because it shows a reason. "And" just adds two things together. "Since" explains WHY.

CONNECTOR WORDS: WHAT THEY DO
• "And" = adds two things (no reason given)
• "Since" / "Because" = explains WHY something happened
• "But" = shows a contrast (opposite ideas)
• "So" = shows a result

When the sentence shows that one thing caused another, use "Since" or "Because" — not "And."

"___ it was raining, we stayed inside." Which connector fits?
A"And it was raining, we stayed inside."
B"Since it was raining, we stayed inside."
YOUR CHECK
Before picking a connector, ask: "Is there a REASON here?" If yes, use "Since" or "Because." If you are just joining two equal ideas, use "And."
