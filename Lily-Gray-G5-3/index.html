Step 2: Appositives and Comma Placement

Q1 — YOUR ANSWER (0 out of 2)

HOW APPOSITIVES WORK
An appositive is extra information about a noun, set off by two commas (one before, one after). It can be removed and the sentence still makes sense.

Example: "The Thompsons, a family of five, went camping near the lake."

Remove the appositive: "The Thompsons went camping near the lake." Still a complete sentence. That is how you know the commas are correct.
Which sentence uses an appositive correctly?
A Claudette, a fifteen-year-old student, refused to give up her seat on the bus.
B Claudette a fifteen-year-old, student refused, to give up her seat on the bus.
THE TEST: CAN YOU REMOVE IT?
After you write an appositive, try removing the words between the two commas. Does the sentence still make sense? If yes, your commas are correct. If no, something is in the wrong place.
