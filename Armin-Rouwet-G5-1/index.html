THE TWO-CHECK READ-BACK
Before you submit any answer, do two things:

1. Run-on check: Read the sentence aloud in your head. If you run out of breath or hear two separate ideas without a period or "and/but/so/because" between them, split it.
2. Spelling scan: Look at any word that felt fast when you typed it. Does it look right? "Changd" and "ows" are both one-letter drops that a quick glance catches.

On G5.1 this habit would have won back roughly 3 points across Q6, Q8, and Q9.
FIXING A RUN-ON: YOUR OPTIONS
When you find two complete ideas crashing into each other, pick one:

Option 1 — Period: End the first idea. Start a new sentence.
Option 2 — Comma + conjunction: Add ", and" / ", but" / ", so" / ", because" between the two ideas.

Run-on: "She cleaned the mess she felt less guilty."
Fixed (period): "She cleaned the mess. She felt less guilty."
Fixed (conjunction): "She cleaned the mess, so she felt less guilty."
WHY THIS MATTERS
Three partial-credit questions, each worth 3 points, each scored 2 out of 3. That is 3 points on the table from one repeated habit. Fix the run-ons and spelling and the 87% moves toward 90%.

Detail + Explanation: Go Deeper in Your Paragraph

Q11 — 17 out of 20 · Passage: "Say What, Horse?"
🔍
What "detail + explanation" means: After you give a piece of evidence from the passage, add a sentence that explains why it matters or what it shows. Evidence alone tells the reader what. Explanation tells them so what.

Armin, your Q11 hook is genuinely strong: "Did you know that horses are trying to communicate with us and we have just found out..." That is an engaging opening that pulls the reader in. Where the paragraph needs more is in the middle — after you give a detail from the passage, you need a "this shows..." or "this means..." sentence that explains why the detail matters.

Your opening (strong): "Did you know that horses are trying to communicate with us and we have just found out..."

The pattern for full-credit paragraphs at G5 is:

Detail + Explanation Pattern
DETAIL A specific fact or quote from the passage
EXPLAIN "This shows..." / "This means..." / "This matters because..."

For example, if you wrote a detail about horses having 17 facial expressions, your explanation sentence might be: "This shows that horses have a much more complex way of communicating than most people realize." That second sentence is what pushes you from 17 toward 19 or 20.

Which pair uses the detail + explanation pattern correctly?
A Horses have 17 facial expressions. They also move their ears when they feel emotions.
B Horses have 17 facial expressions. This shows that horses communicate in more complex ways than most people think.
HOW TO ADD EXPLANATION SENTENCES
After every piece of evidence, ask yourself: "So what? Why does this detail matter?"

Then write a sentence that starts with one of these:
• "This shows that..."
• "This means..."
• "This is important because..."
• "In other words..."

Detail: "According to the article, horses raise the skin above their eyes when they feel negative emotions."
Explanation: "This shows that horses use their faces to express feelings, just like humans do."
YOUR PARAGRAPH NEEDS TWO LAYERS
Your hook is already strong. To push from 17 to 19+, add:

1. A specific detail from the passage (with the article title or a fact)
2. An explanation sentence right after it
3. A second detail + explanation pair (for depth)
4. A closing sentence that wraps up the main idea

That gives you 6-7 sentences total and shows the grader you understand the passage deeply.
WHY THIS MATTERS
Q11 is worth 20 points. You scored 17. The gap is not in your opening or your evidence — it is in the explanation layer. Adding "this shows..." sentences after your details is the single move that closes that 3-point gap.
