Q7: The Comma Trap

Skill: Write one sentence about a change in nature
YOU WROTE
A change I see in nature in springtime, is the arrival of many bluebonnets.

Your answer and your idea were great! The only problem is a sneaky comma that doesn't belong.

Tap to see the improved version
Read the explanation above first
FIXED VERSION
A change I see in nature in springtime is the arrival of many bluebonnets.
THE RULE
Never put a comma between the subject and the verb.

The subject is "A change I see in nature in springtime" and the verb is "is." They belong together — no comma in between!

Quick check: Before you finish a sentence, read it out loud. If you pause where the comma is, ask yourself: "Is this comma separating the who/what from the does/is?" If yes, take it out.

Q11: Use Details from the Article

The biggest way to earn more points

Your paragraph talked about robots in general terms. But the question said: "use evidence from the selection." That means you need to use specific facts from the article.

What you wrote vs. what the article says:

You wrote (general) The article says (specific)
"help put things together like machines" "workers use robot arms to put together machine parts" and make more each hour
"help take deliveries" "robots named Cleo and Leo bring items to guests" at Hotel EMC2
"help you complete chores or do laundry" "small robots clean floors" and "do simple chores"
TRY THIS PATTERN
According to the article, [specific detail]. This shows that [why it matters].

Example: "According to the article, workers in a factory use robot arms to put together machine parts, which helps them make more parts each hour. This shows that robots save people time at work."

Q11: End with a Closing Sentence

Wrap up your paragraph so it feels complete

Your paragraph ends with:

YOUR LAST SENTENCE

This is a supporting detail, not a closing sentence. A closing sentence wraps up your main idea without adding a new fact. It tells the reader: "I'm done, and here's the big takeaway."

EXAMPLE CLOSING SENTENCE

PARAGRAPH PLAN FOR NEXT TIME
Topic Sentence: Answer the question
Detail 1: One example from the article + why it matters

This is called the SPO (Single Paragraph Outline). Use it every time you write a paragraph on the test!
