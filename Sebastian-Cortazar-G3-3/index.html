#2: "A" vs "An" & Capitalize Names

Two tiny grammar fixes that protect easy points
🔍 Pattern alert: Two small grammar slips show up in your answers: writing a before a vowel sound (it should be an), and not capitalizing a person's name. Both are quick to fix once you know the rule.

Sebastian, here are your real slips. On Q7 and Q10 you wrote "a animal" / "and animal" — the word animal starts with a vowel sound, so it needs an. And on Q4 you wrote a great combined sentence, but the name jim needs a capital J.

YOU WROTE (Q7)
"...the family was kind and helped a animal that was in harm..."
CORRECTED
"...the family was kind and helped an animal that was in harm..."
YOU WROTE (Q4)
"Anna and jim ran to the beach, and there they found a baby seal in a fishnet."
CORRECTED
"Anna and Jim ran to the beach, and there they found a baby seal in a fishnet."
THE TWO RULES
1. "An" before a vowel sound. Use an before a, e, i, o, u sounds: an animal, an apple, an egg. Use a before other sounds: a seal, a book.

2. Capitalize people's names. Anna, Jim, Sally — a person's name always starts with a capital letter.
Which sentence is correct?
A The story teaches you to always help a animal in need.
B The story teaches you to always help an animal in need.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
These are tiny fixes, but they are objective — a grader will always count "a animal" or a lowercase name as an error. Catching them on your read-back keeps points that are otherwise easy to lose. Watch for an before a vowel sound, and make sure every name has a capital letter.
