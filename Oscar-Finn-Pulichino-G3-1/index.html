#1 Fix: The Comma Goes Before “But”

Q4 and Q5 — the same slip, twice

Oscar, you did this twice on the same test, so it is worth two minutes. When you join two ideas with but, the comma goes in front of the word but — never after it.

QUESTION 4
“Secondly, dogs might growl or bite but, the reason might be that they are nervous or tired.”
QUESTION 5 — 1 OUT OF 2
“Restaurants are for people but, not for dogs”

In both cases the comma jumped to the wrong side of but. Question 5 also needed a period at the end — the sentence just stops.

Which version puts the comma in the right place?
ADogs might growl or bite but, the reason might be that they are nervous or tired.
BDogs might growl or bite, but the reason might be that they are nervous or tired.
THE RULE
The joining words are and, but, so, because, or. When one of them links two complete ideas, the comma goes right before it:
  • … growl or bite, but the reason…
  • … for people, but not for dogs.
Never but, — that comma is always on the wrong side.

#2 Fix: What an Appositive Is

Q1 — 1 out of 2

Question 1 asked you to combine two sentences using an appositive. An appositive is a short naming phrase that sits right next to a noun and renames it. Here is what you wrote:

QUESTION 1 — 1 OUT OF 2
“My family has two dogs, their names are Lucy and Rocket.”

The information is all correct — but this is two complete sentences joined by a comma, not an appositive. “Their names are Lucy and Rocket” can stand on its own as a sentence, and that is the giveaway.

Which sentence actually uses an appositive?
AMy family has two dogs, their names are Lucy and Rocket.
BMy family has two dogs, Lucy and Rocket.
WHAT QUESTION 1 WAS LOOKING FOR
“My family has two dogs, Lucy and Rocket.”
HOW TO TELL
After you write the part that follows the comma, ask: could this stand alone as its own sentence?
  • “Their names are Lucy and Rocket.” → Yes, it could. So it is not an appositive.
  • “Lucy and Rocket.” → No, it could not. So it is an appositive.
An appositive is just a name or description, never a full sentence. It renames the noun sitting right before it.

YOUR PARAGRAPH, DEVELOPED (closer to 20/20)

“There are a few ways soil is important to Earth. One way soil is important to Earth is that it helps us grow plants and food. Without healthy soil to hold water and nutrients, crops could not take root, and people would lose the food they depend on. Another way soil is helpful is that it provides food for animals like worms, and that gives the Earth life. Worms break down dead leaves and other scraps in the soil, which puts nutrients back into the ground for the next plants to use. Soil matters because it is the starting point for almost everything that grows.”

Notice that nothing was thrown away. Your main idea, your “One way / Another way” structure, your plants-and-food reason, and your worms reason are all still there. What got added is one explaining sentence after each reason, plus a closing line.

SO WHAT?
