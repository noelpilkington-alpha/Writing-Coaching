THE 3-STEP APPOSITIVE PROCESS
An appositive renames a noun. Here is how to build one correctly:

Step 1: Find the noun you want to rename. ("Chimpanzees")
Step 2: Write a short renaming phrase. ("apes that look like people")
Step 3: Set it off with commas right after the noun.

✗ "She went to study wild Chimpanzees in the forest, Chimpanzees are apes, that look like people."
✓ "She went to study wild chimpanzees, apes that look like people, in the forest."
FOR Q4: COMBINING WITH A CONJUNCTION
When two sentences share a subject or topic, connect them with a joining word:

✗ "She would watch birds fly around she also watched insects crawl on the ground."
✓ "She would watch birds fly around, and she also watched insects crawl on the ground."
Which sentence correctly uses an appositive to combine the ideas?
A She went to study wild chimpanzees, apes that look like people, in the forest.
B She went to study wild Chimpanzees in the forest, Chimpanzees are apes that look like people.
THE TEST
After you combine two ideas, read the result out loud. If it sounds like two complete sentences separated by a comma, you have a comma splice. The fix: turn the second part into a short phrase (not a full sentence) and tuck it in with commas.

Q8: Be Specific

You answered, but the passage detail was missing

Kylen, on Q8 you were asked what Jane discovered about chimpanzees. You answered correctly that she learned they are smart — but you did not name the specific detail from the passage that shows how she knows.

YOUR ANSWER (2/3)

3/3 VERSION
"Jane discovered that chimpanzees are very smart animals because she saw them use sticks as tools to get food."

Your answer told the reader what (they are smart) but not how she knew (sticks/tools). The passage said Jane watched chimpanzees use sticks to pull insects out of holes. That specific detail is what earns the third point.

THE "WHAT EXACTLY?" CHECK
After you write your answer to any Q6–Q10 question, ask yourself:

"What EXACTLY from the passage proves this?"

If your answer says something general like "smart animals" or "important discoveries," go back and add the specific detail: what did she see? what tool did they use? what happened?
