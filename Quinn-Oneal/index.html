READ THE QUESTION WORD BY WORD
You have the skill to combine sentences many different ways — that's a strength! But on a test, always use the exact words the question asks for. Before you write, underline the key words in the question: "Use because, but, or so." Then make sure one of those words appears in your answer.

Think about which one fits best:

but = shows a contrast
so = shows the result

Tap to learn about "role model"
This is a common mix-up
ROLE vs. ROLL
role = a part someone plays (actor's role, role model)
roll = to turn over, or a bread roll

A role model is someone who plays the role of an example for others. Think: "role" = the part you play.
PROOFREADING TIP FOR HIGH SCORERS
Since your writing is already very strong, your proofreading focus should be on two things:

1. Names from the passage: Look back at the article to check the spelling of names like "Yacouba." Copy them letter by letter.
2. Homophones: Words that sound the same but are spelled differently (role/roll, would/wood, their/there). Ask yourself: Which meaning do I need?
Common homophones to watch for
role / roll — a part to play vs. to turn over
their / there / they're — belonging to them / a place / they are
would / wood — past of "will" / material from trees
leaf / leaves — one / more than one (irregular plural)

Here's a simple system to avoid this on future tests:
