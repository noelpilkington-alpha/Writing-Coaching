MOVE 1 — THESIS: STATE YOUR CLAIM, NOT THE PROBLEM
A thesis is the one sentence that tells the reader what your essay will prove. Test yourself with this: "What three things will I argue?" If you can't list them, your thesis is describing the problem instead of making a claim.

Template for Q11: "People are helping [topic] in three important ways: [way 1], [way 2], and [way 3]." It works for almost every G6 expository prompt.
MOVE 2 — EVIDENCE + "THIS SHOWS"
You already pull real details from the passage (milkweed, tagging, donations). What is missing is the next sentence — the one that explains why the detail matters.

• Evidence: "Citizen scientists tag butterflies and count caterpillars."
• Analysis: "This shows that ordinary people can help scientists by collecting real data, not just by feeling sad about the problem."

Every piece of evidence needs a "this shows..." or "this proves..." sentence right after it. That is how the evidence row gets to 5/5.
MOVE 3 — CONVENTIONS (PROOFREAD PASS)
You had 6 spelling slips on this essay: clearning (clearing), charge (change), journy (journey), extreamly (extremely, twice), signtings (sightings), scientistis (scientists). None of these are hard words — they are the kind a 30-second reread catches.

Before you submit: read your essay once from top to bottom, out loud to yourself if you can. Your eye will catch what your fingers missed.

The One Multiple Choice You Missed

Q3 — commas with "because"

Ben, 9 out of 10 on the multiple choice is excellent. The one you missed is a small comma rule that is worth locking in, because this exact rule shows up in essays too.

🔍
Pattern alert: The comma-with-"because" rule is also why some of your essay sentences read a little choppy. Fix the rule here and your essay conventions score goes up too.

The question asked which sentence correctly uses because:

YOU CHOSE
"Carter planted milkweed, because it attracts monarch butterflies."
Which sentence uses "because" correctly?
A Carter planted milkweed because it attracts monarch butterflies.
B Carter planted milkweed, because it attracts monarch butterflies.
THE RULE
• No comma when "because" comes in the middle of a sentence: "She was happy because she won."
• Comma when the "because" clause starts the sentence: "Because she won, she was happy."

Same rule applies to "since," "when," "while," and "if." Middle = no comma. Front = comma after the clause.
WHY THIS MATTERS FOR YOUR ESSAY
This same skill — knowing when a comma breaks a sentence — is what the rubric's punctuation category rewards. One multiple choice point here, plus cleaner comma use in Q11, is worth 2+ points total on your next test.

POLISHED 18/20 VERSION (same ideas, the four moves added)
INTRODUCTION — Monarch butterflies migrate every fall from the northern United States all the way to the warm forests of Mexico. In recent years, people clearing land for farms and lawns have reduced the milkweed monarchs need, causing fewer butterflies to complete the trip. People are now helping monarchs survive in three important ways: building way stations, becoming citizen scientists, and gathering donations.

BODY 1 — First, many people are building way stations. A way station is a small garden where monarchs can rest and lay eggs on milkweed plants. This shows that anyone with a backyard can give butterflies a safer place to stop during the long journey south.

BODY 2 — Another way people help is by becoming citizen scientists. The passage explains that they tag butterflies, count caterpillars, and record sightings online. This proves that ordinary people — not only experts — can collect real data that helps scientists track where monarch populations are shrinking.

BODY 3 — Finally, gathering donations is an important way to help. Even small amounts add up, and donations pay for the gardens and tagging kits that the other two efforts need to keep running. This matters because way stations and citizen scientist programs cannot continue without money.

CONCLUSION — In conclusion, people are saving monarchs through backyard way stations, citizen science, and donations. No single action is enough on its own, but together these three efforts give monarchs a real chance to recover.
