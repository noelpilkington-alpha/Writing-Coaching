MODEL PARAGRAPH (full-paragraph level)

Satoshi Tajiri's childhood influenced the creation of Pokémon because of what he loved to do when he was young. As a kid, Satoshi loved collecting bugs and other small creatures near his home. That is why players in Pokémon travel around catching and collecting creatures, just like he once collected bugs. He wanted to make a game that gave kids the same fun he had exploring and finding new things. In the end, the hobby Satoshi loved as a child became the heart of one of the most popular games in the world.

Which sentence actually develops the paragraph with a detail?
A Satoshi made Pokémon because he wanted to make a game, and that is why Pokémon was created by him.
B As a kid, Satoshi loved collecting bugs, which is why players in Pokémon travel around catching creatures.
THE RULE
A full paragraph needs a topic sentence, then 2 or 3 specific details from the article, then a closing. A detail adds new information from the passage. If a sentence just says the main idea again in different words, it is not a detail — it is a repeat.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Question 11 is worth 20 of the 45 points on the test. Your one sentence earned 8. Adding two real details from the article plus a closing is worth many points — this is the fastest way to turn your score around.

Match the Question Type

Read what the question asks for — then give exactly that

Thiago, two questions slipped not because your ideas were wrong, but because your answer did not match the type the question asked for. These are quick wins once you spot them.

FROM G3.3 Q1 — "Change this sentence into a question"
"Why dose Anna name the seal Sally?"
It is a question, which is good! But "Why" changes the meaning. A yes/no question from "Anna named the seal Sally" should start with Did: "Did Anna name the seal Sally?"
FROM G3.3 Q5 — "Add a subordinating word to begin the sentence"
"today there was no nsigh of the seals..."
"Today" tells when, but it is not a subordinating word. This question needs a word like When, Since, or After to start the sentence.
THE TWO QUESTION TYPES TO WATCH
Make it a question: turn the sentence into something you could answer yes or no. Start with Did, Does, Is, or Was, and end with a question mark.

Add a subordinating word: begin the sentence with a connecting word like When, Since, After, Because, or Although — not a time word like "today."
Which one correctly turns "Anna named the seal Sally" into a yes/no question?
A Anna named the seal Sally?
B Did Anna name the seal Sally?
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Your ideas on these questions were close — you just need to match the exact type asked for. Reading each question slowly and asking "what kind of answer does this want?" wins these points back with no extra writing skill needed.
