THE RULE — WHEN "BECAUSE" NEEDS A COMMA
Most of the time, "because" does NOT need a comma after it. It just connects the reason to the rest of the sentence.

✗ "I like dogs because, they are fun."
✓ "I like dogs because they are fun."

The only time you might see a comma NEAR "because" is when the "because" part starts the sentence:
✓ "Because they are fun, I like dogs." (comma after the reason, not after "because")
From G3.1 Q10 — which version is correct?
A People should feed pets twice a day because, a lot of dogs die of starvation.
B People should feed pets twice a day because a lot of dogs die of starvation.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
You lost at least 2 points on G3.1 alone from this one habit. The scoring rubric counts comma errors against grammar and conventions. The fix is literally one keystroke: delete the comma. On your proofread pass, search for the word "because" and check every single time.

Your #2 Repeated Error: Apostrophes and Capitals

Small marks in the wrong place keep costing you points

Parker, the second error pattern across your tests is tiny marks in the wrong place. An apostrophe shifted one letter off. A capital letter missing from a name. A capital letter added in the middle of a sentence where it does not belong. Here are three real examples:

FROM G3.1 Q6 (apostrophe in the wrong spot)
"I would feel annoyed because people should'nt bring their dogs to restaurants."
The apostrophe goes where the letter is missing. "Should not" → "shouldn't" (apostrophe before the t, not after the n).
FROM G3.2 Q11 (missing capital on a place name)
"These camels are trusted with these books and they travel across kenya to the town of Garissa..."
"Kenya" is a country — it is a proper noun. Should be Kenya.
FROM G3.3 Q11 (capital in the middle of a sentence, missing apostrophe)
"For example, Catching bugs and finding new ones was like catching Pokemons... Thats how Satoshi's childhood influenced the creation of Pokemon."
"Catching" should be lowercase — it is in the middle of a sentence, not a proper noun. "Thats" is missing an apostrophe — should be That's.
Which version is correct?
A The camels travel across kenya. Thats how they help the kids.
B The camels travel across Kenya. That's how they help the kids.
THE TWO RULES
Apostrophe rule: An apostrophe marks where a letter was dropped.
• should not → shouldn't (apostrophe where the "o" was)
• that is → that's (apostrophe where the "i" was)
• do not → don't (apostrophe where the "o" was)

Capital letter rule: Capitals go on the FIRST letter of:
• A sentence (always)
• Proper nouns (names of people, places, brands): Kenya, Satoshi, Pokemon
• Nothing else in the middle of a sentence
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
These tiny marks cost you points in the grammar and conventions part of every paragraph. Your Q11 paragraph on G3.3 scored 15 out of 20. Fixing just the capital letters and apostrophes is worth 1–2 more points on Q11 alone. Combined with the "because" fix from the last card, that is your path from 87% to 90%+.

MODEL POLISHED PARAGRAPH (19–20/20 level)

Satoshi Tajiri's childhood influenced the creation of Pokemon because he used his memories of catching bugs as the idea for catching Pokemon. For example, catching bugs and finding new ones as a kid was like catching Pokemon and finding new ones in the game. This shows that Satoshi turned his favorite childhood hobby into the heart of his game. Also, he wanted kids who grew up in cities without bugs to feel the same excitement he felt exploring nature. This shows that his own childhood shaped both the gameplay and the feeling he wanted players to have. That is how Satoshi's childhood influenced the creation of Pokemon.

B Topic + example 1 + "this shows..." + example 2 + "this shows..." + closing.
