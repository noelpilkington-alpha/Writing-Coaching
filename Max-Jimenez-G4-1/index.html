Read the Passage Before You Write

Your paragraph missed the setting — here is the fix

Max, this is the biggest pattern on your G4.1 paragraph. The story "Surprise Kick" is about a soccer game. Mud, Coby, and Amanda are on a soccer field. Coby gets surprised when Amanda, the new kid, plays really well. In your paragraph, you wrote about the kids playing a video game called Soccer Slam — that is not what the passage said.

YOU WROTE (Q11 opener)
"Did you know that sometimes video games can embrace skill? One time there were these kids named, Mud and Coby. They played this video game called, Soccer Slam!"

The writing itself is good — you have a hook, you name the characters, you use a strong verb ("embrace"). But the setting is wrong. The passage is about a real soccer game on a field. When the setting is wrong, graders can only give partial credit, even if the sentences are nicely built.

THE 2-MINUTE READING PLAN
Before you start writing Question 11, spend 2 minutes on the passage:

1. Skim once — get the whole story into your head.
2. Find the setting — where is this happening? A soccer field? A classroom? A park?
3. Find the main people — who are they? What do they want?
4. Find the big moment — what changes in the story?

THEN start writing. Two minutes of reading saves you a whole paragraph of fixing later.
Which opening sentence matches the "Surprise Kick" passage?
A Mud and Coby were playing a video game called Soccer Slam on their game system.
B On the soccer field, Mud and Coby were shocked when Amanda, the new kid, kicked the ball past the defenders and scored.
HOW TO SPOT THE SETTING FAST
Look at the first paragraph of the story. The writer almost always tells you where everyone is in the first few sentences. In "Surprise Kick," the writer talks about the field, the ball, and defenders. Those are soccer-field words, not video-game words. Match your writing to those words.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Your sentences on Q11 were actually strong — good hook, good flow, good verbs. A paragraph with your style plus the right setting jumps from 13/20 into the 17–18/20 range. Two minutes of reading is worth 4 or 5 paragraph points.

Proofread Before You Submit

Four spelling slips on your paragraph — all easy to catch

Max, your Q11 paragraph had four spelling slips. Every one is a word you can spell when you slow down. Reading your paragraph out loud once before you submit catches most of these.

FROM G4.1 Q11 — 4 spelling slips
• "soccor" → should be soccer
• "eas" → should be was (this one looks like a quick-typing slip)
• "surranded" → should be surrounded
• "suprise" → should be surprise (missing the second R)

None of these are hard words for you. They are proofreading misses. When the reading and writing are happening fast, small slips sneak in. A one-minute re-read at the end fixes all four.

Which sentence is spelled correctly all the way through?
A The soccor game had a big suprise at the end.
B The soccer game had a big surprise at the end.
YOUR 60-SECOND PROOFREAD ROUTINE
Before you submit, re-read your paragraph one time and ask:

1. Does every word look right, or does one look weird?
2. Did I spell surprise with two Rs? (Sur-PRISE — say it out loud.)
3. Did I spell soccer with two Cs and -ER at the end?
4. Did I accidentally type one letter when I meant a whole word (like "eas" for "was")?
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Spelling is part of your paragraph score. Four clean spellings instead of four slips can nudge your Q11 score up by 1–2 points. Pair that with the setting fix from the last card, and you are looking at a much stronger paragraph.

MODEL POLISHED PARAGRAPH (18–20/20 level)

Did you know that sometimes a soccer game can show who really has skill? One afternoon, two kids named Mud and Coby were on the soccer field with a new kid named Amanda. At first, Coby thought Amanda would not be any good. But then Mud went darting past defenders with the ball, and when Amanda got her chance, she used amazing ball control to kick past everyone and score. Mud was surrounded by players, but Amanda found the open space. Coby was surprised because he had judged Amanda too quickly — she was actually one of the best players on the field. In the end, Coby changed his mind about the new kid because she showed real skill in the game.
