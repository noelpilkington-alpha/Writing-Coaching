Your Fill-in-the-Blank Paragraph

A skeleton you can use on any Q11 prompt

D'sean, here is a template you can use for Question 11 on your next test. You do not have to write a paragraph from scratch — just fill in the blanks using the article and the prompt.

The 4-sentence paragraph skeleton

Sentence 1 (Topic): turn the prompt into a sentence.

Sentence 2 (Detail 1): One thing from the article is that a fact from the story.

Sentence 3 (Detail 2): Another thing is that a second fact from the story.

Sentence 4 (Closing): That is why re-state the topic in your own words.

Let's try it with the G3.4 prompt. The prompt was "Explain how modern sports developed and became popular in society." Here is the same skeleton, filled in:

Filled-in example
TOPIC Modern sports developed and became popular in a few big ways.
DETAIL 1 One thing from the article is that people started writing down the rules so teams could play fairly.
DETAIL 2 Another thing is that big events like the Olympics helped sports get popular.
CLOSING That is why modern sports are a huge part of society today.
THE EASIEST TRICK EVER
To write sentence 1 (the topic sentence), just turn the prompt into a sentence. If the prompt says "Explain how modern sports developed," your topic sentence starts with "Modern sports developed..."

You just copied the prompt and added a few words. That is a valid topic sentence.
WHAT IF YOU ARE STUCK?
Look back at the article. Find 2 sentences that answer the prompt. Put one of them into sentence 2 of the skeleton and one into sentence 3 — in your own words. That is it. That is a paragraph.
