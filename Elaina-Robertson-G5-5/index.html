Error #1: Fix Comma Splices in Combining

Q1 — 1 out of 2 — two sentences joined with just a comma
Q1 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2)
"She wanted to go to the park, it was raining outside."

The problem: You joined two complete sentences with just a comma. That is a comma splice. You need a conjunction after the comma (like "but") or a different connector.

FIXED
"She wanted to go to the park, but it was raining outside."
THE RULE
Two complete sentences cannot be joined with just a comma. You need:

• Comma + conjunction: ", but" ", so" ", and"
• A connecting word: "although," "because," "when"
• A period to make them two separate sentences

Your check: After combining, look at both sides of the comma. If both sides could be their own sentence, you need a conjunction after the comma.
Which combined sentence is correct?
A"The dog was tired, it kept running around the yard."
B"The dog was tired, but it kept running around the yard."
YOUR CHECK
Every time you combine sentences with a comma, ask: "Did I put a conjunction after it?" If not, add one.

Error #2: Use Valid Conjunctions + Add Detail

Q3 ("plus" is not a conjunction) and Q7-Q8 (too brief)
Q3 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2)
"She likes reading, plus, she enjoys writing stories."

The problem: "Plus" is not a valid conjunction for combining sentences in formal writing. Use "and," "but," "so," "because," "although," or "when" instead.

FIXED
"She likes reading, and she also enjoys writing stories."

Q7 and Q8: Too brief

On Q7 and Q8, your answers were correct but too short to earn full points. When a question asks you to explain or describe, add at least one sentence of specific detail after your main idea.

VALID CONJUNCTIONS TO USE
• and (adds information)
• but (shows contrast)
• so (shows result)
• because (shows reason)
• although / even though (shows contrast with concession)
• when / while (shows time)

NOT valid: "plus," "also," "then" (by themselves)
Which sentence uses a valid conjunction?
A"He finished his homework, plus, he cleaned his room."
B"He finished his homework, and he cleaned his room."
YOUR CHECK
After combining, look at the connecting word. Is it on the valid list (and, but, so, because, although, when)? If not, swap it for one that is.
