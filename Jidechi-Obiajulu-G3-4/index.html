Error #1: Keep the Same Meaning

Q3 — 1 out of 2 — changed meaning + wrong article

On Q3, you needed to convert a sentence while keeping the same meaning. You changed what the sentence was about and used the wrong article.

Q3 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2)
"An geode is a type of rock."

Problem 1: "An" is wrong before "geode" because "geode" starts with a consonant sound ("g"). It should be "a geode."

Problem 2: The original sentence was about something specific (like where geodes are found or what they look like inside). Your answer changed the topic to a general definition.

THE RULES
Article rule: Use "a" before consonant sounds (a geode, a book, a happy day). Use "an" before vowel sounds (an apple, an egg, an idea).

Conversion rule: When you convert a sentence, the meaning must stay the same. Only the form changes. If the original talks about WHERE geodes are found, your answer must also talk about where they are found.
The sentence says: "A geode looks plain on the outside." Convert it to a question that keeps the same meaning.
A"What is a geode?"
B"Does a geode look plain on the outside?"
YOUR CHECK
After converting, ask: "Does my answer talk about the SAME THING as the original?" If it talks about something different, rewrite it.

Error #2: Complete Sentences + Specific Detail

Q5 (fragment) and Q9 (echoes question without detail)
Q5 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2) — FRAGMENT
"Because they are interesting rocks."

The problem: This is a fragment. It starts with "Because" but has no main clause. A complete sentence needs a subject and a verb that can stand alone.

FIXED
