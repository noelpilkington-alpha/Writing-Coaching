Fix 1: Echo the Question, then Because

Q6, Q7, Q8. The answer is there. The reason is not
THE RULE: ECHO THE QUESTION, THEN BECAUSE
Turn the question into a full sentence that gives your answer. Then add because and the reason. Two checks: cover the Yes or No at the start. What is left must still be a complete sentence. Then find the because. No because, no reason, and a point is gone.

YOU WROTE (1 OUT OF 3)
NO ECHO“Yes, because of the statement, ‘This fun event sells out every year’.”
Cover Yes: “because of the statement...” is not a sentence. And a why question cannot be answered with yes.

Fix 2: The Comma Test

Q2, Q5, Q7, and your G3.1 review. The same comma, four times
THE RULE: THE COMMA TEST
Before you put a comma in front of and, because, so or to, cover everything before the comma. If the words after it could stand alone as a sentence, keep the comma. If they cannot, delete it.
YOUR Q10 (3 OUT OF 3). THIS COMMA BELONGS
The ice was covered in teddy bears, so the players skated among the bears.
Cover before the comma: “the players skated among the bears” is a sentence on its own. Keep it.
YOUR Q2. THIS ONE DOES NOT
Fans tossed bears onto the ice, because the Bears scored a goal.
After the comma: “because the Bears scored a goal” cannot stand alone. Delete it.
YOUR Q5 AND Q7. SAME COMMA
It shows that they care about their community, and take this tradition very seriously every year.
I would change it from only being once a year, to spread out through their calendar.
“take this tradition very seriously” has no one doing the taking. “to spread out through their calendar” is not a sentence. Both commas go. Your G3.1 review found the same habit: “is to, play catch.”
KEEPFans tossed the bears, and the players skated around them.
KEEPThe Bears scored, so thousands of bears rained down.
KEEPThe workers cleared the ice, but it took more than 30 minutes.
DELETEThe workers cleared the ice, and posed for photos.
“posed for photos” cannot stand alone.
