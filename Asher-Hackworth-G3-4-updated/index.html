"SINCE" = "BECAUSE" — IT INTRODUCES THE REASON
"Since" means "because" — it introduces the reason something happened. Put the reason in the since-part:

"Since [reason], [what happened]."

Can you spot why this is not a complete sentence?
📈 Fragments are one of the biggest point-losers on writing tests. When you start a sentence with "when" or "because," you need to finish the thought with a main part — a complete idea that could stand on its own. This same rule applies inside your paragraphs: every sentence needs to be complete.

Q10 — Your answer was accepted, but it has a common grammar mistake:

YOUR ANSWER (Q10)
"Leave what you find. Because nature might of put it there for a reason..."

"Might of" is not correct. The correct form is "might have." We say "might've" when speaking, which sounds like "might of" — but "of" is never a verb helper.

TWO RULES TO REMEMBER
1. Fragments: If your sentence starts with "when," "because," or "although," make sure there is a main part (a complete thought) attached to it. "When she put the geode back" is not a sentence by itself — it needs a main idea.

2. "Of" is never a verb helper. It is always:
• might have  •  could have  •  would have  •  should have

THE "WHICH?" TEST
After you write your answer, ask yourself: "Which actions? Which moment? Which detail?" If you cannot point to a specific thing from the passage, your answer is too general. Replace vague words like "actions" or "things" with the actual event.

Spelling Spotlight

Three words to practice
📈 Spelling errors cost you small amounts of points that add up. When your spelling is clean, your whole paragraph looks more polished and easier to read.

Can you spot which spelling is correct?

Tap the correctly spelled word:
A basicly
B basically

Here are all the spelling errors from your test:

"brocked" → broken

The past form of "break" is not regular: break → broke → broken. No "ck" sound.

"basicly" → basically

The root word is "basic." Add -ally (not just -ly) to make it describe how: basic + ally = basically.

"highes" → highest

Do not drop the t at the end. High → highest. The "-est" ending always keeps the T.

SPELLING TIP
When you finish writing, read your work slowly and look at each word by itself. For tricky words, try breaking them into parts: basic + ally, high + est, brok + en. If a word looks wrong, it probably is — trust your eyes and sound it out.

There is one grammar error in your paragraph. Can you find it?

Which version fixes the comma splice?
A "Modern sports also have a thing called records, it is basically the highest score for that specific thing."
B "Modern sports also have records. A record is basically the highest score for a specific event."

So what? A comma splice is when two complete thoughts are joined by only a comma. It makes your writing harder to follow. Fix it by using a period or adding a connecting word like "and," "but," or "so."

YOUR PARAGRAPH
Sports, a big part of life now, was not so big back in the 1700s! Back then, they were only played by rich people who had enough time. Then, rules started to change and there became a set of rules for each game. After that, sports groups became a thing called leagues. Modern sports also have a thing called records, it is basicly the highes score for that specific thing. By the late 1800s, other men started to compete in sports because it gave them respect. Now, sports have spread to a lot of countries and is a big community.

FIXED
"Modern sports also have records. A record is basically the highest score for a specific event."
