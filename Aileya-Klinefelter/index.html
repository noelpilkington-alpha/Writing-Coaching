The Question You Missed: Transitions

Q9 — "Although" vs. "While"

Q9 — Which transition best connects: "Some experts think older students learn better in structured lessons. _____ others argue that younger children pick up new sounds more easily."

YOU CHOSE
"Although" — This suggests one idea is being contradicted or dismissed.

"Although older students may learn better..." = suggests older students don't actually learn better. It challenges the first idea.

CORRECT ANSWER
"While" — This shows two views being compared side by side, without saying one is wrong.

"While some experts think older students learn better, others argue..." = both views are presented equally. Neither is dismissed.

THE RULE
Use "Although" when one idea is being challenged. Use "While" when two ideas are being compared fairly.

In your essay P4, you actually handled this well! "Though it may be true... it is better for them to learn while they are at a young age." There, you were challenging one side — so "Though" was the right choice.
WHY THIS MATTERS
This is the same skill you used in P4 of your essay — comparing two viewpoints. Getting this distinction right will help both your MCQ and your essay writing.

DEVELOPED VERSION (6 sentences)

TS: First, learning a second language offers important benefits for the brain.

E1: According to the passage, various studies show that learning a new language can strengthen memory and improve problem-solving skills.

X1: This means that language learning is not just about communication; it actually helps students become stronger thinkers across all their subjects.

E2: Additionally, scientists have found that younger children learn new sounds more easily because their brains are still in the developing stage.

X2: As a result, starting language learning early allows children to build these cognitive skills at the time when their brains are most ready to absorb them.

CS: Because of these brain benefits, learning a second language at a young age gives students a lasting advantage.

WHAT CHANGED (HIGHLIGHTED)
Semicolon: X1 uses a semicolon to join two related ideas into one compound sentence, making it more sophisticated.

Cause-and-effect transitions: "As a result" and "Because of these brain benefits" show why the evidence matters. These are stronger than "Also" because they explain the connection between ideas.

"Additionally" instead of "Also": A more precise transition that signals you're adding a second piece of evidence.
