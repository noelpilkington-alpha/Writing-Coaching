Should be: benefit (not "benifit"), One way (two words, not "Oneway"), tasks (plural, not "task")
THE PROOFREAD PASS
After you finish writing, read each sentence one more time and ask:

1. Missing words? Read the sentence out loud in your head. If it sounds choppy or incomplete, a small word is probably missing (in, the, a, to, is).

2. Spelling? Look at any word longer than 5 letters. Does it look right? Common fixes: "benifit" should be benefit, "Oneway" should be One way.

3. Plurals? If you are talking about more than one thing, the word needs an -s. "Simple task" should be "simple tasks" because robots do many tasks, not just one.

Your #2 Error: Sentence Rewrites

When you expand a fragment, the new sentence must flow smoothly

On Q1 and Q2, you were asked to rewrite or expand sentences. You knew what to say, but the way you put the words together was awkward. Look at Q2:

G4.2 Q2 — 1 out of 2
"A wildflower with bright blue petals with its color is between Navy and royal blue, is what it looks like."

The prompt asked you to turn a fragment into a complete sentence describing what a bluebonnet looks like. Your answer has all the right details (blue petals, navy and royal blue), but the structure gets tangled with repeated "with" phrases and "is what it looks like" tacked on.

A CLEANER VERSION (same ideas, smoother flow)
"A bluebonnet is a wildflower with bright blue petals, and its color falls between navy and royal blue."
THE RULE FOR SENTENCE REWRITES
When you expand a fragment into a full sentence:

1. Start with the subject. What is the sentence about? Put that first. ("A bluebonnet is...")

2. One describing phrase at a time. Don't stack two "with" phrases back to back. Use "and" or start a new sentence instead.

3. Read it back. If you stumble reading it out loud, the sentence probably needs to be split or simplified.

The same issue showed up on Q1:

Detail + Explanation

The pattern that turns a 13/20 paragraph into an 18/20 paragraph

Hudson, your Q11 paragraph has good ideas — you mentioned that robots help humans and gave an example. But the paragraph scored 13 out of 20 because it stays at the surface. You state facts but don't explain why they matter.

Here is the pattern that fixes this:

Detail + Explanation Pattern
DETAIL State a fact or example from the passage
EXPLAIN "This shows that..." or "This matters because..."

Every time you give a detail, follow it with an explanation sentence. Watch how it works:

YOUR VERSION (detail only, no explanation)
"One way is that robots can do simple tasks..."
WITH DETAIL + EXPLANATION
"One way is that robots can do simple tasks like vacuuming or sorting packages. This helps people because it gives them more free time to focus on harder or more creative work."

See the difference? The explanation sentence tells the reader why the detail matters. Without it, the reader is left thinking "So what?"

Which paragraph uses the Detail + Explanation pattern correctly?
A Robots can do simple tasks. Robots can also work in dangerous places. Robots are helpful to humans in many ways.
B Robots can do simple tasks like sorting packages. This helps people because it saves them time for harder work. Robots can also work in dangerous places like deep oceans. This matters because it keeps humans safe from harm.
HOW TO USE THIS ON YOUR NEXT TEST
Every time you write a detail in your paragraph, pause and ask yourself: "Why does this matter?" Then write one more sentence that answers that question.

Sentence starters that work:
• "This shows that..."
• "This matters because..."
• "This helps people because..."
• "This is important because..."
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Your Q11 scored 13 out of 20. Adding just two explanation sentences (one after each detail) could push that to 17–18 out of 20. That is 4–5 more points from one simple habit.

MODEL PARAGRAPH (18–20/20 level)

Humans benefit from robots in many different ways. One way is that robots can do simple tasks like vacuuming floors or sorting packages in warehouses. This helps people because it gives them more free time to focus on harder or more creative work. Another way robots help is by working in dangerous places, like deep under the ocean or inside burning buildings. This matters because it keeps humans safe from harm while still getting important jobs done. Overall, robots make life easier and safer for people, which is why so many are ready to welcome them.
