The Two Questions You Missed

Q8: Elaboration • Q9: Transitions

Q8 — Which sentence best adds elaboration?

YOU CHOSE
Immersion learning is a method that helps people learn language in classrooms.

This just defines immersion — it doesn't add new information. Elaboration means adding specific detail that develops the idea further.

CORRECT ANSWER
In immersion classes, students must use the new language all day, which helps them learn vocabulary faster.

See the difference? This tells us what happens in immersion classes and what it leads to. That's elaboration.

Q9 — Which transition best connects contrasting sentences?

YOU CHOSE
Although some experts think older students learn better...
CORRECT ANSWER
While some experts think older students learn better...
ALTHOUGH vs WHILE
Both words can show contrast, but they work differently:
"While" = two views exist side by side (balanced contrast)
"Although" = one view is a concession that leads to a stronger point

When presenting two equal but opposing opinions ("some experts say X, others say Y"), "While" is the better fit because it treats both sides equally.

Spelling, Capitalization & Tricky Words

These convention errors cost you the most points

Spelling — these words need fixing:

devolping → developing
grammer → grammar
pratice → practice
bran → brain
structered → structured
belive → believe
youger → younger
natrually → naturally

Doubled words:

it can can strengthen → it can strengthen
they use use the language → they use the language

Capitalization — languages and nationalities always get a capital:

spanish / french / americans → Spanish / French / Americans

Homophones & tricky words:

there language → their language (belonging to them)
speak they language → speak the language ("they" is a pronoun, not an article)
everyday → every day (two words = "each day")
childrens' → children's ("children" is already plural)

Evidence, explanation, sentence variety, and transitions

Your body paragraphs have topic sentences and evidence. To earn full points, add the explanation layer, vary your sentence structure, and use stronger transitions. Here's your Paragraph 3 rewritten:

YOUR VERSION
A good way to help kids learn a new language is through immersion. In an immersion school or class, teachers use the chosen language all day. Students hear it while talking to friends, in lessons and even small requests, like asking to go to the bathroom. Because they use the language daily, students pick up the phrases and grammer quickly.
DEVELOPED VERSION

TS: In addition to starting early, immersion classes offer another effective way for children to learn a new language.

E1: According to the passage, in immersion schools, teachers use the chosen language all day during lessons, conversations, and even simple requests like asking to go to the bathroom.

X1: This shows that students don't just study the language in one class — they are surrounded by it constantly, which makes learning feel natural rather than forced.

E2: The passage also explains that students learn new words because they hear the language in every part of class.

X2: As a result, vocabulary builds through daily exposure rather than memorization alone.

CS: Because of this constant practice, immersion is one of the most effective methods for language learning.

WHAT CHANGED (HIGHLIGHTED)
Transition: "In addition to starting early" connects this paragraph to the one before it — not just "Another way" but a transition that shows how ideas build on each other.

Sentence strategy: The X1 explanation uses a compound sentence (two ideas joined with "which") instead of two short simple sentences. This adds variety and flow.

Cause-and-effect transitions: "As a result" and "Because of this" show why the evidence matters, not just that it exists.

Your Proofreading Checklist

Run through this before you submit every essay

In AlphaWrite, the Polish stage is where you catch errors before your essay is assessed. On a test, you need to be your own editor. Based on the patterns in your writing, here's your personal checklist:

Read backwards, sentence by sentence.
Start with the last sentence and work to the first. This breaks your brain out of reading for meaning and forces you to see each word. This is how you catch "bran" instead of "brain" and doubled words like "can can."
Scan for capital letters on language names and nationalities.
Search for: Spanish, French, English, Americans, etc. If it comes from a country, it gets a capital.
Check every "there" / "their" / "they."
For each one, ask: Am I talking about a place (there), ownership (their), or a person doing something (they)? You mixed these up twice in this essay.
Check possessives.
Irregular plurals like "children" take 's (children's), not s' (childrens'). If the word is already plural and doesn't end in s, just add 's.
Read the introduction out loud — does the thesis preview 3 points?
If you can't hear three clear topics in your last sentence, rewrite it before moving on.
THE 3-MINUTE RULE
This checklist takes about 3 minutes. On a timed test, stop writing with 3 minutes left and run through it. Those 3 minutes are worth more points than an extra sentence of content.
