THE PROMPT-CHECK HABIT
Before you write your answer, read the prompt one more time and ask yourself: "What exactly is it telling me to do?" If it says "add," write something new. If it says "use because/but/so," make sure one of those words is in your sentence. Underline the key instruction word in your head before you start writing.

Run-On Sentences & Spelling

Q6 — 2 out of 3  |  Q5 — spelling

Reed, on Q6 you tried to use the word "because" to connect two ideas. That was the right move! But you crammed two separate ideas into the same sentence after "because," which created a run-on.

Q6 — What you wrote
"Pumpkin seeds need warm soil to sprout, because inside a seed is a hard coat the heat helps it open."

After "because," you wrote two ideas: (1) inside a seed is a hard coat, and (2) the heat helps it open. Those are two separate thoughts pushed together without a connecting word between them. That makes it a run-on.

Tap to see a fixed version
Same ideas, no run-on
Fixed version
"Pumpkin seeds need warm soil to sprout because the seed has a hard coat, and the heat helps it crack open."

Notice: each idea is separated by "and." The sentence flows without cramming.

Spelling note: On Q5 you wrote pumkin instead of pumpkin. The word has a "p" in the middle. When you finish writing, scan your answer for words that look short or funny, and sound them out: pump-kin.

Which sentence correctly uses "because" without creating a run-on?
A Pumpkins need a big garden because they grow on long vines they take up lots of space.
B Pumpkins need a big garden because their vines spread out and need a lot of room to grow.
THE RUN-ON CHECK
After you write a sentence with "because," read everything that comes after it. Ask: "Is this one idea or two ideas pushed together?" If you hear two separate thoughts, add "and" between them or split them into two sentences.

What to sharpen: Notice the phrase "Helping kids how to cook can help in many ways." The word "help" appears twice in the same sentence. When you reread your paragraph before submitting, look for repeated words and swap one out. For example: "Teaching kids to cook can benefit them in many ways."
