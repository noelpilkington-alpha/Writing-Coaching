Your 1 Missed Multiple Choice — G6.2 Q3

Logical conjunctions — match the joining word to the meaning

Des, on G6.2 you went 9 for 10 on multiple choice. The only miss was Q3 — and it is a one-minute rule once you see it.

FROM G6.2 Q3 (logical conjunctions)
Question: Which sentence combines the ideas using a logical conjunction?
"Animated movies are fun. They also include valuable lessons."
Your pick: "Animated movies are fun because they include valuable lessons."
Correct answer: "Animated movies are fun but they include valuable lessons."
THE RULE — MATCH THE CONJUNCTION TO THE MEANING
When you combine two sentences, the joining word has to match the relationship between the ideas:

and = adds a second idea of the same type
but / however = signals a contrast or an unexpected second idea
because / so = cause and effect
or = gives a choice

For G6.2 Q3, the first idea is that animated movies are fun (entertainment). The second idea is that they include valuable lessons (education). Those two qualities are surprising together — most fun things are not also educational. That contrast makes "but" the logical match. "Because" doesn't work because being educational doesn't cause them to be fun.
From G6.2 Q3 — which combination uses a LOGICAL conjunction?
A Animated movies are fun because they include valuable lessons.
B Animated movies are fun but they include valuable lessons.
THE QUICK CHECK
On any combining-sentences question, before you pick an answer, ask yourself:

• Does idea 2 add to idea 1? → use and
• Does idea 2 contrast with idea 1? → use but / however
• Does idea 1 cause idea 2? → use because / so
• Is idea 2 a choice from idea 1? → use or

30 seconds of that check and Q3 becomes automatic.

POLISHED BODY 2 (5/5 evidence-and-explanation level)

Second, animated movies can also teach kids how to share. According to the passage, many animated films show characters learning to work together and put others first — for example, the Merida-like characters who have to stop thinking only about themselves before they can solve the problem. This shows young viewers that sharing is not just a rule adults teach — it is a skill the main character needs to succeed. The passage also mentions that animated movies teach children “how to treat others” as a result of the kindness on screen. This proves that sharing, when shown in a story, becomes something children want to copy instead of something they have to be forced into. That is why a kid who grows up watching animated movies often finds it easier to share toys at home: the habit was modeled before it was practiced.
