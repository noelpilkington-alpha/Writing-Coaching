THE EVIDENCE SANDWICH RULE
Every Q11 paragraph needs three layers of evidence work:

1. Set-up sentence: "The article says..." / "The passage explains..." — tells the grader you are about to bring in the passage.
2. Specific detail (quoted or paraphrased): a short phrase (2–8 words in quotation marks) OR your own words with the specific fact kept exactly right (name, place, number, what happened).
3. "This shows..." sentence: explain what the detail proves — add new thinking, do not repeat the topic.

DO keep quotes short. 2–8 words is ideal. Copying a whole paragraph does not show understanding — it fills space.
DO paraphrase with the fact. "People played sports" is too loose. "British schools wrote down cricket rules in the 1700s" keeps the specific fact.

Which rewrite keeps the original meaning and follows the rules of a question?

THE INTERROGATIVE RULE
To turn a statement into a question:
1. Move the helping verb (or add "Did/Does/Do") to the front.
2. Keep every other word the same. No extras.
3. End with a question mark.

Statement: The geode felt like a perfect souvenir.
Question: Did the geode feel like a perfect souvenir?

✗ Do not add words like "not" — they change the meaning.
✓ Read the original and your rewrite out loud. If they no longer mean the same thing, something is off.
