STEP 2 FOR APPOSITIVES
When the question says "combine using an appositive," Step 2 tells you the skill is appositives. An appositive is a renaming phrase (not a clause with a verb like "to find safety"). It renames the noun and sits between commas.

Appositives: Rename, Do Not Explain

Q3 lost a point because you added a clause instead

Donny, Q3 asked you to combine two sentences using an appositive. You wrote something like "to find safety" — that is a purpose clause, not an appositive. An appositive renames the noun.

Q3 (1/2) — CLAUSE INSTEAD OF APPOSITIVE
You added a phrase like "to find safety" after the noun. This explains why something happens, but it does not rename the noun.
APPOSITIVE = RENAMING PHRASE
An appositive gives the noun a new name or description. It does NOT explain why or how.

Wrong (clause): "The bear, to find safety, climbed the tree."
Right (appositive): "The bear, a large brown animal, climbed the tree."

Test: Can you replace the noun with the appositive? "A large brown animal climbed the tree." Yes — it works. That confirms it is a renaming phrase.
Which uses an appositive correctly?
ASally, to protect her young, swam to the rocky shore.
BSally, a protective mother seal, swam to the rocky shore.
QUICK TEST
After writing your appositive, try replacing the noun with the phrase between commas. If the sentence still makes sense, you have a real appositive. If not, you probably wrote a clause instead.

Avoid Circular Reasoning: Q8

Your answer repeated the question instead of explaining

Donny, on Q8 you wrote something like "it is a good name because it gives the..." — this restates the question rather than answering it. The question asks WHY something is a good name, and your answer says "because it gives the [same thing]." That is circular.

Q8 (2/3) — CIRCULAR REASONING
"It is a good name because it gives the..." — this repeats the idea without adding a new reason or detail from the passage.
NON-CIRCULAR ANSWER
"This is a good name because the word 'shelter' means a place that keeps you safe, and the building does exactly that — it protects animals from storms and cold weather."

This answer gives a specific reason (what the word means + how it connects to what the building does). It does not repeat the question.
THE CIRCULAR TEST
After writing your answer, ask: "Did I say something new that was not already in the question?" If your answer just rephrases the question, add a specific detail from the passage that explains WHY.

Topic Sentence Supporting Details Closing Sentence Needs More D+E

Donny, your paragraph has all the right parts: topic sentence, supporting details, and a closing. The 3 points you lost come from needing more explanation after your details.

The fix is the D+E pattern: after each detail, add a sentence that explains why it matters or what it shows.

D+E PATTERN
Detail: "The community garden gives families fresh vegetables."
+ Explanation: "This matters because fresh food is healthier than what you can buy at a store, so families save money and eat better at the same time."

The second sentence is the "E" — it explains why the detail is important.
YOUR FIX FOR NEXT TIME
After writing each supporting detail, add one sentence that starts with "This matters because..." or "This shows that..." That alone will push your paragraph from 17 to 19+.
