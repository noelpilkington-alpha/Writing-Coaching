THE RULE
A question (also called an interrogative sentence) always ends with a question mark: ? If your sentence asks something — if it starts with words like Does, Is, Can, Do, Will, Why, How — check that the very last mark is a “?” before you move on.

B Does the event sell out every year?
THE ONE-SECOND CHECK
When a question asks you to write a question, read your answer back and look at the last mark. Does it end with ? If it ends with a period, swap it for a question mark. That is the whole fix.

#2 Focus: Fix Run-Ons — One Complete Thought per Sentence

When two complete thoughts run together, give each its own space

Emil, sometimes two complete thoughts get joined with nothing between them — that is called a run-on. The fix is simple: separate them with a period, or join them with a connector like and, but, so, or because. Here is the clearest example from Q5:

YOU WROTE (Q5 — what does the event show about the fans?)
"The fans love the Hershey Bears they are always there for the Teddy Bears Toss."
Two complete thoughts are fused together: “The fans love the Hershey Bears” and “they are always there for the Teddy Bear Toss.” Nothing joins or separates them.
FIXED — JOIN WITH A CONNECTOR
"The fans love the Hershey Bears, and they are always there for the Teddy Bear Toss."
Or split it into two sentences: "The fans love the Hershey Bears. They are always there for the Teddy Bear Toss." Either way, each complete thought gets its own space.
SAME PATTERN IN YOUR PARAGRAPH
Your Q11 has one too: "Then no libraries are moving to schools across Garissa camels are moving and going to schools…" Two complete thoughts run together. A period fixes it: "…across Garissa. Camels are moving and going to schools…"
Which one keeps a single complete thought clear (no run-on)?
A The fans love the Hershey Bears they are always there for the Teddy Bear Toss.
B The fans love the Hershey Bears, and they are always there for the Teddy Bear Toss.
THE RULE
One complete thought per sentence. When two complete thoughts run together, either put a period between them (two sentences) or join them with a connector (and, but, so, because). Never let them run straight into each other with nothing between.
THE READ-BACK CHECK
Read your sentence back. If you find two parts that could each stand alone as their own sentence, they need either a period or a connector between them. That one check catches every run-on.
