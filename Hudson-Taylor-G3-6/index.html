THE RULE
"Because" is a subordinating conjunction — a word that JOINS the two halves of a sentence. When "because" is in the middle, you do NOT put a comma right before it.

✗ "Dogs bite, because they are nervous."
✓ "Dogs bite because they are nervous."
WHEN DOES "BECAUSE" GET A COMMA NEARBY?
Only when "because" starts the sentence. Then the comma goes at the end of the because-clause:

✓ "Because they are nervous, dogs bite."
✗ "Dogs bite, because they are nervous."

Rule of thumb: If "because" is in the middle of the sentence, there is no comma before it. Period. New sentence to remember: the two halves of the thought flow together.
From G3.6 Q2 — which version would have earned full credit?
A They can hold on to tree branches tightly, because they have strong claws and rough paw pads.
B They can hold on to tree branches tightly because they have strong claws and rough paw pads.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
You've done this on every single test. Fixing it is a one-character deletion. On your proofread pass, just scan each answer for "because" and check what's right in front. If it's a comma, delete it. That's it. Easiest points you will ever earn.

THE QUICK TEST FOR THERE / THEIR / THEY'RE
THEIR = belonging to them. → "Their paws." (Like my paws, but for them.)
THERE = a place, or starts a sentence like "there is..." → "There is a porcupine over there."
THEY'RE = "they are" → "They're fast." (Only use when you can replace with "they are.")
POLISHED VERSION
"Another way their body helps them is their paws. Their paws are very grippy, so it helps them climb trees and get food."

MODEL POLISHED PARAGRAPH (19–20/20 level)

A porcupine's body is built to keep it safe in the wild. First, porcupines have sharp quills that cover their back. This shows that a porcupine doesn't need to outrun a predator — its body alone can stop an attack. Second, their paws are very grippy, which helps them climb trees to find food and escape danger. This means their body gives them two different defenses: one for fighting back, and one for getting away. Because of their quills and their strong paws, a porcupine can survive almost anywhere in the forest.

The 5-Part Paragraph (Your Next Level)
TOPIC State your central idea (you already do this!)
DETAIL 1 Specific detail from the passage
SHOWS "This shows..." — explain what detail 1 means
DETAIL 2 A second specific detail from the passage
SHOWS "This shows..." — explain what detail 2 means
CLOSING Connect back to your central idea
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
You already have the hardest part (topic + 2 details + conclusion). Adding ONE "this shows..." sentence after each detail is what pushes from 16/20 into the 18–20 range. Question 11 is worth almost half the test. This is the highest-value upgrade you can make.
