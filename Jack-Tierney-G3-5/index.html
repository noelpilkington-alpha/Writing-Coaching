Your #1 Repeated Error: Appositive Combining

Q4 has been partial on 3 of your 5 tests
🔍 Pattern alert: On G3.3 Q4, G3.4 Q3, and G3.5 Q4, you hit the same rock. When you try to combine two sentences with an appositive or rewrite as a question, you drop a comma where a phrase belongs, or you add a word that changes the meaning. Fixable once you see the pattern.

Jack, look at your most recent G3.5 Q4. The prompt asked you to combine two sentences by adding an appositive. An appositive is a phrase that renames the subject and is set off by commas.

YOU WROTE (G3.5 Q4, scored 1/2)
Combine: "The books are still popular. They are known for their golden spines."

"The books are still popular, they are known for their golden spines."
That comma joins two complete sentences — a comma splice. It is not an appositive.

An appositive would turn the second sentence into a phrase — not a full clause — and tuck it right after the subject it describes.

Which version uses an appositive correctly?
A The books are still popular, they are known for their golden spines.
B The books, known for their golden spines, are still popular.
THE RULE
An appositive is a phrase (a few words, not a full sentence) that renames the subject. It goes next to the noun it describes and is set off by commas.

Two-sentence starter:
1. The Poky Little Puppy was popular.
2. The Poky Little Puppy was a best-selling story.

Appositive combine:
"The Poky Little Puppy, a best-selling story, was popular."

✓ A phrase renaming the subject
✓ Two commas hugging the phrase
✗ Never a full sentence on the right side of the first comma

YOUR PARAGRAPH AT 19–20/20

Artist Jason deCaires Taylor made over 500 statues and placed them under the sea to protect coral reefs. He put them in the ocean because when swimmers visit reefs they hurt the coral. This shows that his sculptures give swimmers somewhere else to go, so the real reefs get left alone. Some statues he made are a man on a bike, and some kids in a circle holding hands. Now ocean life has started to live on the statues. This shows that the sculptures are becoming a new home for sea creatures instead of damaging the coral. All in all, what Jason did for coral reefs is a great thing because his art protects the ocean while giving people a reason to visit.
