Sentence Upgrades — Do What the Prompt Asks

Q3 appositive and Q4 combining both missed the mark

Helio, on Q3 and Q4 the prompt asked you to upgrade a sentence in a specific way. In both cases, you didn't actually add the upgrade — you wrote a clean sentence, but not the one the prompt asked for. Each of these cost you a half point.

FROM G3.3 Q3 — ADD AN APPOSITIVE
Prompt: "Rewrite the following sentence and add an appositive: Sally swam to the other seals."
You wrote: "Sally swam towards the other seals."
Your sentence is fine, but no appositive was added. An appositive is an extra noun phrase that renames or describes a noun in the sentence, set off by commas. Example: "Sally, the rescued pup, swam to the other seals."
FROM G3.3 Q4 — COMBINE TWO SENTENCES
Prompt: "Combine: 1. Anna and Jim ran to the beach. 2. They found a baby seal in a fishnet."
You wrote: "Anna and Jim ran to the beach, and found a baby seal in a fishnet."
Close — but the comma before "and" isn't needed here because the second part doesn't have its own subject. The cleaner combine is: "Anna and Jim ran to the beach and found a baby seal in a fishnet." (no comma) OR "When Anna and Jim ran to the beach, they found a baby seal in a fishnet."
What is an Appositive?
DO An appositive is an extra noun or phrase that renames or describes a noun. Tuck it in with two commas.
Sally, a young baby seal, swam to the other seals.
DO Ask yourself: "Can I rename or describe a noun in this sentence?" Then drop that phrase right after the noun.
Anna, the older sister, ran to the beach.
NO Just fixing a word or adding a few details isn't an appositive. The prompt is asking for a specific kind of upgrade — the noun-rename phrase.

A The baby seal swam quickly to its family.
B Sally, a rescued pup, swam to the other seals.
