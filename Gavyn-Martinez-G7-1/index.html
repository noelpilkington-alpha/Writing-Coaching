Question 3 Fix — Combining Sentences

Semicolons vs. subordinating conjunctions

You scored 0/1 on this question.

The question asked: Which sentence combines the ideas logically? Idea 1: Children could study in the evenings. Idea 2: They could see better.

Which combination shows the correct logical relationship?
A "Children could study in the evenings; they could see better."
B "Because they could see better, children could study in the evenings."
YOU CHOSE
"Children could study in the evenings; they could see better."
SEMICOLONS VS. SUBORDINATING CONJUNCTIONS
Semicolons (;) connect two equal, independent ideas that are closely related but neither one causes the other:
• "The village had no electricity; families relied on candles." (two parallel facts)

Subordinating conjunctions (because, although, since, when, if) show a relationship between ideas — one idea depends on the other:
• "Because they could see better, children could study." (cause → effect)
• "Although the project was expensive, it saved money." (contrast)

How to decide: Ask yourself — does one idea cause, contrast, or depend on the other? If yes, use a subordinating conjunction. If they are just parallel facts, use a semicolon.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Sentence combining is not just a test skill — it is how you build complex sentences in your essays. Using the right connector shows the reader how your ideas relate, which makes your writing clearer and more sophisticated.

Question 7 Fix — Topic Sentence Matching

Matching topic sentences to specific details

You scored 0/1 on this question.

The question asked: Which sentence best matches the topic: "Benefits for local businesses after solar panels were installed"?

Which sentence best matches a paragraph about business benefits?
A "Businesses could stay open for longer."
B "The extra hours gave customers more time to shop."
YOU CHOSE
"Businesses could stay open for longer."
THE DIFFERENCE: CAUSE VS. BENEFIT
Your answer ("Businesses could stay open for longer") describes what changed. The correct answer ("The extra hours gave customers more time to shop") describes the benefit of that change.

When a question asks about benefits or effects, look for the sentence that describes the positive result, not just the change itself:
• Change: Businesses stayed open longer. (what happened)
• Benefit: Customers had more time to shop. (why it matters)
SO WHAT?
This skill transfers directly to your essay writing. When you write a body paragraph, your supporting details should explain why something matters, not just what happened. That deeper thinking is what separates a good essay from a great one.

INTRODUCTION In a very small town, far from metropolitan civilization, there used to be an extremely scarce amount of electricity. Families relied on candles and kerosene lamps for years at a times. Then, the solar panels arrived. This community led project, which was funded by donations and government support. It made sure that every rooftop had row upon it.

BODY 1 (good content) With the help of this new project, children were now able to study in the evenings without putting extra pressure on their eyes. Shops also stayed open for longer hours, which helped grow local buisnesses. The medical clinic that used to close at sunset was able to store vaccines and medicine at night.

BODY 2 (fragment + underdeveloped — only 3 sentences) Although the transition into utilizing these solar panels was very rigorous. Residents of the town often worried about the sustainability of the project. Others wondered how the project would work during cloudy weeks.

BODY 3 (good) The project leaders decided to address these concerns by organizing workshops on panel care and energy-saving habits. As the project progressed, these workshops helped develop trust in the movement towards sustainable electricity.

CONCLUSION Nowadays, the village is a great example to show communities that are in need of affordable and renewable energy resources. People often visit to learn from the experience. Not only has this movement changed the daily life in the village, it has also made the bonds stronger between residents of the community. Now, the evenings bring laughter, jokes, and conversations that fill the streets with joy that would have never been seen before.
WHAT YOU DID WELL
You have strong vocabulary ("metropolitan civilization," "sustainable electricity"). Body 1 covers three specific improvements (studying, businesses, the clinic). Your conclusion has a vivid final image. You show real writing talent, Gavyn.
ISSUE 1: SENTENCE FRAGMENT IN BODY 2
"Although the transition into utilizing these solar panels was very rigorous."

This is a fragment. The word "Although" makes it a dependent clause — it needs to connect to another idea. Fix it by attaching it to the next sentence:

Fixed: "Although the transition into using these solar panels was challenging, residents often worried about the sustainability of the project."
ISSUE 2: BODY 2 IS UNDERDEVELOPED
Body 2 has only 3 sentences and mentions concerns but never explains what specifically worried people or why. What did the passage say about the challenges? Add specific details and explain what they show. A strong body paragraph needs at least 4–5 sentences: topic sentence, specific evidence, and explanation.
ISSUE 3: KEEP IT CLEAR
Your vocabulary is impressive, but sometimes simpler is clearer:
• "extremely scarce amount of electricity" → "very little electricity"
• "utilizing these solar panels" → "using solar panels"

Using big words is great when they add meaning. But if a simpler word says the same thing, go with the simpler one. Clear writing is always stronger than fancy writing.
SO WHAT?
