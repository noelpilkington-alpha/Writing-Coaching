Q3: Keep Appositives Short

3-6 words between the commas

The question asked you to rewrite the sentence and add an appositive. You added one, but it was way too long:

YOU WROTE
Garrett Morgan, a human that had an idea that saved lives and reduced crashes, decided to built an early traffic signal.

That appositive is 13 words — it swallows the whole sentence! An appositive should be a quick description, just 3-6 words. It also has a grammar error: "decided to built" should be "decided to build."

WITH A SHORT APPOSITIVE
Garrett Morgan, an inventor from Ohio, built an early traffic signal that helped save lives.
THE APPOSITIVE PATTERN
Name, [short description], rest of sentence.

Keep it to 3-6 words between the commas. Think of it like a whisper to the reader: "Garrett Morgan — oh, by the way, he was an inventor from Ohio — built an early traffic signal."

More examples:
• Garrett Morgan, a clever inventor, noticed how dangerous traffic was.
• The traffic signal, his greatest invention, saved many lives.

Also remember: after "decided to," use the base form of the verb — "decided to build," not "decided to built."

Grammar: Irregular Past Tense

Q1 and Q8 — Small fixes, easy points

You made two grammar mistakes that are quick to fix once you know the pattern:

Q8: "maked" is not a word. "Make" is an irregular verb — it doesn't follow the normal "-ed" rule.

maked → made

Here are other common irregular past tenses to remember:

builded → built
thinked → thought
goed → went
seed → saw

Q1: "these signal allow" — when the subject is plural ("signals"), the noun needs an -s:

these signal allow → these signals allow
QUICK CHECK
If a past-tense word sounds funny when you say it out loud (like "maked"), it's probably irregular. Try saying "I maked it" versus "I made it" — your ear will catch it! When in doubt, think about how you'd say it in conversation.
