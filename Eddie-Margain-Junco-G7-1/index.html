Recurring Error: Cause vs. Effect

Q7 — same pattern as G6.2

Q7 — YOUR ANSWER (0 out of 2 weighted points — Incorrect)
You selected the option that described what led TO the decision (the cause), instead of what happened BECAUSE OF the decision (the effect).
THE CAUSE-VS-EFFECT TEST
When a question asks "What was the EFFECT of X?" use this test:

Step 1: Find event X in the passage.
Step 2: Ask yourself: "What happened AFTER X? What changed BECAUSE of X?"
Step 3: The answer should be something that came AFTER and was CAUSED BY X.

If your answer describes something that happened BEFORE X or something that CAUSED X to happen, you have picked the cause instead of the effect. Flip it.
A school decides to install solar panels. Which answer describes the EFFECT of that decision?
A "The school's energy costs had been rising for three years, so administrators looked for alternatives."
B "After the solar panels were installed, the school's electricity bill dropped by 40% in the first year."
WHY THIS KEEPS HAPPENING
Cause-and-effect questions are tricky because both the cause and the effect are mentioned in the passage. Your brain grabs the most memorable detail, but that detail might be the cause, not the effect. The fix: always ask "Did this happen BEFORE the event or AFTER it?" If it happened before, it is the cause. If after, it is the effect.

Error: Wrong Conjunction

CONJUNCTION LOGIC
Each conjunction signals a different relationship:

• "because" / "since" → gives a REASON (why something happened)
• "so" / "therefore" → gives a RESULT (what happened next)
• "but" / "however" / "although" → shows a CONTRAST (something unexpected)
• "and" / "also" → adds MORE of the same

Before choosing, ask: "Are these two ideas a reason, a result, a contrast, or an addition?"
Which conjunction fits? "The solar panels cost a lot of money to install, ____ the school saved thousands of dollars in the long run."
A "because"
B "but"
THE FIX
On conjunction questions, read the sentence with your chosen word plugged in and ask: "Does this sound like the right relationship?" If the two ideas are opposites, use a contrast word. If one idea explains WHY the other happened, use a reason word. If one idea is the RESULT of the other, use a result word.

THE DETAIL + EXPLANATION PATTERN FOR ESSAYS
Every body paragraph should follow this structure:

1. Topic sentence: State the point this paragraph will prove.
2. Detail from the passage: Name a specific fact, quote, or event from the text.
3. Explanation: Explain what that detail shows or why it matters to your argument.

Example:
Topic: "Solar panels help schools save money over time."
Detail: "The passage states that after installation, the school's energy bill dropped by 40% in one year."
Explanation: "This shows that even though panels are expensive upfront, they pay for themselves quickly by cutting ongoing costs."
Which body paragraph uses the Detail + Explanation pattern?
