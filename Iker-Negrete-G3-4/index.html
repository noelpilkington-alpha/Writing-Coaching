Quick Fix — “Would Have” Not “Would Of”

A common mistake that’s easy to fix.

This showed up in Question 7, where you scored 2/3.

Which sentence is correct?
A I would of kept the geode to make some big money.
B I would have kept the geode to make some big money.
YOU WROTE
“I would of keep the geode to make some big money.”
CORRECTED
“I would have kept the geode to make some big money.”
WHY THIS HAPPENS
When we talk, “would have” sounds like “would of.” But “of” is never the right word here. The trick: if you can say “would’ve,” it means “would have.”

Also, after “would have,” the verb goes into past form: keep → kept, go → gone, take → taken.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
This error comes up a lot in student writing. Fixing it makes your sentences sound polished and correct — and it shows grading feedback that you know your grammar.

THE 30-SECOND PLANNING TRICK
Before you write, jot down just 3 things:
• Main idea: What is my paragraph about? (Example: “Sports went from rich-only to everyone.”)
• 2–3 details: What facts from the passage support this? (Example: wealthy people played golf, then middle class joined, then schools added sports.)
• Closing: One sentence to wrap up.

This simple plan keeps your paragraph organized and easy to follow.
