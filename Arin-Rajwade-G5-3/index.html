SUBJECT–VERB — FROM G5.3 Q7
"Doing activities together strengthen family relationships by letting them spend time together and bond."
The subject is "Doing activities together" (one action), so the verb should be strengthens.
Which sentence matches its subject and verb correctly?

THE RULE
When your subject is a single action starting with an "-ing" word (like "Doing activities together"), treat it as one thing — it takes a singular verb: strengthens, not strengthen. Read the sentence with just the core subject and verb ("Doing... strengthens") to hear whether they match.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
These are the smallest possible fixes — one letter in "something," one letter in "strengthens." On your final read-through, look at every verb and every word longer than five letters and ask, "does this match, and is this spelled right?" Clean answers keep the points your ideas already earned.

Your #1 Fix: Appositive Placement

An appositive goes right after the noun it renames

Arin, on G5.3 Question 1 you were asked to add an appositive — a short noun phrase that renames another noun — to describe Willow State Park. You had the right phrase ("the best place ever"), but the commas landed in the wrong spot and cut the sentence apart:

YOU WROTE — G5.3 Q1
"Every summer, the Thompsons camp at, the best place ever, Willow State Park."
The commas split "camp at ... Willow State Park," so the sentence stops making sense.

An appositive belongs right after the noun it describes, with commas around it. "The best place ever" renames "Willow State Park," so it goes directly after "Willow State Park":

CORRECTED
"Every summer, the Thompsons camp at Willow State Park, the best place ever."
Which sentence places the appositive correctly?
A Every summer, the Thompsons camp at Willow State Park, the best place ever.
B Every summer, the Thompsons camp at, the best place ever, Willow State Park.
THE RULE
An appositive is a noun phrase that renames the noun right before it. Put it directly after that noun, with a comma on each side (or one comma if it ends the sentence). Read the sentence without the appositive — "Every summer, the Thompsons camp at Willow State Park." — and it should still be a complete, correct sentence. If it isn't, your commas are in the wrong place.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
You already know what an appositive is and you picked a good one. Placing it right after the noun it renames turns a half-point into a full point — and makes the sentence read the way you meant it to.

Your #2 Focus: Use Specific Evidence, Not Filler

Name the detail — don't just say "he gives details"

Arin, your paragraph on G5.3 was well organized — clear topic sentence, First/Then/Lastly transitions, and a conclusion. The one thing holding it back from the top marks is that it stays generic. Look at your opening reason:

YOU WROTE — G5.3 Q11 (opening reason)
"First, he gives specific details supporting his claim."
This announces that evidence exists — but never says what the evidence actually is.

Saying "he gives specific details" is filler: it tells the reader there is evidence without giving any. The fix is to name the actual detail. Point to the exact reason or example from the passage:

WITH SPECIFIC EVIDENCE
"First, the author explains that students can explore places they could never visit in person — like the deep ocean or a faraway country — without leaving their classroom."
Which reason actually gives the reader evidence?
A The author gives specific details that support his claim about VR field trips.
B The author explains that VR field trips cost a school less than real trips, because there is no money spent on transportation to travel to a location.
NAME THE EVIDENCE
Before you write a reason, ask yourself: "What exactly did the author say?" Then write that, not the fact that he said something. Swap "he gives details" for the real detail: a reason, an example, or a fact from the passage. Every "he shows..." should be followed by what he shows.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
You already have the structure the top paragraphs use. Naming the specific evidence in each reason is the difference between a good paragraph and a full-marks one — it is the single biggest lever you have on Question 11.
