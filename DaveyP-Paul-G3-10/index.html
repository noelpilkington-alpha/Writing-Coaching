EXAMPLE 2 OF 2 — FROM G3.9 Q1 (Partial, 0.5/2)
Prompt: Combine using an appositive. Sentence 3: A colony has one leader. Sentence 4: She is the queen bee.

You wrote: "The queen bee, the biggest bee in the colony, is the only leader of the colony."
This one is closer — but "the biggest bee in the colony" is not information from the two given sentences. An appositive should rename the subject using info from the original sentences.
WHAT IS AN APPOSITIVE?
An appositive is a short phrase that renames a noun, set off by commas.

Two sentences: A colony has one leader. She is the queen bee.
Combined with an appositive: A colony has one leader, the queen bee.

The phrase "the queen bee" renames "one leader." That is the appositive pattern.
THE APPOSITIVE RECIPE
1. Find the noun the second sentence is describing (the "leader" in "She is the queen bee").
2. Pull just the new name/description from sentence 2 (the queen bee).
3. Paste it into sentence 1 right after that noun, wrapped in commas:
    A colony has one leader, the queen bee.
Which sentence correctly combines these using an appositive?
Sentence A: My dog is a great swimmer. Sentence B: My dog is named Buddy.
A My dog is a great swimmer, my dog is named Buddy.
B My dog, Buddy, is a great swimmer.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
