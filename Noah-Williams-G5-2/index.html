Use the Conjunction the Prompt Asks For

Q4 — 0 out of 2 — prompt said "because, but, or so" but you used "while"
🔍
What happened: The prompt specifically told you to combine the sentences using "because," "but," or "so." You wrote a perfectly good combined sentence, but you used "while" instead. "While" is a subordinating conjunction that works grammatically, but it was not one of the three options the prompt gave you. The question was testing whether you can use those specific words. Using any other word earns zero credit.
G5.2 Q4 — WRONG CONJUNCTION (SCORED 0 OUT OF 2)
The prompt said: Combine using "because," "but," or "so."
You wrote: "While the pies baked, we sat at the kitchen table, drinking tea or solving crossword puzzles, and sometimes we listened..."
"While" is a valid conjunction, but the prompt asked for "because," "but," or "so." The answer must use one of those three words to earn credit.
WHAT WOULD HAVE EARNED FULL CREDIT
✓ "The pies baked, so we sat at the kitchen table, drinking tea or solving crossword puzzles..."
✓ "We sat at the kitchen table drinking tea because the pies were baking..."

Same ideas, same quality of writing. The only difference: you use one of the three words the prompt asked for.
The prompt says: combine using "because," "but," or "so." Which answer earns credit?
A While the pies baked, we sat at the kitchen table drinking tea.
B The pies were baking, so we sat at the kitchen table drinking tea.
THE PROMPT-FIRST HABIT
Before you start writing your combined sentence, do this:

1. Read the prompt instructions twice.
2. Circle or underline the specific words the prompt tells you to use (like "because," "but," or "so").
3. Write your answer using one of those exact words.
4. Before submitting, check: "Did I actually use one of the words the prompt asked for?"

This takes 10 seconds and it is the difference between 0 out of 2 and 2 out of 2.
WHY THIS IS AN EASY FIX
Your sentence was well-constructed. You know how to combine ideas. The only problem was choosing "while" instead of one of the three words the prompt specified. This is not a skill gap. It is a reading-the-instructions gap. Once you build the habit of checking the prompt before writing, this error disappears.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Q4 was worth 2 points and you earned 0. That is 2 points on a 25-point section. Fix this one habit and your short-answer score jumps from 18/25 to 20/25 before you even touch anything else.
