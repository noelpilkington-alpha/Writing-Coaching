Six questions where a small fix earns the full point

Noah, you earned partial credit on seven questions. That is actually great news — partial credit means you were on the right track and just missed one piece. Here is what happened on each one, in order, so you know exactly what to do differently next time.

Q1 — 1 out of 2
Prompt: Rewrite sentences 1 and 2 as one sentence that includes an appositive.
You wrote: "My famly has two dogs, and thier names are Lucy and Rocket."
What went well: You combined two sentences into one. Good move.
What missed the full point: The prompt asked for an appositive — a little naming phrase tucked into the sentence with commas. You used "and" instead, which is a conjunction. Also two small spellings: "famly" → "family", "thier" → "their".
Q1 FULL-CREDIT VERSION
My family has two dogs, Lucy and Rocket.
See how "Lucy and Rocket" sits right after "two dogs" with a comma? That naming phrase is the appositive. No "and" needed.
Q6 — 2 out of 3
Prompt: How would you feel if a dog walked up to you? Write one sentence to explain.
You wrote: "I would feel confused that a randum dog woudle wack up to me."
What went well: You named a feeling ("confused") and wrote a complete sentence.
What missed the full point: The prompt said "explain," so the grader wants the reason for the feeling. And a few spellings: "randum" → "random", "woudle" → "would", "wack" → "walk".
Q6 FULL-CREDIT VERSION
I would feel confused if a random dog walked up to me, because I would not know who it belonged to or if it was friendly.
Same feeling + "because" + the reason.

The 4-Part Paragraph Shape
TOPIC State your main idea. Turn the prompt into a sentence.
DETAIL 1 One thing from the article that supports your idea
DETAIL 2 A second thing from the article
CLOSING Wrap back to your main idea in new words
