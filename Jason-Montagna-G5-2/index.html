Your #1 Fix: Catch Spelling Errors

Q8 and Q11 — words you know but typed incorrectly
Q8 — YOUR ANSWER (2 out of 3)
"I would start a progect to help the community."

Should be: project (p-r-o-j-e-c-t). The "j" was replaced with "g."

Q11 — SPELLING IN PARAGRAPH
"Starting a Buisness requires dedication."

Should be: Business (b-u-s-i-n-e-s-s). The "i" and "s" are swapped.

THE FIX: SAY IT SLOWLY AS YOU TYPE
Both of these words are ones you use in speech. The fix is to whisper the word as you write it:

• "pro-ject" — you hear the "j" sound, not a "g" sound
• "bus-i-ness" — you hear "bus" then "i" then "ness"

After writing, read back and look for any word that seems longer or shorter than it should be.
Which spelling is correct?
A "Starting a Buisness requires hard work and planning."
B "Starting a Business requires hard work and planning."
YOUR RULE
After finishing each answer, scan for any word that has more than two syllables. Those are the ones most likely to have a typo. Say the word slowly in your head and check that the letters match the sounds.

Your #2 Fix: Name the Specific Feeling

Q6 — 2 out of 3 — answer was too general

On Q6, the question asked you to describe the feeling of learning something new. Your answer talked about the experience in general terms but did not name a specific emotion.

Q6 — YOUR ANSWER (2 out of 3)
"Learning something new is fun and you get better at things."

The problem: "Fun" is vague. The question asks about the feeling of learning. A stronger answer names a specific emotion (proud, excited, curious, accomplished) and explains when you feel it.

A STRONGER ANSWER
"Learning something new feels exciting at first because everything is unfamiliar, and then you feel proud when you finally understand it after practicing."
THE FIX
When a question asks about a feeling, name the specific emotion. Replace "fun" or "good" or "nice" with a real feeling word: proud, excited, curious, nervous, accomplished, frustrated, relieved. Then explain WHEN you feel that way.
The question asks: "How does it feel to learn something new?" Which answer earns full points?
A "It feels good and nice because you learn new things."
B "Learning something new can feel frustrating at first, but then you feel proud when you finally get it right after trying many times."
YOUR PROOFREAD CHECK
After writing an answer about feelings, ask yourself: "Did I use a specific feeling word, or did I just say 'fun' or 'good'?" If you said something general, swap it for a specific emotion.
