Step 2 Error: Connector Words

Q3 and Q5 lost points for wrong connector choice

Quinn, on Q3 and Q5 you used the wrong connector word. This is a Step 2 problem: you need to identify what skill the question is testing and what relationship the connector needs to show.

Q3 — 1 out of 2
You used "because" when the question asked for an appositive (a phrase that renames or explains a noun, set off by commas).
WHAT IS AN APPOSITIVE?
An appositive is a phrase placed next to a noun that gives more information about it. It is set off by commas:

• "Sally, a playful seal, loves swimming." → "a playful seal" is the appositive. It renames Sally.
• "Pikachu, an electric-type Pokémon, can use Thunderbolt." → "an electric-type Pokémon" is the appositive.
Q5 — 1 out of 2
You used the wrong connector word. The relationship between the two ideas needed a different connector.
Which sentence correctly uses an appositive to add information about the seal?
A Sally loves swimming because she is a playful seal who lives in the ocean.
B Sally, a playful grey seal, loves swimming in the cold ocean waves.
STEP 2 IN ACTION

If it says "combine using a connector," check what relationship the sentences have (cause, contrast, time). Name the skill first, then write.

Quinn, your paragraph on Q11 earned 16 out of 20. That means your structure is working. To move from 16 to 18+, you need to add more development and explanation after each piece of evidence.

THE D+E PATTERN
After you state a fact or give an example, add:

D (Development): Explain what the example means in your own words.
E (Explanation): Connect it back to your topic sentence. Why does this example matter?

Without D+E: "Pokémon teaches you strategy." (states a fact, stops)
With D+E: "Pokémon teaches you strategy because you have to think about which moves to use against different types. This shows that games can help you practise making smart decisions under pressure."
