The Body Paragraph Pattern

How to build a strong body paragraph every time

Every body paragraph in a G8+ essay should follow this pattern:

Body Paragraph Formula
TS Topic Sentence — states the paragraph's main point
E1 Evidence 1 — a specific fact, quote, or example from the passage
X1 Explanation 1 — explain why that evidence matters
E2 Evidence 2 — a second fact or example from the passage
X2 Explanation 2 — explain why that evidence matters
CS Concluding Sentence — wraps up the paragraph's main idea

EXAMPLE BODY PARAGRAPH
[TS] Once a memory is encoded, it must be consolidated — moved from short-term to long-term storage — or it will fade within seconds. [E1] According to the passage, short-term memory holds information for only about thirty seconds, and the hippocampus acts like a sorting center that decides which memories to keep. [X1] This shows that the brain does not store every experience equally — it selects which memories are important enough to preserve, which is why some moments stick with us and others are forgotten almost immediately. [E2] The passage also explains that during sleep, the hippocampus replays the day's events and strengthens neural connections, and that students who sleep well after studying retain up to forty percent more information. [X2] This means that sleep is not just rest — it is an active part of memory formation, which explains why staying up all night to study can actually make it harder to remember what you learned. [CS] Without consolidation, even the most vivid experiences would disappear from memory in a matter of seconds.
THE KEY RULE
Every E (evidence) must be followed by an X (explanation). You already include plenty of evidence — that's a strength. The missing piece is the explanation: why does that evidence matter? Ask yourself: "So what? What does this show about how memory works?"

MCQ Q6 — "Although" vs. "Until"
The sentence was: "_______ a memory is consolidated by the hippocampus, it remains in short-term storage and fades within seconds."

You chose "Although" — but "although" means "even though," which would mean the memory fades despite being consolidated. The correct answer is "Until" — meaning the memory stays in short-term storage until the hippocampus consolidates it. "Until" shows a time relationship; "although" shows a contrast.
