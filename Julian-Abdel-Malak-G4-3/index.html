Step 2: Appositives vs. Relative Clauses

Q3 used the wrong grammar structure

Julian, Q3 asked you to add an appositive to the sentence. You added a relative clause instead. These look similar but they are different structures.

Q3 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2 weighted points)
"He ask his mom, who made dinner, for some lettuce."

The problem: "Who made dinner" is a relative clause (it starts with "who" and contains a verb). An appositive is a noun phrase that renames or describes the noun next to it.

CORRECT APPOSITIVE
"He asked his mom, a great cook, for some lettuce."
HOW TO TELL THEM APART
Appositive = a noun phrase set off by commas that RENAMES the noun before it.
• "his mom, a great cook," (renames "mom" as "a great cook")
• "the caterpillar, a tiny green creature," (renames "caterpillar")

Relative clause = starts with "who," "which," or "that" and contains a VERB.
• "his mom, who made dinner," (has verb "made" — this is NOT an appositive)

The test: Can you replace it with "= [noun]"? If yes, it is an appositive.
• "his mom = a great cook" works. "his mom = who made dinner" does not work.
Which sentence correctly uses an appositive?
A"The boy, who loved nature, watched the butterfly."
B"The boy, a curious fourth grader, watched the butterfly."
WHY THIS MATTERS
Q3 is worth 2 weighted points. You earned 1 out of 2. If you use the right structure (noun phrase, no verb, renames the noun), you earn full credit. The test for this is simple: Does it start with "who/which/that" and have a verb? If yes, it is a relative clause, not an appositive.
