Naming the Four Sentence Types

Q2: a statement got labeled a command

James Finn, Question 2 asked you to identify the sentence type. Here is the sentence and the label you chose:

YOUR ANSWER (G7.2 Q2)
"Some residents worried that the lanes would remove parking spots." → you chose imperative

This sentence is declarative — it is a statement that tells you a fact (what the residents worried about). An imperative sentence gives a command or an order. The quick test: an imperative tells someone to do something ("Add more parking." / "Slow down."). This sentence does not tell anyone to do anything, so it cannot be imperative.

THE FOUR SENTENCE TYPES
Declarative — makes a statement, ends with a period. The town added bike lanes.
Interrogative — asks a question, ends with a question mark. Will the lanes remove parking?
Exclamatory — shows strong feeling, ends with an exclamation point. These lanes are amazing!
Imperative — gives a command or order. Add a loading zone here.
Which of these is the imperative sentence (a command)?
A "The residents wanted the town to add more parking near the shops."
B "Add more parking near the shops before the lanes go in."
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Sentence-type questions are pure points once you have the test memorized: imperative = a command that orders an action; everything that just states a fact is declarative. Run that one check and this question type is automatic.

Connector Logic: "Because" vs "And"

Q3: a connector implied a cause that was not there

James Finn, Question 3 asked you to combine two ideas logically. Here is the combination you chose:

YOUR ANSWER (G7.2 Q3)
"Shops stayed open a little later because more customers stopped by on their way home."

The two ideas here are parallel results of the bike lanes — the shops staying open later and more customers stopping by both happened. The word "because" claims the later hours were caused by the customers, which the passage does not say. The right connector is "and": both things happened, side by side.

THE RULE: MATCH THE CONNECTOR TO THE RELATIONSHIP
"because" = one thing caused the other. The shops were busier because the lanes brought more foot traffic.
"and" = both things happened together (parallel). Shops stayed open later, and more customers stopped by.
Before you pick a connector, ask: did one really cause the other, or did they just both happen?
THE LOGICAL COMBINE
"Shops stayed open a little later, and more customers stopped by on their way home."
Which combination joins the two ideas with the correct logic?
A "The town added bike lanes because families felt safe traveling together."
B "After the lanes went in, families felt safer, and they could travel together more easily."
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Connector questions are a one-second check: "because" only works if one idea truly caused the other. If both ideas just happened, use "and." Test the cause claim before you choose and this question type is yours.
