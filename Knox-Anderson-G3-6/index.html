Fix 1: Two Details, Each with Its So What

Your paragraph has one. The passage had three waiting
THE RULE: AT LEAST TWO SUPPORTING DETAILS
After the TOPIC SENTENCE, at least two supporting details. Each one is a SHOW, a fact from the passage, followed by a SO WHAT: This shows… what the fact proves about the question. Then a CONCLUDING SENTENCE. The check: count your facts before you write the concluding sentence. One fact is half a paragraph.

Here is your paragraph, labelled:

TOPIC SENTENCEThe porcupines body helps it stay safe in the wild by have a tail full of quills that they can poke other animals if they get to close. SO WHAT, ALMOSTthis is a really good trait that they have becuase not a lot of animals have good traits to keep them safe.

A topic sentence that answers the question and holds your one fact: the quills in the tail. Then a sentence that starts to say why it matters. That is a 14. The second detail never arrives, and the passage had two more ready: the warning sound and lifted quills, and the claws that grip branches so a porcupine can climb out of reach.

Fix 2: Stay on the Porcupine

Q6, Q7, Q9: a point each for the skunks and the birds

A one-sentence answer about porcupines is about porcupines from the first word to the period. The check: if part of your answer starts with just like, it is about another animal. Cut it. Put a porcupine fact from the passage in its place.

Three answers, each with a good porcupine reason and then a trip to another animal:

YOU WROTE (Q6, 2 OUT OF 3)

STAYING ON THE PORCUPINE
A porcupine lifts its quills when it feels scared because the raised quills and the warning sound tell other animals to stay away before anyone gets hurt.
YOU WROTE (Q7, 2 OUT OF 3)
“Porcupines spend time in trees becuase they have strong claws and rough paw pads to hold on to branches so they can eat food such as leaves and bark. just like birds, birds have strong claws to grab on to braches.”
STAYING ON THE PORCUPINE
Porcupines spend so much time in trees because their strong claws and rough paw pads let them hold on tightly while they eat leaves and bark.
YOU WROTE (Q9, 2 OUT OF 3)

STAYING ON THE PORCUPINE
