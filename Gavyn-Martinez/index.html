From Retelling to Arguing — Add a Thesis

This is the most important change you can make

Right now your essay reads like a chronological retelling — first this happened, then this happened, finally this happened. The rubric wants an organized argument: teamwork helped in these 3 specific ways.

RETELLING (what you did)
First they mapped the river. Then they cleaned it. Later they planted plants. After that, it was cleaner.
ARGUING (what the rubric wants)
Teamwork helped restore the Mithi River through organized planning, community participation, and environmental restoration.

The problem: Without a thesis, the reader doesn't know WHERE the essay is going. Each body paragraph should prove one POINT, not just describe the next EVENT.

WHY THE THESIS MATTERS
The thesis is your essay's roadmap. It tells the reader: here are the 3 things I'm going to prove. Then each body paragraph proves one of them. Without it, the essay reads like a story instead of an argument.

Get the Evidence Right

Accuracy and explanation earn the most points

Two issues with how you use evidence:

1. Wrong number: You wrote "24 tonnes" but the passage says "fourteen tonnes." When you cite a specific number, it MUST match the passage exactly. Getting it wrong is worse than not including a number at all, because it shows the reader you're guessing instead of reading carefully.

"24 tonnes" → "fourteen tonnes"

2. Evidence without explanation: You state what happened but don't explain WHY it matters:

What you wrote What to add (explanation)
"The first step in solving the problem was finding out which part of the river was the most polluted" "This careful planning was important because it allowed Earth5R to focus their limited resources where they would make the biggest difference."
"families, local shops, and even schools joined it" "This widespread participation showed that the entire community, not just one group, was committed to restoring the river."
THE PATTERN
After every piece of evidence, add: "This showed that..." or "This was important because..." or "As a result..." Evidence without explanation doesn't earn full points.

Build a Full Body Paragraph

From 3 sentences to 6 with evidence, explanation, and transitions

Your Paragraph 3 has 3 sentences and a grammar error. Here it is rewritten as a developed TS-E1-X1-E2-X2-CS paragraph:

YOUR VERSION (3 sentences, with grammar error)
Later, as the project became more popular, families, local shops, and even schools joined it. After a couple of months, the project ended up removing 24 tonnes of trash from the river. Earth5R then worked with scientists collectively decided that it would be best if they planted plants along the banks of the river.

Grammar fix: "worked with scientists collectively decided" → "worked with scientists and collectively decided" (missing conjunction)

DEVELOPED VERSION (6 sentences)

TS: As the project grew, people from all parts of the community came together to help restore the river.

E1: According to the passage, families, local shops, and even schools joined the cleanup effort as it became more popular.

X1: This widespread participation showed that restoring the river was not just one group's responsibility; it was something the entire community cared about.

E2: The passage also states that volunteers helped remove fourteen tonnes of trash from the riverbanks.

X2: As a result, the river's pollution was dramatically reduced because so many people contributed their time and effort.

CS: Because of this community-wide commitment, the cleanup achieved far more than any single group could have accomplished alone.

WHAT CHANGED (HIGHLIGHTED)
Semicolon in X1: joins two related ideas into one compound sentence — "not just one group's responsibility; it was something the entire community cared about."

"As a result" transition: shows cause-and-effect, not just sequence. Stronger than "Then" or "After that."

"Because of this" in CS: connects the concluding sentence back to the evidence, showing why community participation mattered.
