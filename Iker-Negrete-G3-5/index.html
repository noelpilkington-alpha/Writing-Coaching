Attempt the Paragraph (Q11)

Q11 — 0 out of 20 — not attempted

Q11 is worth 20 points — almost half the entire test. Right now it is blank. That is your single biggest opportunity to raise your score.

You do not need a perfect paragraph. Even writing 3-4 sentences about the passage earns points. Here is a simple formula:

The 5-Sentence Paragraph (Detail + Explanation)
1 Topic sentence: Say what the passage is mainly about.
2 Detail 1: One specific fact from the passage.
3 Explain: Why does that fact matter? What does it show?
4 Detail 2: A second specific fact from the passage.
5 Explain: Why does that fact matter?
6 Closing: Wrap up by connecting back to your topic sentence.

For example, if the passage was about "Little Golden Books":

EXAMPLE PARAGRAPH (DETAIL + EXPLANATION)
Little Golden Books became very popular in the United States. [Detail] They had golden spines that made them easy to spot on any store shelf. [Explain] This meant that even young children could find and pick out their own books without help. [Detail] The books only cost 25 cents, much less than other books at the time. [Explain] Because they were so cheap, families who could not afford expensive books could finally buy stories for their children. Little Golden Books changed reading for kids everywhere.
YOUR GOAL FOR NEXT TIME
When you get to Q11, write at least 5 sentences using the Detail + Explanation pattern. After every fact you write, ask yourself: "So what? Why does this matter?" Then write that answer as your next sentence. That two-sentence pair (detail + explanation) is what earns the highest points.

Change to a REAL Question

Q1 — 1 out of 2

The question asked you to turn a statement into a question. You restated the idea in present tense, but you did not flip it into question form:

YOUR ANSWER (Q1)
"The books are sold at the department stores."

This is still a statement. It tells something — it does not ask something. To make a real question, you need to move the helping word (is, are, was, were, do, does) to the front:

Tap to see the fixed version
Read the explanation above first
FIXED
"Are the books sold at the department stores?"

Notice: "Are" moved to the front, and the period became a question mark.

THE FLIP RULE
To turn a statement into a question:
1. Find the helping word (is, are, was, were, do, does, can, will).
2. Move it to the front of the sentence.
3. Change the period to a question mark.

Statement: The books are sold at stores.
Question: Are the books sold at stores?

Write Complete Sentences

Q6-Q10 — many blanks and fragments

On questions 6 through 10, you earned very few points because the answers were blank or incomplete. These questions are worth 3 points each — that is 15 points total. Even partial answers earn something.

A complete sentence needs three things:

Complete Sentence Formula
WHO A subject (who or what the sentence is about)
DID A verb (what they did or what happened)
WHY A reason or detail (because...)

Even if you are not sure of the "right" answer, writing a full sentence gets you partial credit. For example:

EXAMPLE COMPLETE ANSWER
"The museum is underwater because the city flooded a long time ago and the buildings sank."

Compare that to leaving it blank (0 points). A complete sentence with a reason can earn 1-2 out of 3 even if the details are not perfect.

WHEN YOU ARE STUCK
If you do not know the answer, use this starter:

"I think [subject] [verb] because [reason from the passage]."

It is better to write a guess in a full sentence than to leave a question blank.

Detail + Explanation

For Q11 when you attempt it next time

Once you start writing your paragraph on Q11, here is how to earn more points. Each detail from the passage needs an explanation — a sentence that says why it matters.

Detail + Explanation Pattern
D Detail: A specific fact from the passage.
E Explanation: Why that detail matters or what it shows.

For example, using the "Dive into a Sunken Museum" passage:

DETAIL
"The museum has over 500 statues sitting on the ocean floor."
EXPLANATION (WHY IT MATTERS)
"This is interesting because people have to scuba dive just to see the art, which makes it different from any other museum."

After you write a fact, ask yourself: "So what? Why does this matter?" Then write that answer as your next sentence.

BUILDING UP
