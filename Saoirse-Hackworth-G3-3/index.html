Q5: Subordinating Conjunctions

Words like "because," "since," "although," "when," "after," "before"

On Q5, you were asked to combine two sentences using a subordinating conjunction. You wrote:

YOUR ANSWER
"At the beach there was no sing of the seals, the family placed Sally in a fish box and motored into the bay."

"At the beach" is a phrase that tells where something happened, not a conjunction that shows a relationship between two ideas (like cause, time, or contrast). The question needed a word like because, since, although, when, after, or before.

COMMON SUBORDINATING CONJUNCTIONS
Cause: because, since
Contrast: although, even though
Time: when, after, before, while, until
Condition: if, unless

These words show how two ideas connect. They go at the start of one idea to link it to another.
CORRECT EXAMPLES
"Because there was no sign of the seals, the family placed Sally in a fish box and motored into the bay."

"Since there was no sign of the seals, the family placed Sally in a fish box and motored into the bay."

"After there was no sign of the seals, the family placed Sally in a fish box and motored into the bay."
Which sentence correctly uses a subordinating conjunction?
A At the beach the family placed Sally in a fish box.
B Because there was no sign of the seals, the family placed Sally in a fish box and motored into the bay.
HOW TO SPOT THE DIFFERENCE
Ask yourself: "Does this word show WHY, WHEN, or ALTHOUGH?"

✗ "At the beach" → tells WHERE (not a conjunction)
✗ "In the morning" → tells WHEN but is a phrase, not a conjunction
✓ "Because" → shows WHY (conjunction!)
✓ "After" → shows WHEN as a linking word (conjunction!)
✓ "Although" → shows a contrast (conjunction!)
