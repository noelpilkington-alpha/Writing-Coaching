Conjunctions & Transition Words

Q3 and Q8 — understanding how ideas connect

Q3 — Combining Sentences:

The two sentences
"Animated movies are fun. They also include valuable lessons."

You chose because, but the correct answer was but.

Why? The word "also" signals that the second idea is surprising or added — fun movies also teach lessons. That's a contrast: "Animated movies are fun but they also include valuable lessons." The word "because" would mean lessons are the reason they're fun — that's a different relationship.

CONJUNCTION CHEAT SHEET
"because" = cause → effect (A causes B)
"but" = contrast/surprise (A is true, yet B is also true)
"so" = result (A happened, therefore B)
"and" = addition (A plus B)

Q8 — Transition Words:

You chose Then, but the correct answer was As a result.

The sentence described a cause-and-effect relationship — something happened, and then something else happened because of it. "Then" is a sequence word (first this, then that). "As a result" shows that the second thing happened because of the first thing.

Tap to see the transition guide
Read the explanation above first
TRANSITION WORD GUIDE
Sequence: First, Next, Then, Finally
Cause → Effect: As a result, Therefore, Consequently, Because of this
Contrast: However, On the other hand, Although
Addition: In addition, Furthermore, Also
THE TRICK
Before picking a transition word, ask: "What is the relationship between these two ideas?" If Idea B happened because of Idea A, use a cause-effect word. If Idea B just comes after Idea A in time, use a sequence word. Matching the transition to the relationship is key.

The Body Paragraph Pattern

How to build a strong body paragraph every time

Every body paragraph in a G6+ essay should follow this pattern:

Body Paragraph Formula
TS Topic Sentence — states the paragraph's main point
E1 Evidence 1 — a specific fact, quote, or example from the passage
X1 Explanation 1 — explain why that evidence matters
E2 Evidence 2 — a second fact or example from the passage
X2 Explanation 2 — explain why that evidence matters
CS Concluding Sentence — wraps up the paragraph's main idea

Here's what your bravery paragraph would look like with this pattern, using passage evidence:

EXAMPLE BODY PARAGRAPH
[TS] One important life lesson animated movies teach is bravery. [E1] According to the passage, [specific character] faces a frightening situation when [specific scene from passage]. [X1] This shows children that being brave does not mean having no fear — it means doing the right thing even when you are scared. [E2] The passage also explains that [second specific detail from passage]. [X2] This matters because it gives young viewers a real example of courage they can look up to and learn from. [CS] Through scenes like these, animated movies show children what bravery looks like in action.

G6 Essay Plan

BP1 Bravery — E1: [passage detail] → X1: why it matters → E2: [passage detail] → X2: what it shows → CS
BP2 Kindness — E1: [passage detail] → X1: why it matters → E2: [passage detail] → X2: what it shows → CS

BP = Body Paragraph • E = Evidence • X = Explanation • TS = Topic Sentence • CS = Closing Sentence

THE KEY DIFFERENCE
Your current body paragraphs define each word (bravery is when..., kindness is..., honesty is when...) and then give a made-up example. Instead, skip the dictionary definition — show the lesson through specific evidence from the passage, then explain why that evidence matters. Evidence + Explanation is always stronger than Definition + Made-Up Example.
