THE DETAIL + EXPLANATION FORMULA
Every time a question asks "what does this show" or "why is this important," your answer needs two parts:

1. The Detail: Name the specific thing from the passage (the action, the moment, the event).
2. The Explanation: Say what it shows, what it tells us, or why it matters.

If your answer only has Part 1, you will usually earn 2 out of 3 instead of 3 out of 3.

THE "HOW" QUESTION PATTERN
When a question asks "How did X help Y?" your answer needs:

1. Name the specific thing X did (the detail from the passage).
2. Explain how that specific thing led to Y (the connection).

"Nature is beautiful" does not explain how it helped. "When Layla saw the butterfly, she realized she should be patient" does explain how nature helped.
The question asks: "How did nature help Layla make her decision?" Which answer explains HOW?

Your #2 Error: Spelling and Comma Accuracy

Q2, Q8, Q9, Q11 — small errors that cost points

James, several of your answers have small mechanical errors that cost you partial credit. These are not about your ideas. They are about catching mistakes before you submit.

Q2 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2)
"Since, Layla loved nature, she decided to explore the trail."

The fix: There should be no comma after "Since." When "Since" starts a sentence, the comma goes at the end of the "Since" clause, not right after the word. Correct: "Since Layla loved nature, she decided to explore the trail."

PROOFREAD PASS: THREE THINGS TO CHECK
Before you submit any answer, read it back slowly and ask:

1. Does the spelling look right? If a word looks strange, try a different way. "Goede" looks off. "Geode" looks right. "Beutiful" looks off. "Beautiful" looks right.

2. Commas after "Since" or "Because"? These words do NOT get a comma right after them. The comma goes at the end of the whole clause they start.

3. Read it out loud in your head. If something sounds choppy or odd, there is probably a small error.
