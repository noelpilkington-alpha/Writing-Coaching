THE 3-STEP RULE: CLOSE, RESTATE, ADD
1. Close the passage. Literally look away from it. You cannot paraphrase while your eyes are on the original sentence — the words will pull you in.

2. Restate the idea in your own words. Say it out loud to yourself first, then write it. Change the sentence structure, not just one or two words.

3. Add one sentence of your own thinking. After the restated fact, write a sentence that explains what it means or why it matters. That sentence cannot come from the passage — it has to come from you.

The only thing keeping this from being a strong essay is that the words are not yours. When you restate and add your own thinking, the same content with your voice is worth a lot more points — and the essay becomes worth reading.

Your One MC Miss: Imperative vs. Declarative

Q2 — the only multiple-choice item you missed on G6.7

Jack, you got 9 out of 10 on the multiple choice — strong. The one miss was Q2, a sentence-type question. Let us lock this one in.

THE QUESTION YOU MISSED
"What type of sentence is this from the passage: 'Make sure your bedroom is cool, dark, and quiet.'"

You picked: Declarative
Correct answer: Imperative
THE 4 SENTENCE TYPES — HOW TO TELL THEM APART
Imperative → gives a command or instruction. Often starts with a verb. Ends with a period or exclamation point.
  "Make sure your bedroom is cool, dark, and quiet." — telling you to do something.

Declarative → states a fact or idea. Ends with a period.
  "Your bedroom is cool, dark, and quiet." — just telling you how it is.

Interrogative → asks a question. Ends with a question mark.
  "Is your bedroom cool, dark, and quiet?"

Exclamatory → shows strong feeling. Ends with an exclamation point.
  "Your bedroom is so cool, dark, and quiet!"
💡 The quick test: If the sentence is telling someone to do something — "Make sure," "Try," "Remember," "Go," "Don't forget" — it is imperative. If it is just stating how things are, it is declarative.
Which sentence is imperative?
A The dog is happy when it sees its owner.
B Take the dog for a walk before dinner.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Your MC was 9/10 on G6.7 — one single question between you and a perfect 10. Sentence-type questions show up on almost every test, and they are easy points once the pattern clicks. Command = imperative. Statement = declarative. Question = interrogative. Strong feeling = exclamatory. Get this one and the MC ceiling is 10/10.
