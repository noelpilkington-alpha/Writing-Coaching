THE RULE
A topic sentence has one job: tell the reader the paragraph's specific point. A vague generalization ("animals affect people") could open almost any paragraph, so it opens none of them well. Hold on to this — it is the exact skill in the next card.

Make the Topic Sentence Specific

Q6 — the one point that got away, two tests running

Asher, the only question you missed was Q6, and it is worth a close look because it is your one repeat pattern — you missed the topic-sentence question on G6.5 too. The stem asked:

Q6 — THE QUESTION
"Which sentence would best begin a paragraph about how therapy dogs support students' emotional health?"
YOU CHOSE
"Being around animals can make people feel different emotions."
CORRECT ANSWER
"Therapy dogs help students feel calmer by reducing stress and worry."

Why your pick did not fit: the paragraph is about emotional health support — how dogs help students feel calmer. Your choice was a vague generalization ("different emotions") that could open almost any paragraph. The correct one names the specific benefit: calmer, less stress and worry. That is what a topic sentence has to do.
