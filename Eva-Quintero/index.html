READ THE QUESTION TWICE BEFORE YOU WRITE
When the question says "use because," you MUST use the word "because" in your answer. Circle the connecting word in the question so you don't forget. Think of it like a recipe — if it says "add butter," you can't add oil instead!

How to pick the right word:

"because" = gives a REASON (why something happened)
"but" = shows a DIFFERENCE (something unexpected)
"so" = shows a RESULT (what happened next)

Plan Before You Write

A quick plan helps you write a longer, stronger paragraph

Your paragraph had one good idea (kids can read books), but a plan helps you find two more ideas so you have enough to write about. Here's what a plan for the Camel Mobile Library prompt could look like:

Paragraph Plan — "Explain how the Camel Mobile Library has affected the people of Garissa"
TS = Topic Sentence  •  D = Detail from the passage  •  W = Why it matters  •  CS = Concluding Sentence
TS The Camel Mobile Library has helped the people of Garissa in many important ways.
D1 Camels carry books to people who live far from schools or libraries.
W This matters because before the library came, many children in Garissa had no books to read at all.
D2 Children can now learn to read and do better in school.
W This is important because reading helps kids learn about the world and do well in their classes.
D3 The library brings the community together — people share books and learn from each other.
W This helps because it gives people a reason to come together and support each other's learning.
CS Because of the Camel Mobile Library, the people of Garissa have more books, better learning, and a stronger community.
THE TOPIC SENTENCE TRICK
Read the prompt, then turn it into your first sentence:

Prompt: "Explain how the Camel Mobile Library has affected the people of Garissa."
Topic sentence: "The Camel Mobile Library has affected the people of Garissa in many important ways."

See? You just flip the prompt into a statement. Then your details come after.
