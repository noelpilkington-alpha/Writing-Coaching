Fix 1: Each Fact, Then What It Shows

Five points on G4.2, six on G4.1. The grader asked for the same thing both times
THE RULE: A FACT FROM THE TEXT, THEN WHAT IT SHOWS
A list of true facts earns the middle score. A fact followed by what it shows earns the top one. After each fact, add the part that answers the question: so the workers finish faster, because the frame takes the weight, which means people have more time. Your Grade 4 lesson paragraphs that scored 17 and 18 did this on every fact. The check: read each fact and try to add so or because after it. If nothing follows, the fact is still waiting to be explained. Pick fewer facts and explain each one. Three explained facts beat five listed ones.

Fix 4: Read Back, Then Submit

Two points on G4.2 went to comma splices, and the spelling slips on Q5 came along. A read-back catches all of it
THE RULE: READ EVERY ANSWER BACK ONCE, LOOKING FOR FOUR THINGS
One. Two complete thoughts need a period between them or a joining word, never just a comma. Two. Its means belonging to it. It’s means it is. Their means belonging to them. They’re means they are. Your means belonging to you. You’re means you are. Three. Sight is what you see. A site is a place. And the words you type fast: surprise, crowd, trapped. Four. When an item asks for an appositive, the phrase after the comma is another name for the thing, not who or there too. You had 33 minutes and used them well. One minute of read-back is three points.
YOU WROTE (G4.2, Q6 AND Q8)
“In spring time I like playing with my family in my backyard and enjoying my time watching the flowers bloom, it’s a beautiful site.” “I once saw a plant, it was bright yellow with a green stem and green leaves, growing on the root of a tree.” TWO THOUGHTS, ONE COMMA
Both answers had the idea and the detail. Each lost one point for the comma holding two complete thoughts together. A period, or because, or and, keeps the point.
