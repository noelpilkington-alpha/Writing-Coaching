Write an Appositive

Q4 — a "where" clause is not an appositive

Julian, on Q4 the question asked you to write a sentence that includes an appositive. An appositive is a naming phrase that sits right next to a noun and renames or describes it, set off by commas. Here is what you wrote:

Q4 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2 weighted points)
"Many people attend bluebonnet festivals, where they buy many things with bluebonnets on them, in small towns all over Te..."

The problem: "Where they buy many things with bluebonnets on them" is a "where" clause. It describes a place, not a noun. An appositive must rename the noun next to it.

WHAT AN APPOSITIVE LOOKS LIKE
An appositive is a naming phrase that renames the noun beside it.

Formula: Noun, naming phrase, rest of sentence.

Example: "Bluebonnet festivals, colorful celebrations held every spring, attract visitors from across Texas."

The naming phrase "colorful celebrations held every spring" renames "bluebonnet festivals." That is what makes it an appositive.
A STRONGER ANSWER USING AN APPOSITIVE
"Many people attend bluebonnet festivals, popular spring events in small Texas towns, where they buy souvenirs decorated with the state flower."

Notice the appositive "popular spring events in small Texas towns" renames "bluebonnet festivals." It is a naming phrase, not a "where" clause.

Which sentence contains a real appositive?
A The park, where families gather on weekends, has a large lake.
B The park, a 200-acre nature reserve near downtown, has a large lake.
QUICK TEST FOR APPOSITIVES
Ask yourself: does the phrase between the commas rename the noun, or does it describe an action/place?

• If it renames the noun (you could swap them), it is an appositive.
• If it starts with "where," "which," or "who" and describes an action, it is a clause, not an appositive.

Explain WHY

Q10 — naming a tradition is not enough

Julian, on Q10 you were asked to describe a fun tradition and explain what makes it special. You named the tradition, but you did not explain why it is fun or what makes it special:

Q10 — YOUR ANSWER (2 out of 3 weighted points)
"One fun tradition is my family hosting Christmas every year."

The problem: You told us what the tradition is (hosting Christmas), but you never explained why it is fun or what makes it special. The question asks for both.

THE "NAME IT + EXPLAIN IT" PATTERN
When a question asks you to describe something AND explain why, your answer needs two parts:

Part 1: Name the thing (the tradition, the event, the moment).
Part 2: Explain what makes it special or why it matters.

Without Part 2, you are only answering half the question.
A STRONGER ANSWER
"One fun tradition is my family hosting Christmas every year. It is special because all my cousins come from different cities, and we play games together that we only play on that one night. It is the only time the whole family is in one place."

The second and third sentences explain why it is fun and what makes it special. That is what earns full points.

Which answer fully explains WHY the tradition is special?
A A fun tradition is going to the beach every summer. It is really fun and my whole family goes.
B A fun tradition is going to the beach every summer. It is special because my grandpa teaches us how to build sandcastles, and it is the only time we get to see him all year.
WHY THIS MATTERS
Q10 is worth 3 weighted points. You earned 2 out of 3. The missing point was the explanation. Any time a question says "describe" AND "explain," make sure your answer has both: the thing itself AND the reason it matters.

Detail + Explanation

Q11 at 16 out of 20 — develop your ideas further

Julian, your paragraph on Q11 earned 16 out of 20. The ideas are there and the structure works. The points you lost came from two things: a subject-verb agreement error and not developing your ideas enough.

Q11 — FROM YOUR PARAGRAPH (16 out of 20 weighted points)

For every point you make in your paragraph, add:

2. An explanation sentence ("This shows..." or "This means...")

Two developed points with explanation will always score higher than four quick points without it.
