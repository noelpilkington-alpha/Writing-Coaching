Error #1: Answer the Whole "Explain" Prompt

You wrote 4 explain sentences. All 4 got partial credit. Here is why.

Jacob, this is the single biggest pattern across your G3.7 test. Four questions (Q6, Q7, Q9, Q10) asked you to explain a reason or include an example. You wrote a real sentence for every one. But every one earned 0.67 instead of 1.0. That is 1.3 points left on the table.

Look at what the prompt asked for vs. what you wrote:

Q6 (0.67/1) — Explain WHY pumpkin seeds need warm soil to sprout
You wrote: "They need warm soil to sprout because it is moist and not dry."
The "because" is there, but "moist and not dry" is not the reason from the passage. Warm soil helps the seed start growing — it wakes the seed up.
Q9 (0.67/1) — Describe what people use pumpkins for. Include an example.
You wrote: "They can use the pumpkin seeds after they bake them to eat."
That IS an example (baked seeds). But the prompt says "what people use pumpkins for" — plural uses. An example answer names the use ("a snack") AND one more use ("decoration" or "pie filling").

The pattern is the same on Q7 and Q10. Your sentence is solid, but it covers one piece of what the prompt asks when the prompt actually asks for two: a claim + a specific reason from the passage, or a use + an example.

Q10 asked: "Explain why someone might save pumpkin seeds to plant in spring." Which answer earns full credit?
A Someone might save pumpkin seeds to plant in spring to get more pumpkins.
B Someone might save pumpkin seeds to plant in spring because pumpkin seeds need warm soil to sprout, and soil warms up in the spring.
THE "UNDERLINE TWO THINGS" TRICK
When the prompt says "Explain why" or "Include an example," underline two jobs before you write:

• Job 1: the claim (what you are saying)
• Job 2: the reason from the passage OR the specific example

Your sentence needs to hit both jobs. Use "because," "so that," or "for example" to connect them.

THE "WHISPER-READ" PROOFREAD
After you finish a sentence, whisper it in your head. If a word sounds strange or looks strange, it probably is. Try it three ways and pick the one that looks most like a book word:

• sence → sense (like "it makes sense")
• responcibility → responsibility (ends in "-sibility")
• drcorate → decorate (starts with "de")
• beaked → baked (vowel is "a," not "ea")
