Appositives: Rename the Noun

Question 3 — you joined with "so" instead

Ben, Question 3 asked you to combine the sentences using an appositive — a phrase that renames a noun, set off with commas. Here is what you wrote:

YOU WROTE (G3.6 Q3, scored 1 out of 2)
"Porcupines are rodents, so they are the slow movers of the forest."

You joined the two ideas with "so," which is a connecting word, not an appositive. An appositive does not connect two sentences — it tucks a renaming phrase right next to the noun, between two commas.

THE APPOSITIVE VERSION
"Porcupines, the slow movers of the forest, are rodents."

See how "the slow movers of the forest" sits right after "Porcupines" and renames it, with a comma on each side? That is an appositive.

Which sentence uses an appositive correctly?
A Porcupines are rodents, so they are the slow movers of the forest.
B Porcupines, the slow movers of the forest, are rodents.
HOW TO SPOT AN APPOSITIVE
Ask: "Is there a phrase that renames the noun, set off by two commas?"

✗ "Bats are mammals, so they can fly." (this uses "so" to connect — not an appositive)
✓ "Bats, the only flying mammals, sleep upside down." (the phrase renames "Bats")
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
When a question names a specific tool — appositive, question, combining with "because" — you have to use that exact tool. You used "so" here, so the answer earned 1 of 2. Renaming the noun with commas earns the full 2.

MODEL FOCUSED PARAGRAPH (19–20/20 level)

The porcupine's body helps it stay safe in several ways. It has long, stiff quills, and if a predator ignores the warning of its tail moving up and down, the predator gets a face full of spines. The porcupine also has sharp claws that let it climb trees, so it can escape danger by going where many animals cannot follow. Together, the porcupine's quills, tail warning, and climbing claws keep it safe from predators.

B Its sharp claws let it climb trees to escape from predators.
