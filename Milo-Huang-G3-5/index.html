THE RULE: SUBJECT, VERB, THEN BECAUSE
Every Q6 to Q10 answer is one whole sentence: it names who or what (a child, the golden spines), says what they do or feel (might feel, helped), and then gives the reason. The check: cover the question and read your answer alone. If it only makes sense with the question showing, it is not a sentence yet. Start with the answer to the question, in words that could stand on their own.
YOU WROTE (Q7, 1 OUT OF 3)

Cover the question. This is a list of feelings with nobody feeling them and nothing happening. The ideas are good, and the passage backs them: sentence 14 says the name page made the books feel special and personal.

Fix 2: An Appositive Renames with a Noun

Q4 described the books. On G3.3, a who-clause. Neither renames
THE RULE: A NOUN BETWEEN THE COMMAS
An appositive is a noun phrase that renames the noun right before it, set off by commas: The Poky Little Puppy, a best-selling story, became… The check: cover everything outside the commas and read what is left. Does it start with a noun that could stand in for the thing it follows? a series, a story, an artist. If it starts with who or which, or with a verb form like known, sold, covered, it describes but does not rename. Close, and one point.

You know the shape. In Q5 you picked the appositive out of a sentence for full marks:

YOURS, Q5, 2 OUT OF 2
The Poky Little Puppy, APPOSITIVEa best-selling story, became the most popular picture book of all time.
Between the commas: a best-selling story. Starts with a noun. Renames the Puppy.

Fix 3: Do the Job the Instruction Names

THE RULE: THE JOB WORD AND THE GIVEN WORDS
Q1 to Q5 each name a job and hand you the materials. Unscramble means every word once, nothing added, nothing left over, and the sentence the words came from. Combine using since means since, not so or and. The check: after you write, find the job word in the instruction, then tick each given word off in your answer. A better sentence that does a different job is one point.
YOU WROTE (Q2, 1 OUT OF 2)
“The very popular puppy was a little poky.” EVERY WORD USED, DIFFERENT SENTENCE
Seven words, seven used, capital and period in place. But little and poky are part of the puppy’s name, from sentence 9, and here a little became an amount. The words unscramble to one sentence about how popular the puppy was.
THE SENTENCE THE WORDS CAME FROM
The poky little puppy was very popular.
YOU WROTE (G3.4 Q2, 1 OUT OF 2)
“Layla saw a butterfly, so she took out her camera.” SO, NOT SINCE
The instruction said using the subordinating conjunction: since. Correct comma, right meaning, wrong word.
WITH SINCE
Since Layla saw a butterfly, she took out her camera.
