The Pattern — Cause vs Effect

Q3, Q5, Q7 all show the same error
🔎 Pattern alert: On all 3 wrong answers, you picked the thing that made something possible instead of the result that actually happened. You are reading accurately — you just grab the wrong side of the cause-effect chain.

Q3: The question asked about the order of events after the solar panels arrived.

YOU CHOSE
"Children could study in the evenings, and this allowed them to see better."
CORRECT ANSWER
"Because they could see better, children could study in the evenings."
WHY YOUR ANSWER WAS WRONG
Your answer says studying caused better sight. That is backwards. The panels gave light (seeing better), and then children could study. Seeing better is the cause; studying in the evenings is the effect. You picked the version with the logic flipped.
Which sentence has the correct logical order?
A "Children could study in the evenings, and this allowed them to see better."
B "Because they could see better, children could study in the evenings."
THE FIX: ASK "WHICH ONE IS THE RESULT OF THE OTHER?"
Every time two things are connected, one makes the other happen. Before you pick an answer, ask yourself:

• "Did A cause B, or did B cause A?"
• The answer that describes the result (the thing that happened second) is usually what the question is asking for.

On Q3: Light → seeing better → studying. Studying is the end result. Your answer reversed that chain.

Question 5 Fix — Conjunction Logic

"Because" vs "although" — reason vs contrast

You scored Q5 — 0 out of 1 on this question.

The sentence was: "The workshops helped residents feel confident ___ of their new skills."

YOU CHOSE
"although"
CORRECT ANSWER
"because"
WHY "ALTHOUGH" DOES NOT FIT
"Although" signals contrast — it means "even though / despite." But the sentence is saying the skills are the reason for the confidence. There is no contrast here. The skills did not work against the confidence; they caused it. So the connector must be "because."
Fill the blank: "The villagers trusted the new system ___ it had worked reliably for six months."
A although
B because
QUICK RULE: BECAUSE = REASON, ALTHOUGH = CONTRAST
Because → "the reason is..." (cause leads to effect)
Although → "even though..." (something unexpected, despite something else)

Test: substitute "the reason is" for the blank. If the sentence still makes sense, the answer is "because." If it sounds wrong, try "although."

• "Residents felt confident the reason is their new skills." — makes sense. Answer: because.
• "Residents felt confident even though their new skills." — implies the skills should have made them doubt themselves. That is the opposite of the meaning.
HOW THIS CONNECTS TO THE CAUSE-EFFECT PATTERN
This is the same underlying error as Q3 and Q7. You saw two ideas connected and picked the word that implies contrast ("although") when the sentence was actually describing a cause-and-effect relationship. Once you train yourself to ask "is this a reason or a contrast?" you fix all three question types at once.

Question 7 Fix — Topic Sentence Matching

The topic says "benefits" — pick the benefit, not the cause

You scored Q7 — 0 out of 1 on this question.

The topic was: "Benefits for local businesses."

YOU CHOSE
"Businesses could stay open for longer."
CORRECT ANSWER
"The extra hours gave customers more time to shop."
WHY YOUR ANSWER WAS WRONG
"Staying open for longer" is the cause — it is what the businesses did. The benefit is what they got from doing it: customers had more time to shop (more revenue). The topic says "benefits," so the answer must be the result of staying open, not the act of staying open itself.
The topic is "Benefits of the solar panels for families." Which is the benefit?
A "Families installed solar panels on their roofs."
B "Families saved money on electricity each month."
THE FIX: READ THE TOPIC WORD AND MATCH IT
The topic said "benefits." That word tells you the answer must describe something gained or received — a positive result.

• "Businesses could stay open for longer" = what businesses DID (action/cause)
• "Customers had more time to shop" = what businesses GOT (benefit/effect)

Before you pick, ask: "Is this what they did, or what they got from doing it?" The topic says "benefits," so pick what they got.
SAME PATTERN, THREE QUESTIONS
Q3: picked the cause (studying) instead of the effect (seeing better enabled studying)
Q5: picked the contrast word instead of the cause word
Q7: picked the cause (staying open) instead of the benefit (customers shopping)

All three times you grabbed the enabling factor instead of the result. One fix — always ask "which is the result?" — solves all three.

WHERE TO PUSH FOR 18+/20: DEEPER DETAIL + EXPLANATION
To move from 16 to 18 or higher, add a second layer to your body paragraphs. Right now you give a detail (what happened) but sometimes skip the explanation (why it mattered or how it connects to your thesis).

Detail: "The panels powered lights so children could study at night."
Explanation: "This meant they no longer fell behind students in towns with electricity, closing the education gap between rural and urban areas."

The detail tells what. The explanation tells so what. Adding even one explanation sentence per body paragraph can push you over the 18-point line.
