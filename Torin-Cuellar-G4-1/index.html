Fix 2: Because, But, or So as the Joining Word

Q5: a comma cannot hold two sentences, and because of is not a joining word
THE RULE: ONE JOINING WORD BETWEEN TWO WHOLE SENTENCES
When the instruction says because, but, or so, the joining word sits between the two sentences and both stay whole. The check: cover the joining word. If both sides could stand alone as sentences, the join is right. If you find only a comma between two whole sentences, the comma is doing the joining word’s job, and it cannot.

Q5 asked you to combine two sentences with because, but or so:

YOU WROTE (1 OUT OF 2)
“His dedication has transformed the school because of his efforts, the enrollment has doubled.”
Cover because of his efforts. What is left is two whole sentences held together by a comma. Because of is a phrase about his efforts, not the joining word between the two ideas.
WITH THE JOINING WORD
His dedication has transformed the schoolJOINING WORD, so enrollment has doubled and more students are now attending college.
