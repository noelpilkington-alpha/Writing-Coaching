Know Your Sentence Types

Q3 — 0 out of 2 — Interrogative means "question"
🔍
On Q3, the prompt asked you to rewrite as an interrogative. You wrote a command instead of a question. The issue: you didn't know that "interrogative" = question.

Ansel, here's what happened. The original sentence was:

"Give me this geode, it feels like a perfect souvenir."

The prompt said: "Rewrite this sentence as an interrogative."

WHAT YOU WROTE (Q3 — 0 out of 2)
"Give me this geode, it feels like a perfect souvenir."
(You kept it as an imperative — a command. The prompt wanted a question ending with a question mark.)
The 4 Sentence Types — Memorize These
. Declarative = a statement. Ends with a period.
The geode feels like a perfect souvenir.
? Interrogative = a question. Ends with a question mark.
Could you give me this geode?
! Exclamatory = strong feeling. Ends with an exclamation point.
This geode is such a perfect souvenir!
cmd Imperative = a command or request. Usually ends with a period.
Give me this geode.
THE KEY WORD
Interrogative = question. Any time you see "interrogative" in a prompt, your answer must end with a ? and ask something. Think: "interrogate" means to ask questions — like a detective interrogating a witness.
The prompt says: "Rewrite as an interrogative: Give me this geode, it feels like a perfect souvenir." Which answer is correct?
A Give me this geode, it feels like a perfect souvenir.
B Could you give me this geode, since it feels like a perfect souvenir?
FULL-CREDIT VERSIONS OF Q3
• "Could you give me this geode, since it feels like a perfect souvenir?"
• "Doesn't this geode feel like a perfect souvenir?"
• "Can I have this geode because it feels like a perfect souvenir?"
YOUR MEMORY TRICK
Inter-ROGATE = ask questions. Picture a detective asking questions. Every time you see "interrogative" in a prompt, your sentence must end with a ? and must ask something. No command words like "give me" at the start.

Fragment vs. Complete Sentence
Fragment "A broken rock, with lots of sparkling crystals inside!" — No verb. Who is doing what? Nothing happens.
Complete "A broken rock revealed lots of sparkling crystals inside!" — Now there's a verb. Something happens.
THE FRAGMENT TEST
Read your sentence and ask: "Who or what is DOING something?" If you can't find an action word (verb), it's still a fragment. Adding punctuation (! or . or ?) doesn't make it a complete sentence. You need a verb.

Detail + Explanation

Question 11 — 16 out of 20 — From good content to full credit
📈
Your Sports and Society paragraph had good content but was short and had spelling errors. At 16/20, you're close. The move from 16 to 18+ is: each passage detail needs a sentence explaining what it shows or why it matters.

Ansel, you know how to organize a paragraph. The structure is there. What separates 16/20 from 18/20 is depth: for every detail you mention from the passage, add one sentence that explains what that detail shows or why it matters.

The Detail + Explanation Pattern
DETAIL A specific fact, name, number, or event from the passage.
"The passage says soccer rules were first written down in 1863."
EXPLAIN A sentence that tells what the detail shows or why it matters.
"This shows that people have cared about fair play for a very long time."
Without explanation: "Sports have rules. The passage says soccer rules were written in 1863."

With explanation: "Sports have rules. The passage says soccer rules were written in 1863. This shows that people have cared about fair play for a very long time."

That extra sentence — the one that starts with "This shows..." or "That means..." — is what lifts a paragraph from 16 to 18+.

B The passage says soccer rules were written in 1863. This shows that people have cared about fair play for a very long time.

MODEL PARAGRAPH (18+ out of 20 level)

Sports rules developed over many years so people could play fair. First, the passage says soccer rules were written down in 1863.
