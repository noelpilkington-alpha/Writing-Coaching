A Naming Phrase

Unit 2 of 3. You find appositives every time. Building one is the gap
THE RULE
An appositive is a naming phrase. It gives the thing another name. The check: cover the noun. Can your phrase stand in its place? It starts with a, an, the or my plus a naming word, and it has no verb inside.

You already do this. Two of yours, full marks:

YOURS
The paterfamilias, NAMING PHRASEthe head of a Roman family, made all of the important decisions.
YOURS
Mr. Green, NAMING PHRASEthe principal, read the morning announcements.

Two that did not pass the check:

YOU WROTE
“The trumpet, that is lound, played loudly.” THAT + VERB
Cover the trumpet: that is lound played loudly. It cannot stand in, and is is a verb.
AS A NAMING PHRASE
The trumpet, NAMING PHRASEa brass instrument, played loudly.
YOU WROTE
“The report card, which i got straight A’s, made her smile.” WHICH + VERB
AS A NAMING PHRASE
The report card, NAMING PHRASEa sheet of top grades, made her smile.

Three naming phrases, all yours, and one phrase that only describes:

NAMING PHRASELeo, a body builder, helped me carry the heavy box of books.
NAMING PHRASEThe Pont du Gard, an ancient Roman aqueduct, carried fresh water across a river in France.
NAMING PHRASESarah, a new student in my class, sat alone at lunch today.
DESCRIBES ONLYThe drum, that was out of shape, was made from a can.
That was: a verb inside. The drum, a loud instrument, was made from a can passes.

Naming Word First

When the naming phrase opens the sentence
THE RULE
Naming word first, small letter after the comma. When the naming phrase comes first, it starts with a naming word, not an -ing or -ed word. A comma follows it. The word after the comma is a small letter, unless it is a name.

You already do this:

YOURS
A brave warrior, SMALL LETTERthe Roman legionary marched many miles into battle.
YOURS
A skilled painter, A NAME KEEPS ITS CAPITALEmma shared her favorite hobby with her new classmate.

Two that did not:

YOU WROTE
“A large curved shield, The scutum protected Roman soldiers from enemy arrows.” CAPITAL AFTER THE COMMA
One letter. the scutum.
YOU WROTE
“Made from fermented fish, Garum was a popular Roman sauce.” -ED WORD, NOT A NAMING WORD
NAMING WORD FIRST
NAMING PHRASEA popular Roman sauce, garum was made from fermented fish.

Three of yours that open with a naming word, and one that opens with an -ed word:

YOURSA famously noisy military jet, the A-10 Thunderbolt II makes a distinct brrt sound as it flies through the sky.
YOURSA dedicated team of military specialists, the ground crew refuel the A-10 Thunderbolt II in just a few minutes.
YOURSA fun activity for making friends, reading books together helps kids share stories.
YOURS, -ED WORDWorn by free Roman citizens, The toga was a traditional garment made of wool.
Two slips: worn is not a naming word, and The kept its capital. A traditional garment made of wool, the toga was worn by free Roman citizens.

Commands and Exclamations

Imperative means command. Exclamatory means exclamation. Interrogative means question. Declarative means statement
THE RULE
A command starts with the action word. No I, you or we in front. An exclamation tells, it does not ask. Say it as a statement first, then put the ! on the end.

You already do both:

YOURS, COMMANDS
ACTION WORD FIRSTStir this big pot of soup.   ACTION WORD FIRSTBump the ball over the net.
YOURS, EXCLAMATION
TELLSI will never try to walk backwards on a balance beam!

Two that kept the old shape:

YOU WROTE, CHANGE INTO A COMMAND
“I know for a fact that swimmers use the freestyle stroke to glide quickly through the water.” A STATEMENT
Everything before the action word has to go.
AS A COMMAND
ACTION WORD FIRSTUse the freestyle stroke to glide quickly through the water.
YOU WROTE, CHANGE INTO AN EXCLAMATION
“Did you even bend your knees before you bumped the volleyball!” STILL ASKS
AS AN EXCLAMATION
TELLSYou bent your knees before you bumped the volleyball!

Three that follow the rule, and one that looks like a command but is a statement:

COMMANDPass the baton carefully.
COMMANDRead the instructions before you start.
EXCLAMATIONThat catch was incredible!
A STATEMENTYou should pass the baton carefully.
You should in front makes it a statement about you. Cut it and the command is left.
