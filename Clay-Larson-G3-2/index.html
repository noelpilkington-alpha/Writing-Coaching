THE 60-SECOND READ-BACK
Before you submit each answer, do this:

1. Read your answer out loud in your head, word by word. Not fast — slow and clear.
2. Point at each word with your finger or your eyes.
3. Ask yourself: "Does this word look right?" If something looks off, it probably is.

Most spelling slips get caught in under one minute per answer.

#2 Growth: Capitalize "I" + Check Your Grammar

Q7 lost 1 point for two small fixes — both can become habits

Clay, your Q7 answer had a great idea (letting any stuffed animal be tossed, not just teddy bears). That is creative thinking. But two small slips pulled the score from 3/3 down to 2/3:

YOU WROTE (Q7)
"If i could change one thing about the event, I would change that your bring any stuffed animal not just only teddy bears."
Two fixes needed: i should be capital I, and your bring should be you bring.

These are the kinds of errors a read-back would catch. Let me show you the two rules:

RULE 1: "I" IS ALWAYS CAPITAL
The word "I" is always a capital letter when it means you, the writer. It does not matter where it shows up in a sentence.

✓ If I could change one thing...
✗ If i could change one thing...

✓ My sister and I went to the game.
✗ My sister and i went to the game.
RULE 2: READ THE SENTENCE OUT LOUD
"Your bring" vs "You bring" — those look similar on the page, but they sound different when you say them.

✓ You bring any stuffed animal. (You = a person doing the action)
✗ Your bring any stuffed animal. (Your = belongs to you — does not fit here)

When you read your answer out loud, your ear will catch it.
Which version of Clay's Q7 answer has all the right capitals and grammar?
A If i could change one thing about the event, I would change that you bring any stuffed animal, not just teddy bears.
B If I could change one thing about the event, I would change that you bring any stuffed animal, not just teddy bears.
THE QUICK CHECK FOR NEXT TIME
After you write each answer, look for every "i" by itself in the sentence. Each one should be a capital I. Then read the answer out loud one time to catch any "your/you" mix-ups.

This one habit is worth 1–2 points per test.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Q7 was a 3-point question. You earned 2. With capital I and "you bring," you would have earned all 3. That is 1 point right there — a fifth of the way to 90%.

MODEL POLISHED PARAGRAPH (18–20/20 level)

The Camel Mobile Library has affected many people of Garissa for many reasons. One reason is that cars cannot cross the rough land of Garissa, so camels bring the books directly to families who move often. Without the camels, many families would never see a book. Another reason is that the library helps people learn stories, share ideas, and talk to others, which helps the whole community grow. That is why the Camel Mobile Library has affected many people in Garissa.

The 4-Part Paragraph Shape
TOPIC State your main idea — you nailed this already
REASON 1 A specific detail from the article (cars cannot cross rough land)
REASON 2 A second specific detail (families move often, share ideas)
CLOSING Wrap back to your main idea — you did this too
