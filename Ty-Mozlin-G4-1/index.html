Write a Complete Sentence

Question 10 — a strong idea that stopped short

Ty, Question 10 asked for a skill that would help your community. Your idea was great — but it came out as a piece of a sentence instead of a whole one.

YOU WROTE (G4.1 Q10)
"A construction worker, because I could fix other's houses"

That has no subject doing an action — it answers "what" but never says who does what. A complete sentence needs a subject + a verb = a complete thought. Adding just a few words turns this into full credit.

Which one is a complete sentence?
A A construction worker, because I could fix other's houses.
B I would be a construction worker because I could fix other people's houses for them.
THE RULE
A complete sentence has a subject (who or what) and a verb (the action), and it makes sense on its own.

✗ "A construction worker, because I could fix other's houses." (no subject doing an action)
✓ "I would be a construction worker because I could fix other people's houses."
QUICK CHECK
Read your answer on its own, without the question. If it sounds like only half a thought, add the missing words: start with "I would..." or name who is doing the action. One small fix, full credit.
