WHAT IS AN APPOSITIVE?
An appositive is a noun phrase that renames the noun right next to it. It sits between two commas like an extra bit of info you could remove and the sentence still works.

✓ My dog, a black lab, loves water. ("a black lab" renames "my dog")
✓ Sally, a curious baby seal, swam to her family.
✗ Sally swam curiously to her family. (That's an adverb, not an appositive.)
✗ Sally swam, because she was free. (That's a conjunction, not an appositive.)
THE TEST: CAN YOU REMOVE IT?
Appositives must be removable. Try this:

The books, a collection of classic stories, are still popular.
Remove the bold part: "The books are still popular." → Still works! That's an appositive.

The books are still popular because they are popular.
Remove "because they are popular": "The books are still popular." → Works, but "because" is a conjunction, not an appositive. Conjunctions show a reason. Appositives rename.
Which sentence adds a real appositive to: "Sally swam to the other seals."?
A Sally quickly swam to the other seals.
B Sally, a young seal pup, swam to the other seals.
CORRECT APPOSITIVES FOR YOUR 4 PROMPTS
• "My family has two dogs, Lucy and Rocket." ("Lucy and Rocket" renames "two dogs")
• "Sally, a curious baby seal, swam to the other seals." ("a curious baby seal" renames Sally)
• "The books, a set of classic stories, are still popular for their golden spines." ("a set of classic stories" renames the books)
• "The appositive is: a best-selling story." (Just the phrase that renames Poky Little Puppy)

Interrogatives: Structure AND Question Mark

The other skill that keeps costing you points on Q3

Vasili, when a question says "rewrite as a question" or "rewrite as an interrogative," the answer MUST meet two rules:

  • Start with a question word (Did, Does, Is, Was, Were, Can, Will, Why, How, What)
  • End with a question mark (?)

You have missed part 2 — the question mark — on two different tests:

FROM G3.2 Q3 (scored 0.5/2)
Prompt: "Rewrite this as a question: This fun event sells out every year."
You wrote: "Why does this fun event sell out every year."
(Correct structure — but ends with a period instead of ?)
FROM G3.4 Q3 (scored 0.5/2)
Prompt: "Rewrite this sentence as an interrogative: The geode felt like a perfect souvenir."
You wrote: "Why did the geode feel like a perfect souvenir."
(Same problem — right structure, wrong ending punctuation.)

The good news: on G3.5 you got this exactly right. "Why were the books sold in department stores?" That shows you know how — you just need to check the last character every time.

Prompt: "Rewrite as a question: The event sells out every year." Which is a fully correct interrogative?
A Does the event sell out every year.
B Does the event sell out every year?
YOUR INTERROGATIVE CHECKLIST
Every time you answer Q1 or Q3 and the prompt says "rewrite as a question" or "interrogative":

1. First word: Does / Did / Is / Are / Was / Were / Can / Will / Why / How / What
2. Keep all the original content. Don't add or remove details.
3. Keep proper nouns capitalized (Anna stays Anna, Sally stays Sally).
4. Last character must be ?
FULL-CREDIT REWRITES FOR YOUR 2 MISSED PROMPTS
• "This fun event sells out every year." → "Does this fun event sell out every year?"
• "The geode felt like a perfect souvenir." → "Did the geode feel like a perfect souvenir?"
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
You already know the structure. The fix is a single character. On your proofread pass, search every Q1 and Q3 answer for an interrogative and make sure it ends with ?. That's the difference between 78% and 85% on a test like G3.4.

MODEL POLISHED PARAGRAPH (80%+ level)

Have you ever heard of Jason's sculptures under the sea? He chose to place them there because he wants to protect coral reefs. For example, plants and animals quickly began to cover his statues. Sponges and seaweed grew on them, and fish began to visit. This shows that his sculptures do more than just look like art — they become a real home for sea life. Another reason is that people visit the sculptures instead of the real reefs. This matters because the real reefs get a rest from crowds of swimmers, so the coral can stay healthy. In the end, Jason's art and the ocean help each other.

The 6-Part Paragraph (Your Next Level)
HOOK Open with a question or strong line (you already do this!)
IDEA State your central idea clearly in one sentence
DETAIL 1 Specific detail from the passage
SHOWS "This shows..." — explain what detail 1 means
DETAIL 2 A second specific detail from the passage
SHOWS "This matters because..." — explain what detail 2 means
CLOSE Tie back to your central idea
