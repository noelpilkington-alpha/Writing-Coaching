Explain, Don't Just Tell the Story

The #1 move: organize by impact, not by what happened next

Ellen, this is the big one. The prompt asked you to explain three impacts of the library on the community. Your essay tells the warehouse's story — first it was abandoned, then it became a library, then things grew. Here is how your essay opened:

YOUR INTRODUCTION
"This story is about the littlest building turning into the biggest activity hub! Alhtough this building was obsolite for years, and abandoned by the village, it made it's comback in an un expected way."

What is great: the energy and voice are excellent — "the littlest building turning into the biggest activity hub" is a fun, vivid hook. What holds it back: it opens with "This story is about..." and tells events in time order, instead of naming the three impacts the essay will explain. The reader does not yet know what you are going to PROVE.

STORY OPENING vs. EXPLAINING THESIS
Story opening (what you wrote): "This story is about the littlest building turning into the biggest activity hub!"
→ Tells what happens, but not the impacts you will explain.

Explaining thesis (what earns full credit): "The old lakeside warehouse became a library that transformed the community in three ways: it expanded learning, it grew a vibrant culture, and it boosted the local economy."
→ Names the three impacts the body paragraphs will prove.

EXPOSITORY REWRITE (YOUR DETAILS, ORGANIZED BY IMPACT)
Thesis: "Once an abandoned lakeside warehouse, the building made an unexpected comeback as a library that changed the community in three ways: it expanded learning, it grew a vibrant culture, and it boosted the local economy."

Body 1 – Learning: "First, the library expanded learning. It ran workshops on everything from boatbuilding to computer design. This shows that residents could gain real, hands-on skills they could not access before."

Body 2 – Culture: "Second, the library grew a vibrant culture. Musicians played music there and painters hung up their art. This shows the library became a place where the arts thrived, not just a place to borrow books."

Body 3 – Economy: "Third, the library boosted the local economy. Small weekend markets started popping up around it. This shows the library drew people in and gave local sellers new business."
Which sentence is a topic sentence for an explaining (expository) body paragraph?
A "The library also boosted the local economy by drawing visitors who shopped at the new weekend markets nearby."
B "And then after that, more people kept coming, and then the weekend markets showed up too, and then it got even busier."
THE FORMULA
Thesis: [The library changed the community] + [3 impacts: learning, culture, economy]

Each body paragraph: open with a topic sentence that NAMES one impact — not "and then..." Start the body with words like "First," "Second," "Another impact was..." Those signal an explanation, not a story.

Say What the Evidence Shows

You list great details — now tell the reader what they mean

Ellen, your details are some of the best in the cohort. Look at this stretch from your essay:

Your Body Excerpt:
"As it started growing in popularity, musicians started playing music there, painters hung up art, and small weekend stores started popping up too! Along with the fine art and stores around, the library started doing workshops too."
Real Detail Real Detail Real Detail Missing: What This Shows

Musicians, art, weekend stores, workshops — all true, all from the passage. But the essay lists them without saying what they show about the library's impact. The "this shows..." sentence is what turns a list of details into an explanation. Watch what happens when we add it:

Tap to see the same details with explanation added
Detail + "this shows..."
WITH EXPLANATION ADDED
"As it grew in popularity, musicians played music there and painters hung up their art. This shows the library became a cultural center where the arts could thrive. Small weekend markets also started popping up nearby. This shows the library boosted the local economy by drawing in shoppers. It even ran workshops from boatbuilding to computer design. This shows it gave the community real chances to learn new skills."
THE PATTERN: DETAIL → WHAT IT SHOWS
After each detail from the passage, add a sentence that answers:

• "This shows that..." (what the detail reveals)
• "This means..." (why it matters to the community)

Example: water taxis → this shows the library improved ACCESS. Workshops → this shows it created LEARNING opportunities. You already have the details. The explanation is the one sentence that tells the reader why each detail matters.
Which version explains the evidence instead of just listing it?
A "The library added water taxis to bring people across the lake. This means it became far easier to reach, opening it up to people who lived too far to walk or bike."
B "The library added water taxis. Also musicians and markets. Which was good."
YOUR ACTION STEP
In your next essay, after you name a detail, ask yourself: "What does this show about the library's impact?" Then write that sentence. One "this shows..." per detail is worth real points and turns your great details into a real explanation.

Proofread & Polish

A slow read-back catches the small spelling slips

Ellen, your ideas come fast — which is great — but a few words slipped through unchecked. None of these are things you do not know; they are speed slips. Here are the ones from your G8.1 essay:

SPELLING SLIPS FROM YOUR ESSAY
"Alhtough this building was obsolite for years... it made it's comback in an un expected way."  ·  "acsesable"  ·  "appering"  ·  "planing"
THE CORRECT SPELLINGS
Although · obsolete · comeback · unexpected · accessible · appearing · planning
THE SLOW READ-BACK
In Step 6, read your essay back one sentence at a time, a little slower than you wrote it. Whisper the words in your head. When a word looks "off," pause on it — that pause is usually right. The most common slips are: a missing letter ("comback" → "comeback"), a split word ("un expected" → "unexpected"), or a single vs. double letter ("planing" → "planning"). You have 90 minutes — there is plenty of room for one careful pass.
Which sentence is spelled correctly?
A "Although the building was obsolete for years, it made an unexpected comeback."
B "Alhtough the building was obsolite for years, it made an unexpected comback."
QUICK CHECK FOR YOUR NEXT TEST
Spelling will not sink a strong essay, but a clean essay reads as more polished and earns the last few points. Build the slow read-back into Step 6 every time — it is the cheapest points on the test.
