Capitalize Proper Nouns in Appositives

Q1 — Names are always capitalized

Q1 asked you to add an appositive giving more detail about Grandma Addie. Your appositive structure was good, but you didn't capitalize the name correctly.

YOU WROTE
Grandma addie, who competes every year, always get second place!

Two issues: (1) "addie" should be "Addie" — proper nouns (names) are always capitalized. (2) "always get" should be "always gets" — Grandma Addie is one person, so the verb needs an "s."

Also, "who competes every year" is a relative clause, not an appositive. An appositive is a noun phrase that renames the noun: "Grandma Addie, a talented baker, always gets second place!"

Read the explanation above first
FIXED
Grandma Addie, a talented baker, always gets second place!
APPOSITIVE VS. RELATIVE CLAUSE
An appositive is a noun phrase: "Grandma Addie, a talented baker, ..."
A relative clause starts with who/which/that: "Grandma Addie, who competes every year, ..."

Both add detail, but when the question asks for an appositive, use a noun phrase (no "who" or "which"). And always capitalize proper nouns — names like Addie, places, and titles.

"A" vs. "An" — Listen to the Sound

FIXED

a inexpensive → an inexpensive
a easy → an easy

THE A/AN RULE
Use "a" before words that start with a consonant sound: a book, a dog, a university.
Use "an" before words that start with a vowel sound: an apple, an easy task, an hour.

Quick test: Say the phrase out loud. If "a" sounds awkward before the next word, switch to "an."

Develop Your Paragraph

Q11 — 17/20. Good ideas, needs deeper evidence

Your paragraph scored 17/20. You have a clear central idea, but your evidence needs more specific detail from the article.

YOUR PARAGRAPH
A recognizable logo is very valuable in the business industry. A strong logo can help these businesses by being easy to print, find, and share. Logos that are easy to print will help create a inexpensive way to help share and promote your business. Your logo being easy to find helps by not making people search for long periods of time. Simple but good logos help make it easy to share by using the human brain's "gist" memory. Recognizable logos are very important and have many other pros to them too!

Your topic sentence is clear. But many of your supporting sentences are general statements rather than specific evidence from the article. You need to quote or cite details from the text and explain why they matter.

Paragraph Plan — TS → D1 → W → D2 → W → D3 → W → CS
TS = Topic Sentence • D = Detail from the article • W = Why it matters • CS = Concluding Sentence
TS A recognizable logo is very valuable in the business industry. (Good start!)
D1 Be specific: What does the article say about logos? Quote or paraphrase a fact.
W Explain why that fact shows logos are valuable. Connect the detail to your main point.
D2 You mentioned "gist" memory — great! Now explain what that means in the article's context.
W Why does "gist" memory make logos valuable? Explain the connection.
D3 Add one more specific detail from the article about why logos matter to businesses.
W Explain why this detail supports your central idea.
CS Your concluding sentence should restate the main idea, not introduce new points. "Many other pros" is too vague.
THE KEY: USE SPECIFIC EVIDENCE FROM THE TEXT
Instead of saying "A strong logo can help businesses," quote the article: "According to the article, the brain remembers logos through 'gist' memory, which means..." Then explain why that matters. Each detail should come from the article, not your general knowledge. And your concluding sentence should wrap up your main point — not add "many other pros."
