The Question You Missed: Kernel Expansion

Q5 — Adding specific detail, not restating the idea

Q5 — Which sentence best expands the kernel?

YOU CHOSE
Volunteers helped because they wanted to help clean up the river.

This repeats the kernel — "helped...help" just restates the same idea without adding any specific detail.

CORRECT ANSWER
Many volunteers helped remove fourteen tonnes of trash from the banks.

See the difference? This adds WHO (many volunteers), WHAT (remove trash), HOW MUCH (fourteen tonnes), and WHERE (from the banks). That's kernel expansion — adding specific detail from the passage.

WHY THIS MATTERS FOR YOUR ESSAY
The same issue shows up in your essay. Your body paragraphs state general ideas — "inviting people to help remove trash" — without citing specific facts like "fourteen tonnes" or "twenty percent cleaner." "Because they wanted to help" just restates the idea. Kernel expansion means adding numbers, places, and actions. The next cards show you how to bring that specificity into your writing.

Build a Full Body Paragraph

Evidence, explanation, sentence variety, and transitions

Here's your Paragraph 2 rewritten from 3 sentences to 6, using the TS-E1-X1-E2-X2-CS pattern:

YOUR VERSION (3 sentences)
The group started by mapping the river, and testing the water. They started to clean up by inviting people to help remove trash from the river. Soon, the whole community was helping with the project.
DEVELOPED VERSION (6 sentences)

TS: First, Earth5R organized the community to take action and clean up the Mithi River.

E1: According to the passage, the group began by mapping the dirtiest parts of the river and testing the water to understand the problem.

X1: This careful planning ensured they would focus their efforts where they were needed most; without this step, the cleanup could have been disorganized and ineffective.

E2: The passage also states that many volunteers helped remove fourteen tonnes of trash from the riverbanks.

X2: As a result, the river's pollution was dramatically reduced because the entire community worked together.

CS: Because of this organized effort, the cleanup made a real and measurable difference.

WHAT CHANGED (HIGHLIGHTED)
Specific evidence: The details now come straight from the passage — "mapping the dirtiest parts," "fourteen tonnes of trash" instead of vague summaries.

Sentence strategies: X1 uses a semicolon to join two related ideas into one compound sentence, making it more sophisticated than two simple sentences back to back.

Cause-and-effect transitions: "As a result" and "Because of this" show why the evidence matters. These are stronger than "Also" or "Soon" because they explain the connection between ideas.

Your Proofreading Checklist

Check for development AND spelling

On a test, you need to be your own editor. Your checklist focuses on both development and spelling:

Does my introduction end with a thesis that names 3 points?
Read your last intro sentence — can you hear 3 topics? If not, add them.
Does each body paragraph have at least 5 sentences?
Count them. If a paragraph has only 3 sentences, it's underdeveloped. Add evidence and explanation.
Did I use specific numbers or facts from the passage?
Look for numbers, names, percentages — not just general summaries.
Check spelling — look for long or unfamiliar words:
disgutsing → disgusting  •  fithy → filthy  •  communtiy → community
mantain → maintain  •  environoments → environments
Did I explain every piece of evidence?
After each fact, add "This shows that..." or "As a result..." Evidence without explanation doesn't earn full points.
THE 3-MINUTE RULE
On a timed test, stop writing with 3 minutes left and run through this checklist. Those 3 minutes are better spent catching spelling errors and adding a missing explanation sentence than writing new content.
