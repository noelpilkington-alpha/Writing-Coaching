Change a Statement to a Question

The one structure you missed on G3.8 Q4

Ariel, Q4 on G3.8 was the only non-Q11 question where you lost points (0.5/1). The skill is turning a statement into a question. Let’s nail this one so you never lose it again.

FROM G3.8 Q4 (0.5/1)
The prompt asked you to change this into a question:
"Jordan asked his classmates to help organize the shelves."

You wrote: "Jordan asked, can you help me organize the shelves?"
WHAT HAPPENED
Your answer kept "Jordan asked" and then made a NEW question inside quotation marks. But the prompt wanted the WHOLE sentence to BE the question — not a sentence that reports someone else’s question.

The trick: when you change a statement to a question, you FLIP the subject and the helping verb. Keep the meaning. Keep the subject (Jordan). Just rearrange.
The Change-to-Question Formula
1 Find the subject (who/what does the action) — here: Jordan
2 Find the verb — here: asked
3 Add a helper like Did at the start OR flip the verb to the front
4 End with ? instead of .
THE FIX
Statement: Jordan asked his classmates to help organize the shelves.
Question: Did Jordan ask his classmates to help organize the shelves?

Notice: same meaning, same subject (Jordan), same action (ask). Just "Did" at the front + "?" at the end.
The prompt says: "Change this sentence into a question: The dog chased the ball." Which answer is correct?
A The dog chased the ball?
B Did the dog chase the ball?
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Q4 is worth 1 point. You got 0.5. Fixing this one structure is +0.5 points every test. Combine that with the spelling pass, and you’re already at 90%.

MODEL POLISHED PARAGRAPH (19–20/20)

Shared gardens help towns in many ways. For example, some small towns plant a garden to share with everyone and work together on it. This shows how gardens bring neighbors together around a common goal. In addition, shared gardens give kids a chance to learn life skills, math, and science. This means children can practice real subjects outside the classroom. Shared gardens also make towns look pretty and green. In conclusion, shared gardens are helpful to towns because they build community and teach important lessons.

Your Paragraph Formula — Use It Every Time
TOPIC Name the main idea in one clear sentence
FOR EX "For example, + [fact from the passage]"
SHOWS "This shows how…" or "This means…" — the missing piece!
IN ADD "In addition, + [second fact]"
SHOWS "This means…" or "This shows…" again
CLOSE Restate the main idea using the word "because"
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
