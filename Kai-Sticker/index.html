The Question You Missed: Elaboration

Q8 — The difference between defining and developing

Q8 — Which sentence best adds elaboration to a paragraph about immersion learning?

YOU CHOSE
Immersion learning is a method that helps people learn language in classrooms.

This just defines immersion — it restates what immersion is without adding new information. Elaboration means adding specific detail that develops the idea further.

CORRECT ANSWER
In immersion classes, students must use the new language all day, which helps them learn vocabulary faster.

See the difference? This tells us what specifically happens in immersion classes (using the language all day) and what that leads to (learning vocabulary faster). That's elaboration.

WHY THIS MATTERS FOR YOUR ESSAY
This Q8 mistake is actually connected to the main issue in your essay. Your body paragraphs tend to state ideas generally rather than develop them with specific detail. The next few cards will show you how to fix this.

Your Intro Needs a Real Thesis

One sentence isn't enough
Your Entire Introduction
There are many effective ways that students can learn new language, which can help people in many ways.

This is your whole introduction — one sentence. There are three problems:

  • No hook — nothing to draw the reader in. Start with something interesting about language learning.
  • Too vague — "many effective ways" and "many ways" don't tell the reader anything specific. What ways?
  • No thesis preview — a strong thesis tells the reader exactly what your three body paragraphs will cover.
STRONGER INTRODUCTION
In the United States, millions of people speak more than one language. Learning a second language can strengthen memory, build connections, and open doors around the world. Students can develop strong language skills through immersion classes, pen pal programs, and getting an early start while their brains are still developing.
THE THESIS FORMULA
[Main idea] + [Point 1], [Point 2], and [Point 3].

Your thesis should be a roadmap. When the reader finishes your intro, they should know exactly what's coming in each body paragraph.

THE RULE OF THUMB
Each body paragraph should have:
• 1 topic sentence (what this paragraph is about)
• 2 pieces of evidence from the passage
• 2 explanations (why each piece of evidence matters)
• 1 concluding sentence

That's 6 sentences minimum. If your paragraph is shorter than that, you haven't developed it enough.

Use Specific Evidence from the Passage

General statements vs. passage-based proof

The rubric says: "Support each point with facts or examples from the passage and explain what they show." Right now, your evidence is too general:

What you wrote (general) What the passage says (specific)
"Immersion classes use that new language all day" "Students must use the new language all day" — during lessons, group work, and simple daily conversations
"They also learn good grammar by listening to how the language sounds" The passage says students "learn new words because they hear the language in every part of class" — vocabulary is built through constant exposure
"Connecting students with pen pals...can help them improve their writing skills" Pen pal programs help students "practice reading and writing in the new language" through letters and emails about their daily lives

See the difference? Specific evidence names concrete details from the passage — not just the general idea, but the actual examples the author used.

AFTER EVERY PIECE OF EVIDENCE, ADD:
"This shows that..." or "This means that..."

This is your explanation. It connects the evidence to your point and tells the reader why it matters. Without it, you're just listing facts.

The Body Paragraph Pattern

Your P2 rewritten with full development

Here's how your Paragraph 2 (immersion) could look using the full pattern:

YOUR VERSION (3 sentences)
One way children learn new languages is through immersion. Immersion classes use that new language all day, making it so that they learn new phrases throughout the day. They also learn good grammar by listening to how the language sounds.
DEVELOPED VERSION (6 sentences)

TS: One effective way children learn new languages is through immersion classes.

E1: According to the passage, in immersion schools, students must use the new language all day during lessons, group work, and even simple daily conversations.

X1: This shows that students don't just study the language in one class period — they are surrounded by it constantly, which makes learning feel natural.

E2: The passage also explains that students learn new words because they hear the language in every part of class.

X2: This means that vocabulary builds automatically through daily exposure, rather than through memorization alone.

CS: Because of this constant practice, immersion is one of the most effective methods for learning a new language.

MEMORIZE THIS STRUCTURE
TS → Topic Sentence
E1 → Evidence from the passage
X1 → Explanation (This shows that...)
E2 → More evidence from the passage
X2 → Explanation (This means that...)
CS → Concluding Sentence

TS → E1 → X1 → E2 → X2 → CS — use it for all three body paragraphs.
