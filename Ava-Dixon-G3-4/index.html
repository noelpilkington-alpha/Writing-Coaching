THE THREE-PART SENTENCE (YOUR NEXT-LEVEL HABIT)
Every Q6–Q10 answer needs all three pieces in ONE sentence:

1. Your opinion or answer (what you think).
2. A reason word: because, so, since.
3. A specific detail from the passage that backs it up.

When you hit all three, the answer earns the full 3 of 3 points every time.

MODEL POLISHED PARAGRAPH (18–19/20 level)

Sports have developed a lot since they were first created. Sports began in Britain in the 1700s, when they were mostly played by wealthy people who had free time after school or work. This shows that sports started as a pastime for people with money, not everyone. Later, schools began writing down the rules so that everyone could play the same game the same way. Today there are hundreds of sports that millions of people play around the world, and many of them cost little or nothing to join. This shows that sports have become something that connects people from every kind of background. In the end, what began as a pastime for a few has grown into a way the whole world plays together.

YOUR 3-MINUTE PROOFREAD PASS
Minute 1 — Spelling. Read every answer out loud in your head. Any word that looks strange (singlr, aloud, restaraunts, anythying) is probably wrong. Fix it.

Minute 2 — The three-part check. On Q6–Q10, look for the word because, so, or since. If it is not there, add it with a reason from the passage.

Minute 3 — The paragraph check. On Q11, look for at least two specific facts (name, place, date, action). If a sentence is too general, replace it with a concrete detail.
Which version would earn full credit on G3.2 Q3?
A Dogs are not aloud to go into restaraunts with us.
B Dogs are not allowed to go into restaurants with us.
THE "BACKWARDS" READING TRICK
Read each answer last word first, then second-to-last, and so on. Your brain stops auto-completing what it expects to see and actually reads each word. Misspellings jump out this way that a normal read-through misses.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
The three minutes you would spend proofreading are currently going unused — you finish in 9–14 minutes on a test that gives you much longer. Spend just 3 of those unused minutes and you catch almost every error in this whole review.
