Q3: Choosing the Right Conjunction

Skill: Combine sentences using a logical conjunction

The question: Combine these two ideas with a logical conjunction:

Animated movies are fun. They also include valuable lessons.

YOU CHOSE
Animated movies are fun because they include valuable lessons.
CORRECT ANSWER
Animated movies are fun but they include valuable lessons.

What happened: "Because" means the lessons are the reason the movies are fun. But the original sentences say two separate things — movies are fun AND they also have lessons. These are two different qualities, not cause and effect.

"But" works because it shows a gentle contrast: you might think fun movies are only entertaining, but they actually also include lessons.

HOW TO CHOOSE
Because = one thing causes the other (cause → effect)
But / Although = both things are true, and the second one is surprising or different
And / Also = both things are true and go in the same direction

Ask yourself: "Is the second idea the reason for the first?" If not, don't use "because."

The Body Paragraph Pattern

Use this exact structure for every body paragraph

You already write well — this pattern will help you write consistently across all three body paragraphs so none is weaker than the others.

THE PATTERN

TS — Topic Sentence (introduces the point)

E1 — Evidence from the passage ("The passage explains that...")

X1 — Explanation ("This shows that...")

E2 — More evidence from the passage ("The passage also says...")

X2 — Explanation ("This means that..." / "This teaches children...")

CS — Concluding Sentence (wraps up the point)

TS → E1 → X1 → E2 → X2 → CS

If you use this in all three body paragraphs, each one will be 5–6 sentences, fully developed, and passage-based. That's exactly what earns full marks on evidence and organization.

YOUR SPECIFIC SITUATION

Using this pattern ensures all three paragraphs are equally strong. Think of it as a checklist: if any paragraph is missing an E or X, add one before you move on.
