THE RULE: REPLACE, DON'T ADD
When a prompt says "change the verb to make it stronger" or "use a more vivid verb," your job is to swap the weak verb for a stronger one — not add words around it.

✗ "Layla looked over at the flower" (you kept "looked")
✓ "Layla gazed at the flower" (you REPLACED "looked")
✓ "Layla studied the flower" (stronger verb chosen)
✓ "Layla admired the flower" (stronger verb chosen)
STRONG VERB BANK FOR "LOOKED"
When something needs a stronger version of "looked", try:
gazed, peered, studied, admired, examined, watched, stared, glanced, spotted, noticed

For "said": whispered, shouted, yelled, asked, answered, muttered, cried, announced
For "walked": marched, strolled, stomped, raced, tiptoed, wandered, hiked

Never Put a Comma Right After "Because"

This one rule has cost you points on multiple tests

Julian, a second grammar rule keeps tripping you up: the comma after "because." Look at these three answers from three different tests:

FROM G3.2 Q2 (scored 0.5/2)
"The fans tossed bears onto the ice because, the bears scored a goal."
FROM G3.2 Q10 (scored 0.67/3)
"The ice was covered in teddy bears so, the players skated among the bear."
FROM G3.1 Q4 (scored 0.5/2)
"Secondly, dogs might growl or bite, so for this reason they might nervous or tired."
(Missing "be" — "they might be nervous" — plus extra words that change the meaning.)

"Because," "so," and "but" are joining words. They connect two parts of one sentence. You never put a comma right after them.

THE RULE
The comma (if any) goes BEFORE "because," "so," or "but," not after.

✗ "Fans tossed bears because, they scored."
✓ "Fans tossed bears because they scored."
✗ "The ice was covered so, they skated."
✓ "The ice was covered, so they skated." (comma goes BEFORE "so")
Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
A The fans cheered because, their team won.
B The fans cheered because their team won.
CORRECT VERSIONS FOR YOUR 3 MISSED ANSWERS
• "The fans tossed bears onto the ice because the bears scored a goal."
• "The ice was covered in teddy bears, so the players skated among the bears." (comma BEFORE "so")
• "Secondly, dogs might growl or bite because they are nervous or tired." (changed to "because" — it's the reason)
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Every time you write an answer that uses "because," "so," or "but," do a quick search. If there's a comma right after any of those three words, delete it. This is a one-second check worth 1–2 points per test.

MODEL POLISHED PARAGRAPH (17–18/20 level)

Modern sports have changed a lot since the 1700s, and two big changes helped them become popular. First, rules were created to make the games fair.
