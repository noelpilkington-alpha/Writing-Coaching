Write Complete Sentences, Not Fragments

Q8, Q10, Q9 — every answer needs a subject + verb + complete thought

A complete sentence has three parts: a subject (who or what), a verb (the action), and a complete thought. Several of your answers are missing one of these pieces.

THE SPECIFICS FORMULA
When a question asks "what does this tell us about the character," use this pattern:
1. Name the specific action from the passage.
2. Say what character trait it shows (honest, brave, caring, etc.).
3. Explain WHY that action shows that trait.
See another example
General: "She is nice."
Specific: "Layla helped the younger hikers find the trail. This shows she is kind and caring because she stopped what she was doing to help someone else."

Detail + Explanation

Q11 — 14 out of 20 — how to develop your paragraph for more points

You wrote a paragraph about how modern sports developed — and it has real content. To push from 14 to 17+, each detail needs an explanation of why it matters.

Detail + Explanation Pattern
D Detail: A specific fact or event from the passage.
E Explanation: Why that detail matters or what it shows about the topic.

Here is what you wrote, and how to expand it:

YOUR PARAGRAPH (Q11)
Modern sports developed and became popular in society in many ways...

You have a topic sentence and some details. Now add the "why" after each one:

Paragraph Plan — TS → D + E → D + E → CS
TS = Topic Sentence  •  D = Detail  •  E = Explanation  •  CS = Closing Sentence
TS Modern sports developed and became popular in society in many ways.
D For example, organized rules were created so everyone played the same way.
E This was important because fair rules let more people join in, not just the wealthy.
D Teams also formed leagues so they could compete against each other regularly.
E Leagues helped sports grow because fans could follow their favorite teams all season long.
CS Because of these changes, sports went from a hobby for a few people to something enjoyed around the world.

See how each E sentence answers "why does this matter?" That extra sentence is what moves you from 14 to 17+.

THE "SO WHAT?" TEST
After you write a detail from the passage, ask yourself: "So what? Why does this matter?" Then write that answer as your next sentence. That is your explanation.
