Question 6 Fix — Explain What a Character Is Like

Score: 1 out of 3 — Your answer needed more detail
PATTERN FROM YOUR WRITING JOURNEY
You've missed Write Sentence from a Prompt questions on 16 of 20 tests — the pattern is the same each time.

The question asked: "What do Layla's actions tell us about her character? Explain in one sentence."

Which answer better explains what Layla is like as a person?
A "It tells us Layla is interested in photography and insects."
B "Layla's actions show she is curious and honest, because she explored the cave carefully and chose to show the geode to the guide instead of keeping it."

Here is what you wrote:

YOUR ANSWER
"It tells us Layla us Layla is interested in photography and insects."

There are two problems here:

  • A small typo — "Layla us Layla" should just be "Layla"
  • The bigger issue: saying Layla "is interested in photography and insects" only describes her hobbies, not her character (what kind of person she is)
Tap to see a stronger answer
Think about what Layla's actions show about who she is
A STRONGER ANSWER
"Layla's actions show she is curious and honest, because she explored the cave carefully and chose to show the geode to the guide instead of keeping it."
THE DIFFERENCE
"Character" means what kind of person someone is — like curious, brave, honest, or kind. It does NOT mean what they like or what their hobbies are. When a question asks about character, name a personality trait and then point to the action that proves it.
See another example
Question: "What does Jake's behavior tell us about his character?"
Weak: "Jake likes soccer and video games." (hobbies, not character)
Strong: "Jake is determined, because he kept practicing even after losing the game." (personality trait + proof)
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
When you write paragraphs and essays, you will need to explain why characters act the way they do. If you can name a trait and back it up with evidence, your writing becomes much more convincing.

The question asked how nature helped Layla decide. Your answer says the geode caused her to take it — but in the story, Layla chose NOT to keep it. Your sentence describes the temptation, not the help.

READ THE QUESTION CAREFULLY
The question said "how nature helped" — that means your answer needs to show nature doing something positive that led to Layla's choice. Before you write, underline the key word in the question (like "helped") and make sure your answer matches it.
See how changing one word changes the whole answer
"How did nature TEMPT Layla?" → "The sparkling geode made Layla want to keep it." (about temptation)
"How did nature HELP Layla decide?" → "The cave's beauty reminded Layla that nature should stay untouched." (about helping)
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
In your paragraph writing, you will also need to answer the prompt exactly. If the prompt says "explain how," your paragraph needs to show HOW. Missing the key word in a question is the fastest way to lose points — even when your writing is good.

Question 8 Fix — Explain the Most Important Moment

Score: 2 out of 3 — Close! Just needed more explanation

The question asked: "What do you think was the most important moment in the story? Explain in one sentence."

Which answer gives a better explanation of WHY the moment is important?
A "When Layla saw the geode because it shows Layla needs to make a big decision."
B "The most important moment was when Layla found the geode, because she had to choose between keeping something beautiful for herself and doing the honest thing by telling the guide."

Here is what you wrote:

YOUR ANSWER
"When Layla saw the geode because it shows Layla needs to make a big decision."

You picked the right moment! The problem is your explanation — saying she "needs to make a big decision" is true but too vague. What makes the decision big? What are her two choices?

Tap to see a stronger answer
Try to explain what the decision was between
A STRONGER ANSWER
"The most important moment was when Layla found the geode, because she had to choose between keeping something beautiful for herself and doing the honest thing by telling the guide."
THE "BECAUSE + DETAILS" RULE
When a question says "explain," you need more than just the word "because." After "because," give specific details about WHY. If your explanation could fit almost any story, it is too vague.

Too vague: "...because she had to make a big decision."
Specific: "...because she had to choose between keeping it for herself and doing the right thing."
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Every time you write a paragraph, you need to explain your evidence. "Because + vague" will always get partial credit. "Because + specific details" is what earns full points.

Which answer explains the rule AND why it matters more clearly?
A "Not to take items from it, because it can destroyer the natural place."
B "People should not take items from nature, because removing rocks and plants can destroy animal homes and stop new things from growing."

Here is what you wrote:

YOUR ANSWER
"Not to take items from it, because it can destroyer the natural place."

Your idea is right — people should not take things from nature. Two things to fix:

  • Spelling: "destroyer" should be "destroy" (destroyer is a noun — like a ship — not a verb)
  • Explanation: "it can destroy the natural place" is too general. Destroy how? What specific harm happens?
Tap to see a stronger answer
Think about what specifically gets harmed
A STRONGER ANSWER
"People should not take items from nature, because removing rocks and plants can destroy animal homes and stop new things from growing."
ADD THE "LIKE WHAT?" DETAILS
Whenever you write that something is "bad" or "destroys" something, ask yourself: "Like what? What specifically happens?" Then add that detail.

Vague: "...because it can destroy the natural place."
Specific: "...because removing rocks can destroy animal homes and stop new things from growing."
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Adding specific details is the same skill you use in paragraphs when you explain your evidence. The difference between partial credit and full credit is almost always one more detail.

Which opening sentence is a stronger central idea for this paragraph?
A "Typical sports began in Britain in the 1700s."

Here is what you wrote (with errors marked):

Typical sports began in Britian in the 17's. First, mostly rich people played sports, but soon the middle class did too. A major change hat soon happened was rules. MAKE YOUR CENTRAL IDEA SENTENCE A PREVIEW

Now the reader knows exactly what's coming, and you have a roadmap for your paragraph.
3-MINUTE PROOFREADING TRICK
Save the last 3 minutes of your test for proofreading. Read your paragraph from the last sentence to the first — reading backward forces your brain to look at each sentence on its own instead of skipping over errors.
