Your #1 Growth Area: Missing Word Endings

Tiny endings that are easy to catch — and worth a lot of points

Thalia, the biggest pattern across your three tests is missing endings. Words that should be plural stay singular. Words that should have 's are missing it. Here are three examples from G3.3 alone:

FROM G3.3 Q11
"Satoshi Tajiri childhood influenced the creation of Pokemon."
Should be: Tajiri's childhood (possessive 's)
FROM G3.3 Q11 (same paragraph)
"As a boy, Tajiiri loved bug, He would spent hours catching them."
Should be: loved bugs (plural -s). Your own next phrase says "catching them" — so you already know there is more than one.
FROM G3.3 Q11 (same paragraph)
"Collecting bug made him very happy. That how Satoshi Tajiri childhood influenced the creation of Pokemon."
Should be: Collecting bugs. That is how (missing "is"). Tajiri's childhood.
YOUR TOP 3 "ENDING" CHECKS
These 3 checks will catch most of the points you are leaving on the table:

1. Plural -s: If there is more than one, add s. (bug → bugs, book → books, bear → bears)

2. Possessive 's: When something belongs to someone, use 's. (Tajiri's childhood, Anna's family)

3. Missing small words: Read every sentence aloud. If it sounds like something is missing, a tiny word like is, are, was, the is probably missing.
From G3.3 Q11 — which version is grammatically complete?
A Tajiri loved bug as a boy. That how his childhood influenced Pokemon.
B Tajiri loved bugs as a boy. That is how his childhood influenced Pokemon.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Missing endings show up on almost every answer on every test. Each one costs a fraction of a point. Across 11 questions, that is 10 to 15 points. Catching these endings during a proofread pass is the fastest way to grow from 68% toward 80%+.
