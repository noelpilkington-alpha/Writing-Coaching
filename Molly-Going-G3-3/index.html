THE FIX: THE 2-PART ANSWER
Every short-answer question on G3 has two parts hiding inside it. Answer both.

Part 1: WHAT do you do/feel/think?
Part 2: WHY — your reason (use the word because).

If your answer is missing the word because, it's probably missing half the answer.

Your #2 Pattern: Appositives Need to Rename

Partial credit on appositives in 2 of 3 tests

Molly, an appositive is a short phrase that renames a noun. Look at two of your answers side-by-side — you used commas, but you didn't rename anyone.

FROM G3.1 Q1 (Dogs test) — 0.5/2
Prompt: "Rewrite sentences 1 and 2 as one sentence that includes an appositive."
You wrote: "My family has two dogs, their names are Lucy and Rocket."
(That's two separate sentences joined by a comma, not an appositive.)
FROM G3.3 Q3 (Seal test) — 0.5/2
Prompt: "Rewrite the sentence and add an appositive: Sally swam to the other seals."
You wrote: "Sweet Sally the seal swam to the other seals."
("Sweet" is just an adjective, not an appositive — and the commas are missing.)
WHAT AN APPOSITIVE ACTUALLY IS
A short phrase that renames a noun, set off by commas.

Pattern: Noun, a noun phrase that renames it, rest of sentence.

✓ "My dog, a fluffy golden retriever, loves walks."
✓ "Sally, the baby seal, swam to the other seals."
✓ "My family has two dogs, Lucy and Rocket."
From G3.3 Q3 — prompt: "Rewrite the sentence and add an appositive: Sally swam to the other seals." Which rewrite uses an appositive correctly?
A Sweet Sally swam to the other seals.
B Sally, the baby seal, swam to the other seals.
HOW TO CHECK YOUR APPOSITIVE
1. Point to the noun you want to rename.
2. Right after the noun, add a comma, then a noun phrase that tells WHO or WHAT that noun is.
3. Close with another comma.
4. If you can remove the phrase and the sentence still makes sense, you have an appositive.
FIXED VERSIONS OF YOUR 2 ANSWERS
• G3.1 Q1: "My family has two dogs, Lucy and Rocket."
• G3.3 Q3: "Sally, the baby seal, swam to the other seals."
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
On each test where you saw an appositive prompt, you earned 1 out of 2. Knowing the rule — rename the noun with a noun phrase, wrap it in commas — is worth a full extra point on every test that has this question.

POLISHED MODEL (16–18/20 level)

Satoshi Tajiri's childhood shaped Pokemon in two big ways. First, he loved catching bugs as a kid, and that is where the idea of collecting creatures came from. He also loved video games but thought they were not very fun, so he decided to create a better game of his own. He called his game Pokemon, and players catch creatures called Pokemon inside special balls. Because of his childhood hobbies, Tajiri built a game that millions of kids still love today.

The Minimum Viable Paragraph (G3 Recipe)
TOPIC Answer the prompt directly in one sentence. Name the central idea.
DETAIL 1 One specific detail from the article that supports your idea.
DETAIL 2 A second specific detail from the article.
CLOSING One sentence that wraps it up. Start with "Because" or "So" or "In all."
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Look at your three tests. Two full paragraphs → 76–80%. One short paragraph → 60%. The paragraph is worth 20 of 45 points. Always write all four parts. If you get stuck, writing a short closing sentence still beats leaving the paragraph short.
