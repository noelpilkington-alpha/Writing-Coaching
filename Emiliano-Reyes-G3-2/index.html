Match the Question Type

Give the exact kind of answer the question asks for

Emiliano, two questions asked for a specific kind of answer, and the answer you wrote was a different kind. Your sentences were fine — they just were not the type that was asked for. Let's look:

Q3 — THE QUESTION ASKED YOU TO REWRITE IT AS A QUESTION
"The consert, a fun event, sells out every year."
This is a statement with an added description. The question wanted a question — it has to end with a ?
Q4 — THE QUESTION ASKED YOU TO NAME THE SENTENCE TYPE
"Workers spent over 30 minutes clearing the ice." → you chose Imperative
This sentence simply tells a fact, so it is Declarative. An imperative gives a command.
THE 4 SENTENCE TYPES
Declarative . Tells something. "The fans cheered."
Interrogative ? Asks something. "Did the fans cheer?"
Exclamatory ! Shows strong feeling. "The fans went wild!"
Imperative · Gives a command. "Cheer for the team."
To turn a statement into a question, start with a word like Did, Does, or Is, and end with a ?
Q3 asked you to rewrite the sentence as a QUESTION. Which answer actually does that?
A The concert, a fun event, sells out every year.
B Does this fun event sell out every year?
WHAT Q3 WANTED
"Does this fun event sell out every year?" — starts with Does, ends with a ?. That is the whole trick: if the question says "write a question," your answer must end in ?

Read Carefully for Facts

Get the passage facts right, then answer what's asked

Emiliano, on Q5 the question asked what the event shows about the fans. Your sentence had a fact mixed up — and once the fact is wrong, the answer can't earn the point. Look:

Q5 — WHAT YOU WROTE
"The Hershey Bears, a toy store, Broke their record by collecting 45,650 stuffed animals."
The Hershey Bears are a hockey team, not a toy store. And the question asked what the event shows about the fans, not what the team did.

The passage is about a hockey team's "Teddy Bear Toss," where fans throw stuffed animals onto the ice after the first goal. So the event shows the fans are generous and want to help others.

Which answer gets the facts right AND answers what Q5 asked (what the event shows about the fans)?
A The Hershey Bears, a toy store, broke their record by collecting 45,650 stuffed animals.
B The event shows the fans are generous because they threw 45,650 stuffed animals onto the ice to give to children.
THE RE-READ HABIT
Before you write a fact, go back to the passage and find the exact words. Ask: Who is this about? What did they actually do? The Bears are a hockey team. The fans threw the toys. Getting those two facts right changes the whole answer.
THE FULL MOVE
1. Re-read to get the facts right (hockey team, fans, Teddy Bear Toss).
2. Answer the actual question: what does the event show about the fans?
3. Result: "The event shows the fans are generous because they threw 45,650 stuffed animals onto the ice to give to children."
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Q5 was worth 3 points. You clearly read the passage — your perfect Q6–Q10 proves that. One quick re-read to lock in the facts, plus answering the exact question, turns this into 3 points you already have the skill to earn.

Build the Paragraph

Topic sentence + details + closing, with periods between ideas

Emiliano, your Question 11 paragraph about the "One Book, One Camel" library had a good heart — you cared about the idea. It just needs a clearer shape. Here is what you wrote:

"The Camel Mobile, a nice librbrary, helps kids in Garissa where they move a lot can learn very well thats why we need Camel Mobiles all overy where kids need learning."
Has an idea One run-on sentence Missing: topic sentence Missing: specific details Missing: closing

The whole paragraph is one long run-on — several ideas crammed together with no stops. It also needs a clear topic sentence, two or three specific details from the article, and a closing. The fix for a run-on is always a period and a new sentence.

Which version is built correctly — with one idea per sentence?
A The Camel Mobile Library helps kids in Garissa it brings books on camels the kids who move a lot can still read.
B The Camel Mobile Library helps kids in Garissa. It brings books on camels to faraway places. Kids who move a lot can still read.
The Paragraph Shape
TOPIC Say your main idea in one clear sentence.
DETAIL 1 A specific fact from the article.
DETAIL 2 A second specific fact from the article.
CLOSING Wrap up your main idea in a new way.

MODEL PARAGRAPH (using article details)

The Camel Mobile Library helps children in Garissa, Kenya keep learning. The library carries books on the backs of camels so it can reach remote areas with no roads. Because the camels bring the books to them, kids whose families move around a lot can still read and learn. This shows that the Camel Mobile Library makes reading possible for kids who might otherwise be left out.

SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Question 11 is worth 20 of the 45 points — the biggest single piece of the test. Breaking your run-on into clear sentences and adding a topic, two details, and a closing is the fastest way to lift this score. Remember: when two complete ideas bump together, the fix is a period and a new sentence.
