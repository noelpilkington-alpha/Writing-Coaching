Fix 1: A Topic Sentence Has Two Parts

The point, and how the paragraph will prove it. Yours had neither
THE RULE: THE POINT, THEN THE HOW
A topic sentence does two jobs in one sentence. POINT answers the question. HOW names the reasons the paragraph will use to prove it, usually two or three. You already write this shape in your short answers. Your Q7, full marks: Setting up tents or watching movies can strengthen family relationships [the point] by causing them to spend time together and know each other more [the how]. A paragraph’s first sentence is that same sentence, and the how is the list of facts you are about to give. Every detail that follows has to belong to one of the hows. The check: underline the point, underline the how, count the hows. Then, for each fact you plan to use, point to the how it belongs to. A fact with no how stays out, even if it is in the article.
YOU WROTE (Q11, FIRST FOUR SENTENCES)
“Imagine wearing a headset that makes you feel like you are floating above earth. This is not a movie. Some schools now use virtual reality (VR) to take students on field trips to places they may never see in their life. There are many benefits to VR field trips.” THE ARTICLE’S OPENING, THEN A POINT WITH NO HOW
Three sentences copied from the article, then many benefits: a point that promises proof without saying what the proof will be. The reader cannot tell which facts are coming, and while you were writing, neither could you.

POINTThe author shows that VR field trips are a good idea for schools HOWby proving that students learn more from them and by showing that they avoid the problems of real trips.
Your two facts, the memory study and the buses, are exactly these two hows. The topic sentence you needed was already inside your paragraph. It was never written down.

Fix 2: The Ending Puts the Reasons Together

Not the point again in new words. What the reasons add up to
THE RULE: NAME THE REASONS, THEN SAY WHAT THEY ADD UP TO
A concluding sentence looks back at the hows and says what they prove together: Together, [how 1] and [how 2] show that [the point, now earned]. It is not the topic sentence in new words, and it is not advice to the reader. The check: does your last sentence mention both reasons? Does it say something the topic sentence could not say yet, because the proof had not been given?
YOU WROTE (Q11, LAST TWO SENTENCES)
“VR field trips are just overall good ideas forfield trips with many benefits. So next time you go to school talk to your teacher about VR field trips, maybe you will get to do some.” RESTATES, THEN CHANGES THE SUBJECT
The first sentence says many benefits again, as vague as it was at the top. The second gives the reader advice about their own school, which the question never asked about. Neither sentence mentions the memory study or the buses, the two things the paragraph actually proved.

Both hows named, then what they add up to. Learn more, risk less is something the topic sentence could not have said before the facts.

Fix 3: A Reason That Says Something New

Q6 repeated the claim. Q10 stopped before the why
THE RULE: THE REASON ADDS A FACT THE CLAIM DID NOT HAVE
One of the three points is for the reason, explanation or example; one is for a clear answer in a sentence, and one for conventions. A reason earns them when it says something the first half did not: a fact from the story, or a concrete example from your life. The check: cover the first half and read the because-half alone. If it could be the claim again in other words, it is not a reason yet.
YOU WROTE (Q6, 2 OUT OF 3)
“Changing plans can still be meaningful even if they are different as long as they keep their key factor of why they are meaningful.” THE REASON REPEATS THE CLAIM
Meaningful because they keep what makes them meaningful. The second half is the first half turned around. The story has the concrete version: the trip was canceled, and the tent, the flashlights and the night together were kept.
WITH A REASON THAT ADDS A FACT
Changing plans can still lead to fun BECAUSEbecause the Thompsons kept the part that mattered, a night in their own tent with games and stories, and only moved it to the backyard.
YOU WROTE (Q10, 2 OUT OF 3)

Correct, and finished too early. Why did a backyard work when the couch and the popcorn did not?
WITH THE WHY
