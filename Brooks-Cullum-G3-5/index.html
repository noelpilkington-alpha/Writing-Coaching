Error #1: Write the Correct Sentence Type

This is a Step 2 skill: the question is testing sentence types

Brooks, Q1 asked you to write a question (interrogative sentence). You wrote a statement (declarative sentence) instead.

Q1 — YOUR ANSWER (0 out of 2 weighted points)
"Little Golden Books were popular with families."

The problem: This is a statement (it ends with a period and declares a fact). The question asked you to write an interrogative sentence, which means a question that ends with a question mark.

CORRECT: A QUESTION
"Why were Little Golden Books so popular with families?"
STEP 2: IDENTIFY THE SENTENCE TYPE
• Declarative = a statement (ends with a period)
• Interrogative = a question (ends with a question mark, starts with who/what/why/how/did/is/are)
• Imperative = a command (tells someone to do something)
• Exclamatory = strong emotion (ends with !)

Before writing, ask: "What type does this question want?" Then make sure your answer matches that type.
The question asks you to write an interrogative sentence about Little Golden Books. Which is correct?
A"Little Golden Books had colorful pictures that children loved."
B"What made Little Golden Books so popular with young readers?"

Error #2: Appositive Structure

This is a Step 2 skill: the question is testing appositives

Brooks, on Q4 your appositive structure was broken, and on Q5 you identified the wrong part of the sentence as the appositive.

THE APPOSITIVE PATTERN
Noun, [renaming phrase], rest of sentence.

The renaming phrase:
• Comes AFTER the noun
• Is set off by commas on both sides
• Renames or describes the noun (tells us what it IS)

Example: "Little Golden Books, affordable stories for children, were sold everywhere."
Which sentence uses a correct appositive?
A"The museum, a sunken art gallery, attracts divers from around the world."
B"The sunken and artistic museum attracts divers from around the world."
WHY THIS MATTERS
Q4 and Q5 together are worth 4 weighted points. You earned 2 out of 4. Lock in the pattern: Noun, [renaming phrase], rest of sentence. Every time you see "appositive" in the question, use this exact pattern.

Error #3: Run-On Sentence

This is a Step 5 skill: proofread for sentence boundaries

Brooks, on Q9 your answer was a run-on sentence. Two complete thoughts were joined without proper punctuation or a connecting word.

Q9 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 3 weighted points)
"The museum is good for the ocean the statues give coral a place to grow animals live there now."

The problem: This is actually THREE complete thoughts with no periods or connectors between them.

FIXED: THREE SEPARATE SENTENCES
"The museum is good for the ocean. The statues give coral a place to grow. Animals live there now because they have a safe home."
THE FIX: ONE IDEA PER SENTENCE
After writing your answer, read it slowly. Every time you reach the end of one complete thought, put a period. Then start the next thought with a capital letter.

If your answer feels like one very long sentence, it probably needs to be split into 2-3 shorter sentences.
Which version correctly separates the ideas?
A"The museum helps the ocean coral grows on the statues fish live there now."
B"The museum helps the ocean. Coral grows on the statues over time. Fish and other animals live there because they have shelter."
