Use the Exact Conjunction the Prompt Gives You

Q2 — 1 out of 2 — Combining Sentences
🔍
When a prompt says "combine using since", you must use that exact word — not a synonym. "So" and "since" mean different things, and the prompt is testing whether you can use the specific conjunction it names.

Griffin, on Q2 the prompt told you to combine two sentences using the word "since." You wrote a perfectly good sentence — but with "so" instead of "since." That one word swap cost you a point.

WHAT YOU WROTE — Q2 (1 out of 2)
"Layla saw a butterfly, so she got out her camera."
(The prompt required "since" as the conjunction. You used "so" — a different conjunction with a different meaning.)
Why "since" and "so" are different
since Gives a reason (cause): "Since it was raining, I brought an umbrella." — the rain is the reason.
so Shows a result (effect): "It was raining, so I brought an umbrella." — the umbrella is the result.
The prompt says: "Combine these sentences using the word since: Layla saw a butterfly. She got out her camera." Which answer follows the prompt?
A Layla saw a butterfly, so she got out her camera.
B Since Layla saw a butterfly, she got out her camera.
FULL-CREDIT VERSION
"Since Layla saw a butterfly, she got out her camera."
Notice: "since" goes at the front and introduces the reason. The result comes after the comma.
YOUR RULE
Before you write, circle or underline the conjunction word in the prompt. Then make sure that exact word appears in your answer. If the prompt says "since," write "since." If it says "although," write "although." Never swap in a synonym.
WHY THIS MATTERS
This question is testing whether you can use a specific conjunction correctly. Using a different one — even one that makes grammatical sense — shows you didn't follow the instruction. It's an easy point to get back.

Punctuation Finishes the Job

Q3 — 1 out of 2 — Change the Sentence Type
✎
You built a solid interrogative sentence — you rearranged the words correctly. But you left off the question mark and misspelled "souvenir." The structure was right; the finishing details cost you the point.

Griffin, on Q3 you were asked to rewrite a sentence as an interrogative (a question). You did the hard part — rearranging the words into a question structure. But two small things kept you from full credit:

WHAT YOU WROTE — Q3 (1 out of 2)
"Did the geode feel like a perfect souviner."
Two issues: (1) Missing question mark — a question must end with ? not . (2) Spelling: "souviner" should be "souvenir".
The rule: sentence type = ending punctuation
. Declarative (statement) ends with a period.
? Interrogative (question) ends with a question mark.
! Exclamatory (strong feeling) ends with an exclamation point.
The prompt says: "Rewrite as an interrogative: The geode felt like a perfect souvenir." Which answer earns full credit?
A Did the geode feel like a perfect souviner.
B Did the geode feel like a perfect souvenir?
FULL-CREDIT VERSION
"Did the geode feel like a perfect souvenir?"
Same structure you wrote — just swap the period for a question mark and fix the spelling.
YOUR 2-STEP CHECK
Step 1: After you write a sentence-type answer, look at the last character. Does it match? Interrogative = ? every time.

Step 2: Read any long or unusual word one more time. "Souvenir" is tricky — remember: sou-ve-nir (the "e" comes before the "n").
WHY THIS MATTERS
You did 90% of the work correctly on this question. The structure was right. But the ending mark is what proves you know the sentence type. Missing it turns a full-credit answer into half credit.

Detail + Explanation

Q11 — 13 out of 20 — paragraph needs passage details
📈
Your paragraph has a clear topic and flows logically. The gap between 13/20 and 17+ is specific details from the passage paired with explanation of what they show.

Griffin, your opening sentence was good: "Sports have evolved a lot over time." That's a clear topic. But after that, your body sentences stayed general. The passage "From Sports and Society" gives you specific facts you can pull in — names, dates, examples — and then explain why they matter.

YOUR OPENING — Q11 (13 out of 20)
"Sports have evolved a lot over time. for example at the begining only rich people could play sports..."
Good start, but: (1) "for" needs a capital F after a period, (2) "begining" should be "beginning", (3) the claim needs a specific detail from the passage.
The Detail + Explanation Pattern
Detail A specific fact from the passage: a name, a number, a year, or a paraphrased line. Not a general statement you could say about any topic.
Explain A sentence that says what this shows or why it matters for your topic. It connects the detail back to your main idea.
Which body sentence would earn more paragraph points?
A At the beginning, only rich people could play sports.
B For example, the passage explains that polo was only played by wealthy people because they were the only ones who could afford horses. This shows that some early sports were limited to the rich.
HOW TO USE THIS ON YOUR NEXT TEST
For each body sentence in your paragraph:
1. Write your claim (what you want to say).
2. Add a detail from the passage (a name, date, or paraphrased fact).
3. Write one sentence explaining what this detail shows.

That three-part move is what separates a 13/20 paragraph from a 17/20 paragraph.
WHY THIS MATTERS
Question 11 is worth 20 out of 45 points on the whole test. Moving from 13 to 17 adds 4 points — that alone takes you from 78% to 87%. The paragraph is your biggest leverage point.
