#1 Skill: What a True Appositive Is

Q3 asked for an appositive — let's lock in exactly what that means

Aya, Q3 asked you to add an appositive to the sentence "Sally swam to the other seals." Here's what you wrote:

FROM G3.3 Q3 — YOU WROTE
"Sally dove into the water and joined the other seals."

This is a fine sentence! But it's a different action sentence — it doesn't add an appositive. That's the one thing to fix.

WHAT AN APPOSITIVE ACTUALLY IS
An appositive is a short noun phrase that renames the noun right next to it, set off by commas. It tells you more about who or what something is — it does not add a new action.
Your Sentence, With a True Appositive
NO APPOSITIVE "Sally dove into the water and joined the other seals." (a new action, no renaming phrase)
APPOSITIVE "Sally, a young seal, swam to the other seals." (the phrase "a young seal" renames Sally)
THE TEST FOR AN APPOSITIVE
Ask: "Does my added phrase rename the noun, set off by commas?" "a young seal," "a friendly pup," "Anna's new friend" — each one renames Sally. If your phrase adds an action instead (dove, joined, swam), it's not an appositive.
Which sentence adds a true appositive to "Sally swam to the other seals"?
A Sally, a curious young seal, swam to the other seals.
B Sally splashed and dove as she swam to the other seals.
C Sally swam to the other seals because she felt happy.
REMEMBER
When a question asks for an appositive, keep the original action and slip a renaming noun phrase right after the noun, between two commas: "Sally, a young seal, swam to the other seals." That single move earns the point.
