Step 2: Appositives — Keep It Simple

Q3 got tangled. Here is how to keep appositives clean.
🔍
What happened on Q3: You tried to write an appositive but it got tangled: "rodents, slow movers...that are rodents." The sentence circled back on itself. The fix: keep the appositive short and simple.
Q3 — YOUR ANSWER (0 out of 2 weighted points)
Your appositive attempt repeated "rodents" and the sentence became confusing.

What an appositive should be: A short phrase between two commas that renames the noun. Think of it as a quick label.

SIMPLE APPOSITIVE EXAMPLES
"Sloths, slow-moving tree animals, sleep most of the day."
"Sloths, gentle creatures found in rainforests, rarely come down from trees."

Keep it short: 3–6 words between the commas. Do NOT repeat the noun you are renaming.
THE APPOSITIVE FORMULA
[Noun], [short rename], [rest of sentence].

• The rename should be 3–6 words
• It should NOT repeat the original noun
• It should tell us WHO or WHAT the noun is
• The sentence should still make sense if you remove it
Which sentence has a correct appositive?
ASloths, animals that are slow animals, sleep all day.
BSloths, gentle creatures of the rainforest, sleep up to 20 hours a day.
WHY THIS MATTERS
Q3 was worth 2 weighted points and you earned 0. The fix is simple: keep your appositive to 3–6 words, do not repeat the noun, and read the sentence back to make sure it flows. Two points earned.
