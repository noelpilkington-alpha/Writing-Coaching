Fix — Run-On Sentences and Fragments

Questions 7, 8, 9, and 10 lost points for sentence structure

This was a pattern across four of your answers on the 3-point questions.

Which is a correctly written sentence?
A Satoshi loved catching bugs he used that idea to create Pokémon.
B Satoshi loved catching bugs, so he used that idea to create Pokémon.

Michael, several of your answers ran two ideas together without proper punctuation, or left sentences incomplete:

QUESTION 8 — RUN-ON
Two complete thoughts joined together without a period or connecting word.
QUESTION 9 — RUN-ON
Two ideas pushed together. Also, "done" should be "did" (past tense verb form).
QUESTION 10 — FRAGMENT
A sentence that does not express a complete thought — it is missing a main part.
HOW TO FIX RUN-ONS
If your sentence has two complete ideas, you have three options:
1. Period: End the first idea with a period. Start a new sentence.
2. Comma + connecting word: Use ", and" or ", so" or ", but" to join them.
3. Semicolon: Use a semicolon (;) between two closely related ideas.
HOW TO FIX FRAGMENTS
Read your sentence out loud. Ask yourself: "Does this express a complete thought?" If it sounds like it is missing something, it probably is. A complete sentence needs a subject (who or what) and a verb (what happened).
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Questions 6–10 are worth 3 points each. Run-ons and fragments usually cost you 1 point per question. Fixing these across four questions could add 4 points to your next test.

Fix — Verb Forms in Questions

Question 1 — making sure verbs match in questions

You scored partial credit because the verb form did not match the question structure.

Which question uses the correct verb form?
A Why did Anna named the seal Sally?
B Why did Anna name the seal Sally?
QUESTION 1 — YOU WROTE
"Why did Anna named the seal Sally?"

The word "did" already puts the sentence in the past. So the next verb stays in its base form:

THE RULE
After "did," use the base form of the verb:
• "Why did Anna name the seal?" (not "named")
• "What did he do?" (not "done" or "did")
• "When did they arrive?" (not "arrived")
QUICK TEST
When you write a question with "did," cover up the "did" and read the verb. Does it sound like a base form (name, do, arrive) or a past form (named, done, arrived)? It should be the base form.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Question 1 asked you to change a statement into a question. Getting the verb form right is the key to earning full credit on questions like this.
