Article Choice: “a” vs. “an”

One tiny letter, but it flags your writing as polished

Axel, your Q3 appositive was excellent — you correctly named Peter Tabichi with a long, informative describing phrase. You still earned full credit, but the feedback called out one small article slip:

FROM Q3 — FULL CREDIT, WITH ONE ARTICLE NOTE
"Peter Tabichi, a award-winning science teacher and Global Teacher Prize recipient, is from Kenya, Africa, and teaches high school science."
The appositive works beautifully. The fix: “award” starts with a vowel sound, so it should be an award-winning, not a award-winning.
THE RULE
Before a word that starts with a vowel sound (a, e, i, o, u), use "an".

✓ an award, an apple, an example, an idea, an orange, an umbrella
✗ a award, a apple, a idea

Test: Say the word out loud. If your mouth opens with a vowel sound, use "an."
Which sentence uses the article correctly?
A Peter Tabichi is a award-winning teacher who changed his school.
B Peter Tabichi is an award-winning teacher who changed his school.
C Peter Tabichi is a award-winning, hard-working teacher.
QUICK SELF-CHECK
When you add an appositive or an adjective, look at the first sound of the next word:

✓ an award-winning teacher · an interesting idea · an old building
✓ a teacher · a school · a friend

It is one letter of difference, but it is one of the easiest “polish” wins in English writing.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
You earned full credit on Q3 — but on a stricter rubric, “a award” would cost a conventions point. Locking this in now protects future tests and makes your polished writing read smoother.

MODEL POLISHED PARAGRAPH (19–20/20 level)

In the story "Surprise Kick," the events on the soccer field cause Cody to completely change his opinion about his teammates. At the start of the game, Cody assumed Mud wouldn't be very involved and expected him to make mistakes. However, Mud proved Cody wrong by darting skillfully between defenders and unleashing a powerful shot that won the first point. This mattered because it showed Cody that Amanda wasn't just a backup player — she was confident and skilled under pressure. By the end of the game, Cody was stunned. The events of the game taught Cody not to underestimate his teammates, because both Mud and Amanda turned out to be far better than he had thought.
