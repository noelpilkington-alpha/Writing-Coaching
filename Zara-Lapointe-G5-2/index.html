Step 5 Error: No Comma After "Because"

Q2 lost half a point for an unnecessary comma

Zara, on Q2 you put a comma after "because" that should not be there. This cost you half a point.

Q2 — YOUR ANSWER (1 out of 2)
"...because, she wanted to make the pie perfect."
THE RULE
Never put a comma directly after "because." The word "because" is a conjunction that introduces a reason. It connects the two parts of the sentence without needing a comma after it.

• Wrong: "She smiled because, the pie turned out well."
• Right: "She smiled because the pie turned out well."

You CAN have a comma BEFORE "because" in some cases (when the sentence could be misread without it), but never directly AFTER it.
Which sentence has correct comma usage with "because"?
A Grandma Addie felt proud because, her granddaughter learned the recipe so quickly.
B Grandma Addie felt proud because her granddaughter learned the recipe so quickly.
YOUR STEP 5 CHECK
When proofreading, scan for "because" in your sentences. If there is a comma directly after it, remove it. This is a quick visual scan that takes two seconds per answer.

Step 1 Error: Name the Feeling (Q6)

You told the story but did not name the specific feeling

Zara, on Q6 you scored 2 out of 3. The question asked you to describe how it felt to learn a new skill. You wrote a nice story about the experience, but you never used a word that names the specific feeling (proud, frustrated, excited, satisfied, nervous).

🔎 Step 1 check: Reading the question twice reveals the key word: "How did it feel?" That means your answer needs an emotion word. Telling the story is not enough. You must label the feeling.
THE FIX: NAME IT, THEN DESCRIBE IT
When a question asks "how did it feel" or "describe your feelings," follow this two-step pattern:

1. Name the feeling: "I felt proud" / "It was frustrating" / "I felt a mix of nervous and excited"
2. Then tell the story: Explain what happened that caused that feeling

Without naming: "I kept trying and eventually I got it right." (tells what happened, but no feeling word)
With naming: "I felt really satisfied when I finally got it right after so many attempts. The moment it worked, I knew all the practice had been worth it."
"Describe how you felt when you learned to ride a bike." Which answer earns full points?
A I kept falling off and my dad held the back of the seat. Then one day I rode all the way to the end of the street without help.
B I felt thrilled and proud when I finally rode all the way to the end of the street without anyone holding the seat. After weeks of falling off, that moment made all the scraped knees worth it.
YOUR STEP 1 CHECK
When a question uses words like "how did it feel," "describe your feelings," or "what was it like emotionally," your answer MUST contain at least one feeling word. After writing your answer, check: "Did I name the feeling?" If not, add one sentence at the start.
