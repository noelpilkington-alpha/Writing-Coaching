Add Detail + Explanation

Q11 — 16 out of 20 — passage detail + what it shows
📝
Why 16 out of 20? Your paragraph about porcupines has the right idea, but it needs more specific details from the passage paired with an explanation of what those details show. Right now you state the main idea, but you don't develop it with enough passage evidence.

Brenna, to move from 16/20 to 18 or higher, each body detail needs two parts: what the passage says (a specific fact) and what it shows or why it matters (the explanation). Most students at this level have the first part but forget the second.

The Detail + Explanation Pattern
DETAIL A specific fact from the passage (names, numbers, what happened, what something looks like).
EXPLAIN Why it matters or what it shows about the main idea. This is the sentence most students leave out.
EXAMPLE USING THE PORCUPINE PASSAGE
Detail: "According to the passage, a porcupine has about 30,000 quills covering its back, sides, and tail."
Explanation: "This shows that porcupines have a powerful defense because any predator that gets too close will be hit by thousands of sharp quills."

See how the explanation connects the detail back to why it matters? Without it, the reader just has a number but doesn't understand its importance.
Which version of a paragraph detail is more developed?
A Porcupines have a lot of quills. They use them when animals come near them.
B Porcupines have about 30,000 quills that detach easily when touched, which means even a large predator like a bear will back off after just one painful encounter.
YOUR DEVELOPMENT CHECK
After writing each detail sentence, ask: "Did I explain what this shows or why it matters?" If your sentence just states a fact without connecting it to the main idea, add one more sentence that explains. The pattern is always: passage detail → explanation of why it matters.

The structure that earns 18–20 out of 20

18–20 Paragraph Structure
