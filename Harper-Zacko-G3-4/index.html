Read Each Question All the Way Through

When a prompt gives you a required word, use that exact word

Harper, one of the easiest points to grab is noticing when a prompt tells you to use a specific word. When it does, that word must appear in your answer. Look at Q2 from your test:

FROM G3.4 Q2
Prompt: Combine these two sentences using the word "since".
You wrote: "Layla walked along the dusty path and then she saw a sparkle."
Your sentence makes sense and is well written. But the prompt required the word "since" — and "and then" is a different connector. That is why it earned 1 of 2 points.
WHAT THE PROMPT ASKED FOR
"Layla walked along the dusty path since she was looking for a sparkle."
Same good idea, same clean writing — just using the exact word the prompt required.
THE "REQUIRED WORD" TRICK
When a prompt says "use the word ___" or "combine with ___", do this:

• Find the required word in the prompt and say it in your head.
• Write your sentence so that exact word appears in it.
• Before moving on, point to the word in your answer to double-check it is there.

If the word is not in your answer, the point will not come.
Prompt: Combine these sentences using the word "because". (1) Maya packed a snack. (2) She was hungry.
A Maya packed a snack because she was hungry.
B Maya was hungry and she packed a snack.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Q2 cost you 1 point this test. That 1 point on its own moves you from 68.9% to over 71%. And on any future test, catching the required word is worth at least 1 to 2 points per test with no new writing skills needed.

Explain WHY with a Specific Reason

A good reason comes FROM the story, not from a general phrase

Harper, your short answers mostly nailed it. The two questions where you lost a point (Q6 and Q7) both asked you to explain why. You gave an answer, but the "why" was a general idea instead of a specific reason from the passage.

FROM G3.4 Q7
Prompt: Why did Layla leave the geode in the forest?
You wrote: "Layla left the geode because it is the right thing to do."
This is a reasonable idea, but it just restates that she did the right thing. It does not say WHY it was the right thing — the answer is sitting right there in the story.
WHAT EARNS FULL CREDIT
"Layla left the geode because the guide said to leave only footprints and take only pictures."
Same topic, but now it names the exact reason from the story. That is what the grader is looking for.
The "Answer + BECAUSE + Story Detail" Shape
ANSWER State what happened or what you think
BECAUSE Add the word "because" (or "since")
STORY Name a specific thing the story actually said — a rule, an action, or a detail
Prompt: Why did Layla leave the geode in the forest?
A Layla left the geode because it was the right choice.
B Layla left the geode because the guide's rule said to leave only footprints.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Q6 and Q7 each cost you 1 point. Together that is 2 points — enough to lift your short-answer score from 21/25 to 23/25. On future tests, pointing to a story detail after "because" is worth a steady 2 to 3 points every time.

The Full-Credit Paragraph Shape
TOPIC State your main idea (you already do this well)
DETAIL 1 One specific thing from the passage
DETAIL 2 A second specific thing from the passage
DETAIL 3 A third detail if you have one — bonus points
CLOSING Wrap back to your main idea
