Fix 3: An Appositive Renames with a Noun

Q3: how Sally swam, instead of who Sally is
THE RULE: AN APPOSITIVE IS A NOUN PHRASE THAT RENAMES THE NOUN NEXT TO IT, INSIDE COMMAS
Sally, the baby seal, swam to the other seals. The appositive says who or what Sally is. It does not say how she swam or where. The check: swap the appositive in for the name. The baby seal swam to the other seals is still a sentence, so it passes. Happily swam to the other seals is not, so it fails.
YOU WROTE (Q3, 1 OUT OF 2)
“Sally swam to the other seals happily.” HOW, NOT WHO
A good detail, and the wrong job. Happily says how she swam. The instruction asked for a renaming.
THE APPOSITIVE
Sally, the baby seal, swam to the other seals.
A noun phrase, inside commas, right after the name it renames. You can keep your detail too: Sally, the baby seal, swam happily to the other seals.
