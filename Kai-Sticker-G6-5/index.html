Build a Full Body Paragraph

From 3 sentences to 6 with evidence, explanation, and transitions

Here's your Paragraph 3 rewritten from 3 sentences to 6, using the TS-E1-X1-E2-X2-CS pattern:

YOUR VERSION (3 sentences)
People worked together to map out the dirtiest parts of the river and test the water. They held cleanup events and worked with scientists. They planted plants to help clean the water and prevent soil from washing away.
DEVELOPED VERSION (6 sentences)

TS: Earth5R also took steps to restore the river's natural environment.

E1: According to the passage, the group planted fresh grasses along the riverbanks to filter dirty water and prevent soil from washing away.

X1: This was an important step because it addressed the root cause of the pollution; instead of just removing trash, they created a natural system to keep the water clean.

E2: The passage also states that the water eventually became twenty percent cleaner than before.

X2: As a result, people began visiting the riverbanks again, which showed that the environmental work was making a real difference.

CS: Because of these restoration efforts, the Mithi River went from a polluted waterway to a place the community could enjoy again.

WHAT CHANGED (HIGHLIGHTED)
Specific evidence: "planted fresh grasses" and "twenty percent cleaner" replace vague summaries with concrete details from the passage.

Sentence strategies: X1 uses a semicolon to join two related ideas into one compound sentence, making it more sophisticated.

Cause-and-effect transitions: "As a result" and "Because of" show why the evidence matters. These are stronger than just listing facts because they explain the connection between ideas.

Your Proofreading Checklist

Development-focused — your conventions are already clean

Your spelling and grammar are solid — so your checklist focuses entirely on development, which is where all your lost points come from:

Does my introduction have a thesis that names 3 points?
Read your last sentence. Can you hear three clear topics? If not, add them: "The cleanup showed the power of [1], [2], and [3]."
Does each body paragraph have at least 5 sentences?
Count them. If a paragraph has only 2-3 sentences, it's too short. Add another piece of evidence and an explanation.
Did I use specific numbers or facts from the passage?
Not "the river got cleaner" but "twenty percent cleaner." Not "they removed trash" but "fourteen tonnes of trash." Specific details earn more points.
Did I explain every piece of evidence?
After each fact from the passage, look for "This shows that..." or "As a result..." If it's missing, add it. Evidence without explanation doesn't earn full points.
Is my conclusion more than one sentence?
Restate your 3 main points in different words, then end with a broader statement. Aim for 2-3 sentences minimum.
Fix comma splices: if you have 3 ideas joined by commas, split them.
Your P4 has: "teamwork can help, you shouldn't wait, and small efforts add up" — three clauses joined with commas. Split into separate sentences or use semicolons.
