Your #1 Pattern: Explain WHY, Not Just WHAT

Q6, Q8, and Q9 all name something but don't explain it

Annabelle, on three different questions you gave the right idea but stopped before explaining it. Here is the pattern:

Q6 — YOUR ANSWER (2 out of 3)
"Layla's actions tell that she is kind and caring and doesn't like to lie."

The problem: You name the traits (kind, caring, honest) but you do not explain which actions show this. The question asks what her actions tell us, so you need to point to a specific thing she did.

A STRONGER ANSWER
"Layla's actions tell us she is kind and caring because when she finds the geode, she puts it back instead of keeping it. This shows she does not like to take things that belong to nature."

The same pattern on Q8:

Q8 — YOUR ANSWER (2 out of 3)
"I think the most important thing in this story was when Layla put back the geode."

The problem: You name the moment (putting back the geode) but you do not explain why it is important. Adding "because..." finishes the thought.

A STRONGER ANSWER
"I think the most important thing in this story was when Layla put back the geode because it shows she cares more about doing the right thing than keeping a treasure for herself."

And Q9:

Q9 — YOUR ANSWER (2 out of 3)
"The breeze and birds seemed to guide her."

The problem: You say nature guided her, but you do not explain how the breeze and birds helped her make a decision.

A STRONGER ANSWER
"The breeze and birds seemed to guide her because when she felt the breeze and heard the birds sing, it helped her feel calm enough to decide to put the geode back where it belongs."
THE FIX: ADD "BECAUSE" + YOUR REASON
Every time you write a claim or name something, finish the sentence with "because..." and explain your thinking.

• Name the thing → then add "because" → then explain WHY or HOW
• This one extra sentence is worth a full point on every question.

Spelling + Capitalization

Q11 errors: "irch" and missing capital letter

Annabelle, your Q11 paragraph has two mechanical errors that cost points. These are not about your ideas. They are about catching mistakes before you submit.

Q11 — SPELLING ERROR
"Games like golf and cricket were played by irch people."

The fix: The word should be "rich" not "irch." The letters got swapped. When you write quickly, the "r" and "i" can flip.

Q11 — CAPITALIZATION ERROR
"...by irch people. then later on more people started..."

The fix: After a period, the next word must start with a capital letter. It should be "Then" not "then."

TWO THINGS TO CHECK BEFORE SUBMITTING
1. Spelling: If a word looks strange when you read it back, try saying it slowly in your head. "Irch" does not sound like a word. "Rich" does.

2. Capitals after periods: Every time you see a period, check that the very next word starts with a capital letter. No exceptions.
You just wrote: "people played sports. then it became popular." What needs fixing?
A Change "then" to "Then" because it comes after a period.
B Add a comma after "sports" instead of a period.
THE PAYOFF
Fixing "irch" to "rich" and capitalizing "Then" takes five seconds during a proofread pass. These small fixes protect points you have already earned with your ideas.

Detail + Explanation

Q11 at 13 out of 20 needs more developed points

Annabelle, your paragraph earned 13 out of 20. You have the right idea and some details from the passage. To score higher, each detail needs a "what it shows" or "why it matters" sentence after it.

THE PATTERN: DETAIL + EXPLANATION
Every time you include a detail from the passage, follow it with a sentence that explains what it shows or why it matters.

Detail: A fact or example from the passage.
Explanation: What that detail shows or why it matters to your point.

Here is an example using the "From Sports and Society" passage:

Detail + Explanation Pattern
Detail Games like golf and cricket were first played by rich people in the 1700s.
Explains This shows that modern sports started as activities only a few people could enjoy.
Detail Later on, more people started playing sports as rules were written down and shared.
Explains This matters because once the rules were shared, anyone could learn to play, not just wealthy people.

Notice: each detail gets its own explanation sentence. That is how you develop your ideas fully.
