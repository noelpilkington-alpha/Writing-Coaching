The Full-Credit Paragraph Recipe

How 13/20 becomes 18–20/20

Bobby, a full-credit paragraph on Question 11 almost always has the same shape. Here is the recipe:

The 6-Part Paragraph
TOPIC Your central idea, in one sentence (what the whole paragraph is about).
DETAIL 1 A specific fact from the article.
SHOWS "This shows..." — one sentence explaining what detail 1 means.
DETAIL 2 A second specific fact from the article.
SHOWS "This shows..." — one sentence explaining what detail 2 means.
CLOSING Connect back to the central idea in a new way.
★ Your paragraph already had: a topic sentence, a closing, and full sentences. That is a big head start. The missing pieces are a second detail and "this shows" sentences that explain what each detail means.
Which middle of a paragraph would earn more points?
A Cars and trucks cannot travel across the rough land of Garissa, so they use camels to carry food and books.
B First, cars and trucks cannot travel across the rough land of Garissa, so the library uses camels to reach far-away villages. This shows the camels do something no other vehicle can do. Second, the camels carry books, food, and medicine. This shows the library helps people in more ways than just reading.
THE MAGIC PHRASE: "THIS SHOWS..."
After every fact you pull from the article, add one more sentence that starts with "This shows..." and explains what that fact means for the people in the story.

Fact: Camels can cross rough land.
This shows: the library can reach people that trucks cannot.

Two facts + two "this shows" sentences = roughly 4–7 extra points on Q11.

The Small Stuff in Your Paragraph

A few quick fixes that add up to real points

Your paragraph had the right ideas, but a handful of small slips cost partial credit. Let us walk through them so you can catch them on G3.3.

★ SLIP 1 — Capitalization after "because": After a conjunction like "because," the next word stays lowercase unless it is a proper noun (like a person or place name).
FROM YOUR G3.2 PARAGRAPH
"The Camel Mobile Library has affected the people in Garissa because It helps carry useful things for people."
FROM YOUR G3.2 PARAGRAPH
"One reason its important is because Cars and trucks cannot travel across the rough land of Garissa..."

In both spots, the word after "because" should start lowercase: it and cars. "Because" connects two ideas inside one sentence — it does not start a new sentence, so the next word is not capitalized.

★ SLIP 2 — "its" vs. "it's": it's (with an apostrophe) = "it is." its (no apostrophe) = "belongs to it." You meant "it is important."
FROM YOUR G3.2 PARAGRAPH
"One reason its important is because..."

Read it out loud the long way: "it is important." If that works, you need the apostrophe: it's.

★ SLIP 3 — List commas: When you list three or more things, put a comma between each one.
FROM YOUR G3.2 PARAGRAPH
"...so they used Camels to carry food medicine and books."

Fix: "food, medicine, and books." Three items means two commas.

Which version is written correctly?
A The library helps Garissa because It carries food medicine and books.
B The library helps Garissa because it carries food, medicine, and books.
YOUR 2-MINUTE PARAGRAPH PROOFREAD
Before you submit G3.3, read your Q11 paragraph out loud one time and ask:

1. After every "because," is the next word lowercase (unless it is a name or place)?
2. Where I wrote "its," did I mean "it is"? If yes, add the apostrophe.
3. In any list of three things, is there a comma between each one?
4. Did I spell the article's main place/person name the same way the article does?
