One Sentence, One Stop

Two complete thoughts need a period, not a comma
🔍 Pattern alert: The same small slip cost you points on both Q5 and Q10 — two complete thoughts joined with only a comma. Fix this one habit and you win back points across the short answers.

Asher, when two complete thoughts each could stand alone as a sentence, a comma is not strong enough to hold them together. That join is called a comma splice. Here are your two from G4.1:

YOU WROTE — G4.1 Q5 (combine with because/but/so)
"His dedication has transformed the school, because of his efforts, enrollment has doubled, and more students are now attending college."
"His dedication has transformed the school" and "enrollment has doubled" are each complete thoughts mashed together with commas.
YOU WROTE — G4.1 Q10 (one skill that helps your community)
"One skill that I have that could help others in my community, is picking up trash, I am very good at picking up trash and I also find it fun."
"...is picking up trash" is a complete thought, and "I am very good..." is a second one — joined by a comma.
Which version is written correctly?
A I am very good at picking up trash, I also find it fun.
B I am very good at picking up trash. I also find it fun.
THE RULE
If both halves of your sentence could each stand alone as a complete sentence, a comma is not enough.

The fix: end the first thought with a period, then start a new sentence with a capital letter.

✗ "Enrollment has doubled, more students are attending college."
✓ "Enrollment has doubled. More students are now attending college."

One Sentence That Lands

Questions 6–10 want one complete sentence with a reason
🔍 Pattern alert: On Q7 your answer drifted into extra sentences and lost focus. Questions 6–10 each ask for one complete sentence that answers the question and gives a reason.

Asher, Questions 6–10 ask for a single sentence that answers the question and includes a reason, usually with a word like "because." When an answer spreads into several sentences, the main point gets buried and the answer loses focus. The trick is to say it once, clearly, and stop.

YOUR ONE-SENTENCE SHAPE
[Your answer] because [your reason from the passage].

That single sentence covers both parts of the question — what you think and why — without rambling into a second or third sentence.
Which is the stronger Question 6–10 answer?
A I think Peter showed courage. He stayed calm, that helped a lot. Also he was kind to everyone and that is good too.
B Peter showed courage because he kept teaching and helping his students even when his school had very few resources.
THE RULE
Answer in one complete sentence that does two jobs at once: it states your answer, and it gives a reason or example using a word like "because." You do not need a second sentence — the "because" part already covers the "why."
THE FOCUS CHECK
After you write a short answer, read it back and ask: "Is this ONE complete sentence that answers the question and gives a reason?" If you wrote two or three sentences, pick the strongest one and cut the rest.
SO WHAT? WHY DOES THIS MATTER?
Each Question 6–10 is worth 3 points. A focused single sentence with a clear reason earns full credit; a rambling answer leaves points behind even when your idea is good.
